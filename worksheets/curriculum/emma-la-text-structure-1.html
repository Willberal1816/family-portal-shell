How Is This Text Built?

Emma · Language Arts · Independent Work
Directions: Identify how each short text is organized. Then explain how that structure helps the author communicate the important idea.
Text A: Heavy rain filled the creek faster than the ground could absorb it. As a result, water spread across the walking path. Park workers later added plants and shallow drainage areas so more rainwater could soak into the soil before reaching the path.
Text B: The old park path was narrow and uneven. The redesigned path is wider and smoother. Both paths follow the creek, but the new path also includes benches and signs.
1. What structure best describes Text A: description, chronology, cause-effect, problem-solution, or compare-contrast? Give two clues from the text.
2. What structure best describes Text B? Give two clues.
3. Explain why the two authors might choose different structures even though both texts are about the same park.
4. Rewrite the main idea of Text A as a one-sentence cause-and-effect statement.
5. Rewrite the main idea of Text B as a one-sentence comparison.
6. Choose a familiar topic. Write two short 3–4 sentence paragraphs about it using two different structures. Label each structure and explain how the structure changes what seems important.

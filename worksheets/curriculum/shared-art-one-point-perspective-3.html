One-Point Perspective: Make Space Look Deep

Art · Independent Practice
Materials: pencil, eraser, and ruler/straight edge if available. Keep construction lines light until you check them.
1. Box warm-up. Use the dashed horizon line. Mark one vanishing point. Draw one box below the horizon and one above it. Every receding edge must aim at the same vanishing point.
2. Road or hallway. Make two edges move toward the same vanishing point. Add at least four repeated features—floor tiles, fence posts, lights, windows, or road marks—that appear closer together as they move away.
3. Room scene. Draw a simple room or indoor space using one-point perspective. Include at least three objects or architectural features, such as a door, window, table, shelf, rug, or picture frame. Keep front-facing verticals vertical and horizontals horizontal.
4. Self-check. Name one place where your perspective works well and one place you corrected after checking the vanishing point.
5. Connect to the last Art lesson. Choose one object in your room scene. Add light and shadow using one consistent light direction. Explain how perspective creates depth differently from shading.

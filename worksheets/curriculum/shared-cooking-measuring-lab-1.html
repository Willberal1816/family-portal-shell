Kitchen Measuring Lab

Emma + Natalie · Cooking / Life Skills
Directions: Use water for the hands-on measurements unless a parent chooses another safe ingredient. Wipe spills as you go.

Kitchen math

1. Write two ways to make 3/4 cup using common measures.
2. How many teaspoons are in 4 tablespoons? Show the relationship.
3. How can you measure 1 1/2 cups with 1-cup and 1/2-cup measures?
4. If 2/3 cup is doubled, what quantity is needed? Explain.

Water lab

5. Measure 1/2 cup water, pour into a 1-cup measure, and check the level. Record small differences and possible causes.
6. Measure 1/4 cup twice and compare the combined amount with 1/2 cup.
7. Make 1 tablespoon using teaspoons. How many did it take?
8. View the same liquid level from above, below, and eye level. Which view is trustworthy and why?

Technique decisions

9. Why can tightly packing flour into a cup change a recipe?
10. Why does “packed” matter when a recipe specifies packed brown sugar?
11. A cook uses 2 teaspoons instead of 2 tablespoons. How much smaller is the amount?

Apply

12. Emma: Design a pretend snack recipe with at least four measured ingredients. Circle each fraction and choose sensible tools.
13. Natalie: Scale 3/4 cup oats, 2/3 cup milk, and 1 1/2 tablespoons honey from 1 batch to 1 1/2 batches. Show arithmetic and tools.
14. Clean and dry tools. Name the measuring habit that mattered most today.

Light & Shadow: Make Forms Look 3D

Emma + Natalie · Art · Practice
Directions: Use the printed forms and frames. Keep one light direction consistent. Do not redraw the setup unless the question specifically asks you to create a form.
1. Light-direction check. The arrows show the light direction. On each printed form, mark H where the highlight belongs, S where the darkest form shadow belongs, and draw the cast-shadow direction.
2. Sphere study. Shade the printed sphere with a smooth value change, a clear highlight, core/form shadow, reflected light, and cast shadow. Reflected light must stay darker than the highlight.
3. Cube planes. In the three boxes below, shade the top, front, and side planes as light, middle, and dark values. Then write one sentence explaining why flat planes can change value more abruptly than a sphere.
Light planeMiddle planeDark plane
4. Cylinder. In the frame, draw a cylinder and shade it from a light band into a darker curved side. Add a cast shadow that agrees with your light direction.
5. Observation drawing. Choose one real object with a simple form. Draw it in the frame and use at least five distinguishable values. Mark the light direction with a small arrow.
6. Compare your drawing to the real object. Name one value relationship that matches and one place you would revise.

Clause Decision Recheck

For 1–5: bracket every whole clause directly in the sentence, label each clause I or D, and name the sentence type. Under every D label, write 2–4 words showing what makes that thought incomplete.

1. We packed the cooler, and Dad loaded the chairs.
2. Because the sidewalk was wet, I wore my old shoes.
3. The lights came on when the sun went down.
4. Although I wanted to stay, the store closed, so we headed home.
5. I called my sister, but she was reading after dinner ended.
6. Correct the reasoning. A student labeled When the movie ended as I and we turned on the lights as I, then called the whole sentence compound. Write the correct I/D labels and sentence type. In 1–2 sentences, explain exactly why one of the original labels was wrong.
7. Compare the patterns.
A. The rain stopped, so we went outside.
B. When the rain stopped, we went outside.

For A and B, bracket and label every clause and name each sentence type. Then write one sentence explaining how so and when change the I/D pattern.

8. Build a fresh example. Write one compound-complex sentence with exactly two independent clauses and one dependent clause. Bracket all three clauses and label them I, I, and D. Use punctuation that matches the structure.
9. Explain the stand-alone test. In 2–3 sentences, explain what happens when you read an independent clause by itself, what happens when you read a dependent clause by itself, and how those results help you name the sentence type.

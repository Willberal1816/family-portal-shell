Strong Details — Show the Reader

For each answer, follow the exact amount requested. Strong details must be specific, relevant to the main idea, and useful to the reader.
1. Make one action visible. Rewrite “The cat did something funny” as one complete sentence showing one exact action.
2. Add two observable details. Rewrite “We had bad weather” as two complete sentences. Include at least two things a person could see, hear, or measure.
3. Choose a precise verb. Replace went in “The horse went across the muddy field.” Then write one sentence explaining what your new verb helps the reader picture.
4. Test relevance.
Main idea: The hike was exhausting. Possible details: steep switchbacks; loose rocks; bright orange shoelaces; heavy backpack.
Cross out the detail that does not help the main idea. Then write 2 sentences: one explaining why it does not belong and one explaining how a different detail supports the main idea.

5. Support one main idea. Main idea: “The kitchen was busy before dinner.” Write four specific supporting details. At least two must use precise action verbs.
6. Show without naming the feeling. Write 3 complete sentences showing that a character is excited without using the words excited, happy, or glad.
7. Improve this weak paragraph.
The park was fun. We did stuff. It was nice outside. I liked it.
Rewrite it as 5–6 complete sentences with one clear main idea and at least four specific, relevant details. Underline two precise verbs in your revision.
Tracking #
8. Error analysis. A writer adds five adjectives to every noun but never gives an action, fact, or example. Write 2–3 sentences explaining why the writing can still feel vague and name one better revision move.
9. Write a full paragraph. Choose a familiar place, activity, meal, animal, or event. Write a 7–9 sentence paragraph with one clear main idea and at least five specific supporting details. After writing, circle your main-idea sentence and underline three precise nouns or verbs.
10. Final revision check. Reread #9. Write two sentences: first name one detail that strongly supports your main idea; then name one word or sentence you revised to make the paragraph clearer.

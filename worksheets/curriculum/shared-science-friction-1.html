Friction Test

Emma + Natalie · Science
Directions: Use a safe parent-approved setup. Use the same object and setup for every surface. Do at least three trials per condition. Predict means choose what you expect before testing and give one reason. Evidence means use your actual measured numbers. Explain means give the answer and one sentence connecting it to friction or the test setup.

Plan

1. Question: How does surface material affect slide distance? Identify independent variable, measured outcome, and four controls.
2. Choose 3 safe surfaces. Before testing, name which surface you predict will resist motion most and write one sentence giving your reason.
3. Describe exactly how you will release/move the object the same way each time.

Collect

4. Surface 1: record three distances with units and average.
5. Surface 2: record three distances with units and average.
6. Surface 3: record three distances with units and average.

Analyze

7. Rank the surfaces from shortest average travel to longest. Then write one sentence saying what that pattern suggests about relative friction in your test.
8. If one trial is noticeably different, copy its measurement and name one setup or measurement reason that could have caused the difference. If none is unusual, write "none."
9. Write a claim and support it with at least two actual measurements.

Extend

10. Emma: Draw a bar graph of average travel distance for the three surfaces. Label axes/units.
11. Natalie: Write two sentences: first, explain how shorter travel can support a claim of more motion-resisting friction in this setup; second, explain why slide distance is not a direct force measurement in newtons.
12. Write one sentence naming the direction friction acts compared with the sliding motion and how that contributes to the object slowing down.

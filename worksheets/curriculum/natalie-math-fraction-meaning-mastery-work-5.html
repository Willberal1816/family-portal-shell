Fraction Division — Same-Size Chunks

Independent evidence. For every story problem, identify what the quotient counts before calculating.

1. Snack cups. A container holds 3 12 cups of fruit. Each snack cup holds 14 cup. How many snack cups can be filled? Convert the mixed number, solve, label the quotient, and multiply back to check.

2. Garden rows. A strip of garden is 5 13 meters long. Each equal row section is 23 meter. How many sections fit? Show the mixed-number conversion and reciprocal correctly. State the answer as a number of sections.
Total = ______   One group = ______   Quotient counts = ______
3. Which label is correct? Maya has 4 liters of juice and pours 25-liter servings. She calculates 4 ÷ 25 = 10. Circle the correct interpretation: 10 liters / 10 servings / 10 liters per serving. Then explain in one complete sentence why.

4. Batch meaning. You have 2 25 cups of rice. A recipe uses 45 cup per batch. Solve. Then write separately: (a) number of full batches, and (b) what any fractional part of the quotient means.

5. Find and fix both mistakes. A student writes 3 34 = 64, then 64 ÷ 38 = 64 × 38. Correct the mixed-number conversion and the reciprocal step, finish the calculation, and state what the quotient would count if this were pieces of ribbon.
6. Reason before calculating. Without solving first, decide whether 4 12 ÷ 34 should be greater than or less than 4 12. Give one sentence using group size. Then solve and compare your result with your prediction.

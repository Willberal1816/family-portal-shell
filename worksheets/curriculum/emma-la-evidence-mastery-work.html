Reference box
Inference: what you figure out from the text.
Clue: the exact word, action, or detail that supports the inference.
Strongest clue: the detail that most directly supports the idea you are explaining.
1.
Maya checked the clock twice and kept her backpack by the door.

a) What can you figure out about Maya?

b) Underline or copy one exact clue from the printed sentence that supports your answer.

2.
Leo carried his model carefully, fixed one loose piece, and checked it twice.

Circle or underline the clue that best shows the model matters to Leo. Then explain in one sentence why that clue is stronger than the other details.

3. Show, do not tell: write three sentences that show someone is impatient. Do not use the words impatient, mad, angry, annoyed, or frustrated. Underline two actions/details that are clues.
4. What should a reader figure out from the two clues you underlined in #3? Make sure the inference matches the clues you actually wrote.

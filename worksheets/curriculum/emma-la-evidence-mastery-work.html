Reference: An inference is an idea you figure out from the text. A clue is an exact word, action, or detail that supports the inference. The strongest clue is the detail that supports the idea most directly.

Find and connect clues

1.
Maya checked the clock twice, kept her backpack by the door, and looked outside every few minutes.

a) What can you infer about Maya? b) Underline one exact clue. c) Explain in one sentence how that clue supports your inference.

2.
Leo carried his model carefully, fixed one loose piece, and checked it twice before setting it down.

Circle the strongest clue that the model matters to Leo. Then explain why that clue is stronger than one of the other details.

3.
Ava put on her shoes before anyone asked, stood beside the door, and looked out the window every minute.

Write one inference and copy two details that support it. Then star the stronger of your two clues.

4.
Ben folded the paper carefully, erased one crooked line, and started the whole drawing again.

Reject weak inferences

5.
Sara whispers, keeps looking toward the hallway, and hides a gift bag behind her chair.

A student writes, “Sara is bored.” Explain why that inference does not fit the printed clues. Then write a better inference.

Student answer: “The strongest clue is that Nina smiled because smiles are in stories.” Write two specific problems with that explanation, then rewrite it so the clue connects to a clear inference.

Create and transfer

7.

Show, do not tell: write three sentences that show a character is relieved without using the word relieved. Underline two actions/details that are clues.

Kinds of Sentences — Clause Transfer

What you are learning

A clause has a subject and a verb. First find each whole clause. Then use the stand-alone test.

I Independent clause = can stand alone as a complete sentence.

D Dependent clause = has a subject and verb but cannot stand alone.

Worked example: Because the rain stopped, we walked to the park, and we stayed until sunset.

D: Because the rain stopped

I: we walked to the park

I: we stayed until sunset

Pattern D + I + I. It contains a dependent clause and two independent clauses. The important first step is finding the whole clauses, not just naming nouns or verbs.

Punctuation follows the structure

Introductory dependent clause → comma: “When the bell rang, we left.”

Two independent clauses joined by and, but, so, or, yet, for, nor → comma before the joining word: “I packed, and Mia checked the list.”

On the printable, mark every whole clause I or D before naming the sentence type.

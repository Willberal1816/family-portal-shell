Community News: Separate Notice, Quote and Report

Natalie | Language Arts | Friday, November 27, 2026 | 55 minutes

What you are learning: Write exact attributed quotations, accurate paraphrases, and source-specific claims in a fresh news context.

Learn First

A direct quotation uses the exact words of the speaker inside quotation marks. A paraphrase expresses a source’s meaning in your own words without quotation marks. Both must name the source. Changing a word inside quotation marks makes the quote inaccurate.

A posted notice documents what is announced, not necessarily what happened afterward. A participant’s statement documents that person’s account. A log gives what its observer recorded. A single statement does not prove a whole group did something.

Use a claim audit: write the claim, name the exact source, then decide whether it is supported or needs correction. If you cannot verify the result, write a limited sentence rather than guessing.

Two worked examples

Notice: "Workshop starts at 2 p.m." Direct quote from Lee: "I made one model." Accurate paraphrase: Lee reported making one model. Incorrect quote: "I made a model" (one was changed).
Log: 10 signed in; Lee said, "I made one model." Supported: the log lists ten sign-ins, and Lee says one model was made. Unsupported: all ten made a model. A completed-model inventory could test that claim.

Source and materials

FICTIONAL REPAIR CAFE RECORD
Posted notice: Repair cafe scheduled Saturday, 1:00-3:00. Bring one item.
Sign-in log: 12 people signed in; 8 repair forms submitted.
Helper Kim: "I repaired two torn bags."
Visitor Jo: "My lamp still needs a new bulb."
No record of the total successful repairs or every visitor’s experience.

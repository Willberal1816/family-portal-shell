Aquarium Log: Three Clear Evidence Sentences

Emma · Language Arts · Thursday, October 29, 2026 · 55 minutes

What you will be able to do: Use semicolons correctly and write exactly three factual sentences without pretending an event caused a measurement change.

Learn First — follow these three steps

  1. A semicolon can join two related COMPLETE sentences. Each side must have its own subject and verb: 'The keeper checked the meter; the assistant wrote down the reading.'
  2. Two events occurring before or after a measured change do not prove what caused the change. Words such as because, made, proved, or caused require evidence that tests the explanation.
  3. A complete report has (1) one factual action, (2) the two exact measurements and times, and (3) one statement of what the log does NOT prove. Count your final three sentences.

See the exact worked examples

Worked example A: 'At 8 a.m., the keeper checked the pump; a coworker wrote down the reading.' Each side stands alone. An incorrect version is 'The keeper checked the pump; and a coworker...'—remove and.
Worked example B — three complete sentences: 'The crew moved the bucket at 9 a.m. The water was 20°C at noon and 18°C the next morning. The log does not show what caused that difference.' None of these claims invents a cause.
Check this common mistake: Common mistake: 'The filter was cleaned, so the fish became healthier.' The log may report filter cleaning, but cannot prove a health effect without the relevant evidence.

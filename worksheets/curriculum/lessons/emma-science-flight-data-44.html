Flight Lab: Compare Trials Fairly

Emma · Science · Wednesday, November 11, 2026 · 50 minutes

Today's goal: Read a flight-test table and explain why changing one thing makes a comparison easier to trust.

Learn First

  1. A TEST is FAIR when you keep most conditions the same and deliberately change one variable.
  2. A variable is something that can change. In these sample trials the design changes, while launch position and straw stay the same.
  3. A measurement describes something observed. Here distance is measured in feet, not a personal opinion.
  4. Repeated trials tell us whether the result is consistent; three trials are stronger evidence than one lucky attempt.
  5. Do not claim the design always works better everywhere; these table results came from only this sample setup.

See the complete models

Model 1: Rocket A goes 6, 7 and 5 feet. Its mean distance is (6+7+5)/3 = 6 feet.
Model 2: Rocket B goes 8, 9 and 7 feet. Its mean is 8 feet; B averages 2 feet farther in THIS test. Same starting point and launch direction are important controls.
A mistake to avoid: If someone launches B closer to the finish line, the comparison is unfair even if the numbers look larger.

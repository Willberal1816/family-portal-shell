One-Point Perspective: Make Space Look Deep

Emma + Natalie · Art · Learn First
What you are learning: Use a horizon line, one vanishing point, and converging guide lines to make a flat page look like it has depth.

Three anchors

Horizon line: your eye level. Vanishing point: the point on the horizon where receding parallel edges appear to meet. Orthogonal lines: light guide lines that travel from object corners toward the vanishing point.

Start with a box: Draw a square below the horizon. Lightly connect its visible corners to the vanishing point. Choose where the box ends, draw the back vertical and horizontal edges, then erase guide lines that should not remain visible.

What stays straight?

In a basic one-point-perspective room, vertical edges stay vertical and front-facing horizontal edges stay horizontal. Only edges moving away from you aim toward the vanishing point.

Depth clues:
  • Objects farther away appear smaller.
  • Equal spaces seem to get closer together as they recede.
  • Guide lines should all agree on the same vanishing point.
  • Draw lightly first; darken final edges after checking the structure.
Error check: If a floor tile edge points to one place and a table edge points somewhere else, the room will feel twisted. Trace each receding edge with your finger: it should lead to the same vanishing point.

Town Hall Records: Which Place and Date?

Natalie · Social Studies · Thursday, October 29, 2026 · 45 minutes

What you will be able to do: Use fresh records to name an exact location and date, compare numbers, and limit claims to the time and place actually observed.

Learn First — follow these three steps

  1. Every record belongs to a specific place and time. Say BOTH when making a historical claim.
  2. A reported number tells what was written in that record; it does not automatically apply to every town or every year.
  3. To compare sources, identify what both cover, what only one covers, and which conclusion would require more evidence.

See the exact worked examples

Worked example A: 'North Pier sign-in book, June 5, 1942: 17 visitors.' Supported: 'Seventeen visitors were listed at North Pier on June 5, 1942.' Unsupported: 'All piers had 17 visitors throughout 1942.'
Worked example B: One day at one location gives a narrow picture. A safe limitation: 'This book cannot tell us the number of visitors in the whole region or on other days.'
Check this common mistake: Common mistake: taking a place name from one source and a date from another and presenting them as one observation.

Read the class source and evidence

Source/data for this class (printed on BOTH pages):

Two fictional town records for evidence practice:

Record A — Hilltown Library door count, April 12, 1934: 'Morning visitors: 18. Afternoon visitors: 25. Total: 43.'
Record B — Hilltown Library door count, April 13, 1934: 'Morning visitors: 20. Afternoon visitors: 30. Total: 50.'

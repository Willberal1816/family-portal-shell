Laundry Lab: Sort and Fold Real Clothes

Emma · Cycle B Life Skills · Friday, October 30, 2026 · 60 minutes

Today you will do: Sort at least six REAL household garments by label and safe wash grouping, fold four items neatly, and demonstrate a finished organized stack or basket.
Materials and safety: Six clean or used clothing pieces (at least two different fabric types when available), a flat bed/table, a safe basket or two labeled areas, pencil and two work pages. No detergent, washer, dryer or heat required. Ask an adult before moving anyone else's clothes.

Watch the real demonstrations before touching clothes

1. How to sort everyday laundry — by kids

Source and playback fallback: Homegrown School — Sorting Laundry. Today you only sort and fold; DO NOT operate a washer or dryer.

2. How to fold clothing

Source and playback fallback: Homegrown School — Laundry Week, Folding.

Learn First — follow these steps in order

  1. Watch the laundry-sorting video and the separate folding demonstration. Pause at the 'sort' and 'fold' sections. You will SORT and FOLD actual clothes, not operate a machine.
  2. Gather at least six safe ordinary garments with a parent’s permission. Check each care label where one is present. Do NOT unfold private clothing or handle soiled/unsafe items.
  3. Make two areas: 'wash together' and 'ask an adult / special care.' Group similar care instructions, colors and washing needs; do not assume an unknown label is safe to wash.
  4. Fold four clean garments on a flat surface. For a T-shirt: front down; fold each sleeve inward; fold bottom toward neck once or twice; turn over and straighten the edges. Fold socks in pairs without stretching tightly.
  5. Arrange the finished folded pieces into one neat stack or basket. Show a parent your finished object, ask about anything labeled 'dry clean' or special, then complete the six record questions.
  6. Return all clothes to their proper locations. You do not have to run a washing machine. This is a practical SORT and FOLD assignment.

Two completed examples

Worked example A — sorting: a dark cotton T-shirt and a dark cotton sock with matching care labels can be listed together as a possible load. A wool garment with 'hand wash only' is placed in 'ask an adult / special care' rather than washed by guessing.
Worked example B — finished work: 2 folded T-shirts + 1 folded towel + 1 folded pair of socks = four organized items in one visible basket. The printed chart records the real items; it does not replace doing the task.

What your REAL finished work should look like

Your finished activity: Two clearly marked sorting areas and one stack/basket with at least four visibly folded pieces.

Supply-free teaching case: Labels can say 'machine wash cold', 'wash separately', 'hand wash', or 'dry clean'. Do not attempt unfamiliar care instructions without an adult.

Turn-in instructions

Do the real sorting and folding first, show the result to a parent, then print and finish the SIX numbered questions across TWO work pages. Scan BOTH sheets with the Tracking number visible and Finish this class. Do not substitute written answers for the physical activity; if you do not have enough garments, mark Need Help and ask an adult for permission.

Time guide: video + examples 15 minutes; real sorting and folding 25 minutes; record, check and scan 20 minutes. Adult needed for permission, unusual labels, and finish check only.

Fraction Division 2

Natalie · Math · Learn First
What you are learning: Divide mixed numbers and fractions by reasoning about groups, using reciprocals correctly, and checking whether answers make sense.

Learn the idea

Fraction division still means how many groups? If you ask 4 1/2 ÷ 1 1/2, you are asking how many groups of 1 1/2 fit inside 4 1/2. Thinking about the groups first gives the procedure a meaning instead of turning the work into a memorized flip-and-multiply trick.

When a mixed number appears, rewrite it as an improper fraction before multiplying. For 2 3/4, multiply the whole number by the denominator, add the numerator, and keep the denominator: 2 3/4 = 11/4. Then identify the divisor—the number you are dividing by. Only the divisor changes to its reciprocal.

The reciprocal works because division asks for an inverse operation. Dividing by 3/4 is the same as asking how many three-fourths-sized groups fit. Multiplying by 4/3 scales by the inverse amount. Estimate before calculating: dividing by a positive number less than 1 should make the quotient larger; dividing by a number greater than 1 should usually make it smaller.

Check a difficult quotient with multiplication. If 7/3 ÷ 2/3 = 7/2, then 7/2 × 2/3 should return 7/3. In word problems, keep units attached so the quotient has meaning: pieces, batches, miles per segment, and so on.

Words you will use

  • dividend: the amount being divided
  • divisor: the amount you are dividing by
  • quotient: the result of division
  • reciprocal: a fraction with numerator and denominator switched

Worked examples

Example 1: 4 1/2 ÷ 1 1/2 = 9/2 ÷ 3/2 = 9/2 × 2/3 = 3. Check: three groups of 1 1/2 equal 4 1/2.
Example 2: 2 2/3 ÷ 4/5 = 8/3 × 5/4 = 10/3 = 3 1/3. Since 4/5 is less than 1, a quotient larger than 2 2/3 is reasonable.
Example 3: A 6 1/4-foot ribbon cut into 1 1/4-foot pieces gives 25/4 ÷ 5/4 = 5 pieces.

Guided checks

Before calculating, should 3 1/2 ÷ 7/8 be greater or less than 3 1/2?
Greater, because the divisor is less than 1.
Which number is flipped in 5/6 ÷ 2 1/2?
The divisor: 2 1/2 = 5/2, so use 2/5.
How can multiplication check a division answer?
Multiply quotient × divisor and see whether it returns the dividend.

Before you practice

Strong work shows: mixed-number conversions, only the divisor reciprocated, simplification, units, and a reasonableness check.

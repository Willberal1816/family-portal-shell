Fraction Division 2

Natalie · Math · Learn First
What you are learning: Divide mixed numbers and fractions by thinking about how many groups fit, using the reciprocal correctly, and checking whether the answer makes sense.

Learn the idea

Fraction division still means how many groups fit? If you ask 4 12 ÷ 1 12, you are asking how many groups of 1 12 fit inside 4 12.

When a mixed number appears, rewrite it as one fraction before multiplying. For 2 34, multiply the whole number by the denominator, add the numerator, and keep the denominator: 2 34 = 114. Then identify the divisor—the number you are dividing by. Only the divisor changes to its reciprocal.

The reciprocal works because dividing by 34 asks how many three-fourths-sized groups fit. Multiplying by 43 uses the inverse group size. Estimate before calculating: dividing by a positive number less than 1 should make the answer larger; dividing by a number greater than 1 should usually make it smaller.

Check a difficult answer with multiplication. If 73 ÷ 23 = 72, then 72 × 23 should return 73. In word problems, keep units attached so the answer has meaning: pieces, batches, miles per segment, and so on.

Words you will use

  • dividend: the amount being divided
  • divisor: the group size you are dividing by
  • quotient: the answer to the division
  • reciprocal: a fraction with numerator and denominator switched

Worked examples

Example 1: 4 12 ÷ 1 12 = 92 ÷ 32 = 92 × 23 = 3. Check: three groups of 1 12 equal 4 12.
Example 2: 2 23 ÷ 45 = 83 × 54 = 103 = 3 13. Since 45 is less than 1, an answer larger than 2 23 is reasonable.
Example 3: A 6 14-foot ribbon cut into 1 14-foot pieces gives 254 ÷ 54 = 5 pieces.

More worked examples

Teaching example: Before calculating, should 3 12 ÷ 78 be greater or less than 3 12?
Answer / reasoning: Greater, because the divisor is less than 1.
Teaching example: Which number is flipped in 56 ÷ 2 12?
Answer / reasoning: The divisor: 2 12 = 52, so use 25.
Teaching example: How can multiplication check a division answer?
Answer / reasoning: Multiply answer × divisor and see whether it returns the starting amount.

Independent Reading: How Setting Changes Events

Emma · Reading · Wednesday, November 11, 2026 · 45 minutes

What you are learning: Read your own book for 25 active minutes and use a detail from the setting to explain what changes in the story.
Read FIRST: 25 active minutes with your own book. Then answer the worksheet; use true page citations.

Learn First

  1. The SETTING is when and where a scene takes place. It can make an action easier, harder or more surprising.
  2. Look for a concrete detail, such as a locked gate, noisy room or sudden rain. A detail is evidence only when it is actually in YOUR book.
  3. A cause-and-effect statement says what happened because of something else. It should not claim a cause the pages do not show.
  4. Record page numbers for two pieces of evidence so the reader can find them.
  5. Reading time is actual time reading your current book; the response comes after the full 25 minutes.

See the complete models

Model 1 (made-up story): 'At the station the lights went out' is a setting change. If the next line says Maya could not read the sign, it is supported to say the darkness prevented her from reading.
Model 2 (made-up story): A character runs during rain. The rain alone does NOT prove why she ran; you need a sentence connecting them before claiming that cause.
A mistake to avoid: Do not invent words from your book. If a page gives no cause, label your explanation as a possibility rather than a fact.

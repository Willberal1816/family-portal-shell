Groups and Extra: Build, Check, Explain

Emma · Math · 50 minutes · Learn First

What you are learning: decide which items repeat in every group and which are added just once. Write one complete numerical expression, calculate it accurately, and explain what the parentheses mean.

2. Warm-up: one extra action happens once

Example A — after sharing: 36 cards are shared equally among 6 friends. Each friend then gets 4 more cards. First, each receives 36 ÷ 6 = 6. Then add 4. The expression is 36 ÷ 6 + 4 = 10. Multiplying the 6 by 4 would change the story; the 4 cards are added to each friend's share.
Example B — finish every action: 8 + 3 × (7 + 2) − 5. Inside parentheses: 7 + 2 = 9. Then 3 × 9 = 27. Then 8 + 27 − 5 = 30. Do not stop after the multiplication or accidentally lose the final subtraction.

3. What repeats: the whole group or only one part?

SituationExpression and meaning
Two packs each contain 5 markers and 3 stickers.2 × (5 + 3) = 16. Both kinds of item are inside every pack; the parentheses hold one complete pack.
Two packs contain 5 markers each, with 3 loose stickers added once.2 × 5 + 3 = 13. Only the markers repeat. The stickers are added one time, so they remain outside the multiplication.
Explain the difference: “In the first story, every pack contains both items, so I group 5 + 3 before multiplying by 2. In the second, the stickers are loose and added once.” Your explanation should name the items and what repeats—not just say “because of parentheses.”

4. Division can group a total, too

Example: 48 trail tags are shared equally among 4 leaders and 2 assistants. There are 4 + 2 = 6 people altogether. 48 ÷ (4 + 2) = 8 tags each. Without parentheses, 48 ÷ 4 + 2 = 14 describes a different action.

5. Guided check before independent work

  1. Read: Four craft kits each contain 3 brushes and 6 papers; 7 extra papers are added to the whole pile. Which items repeat? Both 3 and 6. Which are added once? The 7 extra papers.
  2. Write: 4 × (3 + 6) + 7. The parentheses hold one complete kit.
  3. Calculate: 3 + 6 = 9; 4 × 9 = 36; 36 + 7 = 43.
Four checks for every problem: (1) Every number has a real story role. (2) Every action has the correct operation. (3) Parentheses contain exactly what repeats or belongs together. (4) The final value and a specific explanation are both present.

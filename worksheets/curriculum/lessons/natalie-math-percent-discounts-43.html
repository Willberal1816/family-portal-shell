Store Discounts: Find the True Final Price

Natalie · Math · Tuesday, November 10, 2026 · 50 minutes

What you will learn: Compute discounts from original prices and compare final prices, showing every step and unit.

Learn First

  1. A percentage tells how many parts out of one hundred. 25% = 25/100 = 0.25.
  2. Discount AMOUNT = original price × discount rate. Final price = original price − discount amount. The percent is applied to the ORIGINAL, not the already reduced, amount unless stated.
  3. For two items, calculate each separately, then add. When comparing deals, distinguish a larger dollar discount from a larger percentage.
  4. From yesterday's Math: label function inputs or quantities explicitly. Write 0.25×$40 = $10 savings, then $40−$10 = $30 final cost.
  5. Check reasonableness: a 25% discount on $40 removes about one-quarter, so $10 is sensible. A final sale price must be smaller than the original.

Two fully explained models

Worked example: A $60 jacket discounted 20% saves 0.20×60=$12, final price $48.
Worked comparison: A $30 book at 10% off saves $3 (final $27), while a $20 book at 15% off saves $3 (final $17). The percent rate differs but the dollar savings match.
Check this mistake: Do not subtract '25' dollars for a 25% discount on $40. A rate must be multiplied by the actual original amount.

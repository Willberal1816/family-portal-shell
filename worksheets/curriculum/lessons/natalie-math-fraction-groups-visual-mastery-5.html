See the Groups — Fraction Division

What you are learning: A division answer tells how many groups fit. Today every answer must name the group it counts.

1. Draw the group before calculating

You have 3 cups. Each container holds 12 cup. Think: “How many half-cup containers fit in 3 cups?”

There are 6 containers. The quotient is not “6 cups.” It counts containers.

2. Mixed numbers: convert carefully

4 12 means four wholes plus one half. Multiply 4×2, then add 1: 92.

Example: 4½ ÷ ¾ = 9/2 × 4/3 = 6. Meaning: six ¾-cup groups fit.

3. Use the label test

Before solving, write: Total = ___; one group = ___; quotient counts ___.

After solving, finish: “My answer means ___ full groups fit.” If the quotient has a fractional part, say what that part means.

4. Check by rebuilding the total

If 10 pieces of length ⅜ yard fit, then 10 × ⅜ = 30/8 = 3¾ yards. Multiplication rebuilds the original total.

5. Reason before the arithmetic

If one group is less than 1 whole, more groups can fit. If one group is greater than 1 whole, fewer groups fit. Use that idea to catch impossible answers.

Your independent work: use a quick bar/group sketch on the first problems, then show that you can transfer the idea without the sketch. Always label what the quotient counts.

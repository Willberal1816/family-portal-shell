Knife Basics: Fruit Salad & Parfaits

Emma + Natalie · Culinary Foundations · Lab 2
What you are learning: Set up a stable cutting station, carry and place a knife safely, use a controlled grip and claw guide, make consistent slices and small dice, prepare a fruit salad and yogurt parfait, then fully reset the kitchen.
Adult safety rule: A responsible adult stays nearby for the knife portion. The adult may make the first stability cut on a round or hard piece of fruit if needed. That is a safety setup, not a replacement for the student's knife work. Stop immediately if the cutting board slips, the knife is dull/damaged, or the student cannot keep fingers safely behind the blade.

1. Build a stable cutting station

Wash hands, secure hair and sleeves, clear the counter, and read the full recipe before cutting. Put a damp towel or nonslip mat under the cutting board so it cannot slide. Keep the knife on the board when it is not in your hand, with the blade facing away from the edge.

2. Learn the grip

Hold the handle firmly with your cutting hand. Your guiding hand shapes the food but keeps fingertips curled back in a claw. The side of your knuckles can help guide the blade while fingertips stay behind the cutting edge.

Worked example: To slice a strawberry, place the flat cut side down so it cannot roll. Curl the guiding fingertips back, keep the knife tip and blade controlled, and make even slices at a steady pace.

3. Make food stable before making it small

Round food rolls. Before dicing, create a flat side when practical. For an apple, a responsible adult may halve/core it or make the first stability cut if that is the safest choice for the student's current readiness. Place the flat side down, cut slices, then cut the slices into sticks, then into small dice.

  • slice: a flat piece cut to a similar thickness
  • dice: small cube-like pieces made from slices or strips
  • claw guide: curled guiding fingers that keep fingertips behind the blade
  • mise en place: ingredients and tools prepared before the work begins

4. Consistency matters

Pieces do not have to be machine-perfect, but similar size helps food look intentional and makes later cooking more even. Today you will compare several pieces side by side and make one adjustment if the sizes vary widely.

Recipe — shared fruit salad and two parfaits

  • 1 apple
  • 1 banana
  • about 1 cup strawberries
  • about 1 cup blueberries
  • 2 cups yogurt
  • about 1 cup granola
  • optional: cinnamon or a small drizzle of honey after the knife work is complete

Procedure

  1. Wash hands, read the full recipe, gather tools, and stabilize the cutting board.
  2. Wash fruit. Set yogurt and granola aside until cutting is complete.
  3. Practice controlled strawberry slices. Each student completes a set.
  4. Slice the banana with the same attention to even thickness.
  5. With the adult nearby, prepare the apple safely. Use a stable flat side, then make slices and small dice.
  6. Combine some cut fruit for the shared fruit salad. Keep some pieces for parfait layers.
  7. Compare cut sizes. If one group is much larger, correct a few pieces before moving on.
  8. Build two parfaits with yogurt, fruit, and granola.
  9. Taste the fruit salad/parfait. Notice texture, cut size, and balance.
  10. Wash the knife separately and safely, dry it, return it to storage, wash the board and tools, wipe the station, put food away, and fully reset the kitchen.

Worked safety decisions

If the board moves: Stop cutting. Fix the towel/mat and test the board before restarting.
If a piece rolls: Stop and create a stable flat side instead of trying to chase it with the knife.
If a cut is uneven: Slow down, compare the next slice to the last one, and use the knuckles/claw as a guide rather than cutting faster.

60-minute lab plan

10 minutes: sanitation, recipe read-through, station and knife setup · 25 minutes: slicing/dicing practice and fruit preparation · 10 minutes: assemble fruit salad/parfaits and evaluate · 15 minutes: safe knife wash, food storage, dishes and full reset.

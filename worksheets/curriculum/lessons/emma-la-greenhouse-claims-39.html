Greenhouse Log: Clear Sentences, Careful Causes

Emma · Wednesday, October 28, 2026 · 55 minutes · fresh HOLD practice

Your goal: Join complete facts with a semicolon, repair a sentence fragment, and describe changes without inventing what caused them.

Learn First — complete sentences

An independent clause is a complete thought with a subject and a verb. Two closely related independent clauses can be joined with one semicolon. Do not use and right after the semicolon.

Worked example A: “The gardener counted six trays. The assistant recorded the count.” becomes “The gardener counted six trays; the assistant recorded the count.” Both halves stand alone.

Common mistake: “The gardener counted six trays; and the assistant recorded the count.” Remove and after the semicolon.

A dependent clause such as “Although the reading was 29°C” is not a complete sentence. Attach a main clause with a comma: “Although the reading was 29°C at noon, the next morning's reading was 24°C.” This reports two facts; it does not say one caused the other.

Learn First — evidence is not causation

Observation tells what was measured or recorded. Causation claims something made the change happen. To test a cause, we need evidence that separates competing explanations. A timeline alone cannot establish that one of two actions caused the later temperature reading.

Worked example B: “Opening the vents caused the greenhouse to cool” is NOT supported by this log. Accurate repair: “Staff opened two vents; the temperature was 29°C at noon and 24°C the next morning. The log does not determine the cause.”

Worked example C: “Because the seedlings moved, the temperature fell” invents a reason. Replace because with a chronological fact instead of guessing the cause.

The Curtain Problem: Actions in Order

Emma · Reading · Monday, October 26, 2026

What you are learning: Identify the exact problem, three ordered actions, and the final outcome using a fresh story.

Learn First

When a story describes a problem being solved, keep what went wrong, what people did, and what finally happened separate. The action order matters. Look for precise verbs such as checked, lifted, tested.

Example (a different story): A lamp did not turn on. Maya checked its plug, replaced a loose bulb, and tested the switch. Then the lamp lit up. The problem was a dark lamp; the three actions were checked → replaced → tested; the final outcome was that the lamp lit up.

Do not label an intention as a finished result. Quote or describe a clue from the passage for an explanation, and do not add actions the passage never mentions.

Fully worked example or model

Read the new story below carefully. Underline the exact verb phrases that show what happened FIRST, NEXT, and LAST before writing the outcome.

Source and tools you will need

The Curtain Problem — Original practice passage

The school auditorium was preparing for a music performance. Just before rehearsal, the stage curtain stopped halfway down. The students could not see the entire stage. Ms. Kim told everyone to stay away from the curtain cord while she checked the mechanism. First she unplugged the motor and inspected the track. She found a bent metal clip catching the fabric. Next she asked the custodian to replace that clip while the motor stayed unplugged. Then they moved the curtain by hand to check whether it slid freely. After the hand test worked, Ms. Kim plugged the motor back in and lowered the curtain slowly. It reached the floor without sticking. The students could now see the whole stage, and rehearsal began ten minutes late.

Which Evidence Is Strong Enough?

Natalie · Language Arts · 55 minutes · Learn First
What you are learning: judge evidence by relevance, specificity, source access, and sufficiency—then explain why it does or does not support a claim.

The four tests

Relevant

Does it answer this exact claim?

Specific

Does it include an observable fact, number, example, or quotation?

Source access

Was the source in a position to know?

Sufficient

Is there enough evidence for the size of the claim?

Worked model: Claim: “The new bus route reduced late arrivals.” Evidence 1: “The route started Monday.” Relevant but not sufficient. Evidence 2: “School records show late arrivals fell from 18 in the prior week to 7 in the first full week.” Relevant, specific, and stronger. Limitation: one week cannot prove the change will last.

Strong wording

Avoid “This proves everything.” Use: This evidence supports the claim because… and It does not establish…

When sources disagree

  1. Do not average opinions as if they were measurements.
  2. Identify what each source directly knows.
  3. Look for a record or additional observation that could resolve the question.
Ready check: Can you name the claim, quote the evidence, explain the link, and state one limit?

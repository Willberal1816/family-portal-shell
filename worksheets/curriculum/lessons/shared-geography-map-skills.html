Map Skills

Emma + Natalie · Geography · Learn First
What you are learning: Use map title, legend, direction, scale, and grid information, choose an appropriate map, and recognize every map has a purpose and limits.

Learn the idea

A map is a model of a place. The real world is too large and detailed to fit on paper, so a mapmaker chooses what to include, simplify, and symbolize. Start with the title because it tells what the map is meant to show.

The legend or key explains symbols and colors. A compass rose or north arrow gives direction. A grid helps locate places precisely. Scale connects map distance to real distance, such as 1 inch = 20 miles.

Different maps answer different questions. Physical maps emphasize landforms and water; political maps emphasize boundaries and places; thematic maps show one type of information such as rainfall, population, or crops. The best map depends on the question.

Maps have limits. They can omit information, use old data, or distort shapes and sizes when a curved Earth is shown flat. Good readers ask who made the map, when, and for what purpose.

Words you will use

  • legend/key: explanation of map symbols and colors
  • scale: relationship between map and actual distance
  • thematic map: a map focused on one kind of information
  • grid: rows and columns for locating places
  • map projection: a method for representing curved Earth on a flat surface

Worked examples

Scale: if 1 cm = 25 km, 4 cm represents 100 km.
Map choice: compare rainfall with a rainfall thematic map, not a highway map.
Limit: a population map from 1990 may be accurate for 1990 but poor evidence for current population.

More worked examples

Teaching example: Where do you check an unfamiliar map symbol?
Answer / reasoning: The legend or key.
Teaching example: If 2 cm = 30 km, what does 5 cm represent?
Answer / reasoning: 75 km.
Teaching example: Why is there no single best map?
Answer / reasoning: Different maps select information for different purposes.

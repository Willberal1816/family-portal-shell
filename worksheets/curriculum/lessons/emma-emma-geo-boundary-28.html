One Clock, Two Calendar Days

Emma · Geography · Monday, September 28, 2026 · Learn First

What you are learning: apply a positive or negative clock offset, carry over midnight correctly, and describe the Date Line direction without reversing it. Read the examples, then use the separate Independent Work printable for all answers. This teaching page has no written work to turn in.

Learn the idea

UTC offsets are arithmetic practice. A plus offset moves the clock forward; a minus offset moves it back. Convert 24:00 to next day 00:00 and negative hours to the previous day. If Sunday 17:00 UTC +10 hours equals 27:00, it becomes Monday 03:00, NOT Monday 02:00.

The Date Line is a separate calendar convention. Traveling west across it generally advances the date; east subtracts a date. Time-zone borders do not follow perfect lines, so a classroom offset card does not stand for every location in a whole country.

Words that help

  • UTC offset: hours added to or subtracted from UTC for this classroom card
  • midnight rollover: passing 24:00 or going below 00:00 changes date
  • International Date Line: a convention where crossing direction changes calendar date

Worked example 1

Tuesday 22:00 UTC +4 becomes Wednesday 02:00. Add 4 to 22 to make26, then subtract24 and move the date ahead one.

Worked example 2

Monday 03:00 UTC −7 becomes Sunday 20:00. Subtract7 to get −4, add24 to the hour, and move the date back one.

Use this reference while you work

Classroom UTC offset cards: Town A UTC+2, Town B UTC−5, Town C UTC+10. These are invented practice locations and do not assert the legal clock of a real city.

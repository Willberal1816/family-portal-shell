Ramp Distance Trial: Control, Measure, Conclude

Natalie · Science · Wednesday, October 28, 2026 · 40 minutes

What you are learning: Compare repeated measurements, keep the changed variable separate from controls, and make a conclusion that does not claim more than the trial shows.

Learn First

Change one variable at a time. Here, ramp height changes; the same ball, start point, surface, and release method are controls.

Repeated trials show variation. Compare averages, but do not claim the height alone caused every possible result without more testing.

Fully worked model

Low-ramp distances 84, 88, 86 cm average to 86 cm. High-ramp distances 122, 118, 120 cm average to 120 cm. In these trials, the high ramp averaged 34 cm farther.

Guided model: repeated trials and bounded conclusions

Model 2 (practice results, not worksheet data): A low ramp yields 40, 42, and 44 cm; mean distance = (40 + 42 + 44) ÷ 3 = 42 cm. A high ramp yields 50, 54, and 52 cm; mean = (50 + 54 + 52) ÷ 3 = 52 cm. The average difference is 52 − 42 = 10 cm in these trials. Model 3: A precise conclusion says “The higher ramp averaged 10 cm farther with this ball on this surface.” It does not say all balls always roll exactly 10 cm farther.

Common mistake: Changing the ball or push force and claiming the ramp height alone accounts for the result. Hold the ball, release, surface, and start point steady; vary ramp height only. An average summarizes a set of repeats; show your full addition and division before comparing them.

Try it before printing: For distances 60, 63, 66 cm, find the average and identify one measurement you would repeat to test consistency.

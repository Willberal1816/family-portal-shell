Design a Poster: Guide the Eye With Hierarchy

Natalie · Art · Monday, November 16, 2026 · 50 minutes

What you are learning

Produce an original information poster with a clear headline, supporting line, essential details and measurable revisions.

Learn First

  1. Visual hierarchy means arranging information so a viewer notices the most important part first.
  2. Choose a bold headline larger than the supporting details. Contrast can come from shape, scale, spacing or color; colored pencils are optional.
  3. Leave enough white space around major words to prevent details from competing for attention.
  4. Use the fictional event card below; every date, place and time on your poster must match it exactly.
  5. Draft a layout in a small rectangle, then build a full-size poster in the large drawing area. Use at least three distinct information levels.
  6. Ask a viewer's eye to travel from event title, to date/time, to room. Arrows and shapes can support clarity but should not replace needed words.
  7. Revise a specific design flaw, such as small date lettering or crowded details, and explain how the change helps a reader.

Two worked models

Model 1: Original layout model: Giant top title 'MAKER SHOWCASE'; mid-size 'Saturday, Dec 5'; bottom details '1 p.m. | Library Room C | Bring one finished project'.
Model 2: Original contrast model: Dark outlined title against an unshaded background, with generous blank margin around the date. Contrast does not require expensive materials.
A mistake to avoid: An attractive poster is not useful if the date or place is invented or unreadable. Keep a consistent hierarchy and truthful event details.

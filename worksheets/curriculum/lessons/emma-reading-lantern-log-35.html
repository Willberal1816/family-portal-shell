The Lantern Log: Follow the Record

Emma · Reading · Thursday, October 22, 2026 · 45 minutes

Goal: Use exact story details, order three actions, and state the real ending.

One class: Read this Learn First lesson, work through both examples, complete all six items on TWO printable pages, scan both pages, and Finish Class. Learn First and Printable are not separate assignments.

Learn First

Read your own book for 20–25 minutes, then read this short story. A useful summary gives the problem, the actions that solved it, and what actually happened. A written record can show what was checked, but it does not prove things that were never recorded.

Two worked models

Model 1. Mini-example: A park sign was missing. The ranger checked the sign inventory, found the correct number, and replaced it. Outcome: hikers could read the route again.
Model 2. A weak ending says 'They tried to help.' A strong ending says what changed: 'The new sign showed the trail entrance before the hikers arrived.'
Check for mistakes: Do not invent a later event. If the story says the light was repaired, do not claim the entire town's power was restored.

The Lantern Log

On a cloudy afternoon, Eli helped his aunt prepare a small lakeside history exhibit. Three battery lanterns were supposed to light the display cases, but one lantern would not turn on. The exhibit would open at four o'clock.

Eli suggested replacing all the batteries immediately. His aunt opened the equipment log instead. It recorded when each lantern had last been checked and which spare parts had been used. The entry for Lantern 2 said the battery compartment cover had been loose during the last setup.

First, Eli matched the number on the lantern to the log entry. Next, his aunt checked the cover and fastened it securely. Then they switched on the lantern and watched it stay lit during a short test. They placed the working lantern beside the model boat.

When the first visitors arrived, all three display cases were lit. Eli added a new entry to the log explaining the loose cover and the successful test, so the next volunteer would know what had happened.

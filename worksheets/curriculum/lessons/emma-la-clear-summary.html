Write a Clear Summary

Emma · Language Arts · Learn First
What you are learning: Turn a short passage into a clear 4–5 sentence summary that includes the main event or idea, the most important supporting details, and the result without adding extra opinions.

Use a simple structure

  1. Sentence 1: name the main event or main idea.
  2. Sentences 2–4: include only the most important actions, facts, or changes in a logical order.
  3. Final sentence: state the result, outcome, or most important ending point.

Worked example

Original information: A class planted bean seeds in three pots. One pot received normal light, one stayed in a dark cabinet, and one received light only two hours each day. The students measured plant height for two weeks. The normal-light plants grew strongest and greenest. The dark plants became pale and weak, while the limited-light plants grew but stayed smaller.

Clear summary: A class tested how different amounts of light affected bean plants. They grew three groups under normal, dark, and limited-light conditions and measured them for two weeks. Plants with normal light grew strongest and greenest. Plants with little or no light were smaller, pale, or weak. The results showed that the amount of light changed how well the plants grew.

Keep details connected

Do not make a list of facts that never connect. Use transition words such as first, next, after, while, because, or finally only when they help the reader understand the order or relationship.

Weak version: “There were three pots. One was dark. One had light. They measured plants.”
Why it is weak: The reader does not know why the pots were different or what the results showed.

Stay inside the passage

A summary should not add guesses, opinions, or advice that the passage never gave. If the passage says the bridge became stable after a repair, do not add that the builder “must have been proud” unless the passage actually says that.

Quick editing pass

Before turning in a summary, check three things: every sentence is complete, punctuation separates ideas clearly, and no sentence repeats the same detail in slightly different words.

A Detour, Two Records

Mastery focus: build a claim that is exact, limited, and honest about what the records cannot prove.

Today’s deliverable: an Evidence Board and a Claim Audit card. This is a new structure—not yesterday’s question pattern.

Learn First — 10 minutes

Good evidence writing separates three jobs:

1. Record

Write exactly what a source measured, counted, or observed.

2. Support

Explain the narrow idea that the record makes more likely.

3. Boundary

Name what the record cannot prove by itself.

Worked model

Source A: Route A recorded 18 on-time arrivals Monday and 20 Tuesday. Source B: Route B recorded 16 Monday and 17 Tuesday. Source C: one rider said the new detour felt faster.

Recorded factWhat it supportsWhat it cannot prove
A was 2 higher Monday and 3 higher Tuesday.Route A had more recorded on-time arrivals on both listed days.The detour caused the difference.
One rider felt faster.A rider noticed a possible improvement.Every rider was faster or the schedule improved.
Avoid: “proves,” “caused,” “always,” and “worked” unless the evidence really establishes those claims. A measured difference is not automatically a cause.

Guided Evidence Board — 10 minutes

With an adult or aloud to yourself, classify each source: schedule record, personal observation, or unrelated item. Then practice this sentence frame:

The records show ______. This supports ______. It does not prove ______ because ______.

Independent Claim Audit — 25 minutes

Complete the printable. Use every exact count, keep personal observations separate, correct an overclaim, and finish the four-part Claim Audit.

Check and Revise — 10 minutes

  • Did I copy the exact counts correctly?
  • Did I compare the records with correct arithmetic?
  • Did I use shows/suggests instead of proves/caused?
  • Did I name one meaningful limitation or alternative influence?
Success looks like: exact facts + a narrow claim + clear reasoning + an honest boundary.

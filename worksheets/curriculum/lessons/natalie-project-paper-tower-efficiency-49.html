Paper Tower Challenge: Height Versus Stability

Natalie · Project · Tuesday, November 17 · 60 minutes

What you are learning: Build and improve a real paper tower under material constraints; evaluate height, load time, and the tradeoff between stability and height.

Learn First — lesson and method

  1. You will produce a REAL paper-and-tape tower, tested with an unopened metal food can. The finished model diagram shows a broad base, folded/rolled supports and a flat top deck.
  2. Watch the entire inline Science Buddies paper tower challenge demonstration BEFORE building. The video models the exact construction-and-test problem.
  3. Share the household supply set with Emma: twelve printer sheets total (six per person), office tape, ruler, pencil, scissors if needed, stopwatch, and a 14–16-oz unopened can. Use a stable clear tabletop; ask a parent to place the load.
  4. Your constraint is no more than SIX whole paper sheets. Cut pieces still count against the original sheet. The can must rest freely on the structure, never taped to it.
  5. Before building, sketch TWO unlike structural ideas. Predict which one will be more stable based on base width, column shape and bracing.
  6. Build the first design and measure height in centimeters BEFORE carefully testing whether the can stays up for sixty consecutive seconds. Record REAL time, not a hypothetical example.
  7. Revise ONE structural feature and retest using the SAME can. Compare results; note if added stability reduced height, and do not score untested designs as successful.

Two complete teaching models

Model 1: Concept model A: Three rolled tubes under a flat deck may resist crushing better than one folded sheet. A wider base may resist tipping; real testing decides.
Model 2: Evidence model B: MADE-UP measurements: version A 28 cm/10 sec; version B 26 cm/60 sec. B is shorter but meets the load rule. This is a tradeoff, not simply 'worse because shorter.'
Mistake to avoid: Never report imagined heights or success. A taller unsupported frame can fail; record load duration separately from height.

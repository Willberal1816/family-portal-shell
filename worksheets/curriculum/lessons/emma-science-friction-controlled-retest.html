What is different this time: You are not repeating the old worksheet. This retest focuses on one controlled release method, exact measurements, averages, and deciding whether one odd trial should change your claim.

1. Fix the release before collecting data

Last time, changing how hard the object was pushed made the results hard to interpret. In this retest, the object must start from the same marked position and be released without an extra push.

Release method: Put a tape start line on the ramp. Place the same object with its front edge on that line. Hold it still, then lift your fingers straight up. Do not shove it forward.

3. Use four trials

Four trials give you a better chance to notice an unusual result. Record every measurement with units. Then add the four distances and divide by 4.

Worked average: 42 cm, 40 cm, 43 cm, 41 cm → total 166 cm → 166 ÷ 4 = 41.5 cm average.

4. Look for an outlier before making a claim

If three trials are close together but one is far away, do not erase it. Circle it, name a possible setup reason, and compare the average with and without that trial. Then decide whether the overall pattern still holds.

5. Make a measurement-based claim

Strong claim: “In this setup, felt produced the shortest average travel distance at 24.5 cm, compared with 51.0 cm on cardboard, so felt showed more motion-resisting friction in our test.”

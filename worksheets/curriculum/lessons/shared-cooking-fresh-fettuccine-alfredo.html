Cooking Lab 3: Fresh Fettuccine & Homemade Alfredo

Today’s challenge: each cook makes one small egg-pasta dough, then the team rolls, cuts, cooks, and sauces fresh fettuccine. Plan for about 90 minutes.

Learn first — watch both

  1. Easy Pasta Dough Recipe — step-by-step video. Watch for the flour well, gradual mixing, kneading, resting, rolling, and cutting.
  2. Epicurious 101: Restaurant-Quality Fettuccine Alfredo. Watch how the chef uses butter, Parmesan, heat, and pasta water to form a smooth sauce.

Safety gates

  • An adult must be nearby for boiling water, draining, and stovetop work.
  • Turn pot handles inward. Keep the floor and work area dry.
  • Raw egg dough is not for tasting. Wash hands, tools, and the counter after mixing.
  • Use a bench scraper or dinner knife for cutting if a chef’s knife is not needed.
  • Reserve pasta water with a heat-safe cup before draining. Adult assists with the hot pot.

Ingredients

Each student’s dough: 100 g (about ¾ cup plus 1 tablespoon) all-purpose flour, 1 large egg, a pinch of salt, and up to 1 teaspoon water only if truly needed.

Shared Alfredo: 4 tablespoons butter, 1 cup finely grated Parmesan, ½–¾ cup reserved hot pasta water, black pepper, and a small pinch of salt if needed. Optional American-style extension: up to ½ cup heavy cream, warmed gently and never boiled.

Tools

Digital scale or measuring cups, two bowls or clean work areas, forks, bench scraper, rolling pin or pasta machine, clean towel/wrap, large pot, tongs, colander, heat-safe cup, skillet or large warm bowl, grater, and individual lab records.

Workflow

TimeWorkQuality check
0–15 minWatch both demonstrations and set up.Each student names one pasta technique and one sauce technique.
15–30 minEach student measures flour, makes a well, adds one egg, and gradually incorporates flour.Dough holds together without loose wet egg or large dry pockets.
30–40 minKnead each dough 8–10 minutes.Surface becomes smoother and springs back slowly when pressed.
40–55 minCover and rest dough 15 minutes. Grate Parmesan, start water, and clean the raw-egg station.Station is clean before cooked food returns.
55–70 minRoll thin enough to see a shadow through the sheet; dust lightly; cut fettuccine strands.Strands are mostly even and separated, not buried in flour.
70–82 minBoil in salted water until tender, usually 2–4 minutes. Reserve pasta water first.Taste one strand safely: tender with a slight bite, not raw or mushy.
82–90 minOff low heat, toss pasta with butter and Parmesan; add pasta water a little at a time until glossy. Plate and clean.Sauce coats noodles smoothly instead of clumping or pooling.

Model decisions

  • Dough too dry: knead first; then add water a few drops at a time.
  • Dough sticky: dust lightly; too much flour makes tough pasta.
  • Sauce clumpy: lower/remove heat and add warm pasta water gradually while tossing.
  • Sauce thin: keep tossing; add a little more Parmesan, not high heat.

Done definition

  1. Both videos watched.
  2. Each student makes and labels her own dough.
  3. The team rolls, cuts, cooks, and sauces the pasta safely.
  4. Each student completes her own printable lab record with specific evidence.
  5. Work area and cookware are cleaned.

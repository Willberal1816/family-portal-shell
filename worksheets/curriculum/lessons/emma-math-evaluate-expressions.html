Evaluate Multi-Step Expressions

Emma · Math · Learn First
What you are learning: Evaluate numerical expressions accurately by completing grouped work first, then multiplication or division, then addition or subtraction.

1. Grouped work comes first

Parentheses tell you to treat part of an expression as one quantity before using it in the rest of the calculation.

Worked example 1: 4 × (9 − 3)
First: 9 − 3 = 6.
Then: 4 × 6 = 24.
Worked example 2: (18 + 6) ÷ 4
First: 18 + 6 = 24.
Then: 24 ÷ 4 = 6.

2. Multiply and divide before adding and subtracting

Worked example 3: 7 + 3 × 5
Multiply first: 3 × 5 = 15.
Then add: 7 + 15 = 22.
Worked example 4: 30 − 16 ÷ 4
Divide first: 16 ÷ 4 = 4.
Then subtract: 30 − 4 = 26.

3. Work left to right inside the same level

Multiplication and division have the same priority, so move left to right. Addition and subtraction also share a priority and are handled left to right.

Worked example 5: 24 ÷ 6 × 3
24 ÷ 6 = 4, then 4 × 3 = 12.

4. Compare structure before calculating

Common mistake: Reading only from left to right and ignoring grouping. In 6 + 4 × 2, doing 6 + 4 first gives the wrong structure. Multiply 4 × 2 first.
Check yourself: Before calculating, point to the operation you will do first and explain why. Then show one line for each step instead of trying to do everything mentally.

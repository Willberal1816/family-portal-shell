Surface Evidence: Measure, Explain, Retest

Emma · Science · Wednesday, October 14, 2026 · Learn First · 50 minutes including independent work

Your goal: use a fresh three-surface data set to check an exact difference, trace where motion energy goes, and describe a fair retest. This is a new lesson, not the earlier "Roll, Slow, Transfer" worksheet. Read this first; all answers go on the separate two-page printable.

1. A fair test keeps the question narrow

One identical marble rolls down the same ramp from the same release line. The only thing changed is the landing surface. A student measures how far the marble rolls on that surface after leaving the ramp, in centimeters. Every surface gets three trials. The marble, ramp slope, release line, measuring method, and person releasing it stay the same.

2. Model an average and an exact comparison

Smooth mat: (74 + 70 + 72) ÷ 3 = 216 ÷ 3 = 72 cm.
Corrugated paper: (48 + 51 + 45) ÷ 3 = 144 ÷ 3 = 48 cm.
Difference: 72 cm − 48 cm = 24 cm. Write the subtraction, not only its answer. Always attach units.

Do not claim that the smooth mat always makes every marble go farther. These are only three trials for one marble and setup; a careful conclusion is "In these trials, the average on the smooth mat was 24 cm farther than on corrugated paper."

3. Explain the whole energy path

Before release: the marble has gravitational potential energy from its raised position.
Rolling: gravitational potential energy decreases as kinetic energy (motion) increases.
Slowing and stopping: friction and collisions transfer energy to the surroundings, mostly as tiny amounts of thermal energy, with some sound. The energy did not disappear.

4. Correct a flawed test before running it

Bad plan: "Use a heavy ball from a tall ramp on rubber, and a light ball from a low ramp on a mat." This changes ball mass, height, and surface, so we cannot tell which change caused the result.
Fixed plan: same ball, same ramp height and release method; only change the landing surface; measure distance from a consistent start line in cm three times per surface.

  1. Set up the variables and controls.
  2. Calculate all three averages and exact differences, with units.
  3. Explain what the data support and what they cannot prove.
  4. Label the complete energy path and design the next fair test.

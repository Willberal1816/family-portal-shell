Art Studio: Design a Room in One-Point Perspective

Natalie · Art · Tuesday, November 10, 2026 · 45 minutes

What you will learn: Make an original room with consistent vanishing point, floor/wall depth, furniture and an annotated design choice.

Learn First

  1. Start with a horizontal HORIZON line at eye level and a single dot on it: the VANISHING POINT.
  2. Draw a smaller rectangle inside the page for the room's BACK wall. Use slanted lines from its corners to the big page corners to create the side walls, floor and ceiling.
  3. Make the top and bottom edges of at least two furnishings (bookshelf, rug or table) POINT towards your vanishing point. Use straight up-and-down lines for vertical edges.
  4. Add near and far objects. The near object should be visibly larger; far ones should seem smaller. Make your own design rather than copying the picture.
  5. Finish a full perspective room. Use the work page to show and label the horizon, vanishing point, at least two converging objects and one reason your design suggests depth.

Two fully explained models

Worked model: a central back wall and four diagonal corner guides make a room. The dot on the horizon is used to guide the top/bottom edges of a rug.
Common repair: a desk whose front and back edges tilt to different dots looks distorted. Fix them so they aim toward the SAME vanishing point.
Check this mistake: Perspective edges converge; vertical door posts should normally remain vertical. Shading is optional and does not replace the required geometry.

Look at the source and visual reference

Teacher model: The central wall is farther away. Four diagonal corner guides lead toward it; a horizon and dot help direct furniture depth. Your drawing must be a different room.

Optional approved reference: Bakersfield Museum of Art perspective tutorial. The complete teaching model is here on this page, so the student does not need to visit another site.

Do the actual lesson

Make your own finished artwork, then explain three design choices on the second sheet. Print TWO work pages, use both pages, and scan both Tracking numbers. If a direction is confusing, ask for help before guessing.

Time plan: 10 minutes teaching and demonstration + 30 minutes original drawing + 5 minutes review/scan. Materials: pencil, plain paper or printed sheet, ruler/straightedge, eraser; optional colored pencils.. No purchases necessary.

Art record and self-check

  1. Explain where you placed the vanishing point and how the most important lines converge toward it.
  2. List at least three specific depth cues you drew and explain one correction you made as you worked.
  3. Compare ONE part of your drawing to the demonstration model. Name something original about yours.

Before scanning: Show your original drawing to a parent and submit both pages. The drawing, not the reflection questions, is the main work.

2. Definitions and the clause test

Independent clause: has a subject and verb and can stand alone as a complete thought.
Dependent clause: has a subject and verb but cannot stand alone as a complete thought.

Simple sentence: one independent clause. It can still have a compound subject or compound verb.

Compound sentence: two independent clauses joined correctly.

Complex sentence: one independent clause plus at least one dependent clause.

3. Worked sentence examples

Simple: “My sister and I packed the cooler.” There is one complete clause even though it has two subjects.

Compound: “The clouds grew dark, so we hurried to the car.” Both halves can stand alone; comma + so joins them.

Complex: “Although the trail was muddy, we kept walking.” The opening clause depends on the main clause.

Complex: “Because the bus was late, I called home.” “Because the bus was late” is dependent; “I called home” is independent.

Simple: “The dog barked and scratched at the door.” One subject performs two actions, so it is still one independent clause.

4. Punctuation repair examples

Run-on: “The rain stopped we went outside.” Correct versions include “The rain stopped. We went outside.” or “The rain stopped, so we went outside.”

Opening dependent clause: “Because I finished early, I read my book.” The comma separates the opening dependent clause from the independent clause.

Comma splice: “I was tired, I finished the chapter.” A comma alone cannot join two independent clauses; use a period, semicolon, or comma plus a coordinating conjunction.

5. Common mistakes and corrections

Mistake: calling “My mom cooked dinner and washed the pans” compound because it has two verbs. Correction: one subject performs two actions, so the sentence has one independent clause and is simple.

Mistake: underlining only “clouds” when asked for a clause. Correction: a clause mark must include the whole subject-and-verb thought, not one word.

Mistake: assuming any sentence with and is compound. Correction: first check whether there is an independent clause on both sides of the joining word.

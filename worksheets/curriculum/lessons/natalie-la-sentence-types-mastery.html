Sentence Types — Make the Clauses Clear

Today stays on sentence structure. The goal is to tell compound and complex sentences apart and mark complete clauses before moving on.

1. Find complete clauses first

An independent clause can stand alone as a complete sentence. A dependent clause cannot stand alone.

When a question asks you to mark clauses, underline the whole clause, not just the subject or one important word.

2. Compound vs. complex

Compound: two independent clauses joined correctly.

Example: I finished my book, and I started another.

Complex: one independent clause plus at least one dependent clause.

Example: Because it was raining, we stayed inside.

Two subjects or two verbs do not automatically make a compound sentence.

3. Check the punctuation

  • Two complete sentences joined with and, but, or, so, yet, for, nor usually need a comma before the joining word.
  • An opening dependent clause is usually followed by a comma.

Now do your mastery check

  1. For each sentence, mark every whole clause.
  2. Decide which clauses can stand alone.
  3. Then label the sentence type.
  4. Check the comma rule before you finish.

Garden Log: Write Complete Facts

Emma · Language Arts · Friday, November 20 · 50-minute learning block (55 minutes)

What you are learning: Build complete evidence-based sentences from a dated record without inventing the reason for a change.

Learn First

A complete factual sentence tells who or what the sentence is about and what happened.

Begin with a capital letter and finish with punctuation.

Put the source date beside each fact.

When a record only lists totals and a future plan, it does not tell you who caused a change or whether a later event happened.

Important words: A subject tells whom or what the sentence is about. A verb tells what that subject did or was. Evidence is what the actual record states, rather than an imagined cause.

Worked models — read before independent work

Model 1: Fragment: 'On Monday, twelve bags.' Complete factual sentence: 'On Monday, November 16, volunteers collected 12 bags at the garden.' The subject is 'volunteers' and the action verb is 'collected.'
Model 2: The log records 12 bags Monday and 15 Wednesday. The difference is 15 − 12 = 3 bags. A safe sentence is: 'The Wednesday log records three more bags than Monday.' It cannot prove that rain or more volunteers caused the change.
Common mistake: A comma does not fix two unfinished fragments. Do not add rain, crowd size, bad weather or reasons absent from the record.

Reference for today's work

Monday, November 1612 filled litter bags collected
Wednesday, November 1815 filled litter bags collected

Not reportedWeather, reason for the change, number of helpers

Independent Work — all six questions

Every assigned question is shown on this screen and included on your four-page printable. Write on your worksheet, not on the Learn First page.

Question 1. Copy FOUR facts from the source into a two-column chart labeled 'source detail' and 'what it proves.' Include both dates, the two counts and the planned next cleanup without making up a cause.

Question 2. Rewrite these THREE fragments as THREE separate complete sentences using the record: 'Monday, 12 bags'; 'More bags on Wednesday'; 'Next cleanup Saturday.' Underline the subject and circle the action verb in each.

Question 3. Write FOUR numbered, complete factual sentences about the garden records. Include a date and count in TWO different sentences, a sentence comparing the totals, and one sentence clearly describing the future plan as PLANNED, not completed.

Question 4. A classmate writes 'The cleanup improved because it rained and more people came.' Name TWO unsupported claims. Write TWO safe replacement sentences using only the record and its three-bag difference.

Question 5. An editor copied the wrong lines: '15 bag collected Monday. 12 bag Wednesday.' Correct BOTH day-count pairs and write TWO sentences that use subject, verb, punctuation and an exact date.

Question 6. Write a FIVE-sentence garden announcement for a bulletin board: one complete introductory sentence; one Monday fact; one Wednesday fact; one precise numerical comparison; and one planned-future-event sentence. Circle each subject and underline each finite verb. Do not invent weather, attendance or reasons.

Shading: Light to Dark

Emma + Natalie · Art · Learn First
What you are learning: Create a controlled value range and use light direction, gradual shading, highlights, core shadow, and cast shadow to make flat drawings look three-dimensional.

Learn the idea

Value means how light or dark something appears. Artists use value changes to show form. A circle looks flat when it is one tone; it can look like a sphere when the side facing light stays brighter and the opposite side gradually darkens.

Pressure matters. Start lightly and build darker values in layers. You can shade with smooth strokes, hatching, cross-hatching, or stippling. Technique can vary, but the value relationships still need to match the light source.

Choose a light direction before shading. The highlight is the lightest area. The core shadow is a darker region on the form away from light. A cast shadow appears on the surface beside the object and generally extends away from the light source.

Look at real objects rather than guessing. Squinting slightly can make the big light/dark shapes easier to see. Art practice is observation plus control, not filling every inch with pencil.

Words you will use

  • value: how light or dark a tone is
  • highlight: lightest area receiving strong light
  • core shadow: darker part turned away from light
  • cast shadow: shadow placed on another surface
  • gradient: a gradual change from one value to another

Worked examples

Value scale: five boxes from paper white to darkest controlled pencil value.
Sphere: light from upper left means highlight upper-left, darker lower-right, cast shadow generally down/right.
Common mistake: dark outline plus even coloring shows a symbol of a ball, not light on form.

More worked examples

Teaching example: If light is on the left, which side is generally darker?
Answer / reasoning: The right side.
Teaching example: Why build dark values in layers?
Answer / reasoning: For better control and smoother transitions.
Teaching example: Which direction does cast shadow generally extend?
Answer / reasoning: Away from the light source.

Kitchen Safety: Clean, Separate, Chill

Emma · Health · Friday, October 30, 2026 · 40 minutes

Today's goal: Identify three moments to wash hands, apply clean/separate/chill to a real snack, and make a safer food-prep plan.

Watch the actual demonstration

Exact instructional source: CDC: Always wash your hands before, during, and after preparing food. If playback is blocked, open the original video link and follow the steps shown below. Additional official written safety/recipe reference.

Prepare / parent check: Hand soap, running water, towel, two printable pages, pencil. No food needed for Health.

Learn First — follow in order

  1. Food safety means keeping harmful germs away from what we eat. CLEAN = wash hands with soap and water for 20 seconds; wash utensils and food preparation surfaces. Rinse fresh produce under running water.
  2. SEPARATE = keep raw meat, poultry, seafood and eggs and their juices away from ready-to-eat yogurt, fruit, or granola. Use clean cups and spoons, and do not reuse dirty surfaces.
  3. CHILL = keep perishable yogurt cold until snack preparation; return leftovers to the refrigerator promptly. FoodSafety.gov uses the four words Clean, Separate, Cook, Chill, but today we make a cold snack that does not need heat.
  4. Before snack-making, choose clean materials, check food labels for allergies, and ask an adult to check the food and the refrigerator. Do not cut with a sharp knife or operate an oven today.

Two examples of the finished work and skills

Example 1: A student scratches her nose and then reaches for the yogurt. Safer sequence: stop → wash hands with soap and water for 20 seconds → use a clean spoon.
Example 2: A worker puts raw chicken on a board then berries on the same unwashed board. That is unsafe cross-contact. Keep ready-to-eat fruit separate and use a washed board or prewashed fruit.

Read the safety case

Practice situation: At 1:50, Ava washes her hands with soap for 20 seconds. At 1:52, she puts a clean cup on a wiped counter. At 1:54, a sibling touches raw eggs and reaches for a clean spoon. The yogurt has been kept in the refrigerator. The berry package says 'ready to eat'; an adult checks the package and ingredients for safety and allergies.

CDC reference: Wash hands before, during, and after food preparation; keep raw foods separate from ready-to-eat food; refrigerate perishables promptly.

Find Clues in the Story

Emma · Language Arts · Learn First
Goal: Use exact clues from a story to figure out something the author does not say directly.

1. Clue + what you know = a strong idea

Stories do not explain every feeling, problem, or reason. Good readers notice a clue, connect it to what they already know, and figure something out.

School word: An inference is an idea you figure out from text clues plus what you know.
Text: “Maya checked the clock twice, zipped her backpack before the bell, and stood near the door.”
Clue: She keeps checking the time and gets ready early.
What I know: People often do that when they are eager to leave.
Strong idea: Maya is excited or impatient for class to end.

2. An inference is not a wild guess

A guess might be possible, but the text does not support it. A strong inference must fit specific evidence.

Too big: “Maya hates school.” The clues do not prove that.
Better: “Maya is eager for the bell to ring.” The clues directly support it.

3. Sometimes several clues work together

Text: “Owen stepped onto the porch. He pulled his hood tighter, shoved his hands into his pockets, and hurried to the car.”
One clue suggests cold weather. Three clues together make the idea much stronger.

4. Figure out what is stated and what is inferred

Stated: The author tells you directly. “Lena was nervous.”
Inferred: The author shows clues. “Lena rubbed her palms on her jeans and reread the first line of her speech.”

5. Guided check

Read: “Noah moved the empty cookie plate behind the fruit bowl when Dad walked into the kitchen. He suddenly became very interested in tying his shoe.”
Question: What can you figure out about Noah?
Teaching example: Check your idea
Answer / reasoning: A strong answer is that Noah may have eaten the cookies and does not want Dad to notice. The hidden plate and sudden distraction are the clues.
Read: “Ava opened the envelope, read the first line, and covered her mouth with both hands. Then she ran upstairs calling for her sister.”
Teaching example: What can you infer?
Answer / reasoning: The news is probably surprising and important to Ava. Her reaction and rushing to tell her sister support that idea. We cannot tell from these clues whether the news is good or bad.
Ready for practice when: you can point to the exact clue that supports the idea you figured out.

Percent Increase & Decrease

Natalie · Math · Guided Teaching
What you are learning: Compare a new amount to an original amount, find the amount of change, and express that change as a percent of the original.

Use the original amount

Percent change asks how large the change is compared with where you started. That means the denominator is the original amount.

Example: A ticket rises from $24 to $30. The increase is $6. Compare 6 to the original 24: 6 ÷ 24 = 0.25, so the price increased 25%.

Increase and decrease

  • Percent increase: (new − original) ÷ original × 100%
  • Percent decrease: (original − new) ÷ original × 100%
  • Reasonableness: If the new amount is close to the old amount, the percent change should not be huge.
Decrease example: A $50 item drops to $40. The decrease is $10. Ten is one fifth of 50, so the decrease is 20%.

Common mistake

Do not divide by the new amount. The question is how much the original changed, so the original is the comparison base.

Check: From 80 to 100 is an increase of 20. Since 20 is one quarter of 80, the increase is 25%, not 20%.

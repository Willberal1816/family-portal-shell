What you are learning: use exact wording to make a claim, connect a complete cause chain, and separate what a narrator knows from what remains uncertain. Read these worked examples before opening the separate Independent Work page. There is no written work to complete on this teaching page.

Read for 25 minutes

Read your current book for 25 minutes. Then read the separate short passage on the printable. The book supplies your normal reading time; the new passage lets us practice one precise evidence chain.

Model a complete chain

“The path sign fell, so Leo took the marked detour and arrived after dark.” A = the sign fell; B = Leo took the detour; C = Leo arrived after dark. Each link says what happened and why the next step followed.

Quote two clues and interpret them

For “Leo tightened his coat and glanced at the storm clouds,” the exact clues are “tightened his coat” and “glanced at the storm clouds.” They support concern about the weather. Explain both clues rather than listing two quotations with no interpretation.

Narrator boundary and tone

A third-person narrator can tell us a character's actions and sometimes thoughts if stated, but the passage cannot establish an unseen person's private intentions. Tone must be supported by a particular word such as “hesitated,” not just “it sounded tense.”

Plot & Interpret Coordinates

Emma · Math · Learn First
What you are learning: Plot and read points in all four quadrants and explain what ordered pairs mean in a picture or situation.

Learn the idea

An ordered pair is written (x, y). The x-coordinate tells how far to move left or right from the origin. The y-coordinate tells how far to move down or up. Always move across first, then up or down.

The signs tell direction. Positive x is right; negative x is left. Positive y is up; negative y is down. That creates four quadrants. A point on an axis has one coordinate equal to zero and is not inside a quadrant.

Coordinates can represent locations, movement, game positions, maps, or data. Strong graph reading goes both directions: from an ordered pair to a point and from a plotted point back to its ordered pair.

A common mistake is swapping x and y or ignoring a negative sign. Say the movement aloud: “x = -3 means left 3; y = 5 means up 5.” Then check the quadrant before you finish.

Words you will use

  • origin: the point (0,0), where the axes cross
  • ordered pair: two numbers (x,y) that locate a point
  • quadrant: one of four regions made by the axes
  • axis: a number line on the coordinate plane

Worked examples

Example 1: (-4,3): left 4, up 3, Quadrant II.
Example 2: (5,-2): right 5, down 2, Quadrant IV.
Example 3: (0,-6) lies on the y-axis because x=0.

More worked examples

Teaching example: Where is (-2,-5)?
Answer / reasoning: Quadrant III: left 2 and down 5.
Teaching example: What is special about (7,0)?
Answer / reasoning: It lies on the x-axis.
Teaching example: A point is left of the y-axis and above the x-axis. What signs does it have?
Answer / reasoning: Negative x, positive y.

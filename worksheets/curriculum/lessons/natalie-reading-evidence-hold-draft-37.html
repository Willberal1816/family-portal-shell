Learn First

In an informational passage, evidence has an order. Identify the problem, the actions in their actual sequence, and the observed outcome. Cite the exact phrase that proves each important step. A plan to do something is not proof it happened.

Example: A damaged exhibit sign was hard to read. Staff checked the wording, printed a replacement, and installed it. A visitor then read the sign aloud. This establishes an observed outcome; it does not prove every visitor understood it.

Fully worked example or model

Use the exact passage words to separate the problem, the three completed corrective actions, and the observed outcome. Do not claim more than the record proves.

The Community Notice — Original practice passage

A neighborhood center planned an evening safety workshop. Two days beforehand, the printed notice listed the wrong room. Maya compared it with the center's approved booking sheet, which named Room B. She brought the error to the coordinator. The coordinator corrected the notice, printed six copies, and put them on the entrance doors. The next morning, two families told the desk worker that they saw the new Room B signs. On workshop night, the instructor set up in Room B and began on time. The record does not say that every family noticed the correction.

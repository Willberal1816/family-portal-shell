Shuttle Poll: Bound Every Claim

Natalie · Language Arts · Monday, November 9, 2026 · 55 minutes · Cycle C

Today's objective: Write ONLY claims supported by a new respondent group, precise counts and a separate no-cause limitation.

Learn First: read each step

  1. Respondents are the people who actually answered. Record exactly who, where, and WHEN. Do not enlarge one group into a claim about every traveler or resident.
  2. A count such as 14 of 20 riders means 70% of THOSE 20 respondents. It does not automatically mean 70% of all riders on other routes or days.
  3. A questionnaire that asks only WHICH option someone prefers does not tell WHY. Never invent a cause, such as weather or price, unless the study asked and recorded it.
  4. Your final report is EXACTLY THREE complete sentences: (1) precise count plus respondent group, (2) a second verified result plus the same boundary, (3) a specific thing the source cannot conclude.

See the two fully worked examples

Worked example 1: Fifteen of 25 students surveyed at a single museum workshop choose shorter talks. Correct: 'At Tuesday's museum workshop, 15 of 25 surveyed students preferred shorter talks (60%).' Unsupported: '60% of children everywhere prefer shorter talks.'
Worked example 2: Source says no WHY question was asked. Correct last sentence: 'Because no reason was recorded, this survey cannot tell us why the students made that choice.' That is the limit, not a new guess.
Watch for this common error: Do not write 'most people want it because it is safer' when only the preference count exists. Say '14 of 20 shuttle passengers surveyed on Monday chose it' and keep WHY unknown.

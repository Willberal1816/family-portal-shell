Library Shift: Write Complete Facts

Emma · Language Arts · Wednesday November 18 · 55 minutes

What you are learning: Make fully formed factual sentences and distinguish recorded facts from invented causes.

Learn First

  1. A sentence needs a subject and a verb, not only a date or place.
  2. 'On Tuesday at the desk' is a fragment. 'The desk log records 15 returned books on Tuesday' is a complete fact.
  3. Use the exact number and date each source records. 15 − 12 = 3 more returned books on Tuesday.
  4. That difference does NOT explain why the returns increased, because the log records no cause.
  5. A clear report introduces the source, gives facts and ends with a carefully bounded conclusion.
  6. After writing, underline the subject, circle its verb and inspect each date, quantity and unsupported 'because'.

Worked models

Model 1. Fragment 'On the back shelf.' -> 'The staff note says one box moved to the back shelf on Tuesday.'
Model 2. Accurate: 'Three more books were returned Tuesday than Monday.' Unsupported: 'Rain caused the difference.'
Common mistake to avoid: A sentence fragment lacks an action or subject; don't invent a cause from a time sequence.

Independent practice

Complete all SIX questions on FOUR printed pages. Scan all pages with their Tracking numbers, then return to the same class to Finish Class.

Time: 15 minutes learning, 30 minutes practice, 5 checking.

Your Six Questions

  1. Write FOUR distinct complete sentences using the exact dates and quantities in the table. Underline each subject and circle each verb.
  2. Repair TWO fragments: 'On Tuesday the books' and 'One box to the shelf.' Make each a fully supported sentence.
  3. Calculate the change in book returns; show 15 − 12 and describe the difference in TWO separate factual sentences.
  4. Correct 'Rain caused all the returned books to be moved.' Explain what the sources never state and rewrite it as two precise facts.
  5. Draft a FIVE-sentence shift report: opening, Monday fact, Tuesday fact, calculation and one evidence limit.
  6. Revise the report into a polished complete paragraph; list the exact corrections you made to sentence completeness, numbers and unsupported causal wording.

The four printed pages provide writing space. Scan all four pages after completing your work.

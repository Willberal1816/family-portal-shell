Read and Predict: Two Clues, One Careful Guess

Emma · Reading · Monday, November 16, 2026 · 45 minutes

What you are learning

Read your actual book for 25 active minutes, support a prediction with two page clues, and explain what remains uncertain.

Read your real book for 25 active minutes BEFORE writing the responses.

Learn First

  1. Read your current REAL book for at least 25 ACTIVE minutes before the worksheet. Record the actual author, pages and real minutes.
  2. A prediction is a careful idea about what might happen NEXT; it is not the same as a fact already on the page.
  3. Find one clue from the narrator or character and another different clue from an action or event. Cite the pages actually read today.
  4. A useful prediction connects each clue to the future possibility; it does not simply say 'I think the ending will be good.'
  5. Look for a second possible outcome. Stories sometimes provide clues for more than one reasonable ending.
  6. Do not write that your prediction is guaranteed. Name what the book has NOT yet revealed.
  7. When you continue reading on another day, compare your prediction with what actually happened rather than changing your old evidence.

Two worked models

Model 1: Fictional model: On p.8, the window is open; on p.10, the family hears footsteps outside. Prediction: someone may have left through the window. This is not yet a proven fact.
Model 2: Fictional model: The open window could also be from fresh air. A second explanation makes us check the evidence instead of claiming certainty.
A mistake to avoid: Do not invent book dialogue, write pages you did not actually read, or present a prediction as something that already occurred.

Town Records: Notice, Meeting, Decision

Emma · Social Studies · Wednesday November 18 · 50 minutes

What you are learning: Separate notice, sign-ins and minutes, and distinguish chronology from proof of causation or a vote.

Learn First

  1. A notice announces a planned discussion, not an outcome.
  2. A sign-in sheet measures sign-ins, not opinions or the total number of everyone who attended.
  3. Meeting minutes record comments and votes WHEN THEY ARE WRITTEN DOWN. These minutes record two requests but no vote.
  4. The Monday notice came before Wednesday; the event order doesn't prove the notice caused a purchase.
  5. You may calculate a count from records, but must label what was actually counted.
  6. A careful news report uses precise records and names what would have to be checked next.

Worked models

Model 1. Exact: 'Wednesday minutes record two speakers requesting a bench.'
Model 2. Unsupported: 'All 17 people voted for the bench so the town bought one.' No vote or purchase is recorded.
Common mistake to avoid: Never treat 17 sign-in names as 17 affirmative votes.

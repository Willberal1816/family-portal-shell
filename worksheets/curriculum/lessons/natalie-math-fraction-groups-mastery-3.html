Fraction Division — Group Size Transfer

What you are learning

Division can ask: How many groups of this size fit in the amount I have?

Example: 4 ÷ 14

Each whole has four quarter-size groups. Four wholes have 4 × 4 = 16 quarter-size groups.

[¼][¼][¼][¼]   [¼][¼][¼][¼]   [¼][¼][¼][¼]   [¼][¼][¼][¼]

So 4 ÷ 14 = 16.

A reliable three-step check

  1. Say the question in words: “How many ___-size groups fit in ___?”
  2. Predict: If the group size is smaller than 1, you should usually get more groups than the starting number.
  3. Calculate and multiply back: quotient × group size should equal the starting amount.

Example: 3 ÷ 12

How many half-size groups fit in 3? Predict more than 3. There are 6 halves. Check: 6 × 12 = 3.

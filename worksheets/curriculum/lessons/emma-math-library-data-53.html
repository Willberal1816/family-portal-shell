Library Data: Read, Check, Explain

Emma | Math | Monday, November 23, 2026 | 50 minutes

What you are learning: Read a two-category table, find totals and differences, and explain evidence limits.

Learn First

Read labels before calculating. Add every row exactly once. A total is the sum; a difference is the result of subtraction. A table gives counts, but not the cause of a change.

Worked models

Check 9 fiction + 6 nonfiction = 15 by 15 - 6 = 9.
When fiction rises from 9 to 12, the difference is 3; the reason is not recorded.

Check your work: Read the source labels; check calculations, evidence, or drawing guides before turning in.

Source and materials

FICTIONAL LIBRARY CHECKOUT LOG
Day         Fiction   Nonfiction
Monday          9          6
Tuesday        12          8
Wednesday      10          7
Each book is counted once; no reason for changes was recorded.

Answer all six questions using this teaching and source. Scan all four worksheet pages, then Finish Class in the School Portal. Ask for help when needed.

All six independent-work questions

Question 1. Copy the table and add each day's total. Show addition for all three days.
Question 2. Compare Tuesday and Wednesday totals. Which was greater, by how many, and how can you check?
Question 3. Find fiction and nonfiction totals over all three days, showing the addition and labeling the results.
Question 4. Draw a labeled bar graph of daily totals, using a scale that starts at zero. Explain what the highest bar means.
Question 5. A helper claims a new poster caused Tuesday's increase. State the supported change and explain why the cause is unknown.
Question 6. Four stacks each have three books, and two loose books are outside the stacks. Draw and solve; explain why loose books are counted only once.

Healthy Routines: Make a Fair Observation Plan

Natalie | Health | Friday, November 27, 2026 | 50 minutes

What you are learning: Distinguish observations from interpretations and build a fair repeatable routine check.

Learn First

An observation records what happened or was measured. An interpretation suggests what might explain it. When several things change at once, a single observation cannot tell you which caused an outcome. A routine plan is not medical advice or a diagnosis.

A fair routine comparison changes one safe, practical feature and keeps other features as similar as possible. Choose an outcome that can actually be observed, like the number of times you need to find a pencil. Choose a consistent recording rule and time window. Do not deliberately change sleep, food, medicine or hydration to run a test.

An alternative explanation is another plausible reason for a difference. Before claiming a new setup helped, compare at least two observations and say what you do not know. A school routine can include comfortable setup, water available and normal movement breaks.

Two worked examples

Model: In one 15-minute task, a student looked for a pencil three times. In a later 15-minute task with a pencil tray, the student looked once. The tasks and time of day also differed, so the tray is only a possible explanation.
Better repeat: same desk, same kind of task, same 15-minute duration, same definition of a search (stopping work to look for a tool). Change only the pencil tray. Record each count. This is evidence about searches, not a diagnosis or proof of improved health.

B: 15-minute drawing task, afternoon, tray, 1 tool search.
Both had normal meals and breaks. No focus rating is recorded.
No physical or health experiment is required.

Independent work

Question 1. List three actual observations from the record and two interpretations that are not proven. Label each.
Question 2. Calculate the difference in tool searches. Explain why the two records cannot isolate the tray as the cause.
Question 3. Give two specific alternative explanations from the recorded differences. Name the evidence you would need to distinguish them.
Question 4. Design a safe two-trial routine check on paper, changing only tool location. Complete the plan table with fixed task, duration, setup, counting rule and comparison.

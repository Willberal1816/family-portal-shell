Make Writing Clear

Emma · Language Arts · Learn First
What you are learning: Revise sentences and short paragraphs for clear meaning by fixing vague words, confusing pronouns, repetition, order, and punctuation.

Learn the idea

Editing is not only hunting spelling errors. Clear writing lets a reader know exactly who did what in an order that makes sense. Start with meaning: can the reader follow the idea without guessing?

Pronouns such as he, she, it, they, and this need clear nouns. “When Ava handed Mia her notebook, she smiled” is confusing because “she” could mean Ava or Mia. Repeating a name once can be clearer than avoiding repetition at all costs.

Vague words such as thing, stuff, nice, bad, went, and got can hide the exact idea. Replace them when a more precise noun or verb helps. Also remove repeated information that says the same thing twice.

Finally check sentence boundaries and order. Correct sentences can still form a confusing paragraph when events are out of sequence. Revision may mean moving a sentence, combining two, or splitting a crowded one.

Words you will use

  • pronoun reference: the noun a pronoun points to
  • vague: not specific enough
  • redundant: unnecessarily repeated
  • sequence: logical or time order
  • revise: change ideas or wording for clarity

Worked examples

Confusing: “Lena told Zoe that she lost the key.” → clarify who lost it.
Vague: “We got the thing and went there.” → “We picked up the library books and walked to checkout.”
Redundant: “The tiny, small kitten…” → choose one precise description.

More worked examples

Teaching example: What is unclear in “Sam gave Ben his coat”?
Answer / reasoning: “His” could refer to Sam or Ben.
Teaching example: Should every vague word become a fancy word?
Answer / reasoning: No, only a more exact word when useful.
Teaching example: If events are told B,C,A but happened A,B,C with no reason, what revision helps?
Answer / reasoning: Reorder them logically.

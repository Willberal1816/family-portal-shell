Sound and Vibration: Read a Fair Test

Natalie | Science | Wednesday, November 25, 2026 | 50 minutes

What you are learning: Recheck Fahrenheit drops, then analyze a separate vibration study.

Learn First

Brief carryover: 80°F to 74°F is a DROP of 6°F; 80°F to 76°F is a DROP of 4°F; the difference of drops is 2°F. Temperature is a measured condition, while HEAT is energy transferred due to a temperature difference. Do not use degrees Fahrenheit as a measure of heat energy. New strand: sound comes from vibrations. Change one band length at a time while keeping band and tension fixed.

Two worked examples

Recheck: 80-74=6°F; 80-76=4°F; 6-4=2°F. None of these is a direct heat-energy quantity.
At constant band/tension, 6 inches produced 190 vibrations/second versus 2 inches at 410, in this fictional test.

Source and materials

FICTIONAL BAND FREQUENCY DATA
Same band, tension and pull each trial.
Length 6 inches: 190 vibrations/second.
Length 4 inches: 270 vibrations/second.
Length 2 inches: 410 vibrations/second.
No loudness data; no physical test required.
Never stretch an elastic band toward a face.

Directions: Answer all six questions using the teaching and source. The same six questions appear on the matching four-page printable PDF. Write legibly, scan every page and Finish Class in the School Portal. Ask for help if needed.

All six questions

Question 1. Carryover: calculate both Fahrenheit drops and the 2°F difference; define temperature versus heat.
Question 2. Create a three-row frequency and band-length table with correct units.
Question 3. Name changed variable, measured variable and at least three controls.
Question 4. Graph the three observations and describe ONLY their recorded pattern.
Question 5. Explain why the data cannot show which band was loudest and what evidence is missing.
Question 6. Design a safe fair comparison with controls, a recording chart and a cautious conclusion rule.

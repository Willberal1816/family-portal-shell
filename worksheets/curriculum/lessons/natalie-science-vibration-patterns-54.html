Sound and Vibration: Read a Fair Test

Natalie | Science | Wednesday, November 25, 2026 | 50 minutes

What you are learning: Recheck temperature-drop means and analyze a new vibration study with correct units and a repeatable measurement plan.

Learn First

Temperature is a measured condition (degrees Fahrenheit or Celsius). Heat is energy transferred because of a temperature difference; a thermometer does not directly measure the amount of energy transferred. A temperature DROP equals initial temperature minus final temperature.

Mean = sum of repeated measurements / trial count. Label each mean as soon as it is calculated; compare it with its trial range, then reverse-check mean x count = sum. A mean outside the range or a copied wrong label is a warning.

Sound comes from vibrations. Frequency is vibrations per second (hertz, Hz). In a fair test change only the band length while keeping band material, tension, pull and measuring method fixed. Frequency is not loudness. For a repeat, define the instrument, start/stop rule, trial count and same units; today use the fictional data, no actual band stretching is required.

Two worked examples

Practice temperature data: starts 70 F; ends 65, 63, 62 F. Drops = 5, 7, 8 F. Mean = (5+7+8)/3 = 6.67 F. This is temperature change, not heat energy measured in joules.
Practice frequency trials 100, 110, 120 Hz: mean 110 Hz, within 100-120. Reverse check 110 x 3 = 330. Record the mean beside the correct condition before comparing.

Source and materials

FICTIONAL DATA; NO PHYSICAL TEST REQUIRED
Temperature recheck: both cups start 78 F. Cup X final 72, 70, 74 F; cup Y final 75, 73, 74 F.
Band test: same band type, tension, pull and measurement method.
Length 6 in: 180, 190, 200 Hz. Length 4 in: 260, 270, 280 Hz.
Length 2 in: 400, 410, 420 Hz. No loudness data.

Directions: Read Learn First and both worked examples. Complete all six questions. The same questions and source appear in the four-page native printable. Scan every page, then Finish Class in the School Portal. Ask for help when needed.

All six questions

Question 1. For cups X and Y, calculate each of the three temperature drops, both mean drops and the difference. Label every result F; explain why these are not heat-energy measurements.
Question 2. Create a nine-row trial table for the band data with length, trial and frequency (Hz).
Question 3. Calculate the three mean frequencies, label each length correctly, and reverse-check one mean. Identify the changed factor, measured result and three controls.
Question 4. Graph the three mean observations using the grid, with length in inches and frequency in Hz. Describe the recorded pattern without claiming it applies to every band.
Question 5. Explain why frequency alone does not establish which band was loudest. Name the additional measurement and unit you would need (sound level in decibels).
Question 6. Design a safe two-trial-per-condition repeat on paper. State changed factor, four controls, instrument and start/stop rule, how to record both trials in Hz, and how you would compare results. No physical band test required.

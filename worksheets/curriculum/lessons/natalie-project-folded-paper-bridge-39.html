Folded Paper Bridge: Design, Load, Compare

Natalie · Wednesday, October 28, 2026 · 60 minutes

What you will make

A real bridge needs a gap beneath it and a strong surface above. This model is a folded paper span between two books; you load it with identical coins and compare different paper shapes. A flat span bends; folding the same paper into a channel with vertical walls often increases stiffness. Keep material, span distance, coin type, and loading location the same to isolate the effect of shape.

Materials and safety check

3 sheets of printer paper (same size), two heavy books or stable low boxes, ruler, tape, and 30–50 identical coins (or the same type of small counters). Use a stable desk or table; keep coins away from younger children or pets. No cutting, standing on furniture, or power tools.

Watch and do — Science Buddies demonstration

Video from Science Buddies. If the player fails to load, ask an adult to open the official demonstration video or illustrated source instructions. Do not substitute an unrelated video.

Learn First and worked model

Example (not a promised result): Flat sheet holds 1 coin, a lengthwise folded sheet holds 4, and a taped U-channel holds 18. The strongest design in that example is the channel; changing the number of folds changes the shape but not the sheet material.

Six steps to follow

  1. Watch Science Buddies build and test the paper bridges. Pause after the flat sheet and after the folded channel; use the diagram to recognize the target.
  2. Measure two thick books exactly 25 cm (about 10 inches) apart on a stable table. Ask for help if books are heavy.
  3. Lay one flat paper sheet across the gap; add identical coins carefully until it bends/falls. Record the LAST count held safely.
  4. Repeat with one equally sized sheet folded in half lengthwise, keeping the same gap, coin type and centered loading.
  5. Build the U-shaped channel shown in the video using a new equal-size sheet, fold side walls and tape to hold. Test with the same procedure.
  6. Compare all three loads, justify the strongest design with data and explain one limitation. Clear away coins and scan both finished pages.

Time plan: watch & learn 10 min; gather/check 5 min; make and test 25 min; compare/write 15 min; cleanup/scan 5 min. Print TWO pages, record what YOU observed, scan BOTH pages, then Finish this ONE class.

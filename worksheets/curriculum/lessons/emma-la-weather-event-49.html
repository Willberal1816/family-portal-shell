Weather Bulletin: What the Record Actually Says

Emma · Language Arts · Tuesday, November 17 · 55 minutes

What you are learning: Turn two reports into a complete, clearly dated weather bulletin with supported facts and a precise limitation.

Learn First — lesson and method

  1. A news bulletin answers WHAT happened, WHEN, WHERE, and WHO recorded it. A headline is short; body sentences explain the facts.
  2. Source A is a weather log. Source B is a park notice. They have different jobs: one records a measurement, and one announces a plan.
  3. A source's numbers must be copied exactly. A rain gauge number is a measurement, not an explanation of why a path was closed.
  4. Begin each reporting sentence with a subject and action verb: 'The observer measured ...' is a complete sentence.
  5. Two things happening on the same day do not prove one CAUSED the other. If the notice does not state the reason for a closure, do not invent a cause.
  6. A paragraph needs a clear beginning, linked facts, and a careful end. Write four or more complete sentences when the prompt requires them.
  7. After drafting, underline all dates, amounts and source names; correct unsupported 'because' statements and any fragments.

Two complete teaching models

Model 1: Model A: 'On Tuesday afternoon.' is a fragment. 'The weather log recorded 12 millimeters of rain on Tuesday afternoon' is a complete, checkable sentence.
Model 2: Model B: Source B says 'Path closed Wednesday for inspection.' Safe: 'The park notice schedules a Wednesday inspection closure.' Unsafe: 'Tuesday's rain forced the Wednesday closure' without evidence.
Mistake to avoid: A weather measurement does not prove a reason for a park decision, and '12 mm' does not mean twelve hours.

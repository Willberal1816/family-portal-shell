Box Nets: How Much Paper Wraps a Package?

Emma · Math · Tuesday, November 10, 2026 · 50 minutes

What you will learn: Find the area of every outside face of a box and add the areas to estimate wrapping paper needed.

Learn First

  1. A box has three pairs of equal rectangular faces. A net is the arrangement of faces unfolded onto one flat sheet.
  2. Area is measured in SQUARE units (cm²). Volume measures how much space fits INSIDE (cm³). They are not the same; the difference is important.
  3. To cover a rectangular box with length 4 cm, width 3 cm and height 2 cm: two faces are 4×3, two are 4×2 and two are 3×2. Total = 2(12)+2(8)+2(6)=52 cm².
  4. Imagine cutting and folding the illustrated net. Each outside face must be counted once; two same-size faces occur because the box has a front and back, left and right, top and bottom.
  5. After you solve, check by labeling all six faces; add an extra flap allowance ONLY when the problem explicitly asks for it.

Two fully explained models

Worked example: Box A is 4×3×2 cm. Front and back 4×2 each = 8+8; top and bottom 4×3 each =12+12; ends 3×2 each=6+6. The net adds to 52 cm².
Worked example: Box B is 5×2×2 cm. Two 5×2 faces each of two orientations produce four areas of 10 and two 2×2 ends produce two areas of 4: 4×10+2×4=48 cm². Volume instead is 5×2×2=20 cm³.
Check this mistake: Do not write 52 cm³ for wrapping paper. Surface uses cm² and counts six outside faces; volume uses cm³ and counts space inside.

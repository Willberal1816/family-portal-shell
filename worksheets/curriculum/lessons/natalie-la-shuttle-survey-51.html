Shuttle Survey: Who Actually Answered?

Natalie · Language Arts · Friday, November 20 · 50-minute learning block (55 minutes)

What you are learning: Separate sign-ins from returned surveys and limit each written conclusion to the people counted.

Learn First

A sign-in is not the same as a returned survey.

A person can arrive and choose not to answer; that person should not be counted as supporting either survey choice.

Always state the location, date and size of the responding group.

An observed preference among 12 returned surveys is not evidence about all local residents.

Important words: Respondent = a person who answered the survey. Nonrespondent = a person who did not return one. Denominator = the whole group you actually used to calculate a comparison.

Worked models — read before independent work

Model 1: At the Main Library, 18 people signed in, but only 12 returned the optional survey. Six did not respond because 18 − 12 = 6.
Model 2: Of the 12 returned surveys, eight favored later closing and four did not. The supported sentence is: 'Eight of the twelve respondents at the Main Library on November 18 favored later closing.' It is not 'Eight of all city residents favored later closing.'
Common mistake: Never silently turn a sign-in sheet into votes. A notice of a future schedule change is not evidence that the change was approved.

Independent Work — all six questions

Every assigned question is shown on this screen and included on your four-page printable. Write on your worksheet, not on the Learn First page.

Question 1. Make a labeled count table showing total sign-ins, returned surveys, favorable responses, other responses and nonrespondents. Show the subtraction for nonrespondents and explain in TWO complete sentences why sign-ins do not equal votes.

Question 2. Write TWO accurate result sentences. Each must identify WHERE and WHEN the survey occurred, WHO answered and the correct count. One sentence should discuss eight favorable responses and the other the four other responses.

Question 3. A draft says 'Most city residents demand later closing.' Identify THREE scope errors or missing facts. Rewrite the statement as one supported result sentence and one limitation sentence.

Question 4. A reporter divides eight favorable answers by eighteen total sign-ins, then writes 'Only eight of eighteen survey respondents agreed.' Explain the denominator error. State both eight of twelve respondents and eight of eighteen sign-ins, and distinguish what each number actually means.

Question 5. Write a FOUR-row evidence table: claim, exact source number, people included and what remains unknown. Include one true preference claim, one sign-in claim, one nonresponse claim, and one claim the record cannot justify.

Question 6. Write a SIX-sentence cautious summary for a library committee. Include the date and location, all five numerical facts, one comparison, the scope of the twelve respondents, the six unknown opinions and a specific additional kind of survey evidence needed before a citywide policy claim.

Weather-Station Map: Scale, Location, Coverage

Natalie · Geography · Monday, October 26, 2026

What you are learning: Use a regional station grid to compute two actual distances and decide which weather station covers a location more closely.

Learn First

Geographers use maps to compare real distances and the locations of observation stations. This simplified regional planning grid is made for practice. On this map, moving one horizontal or vertical grid step equals 2 miles; a diagonal move is NOT counted unless the question explicitly allows it.

For a street-grid route, count east–west and north–south steps, then multiply the total by 2 miles. Example: two east steps and three north steps = 5 steps × 2 = 10 miles.

Comparing two station distances requires BOTH calculations. A station being closer does not mean its weather observations must match the destination; local conditions can differ.

Fully worked example or model

Practice model: A station 3 east steps and 1 north step from a town is 4 grid steps away. Distance = 4 × 2 = 8 miles along that route.

Source and tools you will need

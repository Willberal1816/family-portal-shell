Trail Survey: Name the Respondents

Natalie · Language Arts · Wednesday November 18 · 55 minutes

What you are learning: Write a careful synthesis that specifies who answered, where, when and what remains unknown.

Learn First

  1. A number needs its population: 8 of 12 respondents is NOT 8 of all park visitors.
  2. The location is the West Gate and the time is Saturday. Responses from one gate on one day do not establish citywide opinions.
  3. Twelve of eighteen sign-ins replied, leaving six without recorded replies. The missing people may have different views; do not invent them.
  4. Use precise counts to report eight favorable responses among twelve people who returned cards.
  5. A source can be accurate and still too narrow for a broad conclusion. Distinguish respondent results from resident or visitor claims.
  6. A strong memo states source, day, gate, exact population, numerical result, limits and the additional evidence needed.

Worked models

Model 1. Supported: 'At West Gate on Saturday, 8 of the 12 survey respondents favored the longer trail.'
Model 2. Unsupported: 'Most residents want the change.' The source counts 12 respondents at one location.
Common mistake to avoid: Never swap the denominators '12 respondents' and '18 sign-ins' or treat one gate as the whole city.

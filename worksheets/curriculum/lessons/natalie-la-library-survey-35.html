Library Survey: Who Can We Claim?

Natalie · Language Arts · Thursday, October 22, 2026 · 50 minutes

Goal: Distinguish a record, a survey response, and a promotional claim without overstating the evidence.

One class: Read this Learn First lesson, work through both examples, complete all six items on TWO printable pages, scan both pages, and Finish Class. Learn First and Printable are not separate assignments.

Learn First

A survey describes the people who answered it. The response group may be smaller than everyone invited. A count from respondents cannot automatically describe all residents. A maintenance record documents an action; a flyer tries to persuade. Choose claim words that match the source.

Two worked models

Model 1. If 12 of 18 respondents prefer Saturday hours, say '12 of 18 respondents preferred Saturday hours.' Do not say 'Most townspeople preferred them' without a representative sample.
Model 2. A sign saying 'Best library in the county!' is promotion, not a measured comparison. A dated repair record can show a door was fixed on that date, but not whether everyone liked it.
Check for mistakes: Avoid 'everyone,' 'proves,' and 'caused' when the sources only show a small sample or a time sequence.

Today's information

Fictional library documents:

Survey note, October 12: The library invited 60 cardholders to answer a question about longer Saturday hours. Eighteen replied: 12 supported longer hours and 6 did not. No responses from the other 42 were recorded.

Maintenance log, October 13: Staff repaired the reading-room door latch and checked it twice.

Promotional flyer, October 14: 'Everyone in town wants our new Saturday schedule! The best library anywhere!'

Work independently using the printable below. Use U.S. customary measurements (inches, feet, miles, °F) where relevant; do not invent results.

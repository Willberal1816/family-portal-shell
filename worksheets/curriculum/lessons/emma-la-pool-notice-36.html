Learn First

A complete sentence needs a subject and a full idea. A phrase beginning with 'because' can leave the reader waiting for the main statement. Join it to a main clause: 'The indoor room is closed because the floor is wet.' A semicolon connects two complete thoughts and does NOT normally capitalize the next common noun.

Two worked examples

Worked example 1. Fragment: 'Because the afternoon session starts at two.' Fix: 'The doors open at one because the afternoon session starts at two.' Only use this if the source gives BOTH facts.
Worked example 2. Comma splice: 'The pool opens at noon, staff check passes at the gate.' Fix: 'The pool opens at noon; staff check passes at the gate.' Notice the word 'staff' stays lowercase after a semicolon.
Common mistake to avoid: Use only source facts. The punctuation repair is wrong if it makes up a schedule, a reason, or a person. Read every corrected sentence aloud to check completeness.

Historical Inquiry: A Mill Changes a Town

Natalie | Social Studies | Wednesday, November 25, 2026 | 50 minutes

What you are learning: Evaluate a notice, letter and museum note without mistaking claims for proof.

Learn First

Historians ask who wrote a source, when and why. A period notice is a primary source for advertising intentions; a later museum note is a secondary account. A recruitment ad supports that jobs were advertised, not that each applicant was hired. Compare sources and identify missing records before claiming outcomes.

Two worked examples

'Seeks 12 workers' supports advertised positions, not an exact number actually hired.
'More wagons passed' in one letter is that resident's observation, not proof of population increase.

Source and materials

ALL THREE SOURCES ARE FICTIONAL
A (1892): 'Riverton cloth mill seeks 12 workers.'
B (1905): resident letter: 'More wagons passed our street after the mill opened.'
C (1950): museum note: 'Mill opened in 1892.'
No wages, payroll, census, injury or production totals.

Directions: Answer all six questions using the teaching and source. The same six questions appear on the matching four-page printable PDF. Write legibly, scan every page and Finish Class in the School Portal. Ask for help if needed.

All six questions

Question 1. Create a source table identifying year, type, purpose and one claim from each.
Question 2. Draw a timeline and calculate years elapsed from 1892 to 1905.
Question 3. Name two supported and two unsupported claims from the recruitment notice.
Question 4. Compare the letter and advertisement viewpoints and an evidence gap in each.
Question 5. Write a 130-170 word sourced historical account with careful attribution.
Question 6. Design a museum exhibit listing two records that could better verify staffing or population.

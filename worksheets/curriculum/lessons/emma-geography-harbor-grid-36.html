Harbor Map: Read the Grid Exactly

Goal: Use letter-number map addresses, a legend, compass directions and a clear map scale.

Read Learn First, use both worked examples, complete the TWO printable pages, scan both pages, then Finish Class. Learn First and Printable are TWO PARTS of ONE class.

Learn First

Find the lettered COLUMN first, then the numbered ROW. Address B3 means column B, row 3. On a map where north is at the top, moving right is east, moving down is south. A grid scale of one square = ¼ mile means four squares = 1 mile.

Two worked examples

Worked example 1. If the pier is at B2 and the museum is at D2, travel two squares EAST. Distance = 2 × ¼ = ½ mile.
Worked example 2. If the park is at C4 and the station is at C2, go two squares NORTH. Do not write an address as 2C; the letter comes first.
Common mistake to avoid: Don't confuse the row with the column. Count the grid SPACES traveled, not the two endpoint locations. Directions must match the North arrow.

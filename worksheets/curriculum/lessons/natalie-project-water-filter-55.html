Water Filter Challenge: Test One Design Change

Natalie | Project | Tuesday, November 24, 2026 | 70 minutes

What you are learning: Compare two filter trials using controlled inputs and measured results.

Learn First

FINISHED BUILD: an inverted pre-cut bottle top with cloth across its neck, layers of media and a receiving cup. Dirty test water flows through; clearer-looking water is NEVER safe to drink.

Build baseline A and record input/output water amounts and visible dirt. Alter only one layer for B while keeping mixture, starting amount and timing method steady. Compare recovery and clarity without calling either sample purified.

Two worked examples

Filter A: 125 mL in and 75 mL out = 60 percent recovered; recovery does not mean purity.
Filter B: 125 mL in and 65 mL out with fewer specks might improve clarity but retain more water.

Source and materials

NASA/JPL BUILD: adult-pre-cut bottle, cloth/gauze, rubber band, optional sand/gravel and coffee filter, muddy water, measuring cup, 2 receiving cups and towel. NEVER DRINK.

Directions: Use the teaching and examples, then answer the six questions below. All six questions also appear on the matching four-page printable PDF. Show work, scan the pages when requested and Finish Class in the School Portal. Adult prepares cut bottle. Never drink test water.

All six questions

Question 1. Draw and label your finished design with flow direction.
Question 2. Make a two-trial table of changed factor, fixed factors, input/output volume and appearance.
Question 3. Predict how one added layer could affect clarity and recovered volume.
Question 4. Build A and measure input, output, drainage time and residue.
Question 5. Change only one layer for B, retest comparable water and calculate recovery fraction.
Question 6. Defend the better design with two observations, state a limitation and explain no drinking.

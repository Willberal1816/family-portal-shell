Which Water Test Is Stronger?

Natalie · Language Arts · Monday, September 28, 2026 · Learn First

What you are learning: write a narrow claim using exact measured evidence, link each detail to the claim, and state a real limit on what the sources prove. Read the examples, then use the separate Independent Work printable for all answers. This teaching page has no written work to turn in.

Learn the idea

An observed difference is not the same as a proven cause. A measured record can tell you what happened in the particular times and places recorded. A personal comment may tell you how one person felt, but it cannot silently replace counts. An unrelated flyer cannot support a performance claim.

A careful claim contains scope words like “in these two sessions,” names at least two exact details, explains how they support it, then identifies what is still unknown. A limitation must matter: short time period, missing comparison, or another plausible influence—not a vague sentence such as “we need more research.”

Words that help

  • measured evidence: a recorded number or observation with a defined scope
  • scope: the dates, people, place or conditions a claim actually covers
  • limitation: a real reason the record cannot answer a stronger question

Worked example 1

Suppose a log says 14 families visited on Tuesday and 19 on Wednesday. “In this record, Wednesday had five more recorded visits” is supported. “The new sign caused every visitor to come” is not. Difference: 19−14=5, but no source isolates the sign.

Worked example 2

One person writes “I liked Wednesday best.” This is evidence of that person’s experience, not an attendance count. A careful conclusion can mention the personal view separately and name the missing evidence, such as records from additional weeks.

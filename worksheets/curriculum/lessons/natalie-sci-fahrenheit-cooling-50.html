Cooling Cups: Compare the Real Drop

Natalie · Science · Wednesday November 18 · 50 minutes

What you are learning: Calculate temperature drops from Fahrenheit readings and compare controlled evidence without misnaming the measurement.

Learn First

  1. A thermometer records temperature, here in degrees Fahrenheit (°F).
  2. Temperature DROP = starting temperature − ending temperature. If water goes from 80°F to 74°F, the drop is 6°F.
  3. Both cups start at 80°F; only whether they are WRAPPED changes. Water amount, cup, room and 15-minute wait should stay fixed.
  4. Wrapped end readings 74, 73, 75°F produce drops 6, 7, 5°F. Their mean drop is 6°F.
  5. Unwrapped readings 71, 72, 70°F produce drops 9, 8, 10°F. Their mean drop is 9°F.
  6. The mean drops differ by 3°F; that does not mean a thermometer measured three joules of thermal energy.

Worked models

Model 1. Example: 80°F − 73°F = 7°F of cooling, while the FINAL temperature remains 73°F.
Model 2. Wrapped mean 6°F vs unwrapped mean 9°F: the latter cooled 3°F more on average in imaginary trials.
Common mistake to avoid: Don't confuse final temperature with amount of change, or temperature with total thermal energy.

Paper Bridge Optimization — Stronger With Less

Emma · Project · Engineering Extension
What you are learning: Improve a successful bridge by working under a tighter design constraint: use less paper while keeping as much strength as possible. Engineers call this optimization—improving performance while respecting limits.

Why shape matters

Flat paper bends easily. Folding changes the shape and can make the same sheet resist bending much better. Beams, channels, triangles, and repeated folds move material away from the center and help carry load.

Worked design decision: If Bridge A held 40 coins using two sheets and Bridge B held 32 coins using one sheet, B may be more material-efficient even though its maximum load is lower. One useful comparison is load held per sheet of paper.

Your constraint

Build a bridge spanning the same gap as your first project, but use no more than one sheet of paper for the main bridge structure. Tape may hold folds or joints but may not become the bridge itself. Test with the same type of load as before so the comparison is fair.

Engineering cycle

  1. Study your first bridge result and name one feature that helped and one weakness.
  2. Sketch two possible one-sheet designs.
  3. Choose one and explain why.
  4. Build and test it using the same span and load method.
  5. Record the failure point and maximum successful load.
  6. Make one redesign and retest.
  7. Compare strength, material use, and failure mode with your earlier bridge.
Example conclusion: “The channel-fold bridge held fewer coins than my two-sheet bridge, but it used half as much paper. The second version failed later because I added a second fold near the center where the first version buckled.”

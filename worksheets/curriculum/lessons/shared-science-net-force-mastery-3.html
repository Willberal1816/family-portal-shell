Net Force Balance Meter — Compare the Arrows

What you are learning

Today you will decide what forces do by looking at the force arrows first. The important question is not “Is the object moving?” It is: Are the forces balanced, or is one direction stronger?

The Balance Meter method

1. Name the opposite directions. Usually left/right or up/down.

2. Compare the force totals. Equal totals mean balanced. Unequal totals mean unbalanced.

3. Find net force. Subtract the smaller opposing total from the larger one. Keep the direction of the larger force.

4. Predict the change in velocity. Net force 0 means no acceleration. A nonzero net force means velocity changes in the net-force direction.

Worked model 1 — one side is stronger

← 3 N
cart
8 N →

Right is stronger. Net force = 8 N − 3 N = 5 N right.

The cart’s velocity must change toward the right. It could speed up to the right, slow down if it had been moving left, or turn toward the right. The force diagram tells us the change, not the cart’s starting motion.

Worked model 2 — moving does not mean unbalanced

← 6 N
sled
6 N →

Equal opposing forces give 0 N net force. The forces are balanced.

If the sled is already gliding right at steady speed, it can keep moving right at steady speed. Balanced forces do not mean “stopped.” They mean no change in velocity.

Worked model 3 — force can oppose the motion

A bicycle is moving right. The rider brakes, and the important horizontal force is friction to the left.

← 12 N friction
bike
2 N →

Net force = 10 N left. Because the bike is moving right while the net force points left, the bike slows down. The net force points in the direction of the velocity change, not necessarily the direction the object is already traveling.

Worked model 4 — turning counts as a velocity change

A ball rolls north at the same speed, then a sideways force makes it curve east. Its speed might stay similar, but its direction changes. Direction is part of velocity, so turning is acceleration and requires a nonzero net force.

Final check

Balanced = 0 N = no velocity change.
Unbalanced = nonzero net force = velocity changes.
Never decide from “moving” or “stopped” alone. Compare the force arrows.

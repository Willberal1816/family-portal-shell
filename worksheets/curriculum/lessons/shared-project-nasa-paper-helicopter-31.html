Make a Paper Helicopter

Emma & Natalie · Project · Wednesday, October 14, 2026 · 45–60 minutes

What will I make?

Two paper helicopters that spin as they fall. You will build one like NASA demonstrates, make a small change to the second, and test which falls more slowly.

When you are done you will have two real helicopters and a short test record.

Get these supplies

  • One printed two-page project sheet (page 1 has both cut-out helicopter patterns)
  • Scissors
  • Pencil
  • Clear floor space for dropping the paper helicopters

Safety: Use scissors carefully; ask an adult for help if needed. Test while standing on the floor and holding the helicopters at shoulder height. Do not climb furniture, stools, or stairs.

1. Watch NASA show you how

NASA's real instructor builds and drops the same style of paper helicopter. Watch the cutting, folding and test flight. Pause the video if you need to copy the instructor.

Already watched the NASA video in your School Portal class? Go to step 2. If the video will not play, use NASA's official video page as a backup.

2. Build and test

  1. Print the two-page project sheet. On page 1 you'll see Model 1 and Model 2—two ready-made paper patterns. You don't need to draw them.
  2. Cut each model out. Follow the dashed lines. Cut down the middle between A and B. Do not cut solid lines.
  3. Fold on the solid lines. Fold long blades A and B in opposite directions (one toward you, one away). Fold tabs X and Y inward toward the center. Fold the small bottom tab Z up. See NASA do each fold in the video.
  4. Drop Model 1. Hold its body by the bottom and release from shoulder height. It should spin as it falls. If it won't spin, check that A and B are folded in opposite directions.
  5. Make one change to Model 2. Start with exactly the same cuts and folds. Then fold down the top one fingertip's width (about 1 cm) of both blades to make the blades shorter. Do not make any other change.
  6. Try three side-by-side drops. Hold Model 1 in one hand and Model 2 in the other, both at the same height. Let them go together. Which hits the floor last? Circle your answer for Drop 1 on page 2. Do the same for Drops 2 and 3.
  7. Finish the project. Answer the three short questions on page 2 using what you actually saw. Photograph the two helicopters if possible. Scan both pages with your name and then return to the School Portal to Finish Class.
What a completed answer could look like: “Model 1 landed last two times and Model 2 landed last once. The only change was shorter blades on Model 2. Three tests give a clue, but I would do more to check.” This is only an example; use your own observations.

This is a hands-on project. Spend most of the class cutting, folding, and testing—not writing. If you need help, replay that part of NASA's video.

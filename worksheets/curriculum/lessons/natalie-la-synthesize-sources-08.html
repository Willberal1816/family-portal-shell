Synthesize Evidence Across Sources

Natalie · Language Arts · Learn First
Goal: Combine evidence from two sources, choose the strongest source for each kind of fact, and write a neutral 4–5 sentence synthesis.

1. Match the claim to the source

The “best source” depends on the claim. For an exact project date, a dated project record or direct interview with the responsible person is usually stronger than a general news summary. For public reaction, interviews with affected people may be more useful.

Example: A city project log says construction began May 6. A neighborhood article says construction began “in early May.” For the exact date, use the project log; the article can still help explain neighborhood reaction.

2. Separate agreement, contribution, and uncertainty

First identify what both sources support. Then identify what each source uniquely contributes. If the sources leave something unresolved, say so rather than inventing certainty.

3. Keep the tone neutral

Avoid: “Source B is obviously biased and wrong.”
Prefer: “Source B emphasizes cost to residents, while Source A emphasizes the construction timeline.”
4–5 sentence plan: (1) shared finding, (2) strongest evidence from Source A, (3) strongest evidence from Source B, (4) how they fit or differ, (5 optional) remaining uncertainty.

4. Obey the requested output

Yesterday your source comparison was strong. Today, treat the 4–5 sentence limit as part of the task. Precision includes both evidence and format.

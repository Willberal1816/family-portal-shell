Which Sale Really Wins? Discounts, Tax, Delivery

Natalie | Math | Friday, November 13, 2026 | 50 minutes

What you are learning

Evaluate different discount rules, taxable subtotals and delivery fees, then PROVE which offer is best under a budget.

Learn First: follow the reasoning

  1. Two stores can advertise different discounts yet produce a surprising final total. Work from ORIGINAL PRICES to sale prices to subtotal to tax to non-taxed delivery.
  2. Today's fictional stores charge five percent tax on the discounted goods subtotal. Delivery is added AFTER tax and is not taxed in this comparison.
  3. Store A sells an eighty-dollar jacket discounted by twenty-five percent plus thirty-dollar trousers. Jacket discount is twenty dollars; jacket sale price sixty dollars.
  4. Store A subtotal is sixty plus thirty = ninety dollars. Five percent tax is four dollars and fifty cents. Six-dollar delivery makes the final A total one hundred dollars and fifty cents.
  5. Store B sells a seventy-two-dollar jacket discounted by fifteen percent plus thirty-two-dollar trousers. Jacket sale is sixty-one dollars and twenty cents; subtotal ninety-three dollars and twenty cents.
  6. Store B tax is four dollars and sixty-six cents; free delivery makes its final ninety-seven dollars and eighty-six cents. Store B is two dollars and sixty-four cents cheaper.
  7. A responsible comparison quotes the actual promotion and budget, shows calculations in order, and checks whether a small delivery change reverses the winner.

Two fully worked models

Model 1

Worked model A: $80 times 0.25 = $20 savings. ($80 - $20) + $30 = $90 subtotal. $90 times 0.05 = $4.50 tax. $90 + $4.50 + $6 delivery = $100.50.

Model 2

Worked model B: $72 times 0.15 = $10.80 savings. ($72 - $10.80) + $32 = $93.20 subtotal. Tax = $4.66. Total = $97.86, which is $2.64 lower than A.

A mistake to avoid

Do NOT tax the delivery amount under these stated rules; do NOT apply a jacket-only discount to the trousers. Read the scope of each promotion.

Reading: Who Tells the Story?

Emma | Reading | Monday, November 23, 2026 | 45 minutes

What you are learning: Identify the storyteller's point of view and distinguish a statement from an inference.

Learn First

Read your current book independently for 25 minutes. A first-person narrator uses I/we; a third-person narrator often uses character names, he, she or they. Record exact page numbers.

Worked models

'I hid the key' uses first-person narration; the speaker says I.
'Mina opened the door' uses third person. The sentence alone does not prove Mina was worried.

Check your work: Read the source labels; check calculations, evidence, or drawing guides before turning in.

Source and materials

YOUR OWN CURRENT BOOK: read for 25 minutes.
Record the title, author and first/last page read.
Choose a passage you can find again; cite exact pages.
If nonfiction, analyze the author's explanatory voice.

Answer all six questions using this teaching and source. Scan all four worksheet pages, then Finish Class in the School Portal. Ask for help when needed.

All six independent-work questions

Question 1. Write your book title, author and reading page range. Summarize one moment from today's pages.
Question 2. Identify first- or third-person narration. Quote a short clue and give its exact page.
Question 3. Identify one thing the narrator knows for certain, with a page and supporting detail.
Question 4. Give one possible inference about a person or event and state what extra evidence would strengthen it.
Question 5. Rewrite two sentences from the passage in the other point of view. Explain what information the new voice lacks.
Question 6. Write a six-sentence reader note separating a narrator statement, a supported fact, your inference and an unanswered question.

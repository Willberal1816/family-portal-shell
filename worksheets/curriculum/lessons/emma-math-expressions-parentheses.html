Expressions with Parentheses

What you are learning

An expression is a math phrase made with numbers and operations. Parentheses group part of the expression so you know what belongs together and what should be evaluated first.

Words to know

expression: a math phrase such as 6 + 3 × 4. It has no equals sign.

parentheses: grouping symbols ( ) that tell you to treat the inside as one grouped part.

evaluate: find the value of an expression.

Worked example 1 — parentheses change the value

4 × (3 + 2)

First evaluate the grouped part: 3 + 2 = 5.

Then multiply: 4 × 5 = 20.

Compare that with 4 × 3 + 2. Multiplication happens first, so 12 + 2 = 14. The parentheses changed which numbers were grouped together.

Turn words into an expression

“Multiply 6 by the sum of 4 and 5.”

The words the sum of 4 and 5 name one grouped amount, so write 6 × (4 + 5).

Worked example 2 — difference inside a group

“Three times the difference between 12 and 7.”

Difference means subtraction. The difference belongs together: 3 × (12 − 7).

12 − 7 = 5, then 3 × 5 = 15.

Worked example 3 — compare two expressions

(8 + 2) × 5 = 10 × 5 = 50

8 + 2 × 5 = 8 + 10 = 18

Same numbers, different grouping, different values.

Common mistakes

Do not add parentheses randomly. They must match the meaning of the words or the intended grouping.

Do not turn an expression into an equation. If the prompt asks for an expression, you do not need an equals sign unless you are also asked to evaluate it.

Before independent work

Find the grouped words → put that part in parentheses → write the rest of the operations → evaluate only when asked.

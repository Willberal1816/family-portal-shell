Independent Reading: Fact, Inference, Uncertainty

Natalie · Reading · Thursday, November 12, 2026 · 45 minutes

What you are learning: Read for at least 25 active minutes and sort a character claim into what the text states and what you infer.
Read your own book for 25 active minutes FIRST. Then begin the response sheets.

Learn First

  1. Continue your own book for 25 uninterrupted active minutes; then complete the response.
  2. A stated fact is something the text explicitly tells you. An inference is a reasoned idea based on clues, not a direct quote.
  3. A strong inference uses more than one supporting detail from the pages and keeps the claim no stronger than the evidence.
  4. Separate 'the narrator says' from 'I think'. If the book does not confirm a motive, call it a possibility.
  5. Cite the real page numbers for each clue so another reader can independently check the claim.

Two worked examples

Made-up model A: The text says Ari missed the train on page 15. Fact: Ari missed the train. If page 16 says he frowned and sat on the curb, he may be disappointed, but the feeling remains an inference.
Made-up model B: If the narrator says 'Ari was upset,' the emotion is then explicitly stated. A claim should be labeled based on exactly what the text supplies.
A mistake to avoid: Two events happening one after another do not prove a motive. Keep uncertainty visible.

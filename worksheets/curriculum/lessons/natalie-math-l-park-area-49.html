The L-Shaped Park: Area, Fence and a Gate

Natalie · Math · Tuesday, November 17 · 50 minutes

What you are learning: Use two valid decompositions to prove the area and perimeter of an L-shaped region; evaluate a fencing budget.

Learn First — lesson and method

  1. Area covers the inside of a shape and is measured in square units (m²). Perimeter traces its OUTSIDE boundary and is measured in linear units (m).
  2. The park fits inside a 12-m-by-8-m rectangle, except a 4-m-by-3-m corner is missing from the upper right.
  3. Subtract the missing corner's area from the large bounding rectangle: 12x8 - 4x3 = 96 - 12 = 84 m².
  4. A second proof divides the L into a bottom 12x5 rectangle (60 m²) and an upper left 8x3 rectangle (24 m²). 60+24=84 m².
  5. The perimeter must follow every outside edge including the notch: 12+5+4+3+8+8 = 40 m.
  6. A 2-m open gate on the bottom edge means only 40-2=38 m of fencing is installed. At $6 per meter, cost=38x6=$228.
  7. The drawing is schematic, not drawn to exact scale. Use the printed dimension labels, and write units on every final numerical result.

Two complete teaching models

Model 1: Model A: Subtraction proof: (12m x 8m) - (4m x 3m) = 84 m². A missing rectangle is removed ONCE.
Model 2: Model B: Perimeter proof: Walk bottom12, right5, notch4, up3, top8, left8 -> 40m. With an open 2m gate, 38m of fencing costs $228.
Mistake to avoid: Do not subtract the cut-out's perimeter from the big rectangle's perimeter. Trace the new outer edges, including the notch.

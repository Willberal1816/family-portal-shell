Book Club Report: Count Respondents

Natalie · Language Arts · Saturday November 21, 2026 · 55 minutes

What you are learning: Distinguish survey respondents from attendees and make cautious claims.

Learn First

A sign-in is not always a survey answer. The denominator must match the respondents counted.

Two worked models

Model 1: 21 signed in, 15 returned cards, so 6 returned none. Only 15 are respondents.
Model 2: Nine of the 15 respondents picked afternoon; it is unsupported to claim all 21 did.
Check for this mistake: Do not count missing cards as 'no' votes.

Source and facts

  • Fictional library club, November 16: 21 people signed in.
  • 15 choice cards returned: 9 afternoon; 6 evening.
  • 6 signed-in people did NOT return a card. Their reasons are unknown.

Read the models, then complete your six questions using your source. Write only what the source supports.

All six independent-work questions

These exact questions are visible on screen and appear in the matching four-page PDF. Write on paper, scan all pages and finish this class in the School Portal.

Question 1. Make a four-row facts table for sign-ins, returned cards, afternoon and evening; label each counted group.

Question 2. Calculate nonrespondents and write two sentences distinguishing them from evening voters.

Question 3. Correct 'All 21 members voted' and 'Nine of 21 card respondents chose afternoon' using the true groups.

Question 4. Write five factual sentences including date, place, numbers, which answer led and what the count cannot prove.

Question 5. Create a two-column choice-and-count table, then explain why its values total 15 and not 21.

Question 6. Draft a six-sentence correction to 'The whole club prefers afternoon,' giving two facts and one clear limitation.

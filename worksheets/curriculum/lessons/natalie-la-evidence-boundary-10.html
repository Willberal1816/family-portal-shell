Evidence Boundary Audit

Natalie · Language Arts · 25 minutes Learn First + 30 minutes independent evidence

What you are learning

Strong evidence writing does three things: it states exactly what the record supports, explains the reasoning link, and names what the evidence cannot establish.

The boundary sentence

Use this frame: The evidence supports ___ because ___. It does not establish ___.

Worked model:

Claim: A new library checkout station reduced wait time.

Record: Monday, 32 students in 12 minutes. Tuesday after the change, 35 students in 8 minutes.

Careful conclusion: The Tuesday record supports that checkout was faster during the measured period because more students were processed in fewer minutes. It does not establish that the station will always be faster.

Four kinds of limits

LimitQuestion to ask
TimeWas this measured once or across many days?
CauseCould staffing, group size, or another condition explain the change?
AccessWas the source able to observe or measure the claim?
ScopeDoes the wording say always, everyone, best, or never when the evidence is narrow?
Overclaim repair: Do not replace “always works better” with “works better.” Keep the boundary: “The measured Tuesday period was faster than Monday; more days are needed to know whether the improvement lasts.”

Build the five-sentence evaluation

  1. Careful claim.
  2. Best exact evidence.
  3. Reasoning link: explain why the evidence supports the claim.
  4. Weaker or irrelevant evidence and why it is weaker.
  5. Limitation: name what remains uncertain.
Final check: Did I use a number or exact detail? Did I explain the link? Did I name a real limit? Did I remove words such as always unless the evidence truly supports them?

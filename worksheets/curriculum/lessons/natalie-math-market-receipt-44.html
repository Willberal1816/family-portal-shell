Market Receipt: Discounts, Tax and Budget

Natalie · Math · Wednesday, November 11, 2026 · 50 minutes

What you are learning: Calculate a final receipt in the correct order and make a budget decision with labeled money amounts.

Learn First

  1. A discount reduces the original item price. Multiply original price by the percent as a decimal, then subtract the savings.
  2. Sales tax is calculated on the discounted subtotal in this fictional store, not on the original prices.
  3. Combine items in this order: discount each item, add sale prices, calculate tax on that subtotal, and add tax.
  4. Round money to two decimal places when needed. Record each step with dollar signs so a reader knows which number is a price, savings, or tax.
  5. A budget decision compares the final total, not just the original sticker price, with the available money.

See the complete models

Model 1: A $50 bag is 20% off. Savings = 0.20 x 50 = $10; discounted price = $40. At 5% tax, tax = 0.05 x 40 = $2, so the final price is $42.
Model 2: A $25 kit with no discount and a $10 notebook with 10% off cost $25 + $9 = $34 before tax. A 5% tax adds $1.70, giving $35.70.
A mistake to avoid: Do not take tax from the original sticker price after a discount. Use the sale subtotal stated in the store policy.

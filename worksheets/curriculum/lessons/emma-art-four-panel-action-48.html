Four-Panel Comic: Show a Change Without Guessing

Emma · Art · Monday, November 16, 2026 · 50 minutes

What you are learning

Draw a clear beginning, problem, choice and ending using pictures, action cues, and one consistent visual character.

Learn First

  1. A four-panel comic tells a short story using four separate pictures, read from left to right and top to bottom.
  2. Choose a simple, original character such as a robot with a square head. Keep its defining details alike in all four panels.
  3. Panel 1 sets a place. Panel 2 introduces a visible problem. Panel 3 shows the character's own action. Panel 4 shows the result.
  4. Use action lines around a moving object and clear faces or body poses to show movement and feeling without lengthy explanations.
  5. Start with a thumbnail sketch—four small boxes that show what will go in each panel. Do not spend all your time on decorations.
  6. Draw the finished four-panel comic in the large blank comic grid. Include at least two speech bubbles or captions; make text readable.
  7. Your artist's statement explains one specific design change and how a viewer can recognize what happened. Revising a panel is part of making art.

Two worked models

Model 1: Original model: Panel 1 robot carries a square box. Panel 2 rain begins and the box lid lifts. Panel 3 robot holds the lid with both hands. Panel 4 the box is safe under a porch.
Model 2: Original model: A moving hand can have three short motion lines; an excited expression can have wide eyes. The drawing shows meaning before a reader reads the caption.
A mistake to avoid: Four unrelated pictures are not a sequence. Make the same character and the problem-to-action change readable across all panels.

Independent work — after teaching

Complete all six tasks on three paper work pages. For drawing tasks, use the provided large panels. Scan all THREE numbered pages, then return to your same active School Portal class and Finish Class. Do not claim you performed measurements or reading you did not actually do.

Time plan: 10 minutes planning, 30 minutes creating and 10 minutes revising.

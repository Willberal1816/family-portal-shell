Who Was Asked? Write Claims That Fit the Evidence

Natalie · Language Arts · Tuesday, November 10, 2026 · 55 minutes

What you will learn: Independently include WHO/WHERE/WHEN with every surveyed-result claim, then state what cannot be inferred.

Learn First

  1. First name the exact GROUP surveyed: 24 people exiting the NORTH gate of a fictional zoo, during one hour on Tuesday November 3. A survey of ONE gate is NOT a survey of all zoo visitors.
  2. Make a sentence frame: 'Among the 24 ______ surveyed at ______ on ______ between ______ and ______, ___ chose __.' Fill ALL the blanks each time.
  3. When calculating a percent, use COUNT OF CHOICE ÷ COUNT SURVEYED × 100. Here 15÷24×100=62.5%; the statement is ONLY about the 24 surveyed north-gate visitors.
  4. A survey that asked WHAT people preferred did not ask WHY. Do not invent cost, weather, fear or any cause. It also cannot represent people outside that time and gate without more information.
  5. Before turning in, do a boundary check for EVERY result sentence: number ✓, group ✓, gate/place ✓, date/time ✓; plus a separate 'cannot tell' limitation sentence.

Two fully explained models

Example 1: 'Among the 24 north-gate zoo visitors surveyed on Tuesday November 3 between 11 a.m. and noon, 15 preferred longer talks.' Every boundary is present.
Example 2 error and correction: '63% of everyone at the zoo wanted a longer talk because it was fun' is unsupported. Correction: 'Of the 24 visitors surveyed at the north gate during the one-hour Tuesday session, 15 (62.5%) wanted a longer talk.' The survey did not ask why.
Check this mistake: A correct ratio alone is not a correct public claim. The surveyed group, location and time must stay inside the sentence every time.

Look at the source and visual reference

Fictional field study — NOT a zoo-wide sample: North exit only on Tuesday November 3, 11:00 a.m.–noon, 24 respondents. 15 longer talks, 9 shorter talks. No reasons were collected, and no other gates or time windows were sampled.

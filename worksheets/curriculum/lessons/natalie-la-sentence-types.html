Kinds of Sentences

Natalie · Language Arts · Guided Teaching
What you are learning: Identify what a sentence is doing, then use its structure and punctuation to make that purpose clear.

Three core purposes

  • Declarative: makes a statement or gives information.
  • Interrogative: asks a direct question.
  • Imperative: gives a command, direction, or request.
Declarative: The telescope is stored near the window.
Interrogative: Where is the telescope stored?
Imperative: Move the telescope away from the window.

Purpose and structure are related, but not identical

A sentence can have a simple, compound, or complex structure while still serving one of these purposes. When editing, first decide what the writer wants the sentence to do; then check whether the words and punctuation make that purpose clear.

Check the complete thought

A working sentence needs a complete idea. If a group of words leaves the reader waiting for the rest of the thought, it may be a fragment rather than a complete sentence.

Fragment: Because the storm arrived early. Complete: Because the storm arrived early, the game ended before sunset.

Bird Count Notice: Complete Facts

Emma · Language Arts · Saturday November 21, 2026 · 55 minutes

What you are learning: Write complete factual sentences and explain an evidence limit.

Learn First

A complete sentence names who or what and an action. State only facts in the record.

Two worked models

Model 1: Strong: On Monday, two volunteers counted 14 birds by the feeder.
Model 2: No weather was recorded. State the count changed; do NOT claim that rain caused it.
Check for this mistake: Repair fragments by adding a subject and verb; never invent causes.

Source and facts

  • Fictional feeder log Monday November 16: 14 birds; 2 observers.
  • Tuesday November 17: 19 birds; same 2 observers.
  • Each count lasted 20 minutes. No weather or cause was recorded.

Read the models, then complete your six questions using your source. Write only what the source supports.

All six independent-work questions

These exact questions are visible on screen and appear in the matching four-page PDF. Write on paper, scan all pages and finish this class in the School Portal.

Question 1. Write four different complete source-supported sentences and mark the subject and verb of each.

Question 2. Repair these fragments: 'On Tuesday near the feeder.' and 'Nineteen birds during the count.'

Question 3. Calculate the difference between 19 and 14 and give two complete factual sentences using both dates.

Question 4. Correct the unsupported sentence 'Rain caused the increase.' Identify the missing fact and rewrite accurately.

Question 5. Write a six-sentence community notice giving date, counts, duration, observers, calculated difference and one evidence limit.

Question 6. Revise your notice into six clear sentences. List three exact edits to grammar, numbers or unsupported wording.

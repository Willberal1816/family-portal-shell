Food, Energy & Balanced Meals

What this lesson teaches: foods provide different nutrients and jobs. A balanced meal combines useful sources of energy, protein, fruits/vegetables, and fluids rather than treating one food as “good” or “bad.” Required meal design is on the printable.

Definitions

Nutrient/groupMain jobsExamples
CarbohydratesMajor source of usable energygrains, fruit, potatoes, beans, milk
ProteinGrowth, repair, enzymes and body structureseggs, beans, meat, fish, dairy, tofu
FatsCells, stored energy, absorption of some vitaminsnuts, seeds, oils, avocado, dairy
Fruits/vegetablesVitamins, minerals, water, fiber and varietymany colors and types
WaterTemperature regulation, transport and normal body functionswater and water-containing foods/drinks

Worked meal example

A lunch of a turkey or bean sandwich, apple slices, carrots, and water combines carbohydrate-rich bread, protein, fruits/vegetables, and fluid. Balance does not require a perfect plate at every meal; patterns over time matter.

Why “healthy” does not mean “eat as little as possible”

Growing bodies need enough energy and nutrients. Nutrition is about meeting needs with variety and balance, not simply minimizing food.

Improving a familiar meal

If a meal is pasta with sauce, one way to add variety could be a protein food and a vegetable or fruit alongside it. The goal is to add useful components, not label the original meal as bad.

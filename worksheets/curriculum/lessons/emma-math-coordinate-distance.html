Horizontal & Vertical Distance

Emma · Math · Learn First
What you are learning: Find horizontal and vertical distances on a coordinate plane, including across zero, and explain why distance is always nonnegative.

Learn the idea

Two points on the same horizontal line have equal y-coordinates. Their distance comes from the x-coordinates. Two points on the same vertical line have equal x-coordinates, so their distance comes from the y-coordinates.

Distance tells how far apart points are, not which direction you traveled. It is never negative. From x=-4 to x=3, move 4 units to zero and 3 more to 3: 7 units total.

You can also subtract and use the positive size of the difference. For (-4,2) and (3,2), the horizontal separation is 7. Plotting is a useful check when negative coordinates feel confusing.

For a route made of horizontal and vertical segments, find each segment separately and add. Keep track of which coordinate stays the same so you do not accidentally measure a diagonal as if it were horizontal or vertical.

Words you will use

  • horizontal: left-to-right; same y-coordinate
  • vertical: up-and-down; same x-coordinate
  • distance: nonnegative space between locations
  • segment: a straight part of a route

Worked examples

Example 1: (-5,3) to (2,3) is 7 units horizontally.
Example 2: (4,-6) to (4,1) is 7 units vertically.
Example 3: (-2,1) → (5,1) → (5,-3) is 7 + 4 = 11 units.

More worked examples

Teaching example: Points (2,5) and (2,-3) form what kind of segment?
Answer / reasoning: Vertical.
Teaching example: Why is distance from -6 to -2 equal to 4, not -4?
Answer / reasoning: Distance measures size of separation.
Teaching example: What must be true before using only x-values for distance?
Answer / reasoning: The y-coordinates must match.

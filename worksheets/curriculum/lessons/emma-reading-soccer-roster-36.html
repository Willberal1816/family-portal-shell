Learn First

Read your own book for 25 minutes, then read this NEW passage. For a summary, use three parts: visible problem (what was wrong), action sequence (what happened first, next, and last), and verified outcome (what actually changed). A useful written record keeps people from guessing.

Two worked examples

Worked example 1. Model A: 'The equipment box was missing two cones' is a visible problem. 'The cones were blue' is a detail but NOT the problem.
Worked example 2. Model B: To write three actions, use verbs in order: 'checked the list → found the labeled bag → returned the cones.' Finish with the observed result: 'The coach began practice with all six cones.'
Common mistake to avoid: Do not replace the visible problem with only its cause. Do not skip the middle steps or state an imagined outcome. Include how the record avoided guessing.

The Team Roster

At the community soccer field, June arranged the equipment before the Saturday practice. Eight players were expected, but the new roster on the clipboard listed only six names. Practice would start in fifteen minutes.

June thought someone had forgotten to sign up. Instead of guessing which players were missing, Coach Malik opened the dated registration notebook. It showed eight player names, including Ava and Ben, whose entries had been left off the new sheet.

First, June compared the clipboard roster with the dated notebook. Next, Coach Malik carefully copied Ava's and Ben's names onto the roster. Then June counted the eight names and called them out while the coach checked the registration entries one more time.

When the players arrived, the coach had the complete eight-name list. He divided the players into two equal groups and began practice on time. June stored the notebook so it could be checked again at the next practice.

Food Label: Count the Servings

Emma and Natalie · Health · Thursday, October 22, 2026 · 35 minutes

Goal: Use serving size to read a nutrition label and make accurate, nonjudgmental comparisons.

One class: Read this Learn First lesson, work through both examples, complete all six items on TWO printable pages, scan both pages, and Finish Class. Learn First and Printable are not separate assignments.

Learn First

A Nutrition Facts label usually gives amounts for ONE serving. If you eat two servings, multiply each listed amount by two. Check servings per container before saying what is in the whole package. Food labels provide information, not a rule that a food is 'good' or 'bad'.

Two worked models

Model 1. If one serving contains 120 calories and a container has two servings, the whole container has 2 × 120 = 240 calories.
Model 2. If one serving has 180 mg sodium, two servings have 360 mg. Write the unit (mg) and explain that the label cannot tell what a particular person actually ate.
Check for mistakes: Do not confuse serving size with package size. Do not turn a label fact into a claim about health outcomes.

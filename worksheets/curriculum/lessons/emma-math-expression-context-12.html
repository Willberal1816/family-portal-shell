Expressions in Context — Preserve the Operation

Emma · Math · Learn First · about 35 minutes
Goal: Write and evaluate one numerical expression for a multi-step situation while preserving every operation and using parentheses only when the story groups quantities together.

1. Translate the story in chunks

Underline the quantities and operation words. Decide what must happen first, then write one expression before calculating.

Worked example 1: Four bags hold 6 marbles each. Then 5 marbles are given away.
Expression: 4 × 6 − 5
Evaluate: 24 − 5 = 19.
Worked example 2: A $30 cost is shared equally by 5 people, and each person adds a $2 tip.
Expression: 30 ÷ 5 + 2
Evaluate: 6 + 2 = 8.

2. Parentheses group a quantity—they do not replace an operation

Worked example 3: Three teams each collect 8 cans Monday and 4 cans Tuesday.
Each team has (8 + 4); there are 3 teams.
Expression: 3 × (8 + 4) = 3 × 12 = 36.
Friday’s trap: After simplifying parentheses, keep the operation outside them. If the expression is 24 ÷ (8 − 2), first get 6, then the expression becomes 24 ÷ 6—not 24 − 6.

3. Check whether parentheses actually change meaning

Compare: 5 + 3 × 4 = 17, but (5 + 3) × 4 = 32. The parentheses matter because they force addition before multiplication.
Three-step check before you submit:
  1. Point to the outside operation after any parentheses.
  2. Make sure your expression includes every action in the story.
  3. Estimate whether the final answer is reasonable.

When you can explain why 24 ÷ (8 − 2) becomes 24 ÷ 6, open the Independent Work.

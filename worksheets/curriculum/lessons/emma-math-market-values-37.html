Market Shelf: Compare the Cost per Item

Emma · Math · Monday, October 26, 2026

What you are learning: Find the cost of one item before choosing the better offer; show the calculation and the meaning of the result.

Learn First

Stores often package different numbers of items together. The larger package is not always the better deal. To compare fairly, calculate price of one item = package price ÷ number of items. Then compare the costs for one item.

A package of 3 pencils costs $9. One pencil costs $9 ÷ 3 = $3. A package of 5 pencils costs $20. One pencil costs $20 ÷ 5 = $4. The 3-pencil pack is less expensive per pencil, even though the full pack costs less too.

Check by multiplying the single-item price by the item count: 3 × $3 = $9. Never compare only the package sticker price when the counts differ. All prices in today's fictional store are exact; there is no tax.

Fully worked example or model

An 8-card set for $16 costs $2 per card. A 5-card set for $15 costs $3 per card. For the same number of cards, the first is the better value.

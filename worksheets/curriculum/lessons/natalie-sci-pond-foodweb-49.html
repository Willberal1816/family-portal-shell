Pond Food Web: One Species, Several Pathways

Natalie · Science · Tuesday, November 17 · 50 minutes

What you are learning: Interpret a food-web network, predict limited responses to a disturbance, and identify evidence needed to test a claim.

Learn First — lesson and method

  1. A food web combines several food chains because organisms may have more than one food source or predator.
  2. Arrows show energy moving from what is eaten TO the eater, not which creature is bigger. A single organism can receive more than one arrow.
  3. The example has algae feeding snails and insect larvae; both may feed fish; fish feed herons. The arrows form two separate routes from algae to heron.
  4. When one food source declines, a species with an alternative food source may still obtain energy. The diagram alone does not measure how MUCH food each receives.
  5. Trophic levels describe feeding position in a particular chain, not a permanent single rank for every species in every environment.
  6. Disturbance reasoning is conditional: state the proposed change, at least two plausible paths and a limitation.
  7. A scientific investigation would count real organisms, measure change over time and control for season, habitat and sampling method; invented diagram alone does not prove actual population decline.

Two complete teaching models

Model 1: Worked model A: algae -> snail -> fish -> heron and algae -> insect larvae -> fish -> heron are TWO distinct paths through the same web.
Model 2: Worked model B: Fewer snails could reduce one food route to fish, but larvae still feed fish in this model. Without quantity data, we cannot conclude fish must decline by a specific amount.
Mistake to avoid: Don't count a food-web arrow as a verified population measurement or assume removing one link makes every predator disappear.

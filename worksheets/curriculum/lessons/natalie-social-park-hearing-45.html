Park Hearing: Plans, Testimony and Source Limits

Natalie · Social Studies · Thursday, November 12, 2026 · 50 minutes

What you are learning: Distinguish what a public meeting notice proves from a witness comment and attendance count; avoid unsupported cause claims.

Learn First

  1. A meeting notice reports when and where an event is scheduled. It does not prove what attendees thought.
  2. A witness statement reports one person's experience or opinion. It cannot speak for every attendee without additional evidence.
  3. A sign-in count is a number of visitors who signed in, not necessarily every person who came.
  4. A chronology tells the sequence of dates; 'after' is not automatically 'because'. A cause claim needs a source that actually connects events.
  5. Cite the named source with its exact scope: notice, one witness, or sign-in list.

Two worked examples

Model A: Notice says 'Meeting Wednesday at 6:00 p.m.' Supported: 'The committee announced a Wednesday evening meeting.' Unsupported: 'All neighbors attended.'
Model B: Sign-in sheet lists 24 names. You may say '24 names appear on the sign-in sheet'; you may not say exactly 24 people attended if someone might not have signed.
A mistake to avoid: One visitor's support does not establish that every park user supported the proposal, and a count alone does not establish the reason for any change.

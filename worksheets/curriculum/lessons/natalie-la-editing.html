Edit Your Sentences

Natalie · Language Arts · Learn First
What you are learning: Edit sentence boundaries, clause connections, punctuation, concision, and parallel structure while preserving meaning.

Learn the idea

Editing starts with structure. Find independent clauses—groups of words that could stand as complete sentences. Two independent clauses joined only by a comma create a comma splice; pushed together with no punctuation, they form a run-on.

Choose the repair that matches the relationship: a period creates separation; a semicolon joins closely related complete ideas; a comma plus a coordinating conjunction can show contrast, cause, or result. Dependent clauses must attach to complete ideas.

Clarity also depends on concision. Remove repetition, empty phrases, and wording that says the same thing twice. Concision does not mean making everything short; it means every word earns its place.

Parallel structure helps lists and comparisons. “She likes hiking, to cook, and drawing” mixes forms. “She likes hiking, cooking, and drawing” is parallel. Read aloud to hear whether repeated structures match.

Words you will use

  • independent clause: a subject-verb group expressing a complete thought
  • dependent clause: a clause that cannot stand alone
  • comma splice: independent clauses joined with only a comma
  • concision: clear expression without unnecessary wording
  • parallel structure: matching grammatical forms

Worked examples

Comma splice: “The storm passed, the roads stayed flooded.” → “The storm passed, but the roads stayed flooded.”
Concision: “At this point in time, we are currently ready.” → “We are ready now.”
Parallel: “The job requires measuring, cutting, and checking.”

More worked examples

Teaching example: Is “Although the road was closed.” complete?
Answer / reasoning: No; “although” makes it dependent.
Teaching example: Two repairs for “I finished the draft, I still needed to revise”?
Answer / reasoning: Use period/semicolon, or comma + but.
Teaching example: What is wrong with “collect food, helping neighbors, and a fundraiser”?
Answer / reasoning: The list is not parallel.

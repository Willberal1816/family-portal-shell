Tote Bags: What Repeats, What Is Extra?

Emma | Math | Friday, November 13, 2026 | 50 minutes

What you are learning

Draw all repeated groups, place loose extras in ONE outside area, and match each drawing to an exact expression.

Learn First: follow the reasoning

  1. Imagine packing identical tote bags for a club. Each bag is ONE group. Draw a rectangle for EACH bag before you choose a mathematical expression.
  2. An item that belongs in every bag repeats. Four bags holding three pencils each contain four groups of three: four times three equals twelve pencils.
  3. An item sitting on the counter outside all bags is counted once. Two loose pencils mean four times three PLUS two, giving fourteen pencils. Draw those two extras only once.
  4. If EVERY bag holds three pencils AND two erasers, the three and two are both INSIDE the repeated group. Each bag holds five items. Four times (three plus two) equals twenty.
  5. A multiplication sign shows the number of equal groups; parentheses hold together the items repeated inside each group. The drawings tell you which expression fits the story.
  6. To check your answer, count all drawn objects once. If two loose items appear under four different bags, you accidentally counted extras four times.
  7. An accurate explanation names the repeated items, names the one-time extras, gives the expression, and verifies the total.

Two fully worked models

Model 1

Example 1: Draw four bag outlines. Put PPP in each. Draw EE on the counter only once. 4 x 3 + 2 = 14. The two E marks are NOT inside any bag.

Model 2

Example 2: Draw four bags, each containing PPP and EE. There are five objects per bag. 4 x (3 + 2) = 20. Parentheses belong here because both kinds of item repeat.

A mistake to avoid

If you put two extras beside EVERY tote, you have changed the story. Count how many groups have the extra before writing parentheses.

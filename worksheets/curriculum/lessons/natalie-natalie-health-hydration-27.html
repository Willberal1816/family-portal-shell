Hydration: Notice, Choose, Recheck

Natalie · Health · Sunday, September 27, 2026 · Learn First

What you are learning: notice ordinary thirst and activity cues, make a reasonable water plan, and name when to tell a parent. Read the examples, then use the separate Independent Work printable for all answers. This teaching page has no written work to turn in.

Learn the idea

Water supports daily body functions. People may need more fluids during heat and activity than while sitting quietly. Check thirst, activity, weather and access to a safe drink instead of using one rigid number for everyone. Water is a practical routine choice. Do not force excessive water or treat this worksheet as a medical diagnosis.

A useful plan has an action and a recheck: locate water, take a sensible break, and notice how you feel after activity. A parent should be told when someone is unusually dizzy, confused, ill or unable to drink normally. The class is about decision-making and routines, not managing a medical condition.

Words that help

  • hydration: having enough fluid for the body’s usual needs
  • cue: a sign such as thirst or extra activity that informs a choice
  • recheck: notice whether the planned routine is working

Worked example 1

Card B: It is warm and the student is active. Before leaving, fill a bottle; during the outing, take normal water breaks; afterward, check thirst and tell an adult if feeling unwell. The reason is the combination of heat and activity, not a universal quota.

Worked example 2

Card A: A child is reading comfortably. Keep water available and drink normally when thirsty. It would be unsupported to claim the exact same bottle count is necessary for every person in every weather condition.

Use this reference while you work

Practice cards, not personal medical advice: (A) A student sits reading in a cool room. (B) A student hikes on a warm day and sweats. (C) A student finishes active play and sees a refill station. (D) A student feels unusually dizzy or unwell. Decision pattern: Notice thirst/activity/heat → choose a reasonable drink or water break → recheck; tell a parent for unusual or ongoing symptoms.

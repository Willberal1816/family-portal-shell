Community Journeys: Why Families Move

Emma | Social Studies | Wednesday, November 25, 2026 | 50 minutes

What you are learning: Compare sourced reasons for moving without guessing a family's experience.

Learn First

People move for work, family, learning and other reasons. A family's record proves only what that account states; never treat one account as everyone's experience. Compare dates, origins and stated reasons. Missing travel costs or feelings remain unknown.

Two worked examples

'We moved to live near Grandma' supports family connection, not an invented job motive.
A dated journey record can support a timeline but not the unrecorded cost or emotions.

Source and materials

THREE FICTIONAL FAMILY JOURNEY NOTES
A (1920): Hillton to Lakeport for mill work.
B (1950): Eastville to Pine City to be near grandparents.
C (1980): Plainford to Rivertown because a parent began college.
No travel method, cost, feeling or later outcome recorded.

Directions: Answer all six questions using the teaching and source. The same six questions appear on the matching four-page printable PDF. Write legibly, scan every page and Finish Class in the School Portal. Ask for help if needed.

All six questions

Question 1. Make a table of three families' dates, origins, destinations and stated reasons.
Question 2. Draw a timeline and calculate years between adjacent journeys.
Question 3. Write one source-backed sentence for each family and cite the note letter.
Question 4. Name two missing details for each family and explain why guessing is inappropriate.
Question 5. Write six museum-caption sentences comparing the experiences respectfully.
Question 6. Draft four open interview questions and identify a source that could verify answers.

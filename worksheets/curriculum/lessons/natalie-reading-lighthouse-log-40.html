The Lighthouse Check: Times and True Results

Natalie · Reading · Thursday, October 29, 2026 · 45 minutes

What you will be able to do: Identify the exact time of an observed event, report the verified immediate result, and avoid explaining causes the passage does not prove.

Learn First — follow these three steps

  1. A time-stamped log tells you exactly when someone observed something. Use those times, not an approximate guess.
  2. An action is what the people did. An immediate verified outcome is what the NEXT check showed. A later maintenance visit is a separate later event.
  3. Do not turn two events in sequence into a claim that one caused the other unless the log makes that causal link.

See the exact worked examples

Worked example A: 7:10 sign bent; 7:13 staff photographed it; 7:18 they set cones; 7:22 a check showed the path was blocked. 7:22 is the immediate verified outcome.
Worked example B: If a worker came at noon, that repair is NOT the result of the 7:22 check. Say what the check observed and keep the noon event separate.
Check this common mistake: Common mistake: 'The wind broke the sign'—unless weather evidence exists, the log shows only that the sign was bent, not the cause.

Clauses — Build the Connector Map

What you are learning: Separate a sentence into whole clauses, identify what the connector does, and use that map to name the sentence type.

You can build good sentence combinations. The problem has been deciding whether each whole clause is independent or dependent before naming the sentence. Today we will stop guessing from the number of clauses and instead build a connector map.

1. Find a whole clause

A clause needs a subject and a verb. Keep all words that belong to that subject–verb idea together. Do not split a clause just because you see a comma.

Then ask: Does this whole idea make a complete statement by itself? If yes, label it I. If a starter such as because, when, while, although, if, since makes it lean on another idea, label it D.

2. Coordinating connectors join equal complete ideas

I
The porch light came onANDI
the moths gathered near it

Both clauses can stand alone. and joins two equal independent clauses: I + I = compound sentence.

3. Subordinating connectors turn one clause into a helper

D
While the cookies cooled,I
we washed the pans

While makes the first clause depend on another idea. D + I = complex sentence.

4. A subordinating connector can come in the middle

I
I packed a jacketBECAUSED
the forecast predicted rain

“The forecast predicted rain” would be complete without because, but the actual clause is because the forecast predicted rain. The connector is part of the clause, so it is dependent. I + D = complex.

5. Compound-complex means both patterns are present

I
Dad opened the windowsBUTI
we closed themWHEND
when the storm arrived

There are at least two independent clauses and at least one dependent clause, so the sentence is compound-complex.

6. Use the map, not a clause count

  • One I clause only → simple.
  • Two or more I clauses joined correctly, no D clause → compound.
  • One I plus at least one D → complex.
  • Two or more I plus at least one D → compound-complex.

A sentence with three clauses is not automatically compound-complex. The labels I and D decide.

7. Punctuation follows the map

D first: When the rain stopped, we went outside.

I + coordinating conjunction + I: The rain stopped, and we went outside.

I then D: We went outside when the rain stopped.

Independent-work routine: bracket each whole clause → circle the connector → label each clause I or D → name the sentence type → check punctuation.

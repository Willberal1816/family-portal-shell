Sentence Workshop: Join Ideas Clearly

Emma | Language Arts | Friday, November 27, 2026 | 55 minutes

What you are learning: Write complete sentences and combine related facts with and, but, or so only when the source supports the relationship.

Learn First

A complete sentence has a subject (who or what it is about) and a verb (what happens or is). A noun phrase such as "three new books" is not a complete sentence. The sentence "Three new books arrived" has both.

And joins related facts. But shows a contrast. So signals a result and should only be used when the source actually supports the connection. Use a comma before the joining word when joining two complete sentences. If the ideas are separate, use two sentences.

In editing, first underline the subject and identify the verb. Next read the whole sentence aloud. Check whether your joining word changes the meaning of the source. If a reason is not supplied, say it is unknown.

Two worked examples

Fragment: "Two wet coats on the rack." Complete: "Two wet coats hung on the rack." Subject = coats; verb = hung. Two facts: "The coat rack was full. The shelf was empty." Combined: "The coat rack was full, but the shelf was empty."
"The bell rang at ten. Three children entered at ten-five." Safe join: "The bell rang at ten, and three children entered at ten-five." Do not use so unless the record explains that the bell caused the entry.

Source and materials

FICTIONAL LIBRARY RETURN DESK RECORD
9:00: librarian opens the desk; 6 books on the return cart.
9:15: 4 more books arrive; one book has a torn cover.
9:25: librarian places the torn book in a repair tray.
The reason for the tear and the identity of the reader are not recorded.

Independent work

Question 1. Repair these three fragments into complete sentences: "Six books on the return cart." "Four more books at 9:15." "One torn cover." Underline each subject and circle its verb.
Question 2. Join two separate true facts with and. Then join two true facts from the record using but to show a contrast; if you need to use a contrast, use the torn cover versus the other returned books only without inventing their condition.
Question 3. A draft reads: "Four books arrived, so the cover was torn." Explain the unsupported connection, then rewrite as two complete factual sentences.
Question 4. Choose and or but to combine these model facts: "The desk opened at 9:00. The repair tray was used at 9:25." Write a second version as two separate sentences. Explain which is clearer.
Question 5. Write a short paragraph of five complete sentences describing the return desk. Include the three exact times, at least one joined sentence, and one explicitly unknown detail.
Question 6. Edit the paragraph. Use the three-row checklist to copy one fragment you corrected (or a complete sentence you verified), one joining word and one source fact. Write the polished paragraph.

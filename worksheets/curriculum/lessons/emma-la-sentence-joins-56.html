Sentence Links: Combine Clear Ideas

Emma | Language Arts | Friday, November 27, 2026 | 55 minutes

What you are learning: Combine complete sentences without inventing a cause; check the subject and verb.

Learn First

A complete sentence names a subject (who or what) and has a verb (what it does or is). A fragment is missing a complete thought. Join two complete ideas with and, but or so only when the connection is supported. Because explains a reason and needs evidence.

A time connection is not a cause. Use first, later or after when a record gives only order. Reread each sentence to locate its subject and verb. Split a long sentence if it becomes unclear.

Worked examples

Fragment: Three blue bins near the door. Complete: Three blue bins stood near the door. Subject: bins; verb: stood. Combine: Three blue bins stood near the door, and two red bins stood by the shelf.
Record: 9:10 the light went out; 9:20 the door opened. Model: The light went out at 9:10, and the door opened at 9:20. We do not know why either happened. Do not write that the light caused the door to open.

Reference

FICTIONAL CLUB LOG
9:00 | Six notebooks on the desk.
9:15 | Mia placed two pencils in a tray.
9:30 | The tray was moved to the shelf.
10:00 | Leo counted eight notebooks.
No reason for moving the tray is recorded.

All six questions

Question 1. Turn the 9:00 note into a complete sentence. Underline the subject and circle the verb.
Question 2. Combine the 9:15 and 9:30 events into one clear sentence using and or later. Underline both verbs.
Question 3. Correct these fragments: "Two pencils in the tray." and "Counted eight notebooks at 10:00." Use the log for missing subjects and verbs.
Question 4. A draft says, "The tray moved because the desk was crowded." Explain what the log proves and rewrite as two complete sentences without the invented cause.
Question 5. Write a four-sentence club update using all four log entries. Use one compound sentence, one time connector and exact quantities.
Question 6. Audit your update: copy two complete sentences, mark subjects/verbs, name the matching log entry for each, and revise one sentence to be clearer.

Independent Reading: How Reliable Is This Account?

Natalie · Reading · Saturday, November 14, 2026 · 45 minutes

What you are learning

Use precise page evidence to distinguish what the narrator knows, what a character assumes and what the text has not yet established.

Read your OWN book for 25 actual minutes first, then do the work.

Learn First — Teaching

  1. Continue your own book for at least 25 ACTIVE minutes and record the real title, author, pages and minutes.
  2. The narrator is the voice that tells the story. First ask what the narrator describes directly and what is somebody's opinion.
  3. A reliable detail is one the text directly establishes. An inference can be useful, but it is not identical to stated fact.
  4. An omitted detail may make an explanation uncertain. That does not automatically mean the narrator is lying.
  5. Check for conflicts: does one character report something that another character or event contradicts?
  6. Use two distinct clues from today's pages with accurate citations and explain which interpretation each supports.
  7. An alternative interpretation should be plausible under the printed evidence, rather than a random unrelated possibility.

Two fully worked models

Model 1

Invented model: A narrator reports that the gate was locked on page 20. That is stated. A character says the guard locked it because he was angry; without more evidence, the motive is not confirmed.

Model 2

Invented model: One person heard a crash while another saw a fallen box. Either may accurately report part of the event. The narrator has not yet supplied every step.

Important mistake to avoid:

A page outside your logged range or a guessed quote cannot support a reading claim. Use the exact pages you truly read.

Water Collection: Find the Real Averages

Natalie · Science · Thursday, October 29, 2026 · 45 minutes

What you will be able to do: Add three measurements accurately, divide by three, compare the two averages and report a limited conclusion.

Learn First — follow these three steps

  1. A repeated-trial average is the SUM of all trials divided by the NUMBER of trials. Add first, then divide.
  2. A difference between averages is the larger average minus the smaller. Always keep the unit in your answer.
  3. Do not state 'this method always works better' from only three practice trials. Describe what these trials showed and propose a fair repeat.

See the exact worked examples

Worked example A: Cup A collects 7, 8, 9 mL. Total 24 mL; average 24÷3=8 mL. Cup B collects 10, 11, 12 mL. Total 33; average 11 mL.
Worked example B: The average difference is 11−8=3 mL. A safe conclusion: 'Cup B averaged 3 mL more in these three practice trials.' An unsupported claim would be 'B always collects 3 mL more in all weather.'
Check this common mistake: Common mistake: adding 7+8+9=24 and writing 24 mL as the average. The average requires dividing the total by 3.

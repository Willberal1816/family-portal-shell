Ferry Archive: Three Records, Three Limits

Natalie · Social Studies · Wednesday November 18 · 50 minutes

What you are learning: Audit a schedule, ledger and diary as independent historical evidence; refuse unsupported passenger and delay claims.

Learn First

  1. A printed schedule gives intended service, not a guarantee that a boat departed.
  2. A ticket ledger counts tickets, not necessarily everyone who boarded.
  3. A diary tells one person's experience; that account need not represent every traveler.
  4. The planned 9 a.m. departure and diary's noon crossing may involve different sailings.
  5. A time difference does not alone prove delay or a specific cause.
  6. An accurate historian states exact records, competing explanations and a record—like a captain's log—that could resolve uncertainty.

Worked models

Model 1. Safe: 'The Friday ledger recorded 23 tickets, while one diary writer reports crossing at noon.'
Model 2. Unproven: 'Twenty-three passengers rode the delayed 9 a.m. ferry at noon.' None of the three documents confirms that chain.
Common mistake to avoid: Never exchange 'tickets recorded' for 'exact passengers' or a planned sailing for an actual one.

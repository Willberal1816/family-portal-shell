Pool Survey: Who Answered, What We Know

Natalie · Language Arts · Thursday, October 29, 2026 · 55 minutes

What you will be able to do: Distinguish a surveyed group from an entire community, make an exact supported percentage claim, and remove invented causes.

Learn First — follow these three steps

  1. The respondent group is the actual people who gave answers, not everyone who lives in the area. A fact about a small group does not automatically describe all residents.
  2. To report a proportion accurately, state BOTH the count and the total: '18 of 30 visitors surveyed chose option A' = 60% of THOSE 30 respondents.
  3. The survey can count preferences but cannot explain WHY a person chose an answer unless it asks and records reasons.

See the exact worked examples

Worked example A: At a library, 15 of 25 visitors choose earlier hours. Correct: '15 of the 25 visitors surveyed wanted earlier hours (60%).' Unsupported: '60% of all residents want earlier hours.'
Worked example B: If the survey did NOT ask why, do not say people preferred it 'because of work.' Better: 'The reason for the preference was not measured.'
Check this common mistake: Common mistake: replacing the word respondents with 'the entire town.' Your conclusion becomes much larger than the people actually counted.

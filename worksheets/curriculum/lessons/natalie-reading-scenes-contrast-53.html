Reading: How Two Moments Differ

Natalie | Reading | Monday, November 23, 2026 | 45 minutes

What you are learning: Analyze a contrast between two moments using evidence and cautious interpretation.

Learn First

Read your current book for 25 minutes. Contrast means a meaningful difference across moments in the story or explanation. Choose two moments you can find in the text, from today or an earlier passage.

Worked models

Earlier the character answers quickly; later she hesitates. The action changed, but the emotion is uncertain.
'Earlier X did A (p. 12), but later X did B (p. 27)' is supported when both passages exist.

Check your work: Read the source labels; check calculations, evidence, or drawing guides before turning in.

Source and materials

YOUR OWN CURRENT BOOK: read for 25 minutes.
Record title, author, today's page range and any earlier cited pages.
For nonfiction compare two examples or supporting sources.
Never invent quotes, events or missing page numbers.

Answer all six questions using this teaching and source. Scan all four worksheet pages, then Finish Class in the School Portal. Ask for help when needed.

All six independent-work questions

Question 1. Record book information and today's page range. Summarize one important moment in three sentences.
Question 2. Find two moments and make a table of page, action or detail and difference.
Question 3. Quote one short phrase from each moment and explain their contrast.
Question 4. Give an interpretation of the contrast and a plausible alternative explanation. Describe needed evidence.
Question 5. Write a six-sentence paragraph connecting the contrast to conflict or author purpose, with two page citations.
Question 6. Analyze one word or phrase with two possible meanings and decide which is supported by its context.

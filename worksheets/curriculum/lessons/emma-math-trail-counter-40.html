Learn First — follow these three steps

  1. Median means the middle after sorting. With six values, there are two middle positions: 3 and 4. Add those TWO values and divide by 2; do NOT choose only one.
  2. Mean means add EVERY value, then divide by the number of values. An unusually high value can raise the mean even when the median barely moves.
  3. To describe a typical day, say which statistic you chose and why the actual data support it.

See the exact worked examples

Worked example A: 4, 6, 7, 9, 10, 12 are already sorted. The middle pair is 7 and 9; median = (7+9)÷2 = 8. The total is 48, so mean = 48÷6 = 8.
Worked example B: 4, 6, 7, 9, 10, 30 has median 8 and mean 66÷6 = 11. The one value 30 pulls the mean up; the median remains 8. Never divide by 5 when there are 6 readings.
Check this common mistake: Common mistake: 11 and 13 are the middle two, but saying median = 11 misses the averaging step. Correct: (11+13)÷2 = 12.

Write the Situation as One Expression

Emma · Math · 50 minutes · Learn First
What you are learning: turn a situation into one numerical expression, preserve every operation, and use parentheses only when the story requires a grouped action.

1. Read for actions and groups

Underline each action. Box the amount that repeats. Circle words such as each, total, left, shared equally, altogether, and after. Do not calculate yet.

Worked model A: Four teams collect 7 cans each, then add 9 loose cans. The repeated group is 4 × 7; the extra amount is +9. Expression: 4 × 7 + 9. Value: 37.
Worked model B: A coach buys 3 bags. Each bag holds 8 balls and 2 cones. One complete bag is grouped first: 3 × (8 + 2) = 30. Without parentheses, 3 × 8 + 2 would describe only two loose cones.

2. Keep every operation

Before evaluating

  1. Match each number to the story.
  2. Point to the operation for each action.
  3. Explain any parentheses in words.

Order check

Multiplication and division happen before addition and subtraction unless grouping changes the order.

8 + 6 × 5 − 7 = 31

3. Decide whether parentheses matter

Compare 5 × 6 + 4 = 34 with 5 × (6 + 4) = 50. Ask: “Are the 4 items inside every group, or are they added once?”

Ready check: Say what each number and operation represents. If one symbol has no story job, revise before printing or starting the work page.

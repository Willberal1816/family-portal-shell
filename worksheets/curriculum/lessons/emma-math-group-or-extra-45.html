Four Boxes: Inside Every Group or One Extra?

Emma · Math · Thursday, November 12, 2026 · 50 minutes

What you are learning: Build equal groups and decide whether each number belongs inside every group or is added only once.

Learn First

  1. A group is one box or bag containing its own items. Draw every group before writing the multiplication.
  2. If there are four boxes, each with three markers, the repeated part is 4 times 3 = 12 markers.
  3. If TWO spare markers sit outside all boxes, they are added ONCE: 4 times 3 + 2 = 14.
  4. If every box has three markers AND two pencils, both items are inside EACH box: 4 times (3 + 2) = 20 items.
  5. Parentheses show which quantities repeat together. A quick drawing of all the boxes prevents grouping mistakes.

Two worked examples

Worked model A: Draw [MMM] [MMM] [MMM] [MMM] plus two loose M. 12 markers are inside boxes and two are outside. The expression is 4 x 3 + 2 = 14.
Worked model B: Draw [MMM PP] [MMM PP] [MMM PP] [MMM PP]. Each box contains five objects, so 4 x (3 + 2) = 20. The two pencils repeat four times.
A mistake to avoid: 4 x (3 + 2) is NOT the same story as 4 x 3 + 2. Ask whether the two are inside EACH box or outside all boxes.

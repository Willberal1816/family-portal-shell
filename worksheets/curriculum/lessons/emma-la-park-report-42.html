Park Report: Build an Evidence Paragraph

Emma · Language Arts · Monday, November 9, 2026 · 55 minutes · Cycle C

Today's objective: Write one accurate topic sentence, two sourced detail sentences, and one sentence limiting what the source can prove.

Learn First: read each step

  1. A topic sentence says what the paragraph is ABOUT without claiming something the source never measured.
  2. An evidence sentence names an exact fact and the correct place/date. A detail should support the topic sentence, not be an unrelated opinion.
  3. A limitation sentence explains what a source does NOT show. Your paragraph should be exactly four sentences: topic, fact 1, fact 2, and limit.

See the two fully worked examples

Worked example 1: Source: 'At Lake Hill Garden on Monday, volunteers planted four trees and watered six beds.' Supported topic: 'Volunteers completed two kinds of tasks at Lake Hill Garden on Monday.'
Worked example 2, four-sentence paragraph: 'The garden group worked on Monday. The log lists four trees planted. It also lists six beds watered. The log does not measure how much visitors enjoyed the garden.' The four sentences follow one clear idea.
Watch for this common error: Avoid 'These changes made the whole city healthier' when the source only counted tasks. Dates, measurements, and who did something are not proof of an unrelated outcome.

Positive and Negative Space: Cut a Window

Natalie · Art · Tuesday, September 29, 2026 · Learn First

What you are learning: create two different compositions by deliberately swapping the filled object and the unfilled space around it. Read the examples, then use the separate Independent Work printable for all answers. This teaching page has no written work to turn in.

Learn the idea

Positive space is the object or main shape; negative space is the empty area around and between shapes. A drawing changes meaning when you darken the object rather than its surroundings. You do not need a vanishing point for this exercise. First trace a simple leaf shape in a frame. Fill the leaf dark and leave the outside white. In a second frame draw a bottle or mug and shade the space OUTSIDE its edge, leaving the object white. Both are original silhouette studies, not the previous perspective or value exercise.

Keep contours clear. Use only two tones first so you can check which area is positive and which is negative. Compare whether the main shape is still readable after you reverse the tones. If a gap closes unexpectedly, revise the edge carefully. Use the two frame boxes supplied on the paper instead of drawing extra layout grids.

Words that help

  • positive space: the main object or filled shape
  • negative space: the area around or between main objects
  • silhouette: a simplified solid outline that remains recognizable without internal details

Worked example 1

Worked model: draw a leaf with a stem. In the first frame darken inside the leaf only. You can tell the leaf is positive because its shape is filled. Keep the outside clean.

Worked example 2

Worked model: trace the same leaf in another frame, but darken the area around it. The white leaf is still recognizable; attention now depends on the negative space. If the narrow gap by the stem disappears, widen the gap rather than adding a perspective line.

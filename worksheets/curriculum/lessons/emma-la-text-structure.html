How Is This Text Built?

Emma · Language Arts · Guided Teaching
What you are learning: Identify how an informational text is organized and explain how that structure helps the author communicate the main idea.

Common text structures

  • Cause and effect: explains why something happened and what happened because of it.
  • Compare and contrast: shows similarities and differences.
  • Problem and solution: presents a difficulty and one or more responses.
  • Sequence: explains events or steps in order.
  • Description: develops a topic with traits, facts, and examples.
Example: A paragraph explains that heavy rain filled a creek, roads flooded, and school buses were rerouted. That organization is cause and effect because the rain leads to later events.

Do not rely on one signal word

Words such as “because,” “however,” “first,” and “solution” can help, but the stronger test is to ask what relationship the author is building across the whole paragraph.

Think aloud: If two animals are discussed trait by trait, the author is probably comparing them even if the phrase “compare and contrast” never appears.

Connect structure to purpose

After naming the structure, explain why it helps. A sequence helps a reader follow steps. Cause and effect helps a reader understand why an outcome occurred. Comparison helps a reader notice meaningful differences.

Dissolving Tests: Identify the Real Cause

Natalie | Science | Tuesday, November 24, 2026 | 50 minutes

What you are learning: Compare repeated experimental trials using averages and controls.

Learn First

To test temperature's effect on dissolving, keep sugar mass, water volume, cup and stirring method constant. Change temperature and record seconds to dissolve.

Use repeated trials and compute a mean to reduce reliance on one result. Describe evidence cautiously; a single activity does not prove the same outcome for every substance or liquid.

Two worked examples

Equal 5-gram sugar samples: cool water took 72 seconds and warm took 40. This trial differed by 32 seconds.
If warm gets twenty stirs but cool gets five, stirring is a confounding variable; redo with the same method.

Source and materials

FICTIONAL LAB: each cup 200 mL water, 5 g sugar and five equal stirs. Cool trials 72,70,74 seconds; warm trials 40,44,42 seconds. Do not drink.

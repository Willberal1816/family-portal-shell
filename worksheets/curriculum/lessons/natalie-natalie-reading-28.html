The Quiet Observatory

Learn the idea

A good reading claim is specific enough that the story can prove it. Select two separate short details and explain separately how each supports your claim. A chain needs every link: what happened first, how someone responded, and what happened next. The narrator can report events and choices in the passage; do not claim to know a character’s future thoughts or an unwritten outcome.

Read your current book for 25 minutes first. Then use this fresh passage and finish with exactly five numbered sentences; if you quote, copy the precise words rather than only a summary.

Words that help

  • text clue: a short exact phrase that supports an answer
  • event chain: each connected step from cause to consequence
  • evidence limit: something the passage does not reveal

Worked example 1

In a different story a bell rings, a class lines up, and then the teacher opens the door. The full chain is bell → class lines up → door opens. “The door opened” alone omits a necessary middle step.

Worked example 2

If a narrator says “the path glistened,” that detail may support a careful walking choice. It cannot prove that someone will fall tomorrow. One exact phrase supports only what that phrase actually shows.

Use this reference while you work

Original short story: Tessa entered the observatory expecting to see the new telescope. Instead, a sign said the dome was closed for alignment. The technician explained that a small adjustment was needed before photographs could be compared fairly. Tessa watched workers level the mount and record its angle. She could not see through the telescope that night, but she copied the setup notes for tomorrow. When the dome reopened, the technician planned to repeat the same star photograph to check the adjustment. Tessa understood that an apparently small change could matter if every observation was meant to be compared.

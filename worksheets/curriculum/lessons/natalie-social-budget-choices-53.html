Civic Budget: Compare Two Public Choices

Natalie | Social Studies | Monday, November 23, 2026 | 50 minutes

Your goal: Calculate choices under a limited fund and explain opportunity costs and missing evidence.

Learn First

Public decisions use limited resources. Opportunity cost means an alternative you must give up. A fair recommendation shows correct arithmetic, likely benefits and what you still need to measure.

Worked models

With $12,000, a $8,000 path repair leaves $4,000. Buying it together with $7,000 seating costs $15,000, $3,000 too much.
An estimate gives cost, not proof of outcomes. The budget cannot guarantee any proposal's future benefit.

Check your work: Read the source labels; check calculations, evidence, or drawing guides before turning in.

Source and materials

FICTIONAL TOWN IMPROVEMENT FUND: $12,000
A: footpath repair = $8,000
B: shaded bus stop seating = $7,000
C: rain garden = $5,000
No bids, maintenance costs, surveys or outcome data.

All six independent-work questions

Question 1. Create a table showing each option, cost, money left if selected alone and an intended benefit.
Question 2. Calculate every pair of options and identify which pairs fit the $12,000 fund.
Question 3. Choose one feasible option or pair and explain its opportunity cost and who is affected.
Question 4. Draft five public questions before a vote, including fairness and future maintenance.
Question 5. A headline says the cheapest choice is certainly best. Write a six-sentence fact check using data limits.
Question 6. Write a one-page council recommendation with arithmetic, two alternatives and a six-month outcome check.

Garden Gate Log: Exact Sequence and Verified Result

Natalie · Reading · Wednesday, October 28, 2026 · 45 minutes

What you are learning: Build an exact sequence from a short record, distinguish planned action from observed result, and summarize only what the log supports.

Learn First

Use timestamps to order evidence and action. The final result is what the record says happened, not what someone hoped would happen.

A later repair can be related without being the immediate result of the earlier response.

Fully worked model

8:05 gate found open; 8:08 latch checked; 8:12 temporary strap added; 8:20 animals remained inside. The observed result is the 8:20 check.

Source and materials

At 6:40 a.m., the garden volunteer found the east gate open. At 6:43, she photographed the loose latch and called the coordinator. At 6:50, they tied the gate shut with a temporary cord. At 7:05, a second check found all garden tools still inside. At noon, a maintenance worker replaced the latch.

Guided model: reported fact, verified result, later repair

Model 2 (separate practice record): At 7:15 staff found a museum door ajar; at 7:18 they photographed a damaged latch; at 7:23 security secured the door; at 7:35 an inventory check found the displayed objects still present. A technician fitted a new latch in the evening. The immediate verified result is the 7:35 inventory, not the evening repair. Model 3: “A visitor forced the door open” is an unsupported cause, because the log records no visitor or reason.

Common mistake: Replacing the timed check with a later repair, saying a hoped-for result was verified, or adding a person who is not in the record. Mark observed evidence, each response, and the earliest verified outcome in separate phrases.

Try it before printing: In four sentences summarize the museum record's problem, first evidence, response, and verified result while leaving out unsupported cause claims.

Reading: Can a Summary Be Trusted?

Natalie | Reading | Wednesday, November 25, 2026 | 45 minutes

What you are learning: Check an accurate concise summary against exact page evidence and distinguish optional detail from essential events.

Learn First

Read independently for 25 minutes and record the exact pages. A useful summary is shorter than its source and includes the events that change the situation. Choose a short real passage from today's reading.

Use an event chain: event -> what changed. Remove a detail if it does not change the main event or outcome. “Then” reports sequence; “because” and “so” imply a cause and need stronger textual support. Do not turn a character's possibility into certainty.

Verify a summary with a claim/page/clue table. Copy a short exact phrase and record its real page number. If the evidence is missing, revise the claim rather than invent it.

Two worked examples

Practice text: “The door was locked. Rae walked to the office. The clerk gave Rae a key. A blue vase stood nearby.” Summary: “Rae found the door locked and went to the office. The clerk gave Rae a key.” Optional: blue vase. The key changes what Rae can do; the vase does not.
Practice text: “Rae might return later.” Keep the qualifier “might.” Do not write “Rae will return.” A summary must preserve the author's certainty.

Source and materials

USE YOUR OWN CURRENT BOOK for 25 minutes.
Record title, author and exact first/last reading pages.
Choose a real 1-2 page passage from today's reading.
Cite exact pages; do not invent wording or motives.

Directions: Read Learn First and both worked examples. Complete all six questions. The same questions and source appear in the four-page native printable. Scan every page, then Finish Class in the School Portal. Ask for help when needed.

All six questions

Question 1. Record book title, author, exact reading range and the source passage's page numbers.
Question 2. List three important events or ideas from the passage. Beside each, explain what changes or why it matters, using the actual text.
Question 3. Write a three- or four-sentence summary. Mark one optional detail in the source that you deliberately left out.
Question 4. Make a three-row claim / exact page / supporting phrase table for your summary. Revise any unsupported cause or motive.
Question 5. Quote one important verb or qualifier exactly, cite its page, and explain how changing the word would change the meaning.
Question 6. Write the corrected concise summary in three or four sentences. Explain the two strongest checks you made and label one claim that would need more evidence.

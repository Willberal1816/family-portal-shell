Reading: Can a Summary Be Trusted?

Natalie | Reading | Wednesday, November 25, 2026 | 45 minutes

What you are learning: Check your own passage summary against exact page evidence.

Learn First

Read independently for 25 minutes. A factual summary is shorter than its source and should not introduce invented motive or emotional conclusions. Mark source-supported statements as supported and unsupported guesses as unsupported. Recheck one short word quotation; dependent interpretation is not part of this task.

Two worked examples

Text: 'Rae carried a red case.' Supported: 'Rae carried a case.' Not supported: 'Rae stole it.'
'Might' cannot become 'certainly' in a summary without changing the claim.

Source and materials

USE YOUR OWN CURRENT BOOK for 25 minutes.
Record title, author and today's reading pages.
Choose one real 1-2 page passage.
Cite exact pages and do not invent text.

Directions: Answer all six questions using the teaching and source. The same six questions appear on the matching four-page printable PDF. Write legibly, scan every page and Finish Class in the School Portal. Ask for help if needed.

All six questions

Question 1. Record book title, author, reading range and the source passage's page numbers.
Question 2. Write a five-sentence summary preserving the passage's actual facts.
Question 3. Make a three-row claim/source-page table; correct anything not supported.
Question 4. Write two tempting unsupported claims and name the evidence they would need.
Question 5. Choose one qualifier or verb; quote and cite it exactly, explaining meaning changes.
Question 6. Revise summary to 70-100 words, then explain your two strongest evidence checks.

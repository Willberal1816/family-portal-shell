Trail Signs: Compare Real Lengths

Natalie · Math · Friday, November 20 · 50-minute learning block

What you are learning: Convert feet, inches and yards accurately, label units and refuse a conclusion when a measurement is missing.

Learn First

A length has a number AND a unit.

One foot contains 12 inches, and one yard contains 3 feet or 36 inches.

Convert everything to a common unit before adding or comparing.

To check, divide a number of inches by 12 and use the remainder as extra inches.

Do not claim that material is sufficient until the needed total length is actually known.

Important words: Conversion means expressing the SAME length in another unit. Remainder inches are inches left after counting complete feet.

Worked models — read before independent work

Model 1: A 4-foot 7-inch sign is 4 × 12 + 7 = 55 inches. A 68-inch board is 5 complete feet with 8 inches left, because 5 × 12 + 8 = 68.
Model 2: A rope of 3 yards 1 foot is (3 × 3 + 1) = 10 feet = 120 inches. This proves its length, but it does NOT prove it will be long enough for a fence whose required perimeter was never given.
Common mistake: Never compare 68 inches and 4 feet 7 inches as if 68 and 4 were the same unit. A supported length conversion is not evidence of a price, quality or material shortage.

Reference for today's work

Wooden board68 inches
Rope roll3 yards 1 foot
Two equal support posts2 feet 4 inches each
Marker strips23 inches and 31 inches

Independent Work — all six questions

Every assigned question is shown on this screen and included on your four-page printable. Write on your worksheet, not on the Learn First page.

Question 1. Convert the sign face to inches. Show multiplication and addition, include the inch unit, and reverse-check by rebuilding the original feet-and-inches measure.

Question 2. Convert the wooden board's 68 inches into complete feet and leftover inches. Compare it to the sign face in a common unit, stating which is longer and by how many inches.

Question 3. Express the rope roll in feet AND inches. Show both conversion paths and one reverse-check. Then explain in TWO sentences why the record alone does not prove that the rope can fence the trail.

Question 4. Convert both support posts into inches. Calculate their combined length and show the result also in feet and leftover inches. Explain which number describes ONE post and which describes BOTH.

Question 5. The two marker strips need to be placed end to end. Give their combined length in inches and then feet-and-inches. A helper reports '4 feet 3 inches.' Diagnose the exact subtraction or remainder error and supply a corrected check.

Question 6. Write a FIVE-sentence installation note using the actual record: include the sign, board and rope lengths in comparable units; one supported size comparison; one fact the record does not tell us; and the specific additional measurement needed before a claim that there is enough rope.

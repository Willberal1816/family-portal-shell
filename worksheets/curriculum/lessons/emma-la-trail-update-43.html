Trail Update: A Report with Exact Facts

Emma · Language Arts · Tuesday, November 10, 2026 · 55 minutes

What you will learn: Write a focused 5-sentence paragraph that uses exact numbers and refuses to invent unmeasured changes.

Learn First

  1. A useful report has a clear TOPIC sentence: what happened, where and when.
  2. Support the topic with exact details. Names, dates, numbers and actions matter: 'four boards were repaired' is more informative than 'some work was done.'
  3. Distinguish a documented RESULT (the log says a marker was replaced) from a claim about visitor feelings or safety. If there is no survey, it cannot tell what visitors thought.
  4. Build a five-sentence report: 1 topic, 2 exact fact with number, 3 second exact fact with number, 4 one verifying/corroborating detail if available, 5 one explicit limit.
  5. Revise for complete sentences and punctuation; do not start with a free-standing date fragment. Read the paragraph aloud to check its flow.

Two fully explained models

Worked example from a DIFFERENT source: At Blue Lake on Monday, six students painted two benches. Report: 'The students completed a small maintenance project at Blue Lake on Monday. Six students painted two benches. A teacher inspected the finished paint. The inspection showed that the benches were painted. The source does not report how many visitors later used them.'
Error and repair: 'The repair made every visitor safer.' Unsupported. Better: 'The log records four repaired boards, but it does not measure visitor safety.'
Check this mistake: A report can be clear without claiming that a task caused an outcome. Do not replace exact counts with the word 'some' when the source gives numbers.

  3. Revise the unsupported claim 'Every visitor felt safer afterward.' Write a factual replacement and a sentence explaining the missing evidence.

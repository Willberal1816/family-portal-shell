Clean Hands, Clean Station: A Real Safety Check

Emma | Health | Friday, November 13, 2026 | 50 minutes

What you are learning

Practice proper handwashing, separate clean from dirty work items, and explain exactly WHY each safety step matters.

Learn First: follow the reasoning

  1. You will actually perform a safe handwashing and cleanup practice today. You do NOT need food, raw meat, knives, heat, cleaners, or new supplies.
  2. Germs are tiny organisms that can spread on hands and shared objects. Cleaning hands and surfaces before touching food helps reduce spread.
  3. Wash: turn on safe tap water; wet hands; apply soap; rub palms, backs, between fingers and beneath nails for at least TWENTY seconds; rinse; dry with a clean towel.
  4. Wash before preparing food, after touching trash, after using the bathroom, and after handling raw food (today you will NOT handle any raw food).
  5. Clean and dirty objects should have separate places. A used cloth does not belong on a clean measuring tool. Drying with a clean towel avoids re-soiling washed hands.
  6. Before using a pretend food prep area, check that the table is clean, your hands are washed, and measuring tools are clean and dry.
  7. Record REAL actions you actually performed. For a safety scenario you only READ, mark it as imagined and do not claim you did it.

Two fully worked models

Model 1

Worked model A: You wash for 5 seconds, then pick up a dirty towel. This is not a ready station; rewash for 20 seconds and use a clean towel.

Model 2

Worked model B: A used cleaning cloth sits beside a clean measuring cup. Move the used cloth away, clean the work area with adult-approved method, then place the clean cup separately.

A mistake to avoid

Rinsing for two seconds is not the same as washing with soap and scrubbing at least twenty seconds. Never handle raw meat for this lesson.

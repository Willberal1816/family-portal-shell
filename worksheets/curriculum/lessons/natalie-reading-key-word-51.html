Read Your Book: Why That Word?

Natalie · Reading · Friday, November 20 · 50-minute learning block (45 minutes)

What you are learning: Explain how one precise word affects mood or meaning by using an exact quote, a close substitute and an alternative interpretation.

Learn First

Read your own book for 25 real minutes, recording the title, author and page range.

An author can choose one verb or adjective that changes how a moment feels.

To explain word choice, quote the actual word, identify its meaning in context, propose a plausible substitute and compare what the substitute would suggest.

Your opinion needs a page citation.

Important words: Mood is the feeling that a passage creates. A synonym is a word with a similar meaning. Context means the words and events around the chosen word.

Worked models — read before independent work

Model 1: Invented teaching sentence: 'The lantern flickered beside the gate.' Here 'flickered' suggests unstable, uneven light, which may create uncertainty; 'glowed' suggests steadier light and could feel calmer.
Model 2: A cautious explanation says: 'The author uses flickered rather than glowed, so the light seems less reliable in this moment.' That does not prove the character was afraid unless the surrounding passage supports fear.
Common mistake: 'It is dramatic' is too vague; identify the exact word, comparison and effect. Do not invent quotations from Caraval or any other book whose page text is unavailable.

Reference for today's work

Word-choice chain: Exact quoted word + page → context → close synonym → different feeling → what remains uncertain.

Independent Work — all six questions

Every assigned question is shown on this screen and included on your four-page printable. Write on your worksheet, not on the Learn First page.

Question 1. Record your book's full title, author, pages you truly read today and real active reading minutes. Name the character or passage you will examine, without inventing an event.

Question 2. Quote ONE exact sentence from today's reading and its page number. Underline the ONE word that most strongly shapes the mood or meaning. Define that word in context using your own plain words.

Question 3. Suggest TWO close synonyms for the underlined word. In TWO complete sentences, explain how each might change the mood, intensity or precision of the sentence without changing the basic event.

Question 4. Quote a second, distinct short phrase from within your logged page range. Identify its most important word and explain in THREE sentences one likely interpretation and one plausible alternative supported by the surrounding context.

Question 5. A reviewer says 'The word proves the character was scared.' Identify why this may overclaim. Use an actual page detail to explain which feeling is supported and which part remains an inference; write TWO cautious sentences.

Question 6. Write a FIVE-sentence literary response: give the exact original word and page, a context clue, a close synonym, how the author's choice changes mood or meaning, and one reasonable alternate reading that you cannot completely rule out.

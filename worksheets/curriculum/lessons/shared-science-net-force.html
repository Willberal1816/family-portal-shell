What Changes Motion?

Emma + Natalie · Science · Learn First
What you are learning: Combine forces, determine net force, and use it to predict whether an object’s motion can change.

Learn the idea

Objects often have more than one force acting at once. Net force is the overall result after forces are combined. Forces in the same direction add; forces in opposite directions compete, so subtract the smaller from the larger and keep the stronger direction.

If net force is zero, the forces are balanced. That does not mean the object must be stopped. A moving object can keep moving at constant speed and direction with zero net force. This connects directly to inertia.

If net force is not zero, forces are unbalanced and velocity can change—the object may speed up, slow down, or change direction. Net-force direction tells the direction of the change, not necessarily the direction the object is already moving.

Draw arrows when a situation is confusing. Direction represents force direction and arrow length can represent force size. A diagram helps prevent adding forces that actually oppose one another.

Words you will use

  • net force: combined overall force on an object
  • balanced forces: forces combining to zero net force
  • unbalanced forces: forces producing nonzero net force
  • velocity: speed with direction

Worked examples

Example 1: 8 N right and 3 N left → 5 N right.
Example 2: 6 N left and 6 N right → 0 N; an already-moving object need not stop.
Example 3: A sled moving right with net force left can slow before possibly reversing.

Guided checks

10 N east and 4 N west gives what?
6 N east.
Can an object move while forces are balanced?
Yes, at constant velocity.
A ball moves right but net force points left. What may happen first?
It can slow.

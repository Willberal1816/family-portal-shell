Read Your Book: Why That Word?

Natalie · Reading · Wednesday November 18 · 45 minutes

What you are learning: Analyze how two real word choices shape a reader's understanding without inventing the author's motive.

Read your REAL current book for 25 active minutes BEFORE writing.

Learn First

  1. Read for at least 25 active minutes and record actual title, author and page range.
  2. Authors choose particular words to shape a scene. 'Stumbled' and 'walked' describe different kinds of movement.
  3. Find two short exact phrases on your actual pages, including their page numbers.
  4. Explain one plausible effect of each word, supported by neighboring details; the effect is usually an interpretation, not proven author intent.
  5. Test a synonym and explain what nuance it changes.
  6. Compare both phrases, acknowledge another reasonable reading and preserve exact citations.

Worked models

Model 1. Fictional phrase: 'The gate groaned' might make a scene feel eerie; check nearby details.
Model 2. Fictional contrast: 'the lantern flickered' suggests unstable light; 'the lantern shone' suggests a steadier light.
Common mistake to avoid: Do not make up book quotations or treat an author's unstated intention as fact.

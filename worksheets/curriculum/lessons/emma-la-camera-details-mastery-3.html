Camera Details — Make the Reader See It

What you are learning: Turn vague writing into specific details that help a reader picture the same scene you have in mind.

The camera test

Ask: Could a camera or microphone capture this detail? If yes, it is probably specific. “It was exciting” is vague. “Dad tossed the pancake high and everyone leaned over the counter to watch” gives the reader something visible.

Build a detail ladder

Vague: The dog was funny.
Specific: The dog slid across the kitchen floor and barked at its own reflection.
Vague: The park was busy.
Specific: Children raced between the swings while two soccer games filled the grass.

Choose details that support one main idea

Main idea: The kitchen was busy before dinner.

Useful details: water boiling, vegetables being chopped, timer ringing, someone setting plates.

Not useful unless connected: the color of someone’s shoelaces.

Use precise verbs

Replace “went” with a verb that shows the action: raced, wandered, stomped, hurried, tiptoed. The precise verb should fit what really happened.

Complete the whole direction

Before finishing a question, check every requirement. If it asks for 5–6 sentences, four sentences is not finished. If it asks for five details, count five. If it asks why, include the reason.

Use the Khan detail-expansion example as another model, then complete the new independent work. The final paragraph is the clean mastery evidence.

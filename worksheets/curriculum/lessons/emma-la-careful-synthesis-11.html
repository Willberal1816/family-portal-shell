Build a Careful Synthesis

Emma · Language Arts · 55 minutes · Learn First
What you are learning: combine compatible evidence from two accounts without erasing viewpoint, exaggerating a claim, or adding details the sources do not support.

Two sources, three jobs

Source A can showSource B can showNeither can prove alone
what its writer observed or recordedwhat its writer observed or experiencedeveryone’s motives, feelings, or every event
Worked model: A park log says volunteers cleared the east path on May 4. A volunteer interview says the path was difficult to walk before the cleanup. Careful claim: Records and an eyewitness both show that the east path needed work and was cleared in early May. Limitation: neither source proves that every park path was blocked.

Use the four-part synthesis frame

  1. Claim: state only what both sources can support together.
  2. Evidence A: use one exact fact and identify the source.
  3. Evidence B: use one compatible fact and identify the source.
  4. Limit: name one conclusion the sources cannot establish.

Role changes detail selection

A maintenance worker may record dates, locations, damage, or repairs. A participant may describe experience, difficulty, or sequence. Neither role automatically makes a source “better”; the best source depends on the question.

Accuracy check: underline every factual phrase in your synthesis and point to the exact line that supports it. Replace any unsupported detail.

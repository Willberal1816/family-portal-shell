Fabric Test: Change One Thing, Measure One Result

Emma · Science · Saturday, November 14, 2026 · 50 minutes

What you are learning

Practice naming a changed variable, a measured outcome and three fixed conditions, then calculate trial means accurately.

Learn First — Teaching

  1. A fair test changes one thing on purpose and keeps other conditions alike. Here the changed thing is the TYPE OF FABRIC.
  2. Two equal-size fabric squares each receive the same starting water amount in a pretend trial. The material is changed; the SIZE and starting amount are controls.
  3. The measured outcome is the amount of water absorbed, in milliliters (mL). A result such as 13 mL is a VALUE, not the name of the outcome.
  4. To calculate the mean of three trials, ADD the three absorption amounts then DIVIDE the total by THREE. Write the division sign and unit mL.
  5. Example: 12 + 14 + 13 = 39 mL; 39 divided by 3 = 13 mL. Do not stop after adding or accidentally copy one trial value.
  6. The difference between two means is the larger mean minus the smaller mean; both were measured in mL, so the difference is also in mL.
  7. These numbers are invented for reasoning practice. They are NOT a real wet-towel experiment; you must not claim to have physically tested anything.

Two fully worked models

Model 1

Worked model: Cotton trials 12, 14 and 13 mL. Total = 39 mL; mean = 39 divided by 3 = 13 mL.

Model 2

Worked model: Microfiber trials 16, 17 and 18 mL. Total = 51 mL; mean = 17 mL. Difference = 17 - 13 = 4 mL for these fictional trials.

Important mistake to avoid:

Never call '12 mL' the measured VARIABLE. The result is 'water absorbed in mL'; 12 mL is a single measurement. Same-size squares are controls, not the changed variable.

Museum Visit: Count the Real Minutes

Emma · Math · Tuesday, November 17 · 50 minutes

What you are learning: Read a schedule, add travel and waiting time across an hour boundary, and distinguish elapsed time from clock time.

Learn First — lesson and method

  1. Clock time tells when something happens, such as 9:43 a.m. Elapsed time measures how many minutes passed, such as 28 minutes.
  2. When you cross an hour, count minutes to the next full hour, then keep going. From 9:43 to 10:00 is 17 minutes, not 57.
  3. Write the schedule as a timeline. Travel, waiting and the tour are separate intervals; all consume time even when nothing is moving.
  4. To add a 28-minute ride from 9:15, add 15 minutes to reach 9:30 and 13 more to reach 9:43.
  5. To check a multi-step schedule, add each interval separately and then count the total from the original start to final finish.
  6. A planned timetable can state when an activity SHOULD happen, but cannot prove a real bus was on time.
  7. Always label start/end times with a.m. or p.m. and durations with 'minutes'; a time of day is not a duration.

Two complete teaching models

Model 1: Model A: Leave at 9:15 a.m. Ride 28 minutes -> 9:43 a.m.; wait 17 more -> 10:00 a.m. Total travel+wait = 45 minutes.
Model 2: Model B: A gallery tour begins at 10:00 and takes 45 minutes -> 10:45. From 9:15 until 10:45 is 90 minutes; 28+17+45=90 confirms it.
Mistake to avoid: 9:43 + 17 minutes is 10:00, not 9:60. Do not add a.m. times as if they were ordinary numbers.

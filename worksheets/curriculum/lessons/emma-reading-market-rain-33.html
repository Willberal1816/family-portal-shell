Goal: read a new story, explain the main problem, why actions mattered, and the complete resolution; include an independent-book reading log.

Plan: 45 minutes. Read the example, study the fresh information, then complete the two printable pages. This is one class, not two assignments.

Learn First

A complete summary names the problem, important actions and clues, and how the problem ended. If the ending is missing, the summary is unfinished. Use exact details from the passage instead of guessing.

Read your own book for 20–25 minutes before completing the questions.

Worked example

Mini-example: A sign blew away; Nora followed the paper trail and found it near the gate; she returned it before visitors arrived. A complete summary includes the returned sign, not only the search.

The Rainy Farmers Market

At eight o’clock, Jonah helped his aunt arrange apples and jars of honey at the farmers market. The sky was cloudy, but the forecast on the radio had promised only a light shower. A row of paper price signs stood in front of the produce.

Just before the first customers arrived, a hard gust pushed rain under the tent. Water splashed onto the paper signs, and two of the prices became unreadable. Jonah wanted to guess the missing numbers, but his aunt stopped him. She kept a dry price list in a plastic folder behind the table.

Jonah moved the apples away from the edge of the tent. His aunt read the prices aloud from the folder while Jonah wrote new signs with a thick marker. They covered the signs with clear sleeves and clipped them to the table so the wind would not blow them away.

When the first family approached, every item had a readable price. Jonah explained that the dry folder helped them replace the signs accurately. His aunt smiled and put the folder back where they could find it next time.

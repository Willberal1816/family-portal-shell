Bus-Stop Survey: Write the Claim's Limit

Natalie · Language Arts · Thursday, November 12, 2026 · 55 minutes

What you are learning: Compare a public notice and a small survey; describe only what each source actually proves.

Learn First

  1. A notice reports a rule or event published by the organizer. A survey records responses from only the people who answered.
  2. Name the sample group and date when reporting a survey count. 'Six of ten Tuesday respondents' is precise; 'everyone' is not.
  3. A notice tells what is planned; it cannot prove that everyone attended or approved. A survey tells what respondents said; it cannot prove an official rule.
  4. A strong paragraph cites Source A for an official plan and Source B for a stated preference, while explaining one limit.
  5. When correcting an overclaim, remove the unproved universal word and replace it with the source's exact group.

Two worked examples

Model A: 'All residents want benches' is unsupported. Source B says '7 of 12 surveyed bus riders at the north stop wanted benches.'
Model B: Source A announces a new shelter for December 3; it does not prove construction was completed. Correct: 'The city notice schedules the shelter opening for December 3.'
A mistake to avoid: Never infer the opinion of every rider from only 12 surveyed people or claim a planned event has already happened.

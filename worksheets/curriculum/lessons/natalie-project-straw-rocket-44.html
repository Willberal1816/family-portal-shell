Engineer a Paper Rocket: Fins vs No Fins

Natalie · Project · Wednesday, November 11, 2026 · 50 minutes

What you are learning: Build two real straw-launched paper rockets, test them repeatedly and make a cautious evidence-based comparison.

Learn First

  1. Finished work: TWO similarly sized paper tubes with taped noses that fit a drinking straw. One has no fins; the other has four paper fins near the open end.
  2. Use the demonstrated Science Buddies paper-rocket build. A rolled tube, closed nose and open straw end form the body; two folded fin pieces make four small fins on one rocket.
  3. To isolate the influence of fins, keep the paper, body length, straw, start mark and room conditions as similar as possible.
  4. Launch each design at least three times, measure landing distance in feet, and calculate its mean distance. The results may differ from the video.
  5. Make a scientific claim only about your recorded trials; note launch force and measurement uncertainty. Adult checks scissors and the clear, safe launch direction.

See the complete models

Build model: A = paper tube with taped closed nose and open base. B = matching paper tube plus four equally placed fins near base.
Evidence model (example, NOT your data): A flights 5, 6, 7 ft, mean 6 ft. B flights 7, 8, 9 ft, mean 8 ft. B averaged 2 ft farther under that setup, but more trials would be useful.
A mistake to avoid: Do not aim at people, animals, eyes, glass or valuable items. Use a personal straw and do not share the mouthpiece.

Build and test - follow the video

  1. Prepare two paper rectangles, tape, pencil, ruler, two small fin pieces, scissors and your personal straw. Ask an adult to check the cutting and launch area.
  2. Watch how the video rolls paper around a pencil, seals the tube's nose with tape and keeps the rear open. Build rocket A this way.
  3. Make a matching rocket B. Fold two paper triangles to make four fins; tape near the open end as the video demonstrates.
  4. Mark a safe launch line. Launch A three times and B three times; use the same straw, direction and measurement method. Keep the finished rockets for parent inspection.
  5. Complete the two printed observation pages using YOUR measurements. Scan both pages after the build.

Independent student work

Print TWO pages and complete the numbered tasks on the paper. Show the requested arithmetic, page evidence, data or full sentences. Scan both pages with their Tracking numbers, then Finish Class.

Materials: 2 sheets paper, tape, pencil, scissors (adult check), 1 personal drinking straw, ruler/measuring tape, clear launch area. Adult safety check required.

Time plan: 8 minutes video/demo + 25 minutes build + 12 minutes test and record + 5 minutes check.

Independent Reading: Causes and Turning Points

Natalie · Reading · Wednesday, November 11, 2026 · 45 minutes

What you are learning: Read for 25 minutes and show how two events in your own book connect without confusing fact with inference.
Read FIRST: 25 active minutes with your own book. Then answer the worksheet; use true page citations.

Learn First

  1. A turning point changes the direction of events. Identify what happened before it and what happened after it.
  2. A cause has evidence linking it to an outcome. Events occurring close together are not automatically cause and effect.
  3. Use page numbers for both the earlier event and later result. Distinguish the narrator's statement from your inference.
  4. The words 'because' and 'therefore' should connect supported ideas, not guesses.
  5. Read your current book continuously for 25 active minutes before answering on paper.

See the complete models

Model 1 (fictional): A map is lost on page 18. On page 20 the team returns to camp because they cannot find the route. The lost map is an explicit cause of the return.
Model 2 (fictional): A train passes, then a door opens. Without text connecting them, you can report the sequence but not prove the train caused the door to open.
A mistake to avoid: A precise page citation supports a claim; it does not make an invented connection true.

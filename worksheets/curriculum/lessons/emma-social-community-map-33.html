Goal: read a map key, use compass directions, calculate walking distance in miles, and explain what a map cannot prove; this map-skills strand does not depend on yesterday's unfinished history work.

Plan: 50 minutes. Read the example, study the fresh information, then complete the two printable pages. This is one class, not two assignments.

Learn First

On a map, north is up, south is down, east is right, and west is left. A route along a street grid counts each horizontal or vertical block. In this example 1 grid block = ¼ mile. Two blocks equal ½ mile; four blocks equal 1 mile.

A map can show locations and approximate distance, but it cannot prove whether a street is safe right now.

Worked example

From A1 to C1 is two blocks east. At ¼ mile per block, the distance is ½ mile. Do not measure diagonally when the route follows the streets.

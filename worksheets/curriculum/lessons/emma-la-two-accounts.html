Compare Two Accounts

Emma · Language Arts · Guided Teaching
What you are learning: Compare two accounts of the same event or topic and explain how point of view, purpose, and selected details shape each account.

Start with what both accounts agree on

List the facts both writers include. Shared facts create a baseline before you study differences.

Then notice the choices

  • Point of view: Who is telling the account, and what did that person know or experience?
  • Purpose: Is the writer informing, explaining, persuading, remembering, or defending a position?
  • Details selected: Which moments or facts get included, and which are left out?
  • Tone: What attitude comes through in the writer’s word choices?
Example: A firefighter’s account of a storm may focus on rescues and danger. A shop owner’s account may focus on lost inventory and reopening. The accounts can differ because the writers experienced different parts of the event.

Explain, do not accuse

Different accounts do not automatically mean one is false. Explain how the writer’s role or purpose could reasonably shape what the account emphasizes.

Strong response: Both accounts agree that the road closed at noon. The reporter emphasizes official safety warnings, while the resident emphasizes how the closure affected families trying to get home.

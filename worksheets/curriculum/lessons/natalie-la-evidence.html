Use a Clue from the Text

Natalie · Language Arts · Learn First
What you are learning: Make a claim about a text, select precise evidence, and explain how the evidence supports the claim.

Learn the idea

Text evidence is not any sentence copied from a passage. Useful evidence is the detail that best supports the idea you are trying to prove. Start with the claim, then ask which exact words or event make you think it.

You can quote a short phrase or paraphrase accurately. Either way, the next step is reasoning: explain how the clue connects to the claim. This prevents “claim + quote” responses with no explanation.

Sometimes one clue is not enough. Two independent details can make an inference stronger, especially when they point in the same direction for different reasons. If evidence could support more than one interpretation, acknowledge uncertainty.

Precision matters. “She was upset” is not evidence if that is your inference. Better evidence is an observable action such as “She folded the letter twice, put it in her pocket, and stopped answering questions.”

Words you will use

  • claim: the idea being argued or explained
  • evidence: specific text information supporting a claim
  • inference: a conclusion based on clues plus reasoning
  • paraphrase: accurately restating information in your own words

Worked examples

Clue: “Luis checked the clock three times and kept his backpack on.” Inference: he may be eager or anxious to leave.
Weak evidence: “Luis wanted to leave.” That repeats the inference instead of citing an observed clue.
Two-clue reasoning: several details pointing the same way can increase confidence.

More worked examples

Teaching example: What comes after evidence?
Answer / reasoning: Reasoning that explains the connection to the claim.
Teaching example: Is paraphrased evidence allowed?
Answer / reasoning: Yes, if accurate and specific.
Teaching example: Why can two clues be stronger than one?
Answer / reasoning: They can independently support the same conclusion.

Before the worksheet

Strong work shows: a clear claim, precise evidence, explanation, and careful words such as “suggests” when the text does not prove something directly.

Why Things Keep Moving — Rebuild Newton’s First Law

This is a new class on the same prerequisite. The important correction is: an object can be moving at a steady speed even when the net force is zero. Moving does not automatically mean unbalanced forces.

2. Separate motion from change in motion

Velocity stays the same
Same speed + same direction → net force can be zero.
Velocity changes
Speeding up, slowing down, or turning → net force is not zero.

Forces do not “keep motion alive.” A nonzero net force changes motion.

3. Worked example — steady-moving puck

A puck slides right at a steady speed on nearly frictionless ice.

  1. It is moving, but its speed and direction are not changing.
  2. That means its acceleration is zero.
  3. A zero net force is consistent with that steady motion.

The statement “it needs a forward net force because it is moving” is the misconception to avoid.

4. Worked example — backpack in a stopping car

The car and backpack are already moving forward. When the car stops, the backpack tends to keep its existing motion because of inertia. A seat belt changes a passenger’s motion by exerting a force on the passenger.

When naming forces, use real names: gravity, support/normal force, friction, tension, push, pull. “Balanced” and “unbalanced” describe the relationship between forces; they are not force names.

5. Try a simple demonstration

Use a small book or toy on a smooth table. Give it one gentle push and watch what happens after your hand stops touching it.

  1. Does your hand keep pushing after contact ends?
  2. What force makes the object slow on the table?
  3. If friction were much smaller, would the object stop sooner or keep moving longer?
Check your reasoning

Your hand stops exerting the push after contact. Friction opposes the motion and changes the velocity. With less friction, the object would keep moving longer.

6. Guided reasoning

  1. A cart moves east at a steady 2 m/s. Net force: zero or east?
    CheckZero is possible because velocity is not changing.
  2. A rolling ball slows on carpet. What changed, and what force caused the change?
    CheckIts speed decreased; friction provided a force opposite the motion.
  3. A book rests on a table. Name two important forces and say whether the net force is zero.
    CheckGravity downward and support/normal force upward; they balance, so net force is zero.
  4. A passenger moves forward with a car, then the car stops. What force changes the passenger’s motion?
    CheckThe seat belt exerts a force on the passenger.

7. Mistake detective

A student says: “The bicycle is moving, so there must be more forward force than backward force.”

Write one sentence explaining what information is missing before you can decide whether the forces are unbalanced.

Check

You need to know whether the bicycle’s velocity is changing. If it is moving steadily, the net force can be zero.

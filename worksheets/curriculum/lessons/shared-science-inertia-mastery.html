Why Things Keep Moving — Rebuild Newton’s First Law

This lesson reteaches the same prerequisite from a different angle. The key correction is that an object can move steadily while the net force is zero.

2. Definitions

Inertia: the tendency of an object to keep its current state of motion unless a net force changes it.

Net force: the overall force after all forces are combined.

Balanced forces: forces that combine to zero net force.

Velocity: speed with direction.

Velocity stays the same
Same speed + same direction → net force can be zero.
Velocity changes
Speeding up, slowing down, or turning → net force is not zero.

3. Worked example — steady-moving puck

A puck slides right at a steady speed on nearly frictionless ice. It is moving, but its speed and direction are not changing. Its acceleration is zero, so a zero net force is consistent with the motion.

Correction: “It needs a forward net force because it is moving” is false. Net force is needed to change motion, not merely to keep steady motion going.

4. Worked example — backpack in a stopping car

The car and backpack are already moving forward. When the car stops, the backpack tends to keep its existing motion because of inertia. A seat belt changes a passenger’s motion by exerting a force on the passenger.

Useful force names include gravity, support/normal force, friction, tension, push, and pull. “Balanced” and “unbalanced” describe the relationship between forces; they are not force names.

5. Demonstration explanation

A teacher demonstration can use a small book or toy sliding on a table. The hand provides a push only while it is touching the object. After contact ends, friction opposes the motion and causes the object to slow. If friction were much smaller, the object would continue moving longer.

This demonstration shows why the force that started the motion does not have to keep acting after the object is already moving.

6. More worked reasoning examples

Cart moving east at a steady 2 m/s: zero net force is possible because the velocity is not changing.

Rolling ball slowing on carpet: the speed decreases because friction acts opposite the motion.

Book resting on a table: gravity downward and the support/normal force upward balance, so the net force is zero.

Passenger in a stopping car: the seat belt provides the force that changes the passenger’s forward motion.

7. Common mistake and correction

Mistake: “The bicycle is moving, so there must be more forward force than backward force.”

Correction: first determine whether the bicycle’s velocity is changing. If it moves at constant speed in a straight line, the net force can be zero.

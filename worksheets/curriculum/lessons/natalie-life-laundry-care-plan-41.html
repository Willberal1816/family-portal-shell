Laundry Skills: Read Labels, Sort, Fold and Plan

Natalie · Cycle B Life Skills · Friday, October 30, 2026 · 60 minutes

Today you will do: Sort a real sample of garments using care-label evidence, produce an organized folded stack, and design a safe laundry plan without operating machinery.
Materials and safety: Six ordinary garments, 2 laundry baskets or two labeled areas, flat clean folding area, a ruler if available, pencil, 2 printed work pages. Adult permission for clothing; no detergents, washer, dryer, machines, heat, or hazardous fabric cleaners needed.

Watch the real demonstrations before touching clothes

1. How to sort everyday laundry — by kids

Source and playback fallback: Homegrown School — Sorting Laundry. Today you only sort and fold; DO NOT operate a washer or dryer.

2. How to fold clothing

Source and playback fallback: Homegrown School — Laundry Week, Folding.

Learn First — follow these steps in order

  1. Watch the exact sorting and folding demonstrations. Stop after the sort explanation and after the fold example. You will use actual clothes and make an organized storage result.
  2. Get six normal garments with household permission. Look at each care label and record specific text/symbols. If labels are missing, put the garment in 'needs adult advice.'
  3. Make a simple inventory: garment, color, readable label instruction, and sorting decision. Make 2–3 sensible groups, such as ordinary washable pieces and special care; explain EACH questionable item.
  4. Fold at least four CLEAN garments using the demonstrated fold. Make a compact, stable stack or basket. Use the same fold for matching items and leave fragile/special-care clothes untouched if uncertain.
  5. Check your folded result: straight edges, no dirty clothes mixed with clean, labeling that will make it easy to put away. Present it to an adult; ask for permission if you plan to run a load in the future.
  6. Finish the planning sheet: propose a sensible future load, explain how a care label affects that choice, and name one risk that your grouping prevents. Return everything to its place. You do NOT operate a washer.

Two completed examples

Example A — evidence-based sort: Navy cotton tee labeled 'machine wash cold' and black socks labeled 'machine wash cold' may be grouped only after checking color/soil level. A red sweater marked 'hand wash only' goes into a separate 'ask adult' area.
Example B — finished-object quality test: Four folded garments with similar rectangle sizes in a tidy stack will fit in a drawer better than uneven crumpled garments. We can compare width, label visibility, and separation without washing clothes.

What your REAL finished work should look like

What you will make: A real label-based clothing sort, four clean folded garments, and a written plan for a safe next load.

Sample label legend: 'Machine wash cold' means cold-water machine cycle is permitted as shown; 'hand wash only' calls for a different method; 'dry clean' should be referred to an adult. This is a sorting/planning exercise, not permission to use chemicals or machinery.

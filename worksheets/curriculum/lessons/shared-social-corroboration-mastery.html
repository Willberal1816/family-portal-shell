Check Another Source — Corroboration Mastery

Before moving to a new historical-thinking skill, make this one solid: use more than one source to check a claim.

1. Find the same fact

Corroboration means checking whether another independent source supports, challenges, or adds to a claim. Look for a fact that appears in two different sources.

2. Worked example

Source A is a diary that says the bridge closed in the afternoon. Source B is an official notice that says the bridge closed at 3:15 PM.

A strong conclusion is: Both sources support that the bridge closed that afternoon, and the official notice adds the exact time.

3. Name a genuinely different source

If asked what you would check next, do not name the same source again. Name a new source, such as another diary, a newspaper report, a photograph, a map, or an official record, and say what that source could confirm or add.

Use only details the sources actually provide. Do not invent a detail to make the conclusion sound stronger.

Now do your mastery check

  1. Name the fact you are checking.
  2. Name Source 1 and what it says.
  3. Name Source 2 and what it says.
  4. Write one conclusion that says what the sources together support.
  5. If asked for another source, name a new independent source and what it could add.

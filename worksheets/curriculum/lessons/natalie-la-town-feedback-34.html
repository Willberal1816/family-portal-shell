Learn First

A survey result describes the people who answered, not automatically every person invited or registered. Write the numerator and denominator clearly. A poster may be promotional rather than independent evidence. A safe claim names the source, number of respondents, and what was actually asked.

Worked example

A survey of 10 responding members found 7 favor longer hours. Supported: 'Seven of the ten people who responded favored longer hours.' Unsupported: 'Everyone in the club wants longer hours.' We do not know the other members' views.

Today's information

Fictional town park project, October 21: The park has 50 registered volunteers. Only 12 volunteers returned comment cards about adding a shaded bench. Of those 12, 8 supported the bench, 4 opposed it. The cards did not ask why people chose their answer. A publicity flyer states, 'Everybody at the park wants our new bench!' A dated clerk's memo accurately records 8 yes, 4 no, and 38 nonresponses.
Before you begin: Read the example and the new source. Work independently. Use U.S. customary units (inches, feet, miles, °F) when applicable; do not invent data or perform unsafe experiments.

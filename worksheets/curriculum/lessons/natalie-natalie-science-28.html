Impact Test: Which Change Counts?

Natalie · Science · Monday, September 28, 2026 · Learn First

What you are learning: design or interpret a fair energy-and-motion test, convert or average the data accurately, and explain energy transfer without claiming it disappears. Read the examples, then use the separate Independent Work printable for all answers. This teaching page has no written work to turn in.

Learn the idea

Changed variable is the landing surface; controlled variables are ball, ramp and release. Use all trials, not an extreme result, to support a narrow conclusion.

A fair test changes ONE factor at a time. An average uses all readings in the same unit: add them and divide by the number of trials. To support a claim, quote both means and their units, then explain what the test actually compared. A limited model does not prove what every outdoor surface or every animal/object would do.

Potential energy is stored because of position; kinetic energy is energy of motion. Some motion energy transfers through friction to warmth and sound, so it does not vanish. Do not pretend a model can measure heat or sound unless those were actually measured.

Words that help

  • variable: a factor that may change in a test
  • control: a factor deliberately kept the same
  • mean: sum of measurements divided by trial count
  • energy transfer: energy changing form or moving to surroundings

Worked example 2

Suppose two surfaces are tested with the same ramp and ball. If the ramp height also changes, the result cannot isolate which factor changed the distance. Re-run at one fixed height. A defensible conclusion names the changed surface and admits the test used a small model.

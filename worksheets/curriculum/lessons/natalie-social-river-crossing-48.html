A River Crossing: Compare Three Historical Records

Natalie · Social Studies · Monday, November 16, 2026 · 50 minutes

What you are learning

Distinguish what a transport schedule, traveler letter and maintenance receipt can establish about a fictional bridge reopening.

Learn First

  1. A primary source is a record created around the event it describes, but its scope is limited by the author's knowledge and purpose.
  2. A ferry schedule tells planned departures; it is not a complete record of every trip that ACTUALLY departed.
  3. A dated traveler's letter tells what ONE traveler experienced and may not describe everyone else's journey.
  4. A repair receipt records materials billed; it does not by itself prove repairs were installed or caused the ferry to run differently.
  5. Compare exact dates and locations before creating a timeline. Do not turn three nearby dates into a claim that one event caused another.
  6. Corroboration occurs when sources independently support the same claim. A disagreement or missing detail should be named rather than erased.
  7. A historical conclusion should state evidence, what the sources cannot prove and which additional primary record would help.

Two worked models

Model 1: Model A: Schedule A lists May 4 morning ferry trips. A letter dated May 5 says its author crossed on May 5. Neither alone confirms every May 4 trip departed.
Model 2: Model B: Receipt C bills rope on May 3. It cannot prove the rope was used to repair the ferry or caused successful crossings.
A mistake to avoid: Do not describe a billed purchase as an observed installation or a printed schedule as guaranteed actual service.

Percent of a Quantity

Natalie · Math · Learn First
What you are learning: Use percent as “out of 100,” connect it to a unit rate or proportion, and find a percent of a quantity in practical situations.

Percent means per hundred

Thirty percent means 30 out of every 100. You can think of percent as a comparison to 100, a decimal, or a rate. The useful question is: what part of this quantity matches that percent?

Worked example 1: Find 25% of 80. Since 25% is one quarter of 100%, take one quarter of 80. 80 ÷ 4 = 20, so 25% of 80 is 20.
Worked example 2: Find 15% of 60. Ten percent of 60 is 6. Five percent is half of 10%, so it is 3. Then 15% is 6 + 3 = 9.
Worked example 3: A $48 item is discounted 30%. Ten percent of 48 is 4.8, so 30% is 14.4. The discount is $14.40. The sale price is $48.00 − $14.40 = $33.60.

Three ways to reason

  • Friendly percent: Break the percent into 10%, 5%, 25%, 50%, or another easy amount.
  • Decimal: Convert the percent to a decimal and multiply when that is efficient.
  • Proportion/unit-rate reasoning: Compare the quantity to 100 and scale.

Estimate first

Before calculating, predict a reasonable size. If you are finding 20% of 90, the answer must be less than half of 90 and close to one fifth. An answer like 180 is impossible.

Reasonableness example: 35% of 200 should be more than 25% of 200 (=50) but less than 50% (=100). The exact answer, 70, fits that range.

When percent is more than 100

A percent can be greater than 100. For example, 120% of 50 means the full 50 plus another 20% of 50. That is 50 + 10 = 60.

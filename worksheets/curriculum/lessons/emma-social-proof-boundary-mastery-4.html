Proof or Guess? — Stay Inside the Sources

What you are learning: Separate what a source proves from what sounds possible but is still unknown.

The three-box proof ladder

1. CLAIM
What are we trying to decide?
2. PROOF
Which exact source fact supports it?
3. UNKNOWN
What part is not actually shown?

Worked example

Source A: “At 2:15 PM, water covered the low bridge.” Source B: “Staff placed a closure sign at 2:30 PM.”

Claim: “The trail was closed after water covered the bridge.” Supported. A proves water was present first; B proves the closure came afterward.

Claim: “Heavy rain broke the bridge.” Not proved. Neither source says the bridge broke or identifies rain as the cause.

Use source-safe words

Proved: “Source B states…” “Sources A and C both show…”

Not proved: “The sources do not tell us…” “This is possible, but the evidence does not establish it.”

A new source must be genuinely new

If the sources already include witness statements and a park notice, another useful source might be a maintenance report, weather station record, timestamped photograph, or staff log. Name the source and the exact missing fact it could answer.

Independent work routine: underline the exact proof, cross out any unsupported added detail, and write one “we still do not know…” statement before your conclusion.

Fraction Division — Groups in Real Problems

Natalie · Math · Learn First
What you are learning: Use fraction division to answer “how many groups fit?” problems, interpret a quotient in the situation, and prove an answer by multiplying back.

Use a three-question check

  1. What is the total amount? That is the dividend.
  2. What is one group’s size? That is the divisor.
  3. What will the quotient count? Pieces, batches, segments, containers, or another kind of group.

This lesson is about meaning before procedure. You already know how to change mixed numbers and multiply by the reciprocal. The new habit is to label what every number means so the arithmetic cannot become detached from the story.

Worked model 1 — full groups and leftover

A board is 5 14 feet long. Each shelf piece must be 34 foot.

Total: 5 14 ft. Group size: 34 ft per piece. Question: how many pieces fit?

214 ÷ 34 = 214 × 43 = 7.

Meaning: seven shelf-size pieces fit exactly. Check: 7 × 34 = 5 14.

Worked model 2 — a quotient that is not a whole number

You have 3 12 cups of yogurt. One batch needs 23 cup.

72 ÷ 23 = 214 = 5 14.

The quotient means there is enough yogurt for five full batches and one-fourth of another batch. If the question asks only for full batches, the answer is 5 full batches, with yogurt left.

Worked model 3 — catch the wrong operation

A student writes 4 ÷ 14 = 4 × 14 = 1.

Stop and use meaning: quarter-size groups are smaller than whole groups, so more than four of them should fit into 4. The divisor must be reciprocated: 4 × 4 = 16. Check: 16 × 14 = 4.

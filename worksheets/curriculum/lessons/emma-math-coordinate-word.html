Coordinate Plane Word Problems

Emma · Math · Learn First
What you are learning: Use coordinates, routes, and horizontal/vertical distances to solve real-world-style problems and explain what points mean.

Learn the idea

Coordinates become useful when they represent locations. First decide what the axes mean and where the origin is. A point such as (-3,4) may mean 3 blocks west and 4 blocks north of a starting point.

When a route stays horizontal or vertical, find each segment by comparing one coordinate at a time. Horizontal moves change x while y stays the same; vertical moves change y while x stays the same.

Direction words matter. If positive x is east, negative x is west. Positive y can be north and negative y south. A negative coordinate does not mean negative distance; it tells which side of the origin a location occupies.

Strong solutions include a sketch, ordered pairs, distance calculation, and a sentence answering the real question. If the problem asks which stop is closer, make the comparison—not just two separate calculations.

Words you will use

  • coordinate model: a graph representing real locations or quantities
  • route: a path through points
  • horizontal segment: endpoints share y
  • vertical segment: endpoints share x

Worked examples

Example: Gate (-4,2) and bench (3,2) are 7 units apart.
Route: (1,-3) → (1,4) → (5,4) travels 7 + 4 = 11 units.
Scale: if each grid unit is 2 meters, 7 grid units represent 14 meters.

More worked examples

Teaching example: Same y-coordinate means what direct path?
Answer / reasoning: Horizontal.
Teaching example: Why can (-6,2) be 9 units from (3,2)?
Answer / reasoning: The separation crosses zero and totals 9.
Teaching example: One grid unit = 5 feet; 4 units = ?
Answer / reasoning: 20 feet.

Before the worksheet

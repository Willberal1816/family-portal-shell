Longitude & Time Zones

Emma and Natalie · Geography · 45 minutes · Learn First
What you are learning: explain why longitude connects to local time, estimate time differences, and use east/west reasoning without treating political time-zone borders as exact longitude lines.

Earth’s rotation gives the estimate

Earth rotates about 360° in 24 hours. 360 ÷ 24 = 15° per hour. Moving east generally means later local solar time; moving west generally means earlier local solar time.

Worked model: Place A is near 30°W and Place B is near 15°E. Difference: 45°. Estimate: 45 ÷ 15 = 3 hours. B is east, so its local solar time is about 3 hours later.

Use a four-step method

  1. Find the longitude difference.
  2. Divide by 15° per hour.
  3. Decide which place is east.
  4. Move the clock later east or earlier west.

Important limitation

Official time zones bend around countries, states, islands, and policies. The 15° method is an estimate based on Earth’s rotation, not a promise of the legal clock time.

Date line idea

Near 180°, neighboring places may use different calendar dates. Do not assume “later time” always means the same date.

Materials

Pencil, printable, optional globe or world map. No online account is required.

Quick check: If it is noon at 0°, about what solar time is it at 45°E? Answer: about 3:00 p.m.

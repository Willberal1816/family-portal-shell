2. Start with groups, not a rule

Division can ask: How many groups of this size fit in that amount?

For 2 ÷ 2/3, picture two wholes split into thirds. There are six thirds total. A group of 2/3 uses two thirds, so three groups fit.

2 ÷ 2/3 = 3 because three groups of 2/3 make 2.

That sentence matters. It tells you what the quotient counts.

3. Worked example — mixed number + meaning

2 1/4 ÷ 3/4

  1. Convert the mixed number: 2 1/4 = 9/4.
  2. Ask the meaning: how many 3/4-sized groups fit in 9/4?
  3. Only the divisor gets the reciprocal: 9/4 × 4/3.
  4. Simplify: 9/3 = 3.
  5. Interpret: three groups of 3/4 fit in 2 1/4.

4. Worked example — the quotient is not always a whole number

3 1/2 ÷ 2/3

  1. 3 1/2 = 7/2.
  2. 7/2 × 3/2 = 21/4 = 5 1/4.
  3. Because 2/3 is less than 1, getting an answer larger than 3 1/2 makes sense.

If this were ribbon, 5 1/4 means five full 2/3-foot groups plus one-fourth of another 2/3-foot group.

5. Guided practice — try before checking

  1. Meaning only: What is 3 ÷ 1/2 asking you to count?
    Check after you answerHow many one-half-sized groups fit in 3.
  2. Convert first: Rewrite 1 3/5 as an improper fraction.
    Check8/5.
  3. Setup: Write the multiplication setup for 1 3/5 ÷ 4/5 without solving.
    Check8/5 × 5/4.
  4. Estimate: Should 4 ÷ 3/4 be greater or less than 4? Explain why.
    CheckGreater than 4, because groups smaller than 1 fit into 4 more than four times.
  5. Interpret: A 5-foot board is cut into 1 1/4-foot pieces. What does the quotient count?
    CheckThe number of 1 1/4-foot pieces that fit in 5 feet.

6. Mistake detective

Explain the mistake before fixing it.

  1. A student writes 2 1/2 ÷ 3/5 = 5/2 × 3/5. What did the student forget to do?
  2. A student gets 3 ÷ 1/2 = 1 1/2. Without recalculating everything, what size clue tells you that answer cannot be reasonable?
  3. A student correctly gets a quotient of 4 batches but writes only “4.” What important part of the answer is missing?
Check your reasoning after all three
  1. The divisor must be reciprocated: 3/5 becomes 5/3.
  2. Dividing by 1/2 should make the quotient larger than 3, not smaller.
  3. The context/units: the answer should say 4 batches.

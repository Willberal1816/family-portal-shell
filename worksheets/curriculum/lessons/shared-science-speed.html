Speed: Distance and Time

What this lesson teaches: average speed compares how much distance is covered with how much time passes. The work is on the printable; this page is for explanation, definitions, and worked examples.

Definitions

Distance: how far an object travels.

Time: how long the motion lasts.

Average speed: total distance divided by total time.

Units: the labels attached to a measurement, such as miles per hour, meters per second, or kilometers per minute.

The relationship

Average speed = distance ÷ time.

The word per means “for each.” A speed of 6 miles per hour means an average of 6 miles for each hour of travel.

Worked example 1

A cyclist travels 12 miles in 2 hours.

12 miles ÷ 2 hours = 6 miles per hour.

Worked example 2

A toy car travels 6 meters in 3 seconds.

6 meters ÷ 3 seconds = 2 meters per second.

Worked comparison

Two runners travel the same 1-mile distance. Runner A takes 8 minutes and Runner B takes 10 minutes. Because the distance is the same, the runner who takes less time has the greater average speed. Runner A is faster on average.

What average speed does not tell you

If a car travels 60 miles in 1 hour, its average speed is 60 mph. That does not prove it moved at exactly 60 mph every second. It could have stopped briefly and moved faster at another time. Average speed summarizes the whole trip.

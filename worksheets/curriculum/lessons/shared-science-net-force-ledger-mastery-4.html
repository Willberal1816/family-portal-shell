Net Force Ledger — Combine by Direction

What you are learning

Today you will find net force when two, three, or more forces act on an object. Instead of guessing from the picture, you will keep a short direction ledger: total everything pointing one way, total everything pointing the opposite way, then compare.

The direction-ledger method

1. Sort. Put every force into a direction: left/right or up/down.

2. Combine same-direction forces. Add all forces pointing the same way.

3. Compare opposite totals. Subtract the smaller total from the larger.

4. Keep the stronger direction. That direction belongs on the net force.

5. Interpret. Zero net force means no acceleration. Nonzero net force means velocity changes in the net-force direction.

Worked model 1 — three horizontal forces

A cart has 5 N →, 4 N →, and 6 N ←.

Right totalLeft totalNet force
5 + 4 = 9 N6 N9 − 6 = 3 N right

The two rightward forces work together first. Right is stronger by 3 N, so the cart accelerates toward the right.

Worked model 2 — balanced does not mean stopped

A scooter is already moving east at steady speed. Forces are 7 N → and 7 N ←.

Right totalLeft totalNet force
7 N7 N0 N

The scooter can keep moving east at the same velocity. Balanced forces mean no change in velocity, not “must be stopped.”

Worked model 3 — net force can oppose motion

A ball is rolling right. Forces are 2 N right and 8 N left.

Right total = 2 N. Left total = 8 N. Net force = 6 N left.

Because the ball is moving right while the net force points left, its rightward velocity decreases. Motion direction and net-force direction are different pieces of information.

Common mistake

Wrong: “There are more arrows on the right, so right wins.”
Better: Add the force amounts. One 12 N arrow can be stronger than two 4 N arrows.

Worked model 4 — vertical forces

A sign has 20 N down from gravity and support forces of 12 N up + 8 N up.

Up total = 20 N. Down total = 20 N. Net force = 0 N. Its vertical velocity does not change.

Before independent work

Sort → combine → compare → keep the stronger direction → explain the velocity change.
If a problem has three forces, do not subtract until you have combined forces pointing the same way.

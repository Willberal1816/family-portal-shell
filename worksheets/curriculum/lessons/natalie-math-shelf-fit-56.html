Design Check: Will the Storage Bins Fit?

Natalie | Math | Friday, November 27, 2026 | 50 minutes

What you are learning: Compare dimensions along the same axis, calculate clearance, and verify scale conversions before making a design claim.

Learn First

Length and width are different directions. A rectangle fits inside another only if its length and width each fit along the corresponding direction. Adding a width to a length gives a meaningless fit check. When placing objects beside each other, add the dimensions along the direction where they sit side by side.

Clearance is the unused distance after objects and required gaps are accounted for. Compare required space with available space: a positive leftover means the layout fits along that axis; a negative result means a shortfall. Both axes must fit.

Scale 1 drawing inch = 6 actual inches. Convert actual to drawing by dividing by 6; convert back by multiplying by 6. Area measures square inches and does not establish fit by itself: two shapes can have the same area but different dimensions.

Two worked examples

Example layout: shelf 30 in long by 18 in deep. Box 12 by 10 and required side gap 6. Along length: 12 + 6 = 18, leaving 30 - 18 = 12 in. Along depth: 10 fits in 18, leaving 8 in. Both fit.
Example scale: 24 actual inches become 4 drawing inches. Reverse: 4 x 6 = 24 actual inches. A 12 x 18 inch box has area 216 square inches but cannot fit through a 10-inch wide opening.

Required gap between bins: 4 inches. Required rear clearance behind both: 6 inches.
Layout 1: both bins as listed, side by side along shelf length.

Latitude & Longitude: Find a Place

Emma + Natalie · Geography · Learn First
What you are learning: Use latitude and longitude as Earth’s coordinate system, identify hemispheres, and read a location accurately without reversing the two numbers.

Latitude goes first

Lines of latitude run east–west around Earth, but they tell how far north or south a place is from the Equator. The Equator is 0° latitude. Latitude ranges from 0° to 90° N or S.

Longitude goes second

Lines of longitude run from pole to pole, but they tell how far east or west a place is from the Prime Meridian. The Prime Meridian is 0° longitude. Longitude ranges from 0° to 180° E or W.

Read coordinates like an ordered pair: 35° N, 80° W means first move to 35 degrees north of the Equator, then find 80 degrees west of the Prime Meridian. Always read latitude first, longitude second.
Four hemispheres:
  • Northern: north of the Equator
  • Southern: south of the Equator
  • Eastern: east of the Prime Meridian
  • Western: west of the Prime Meridian

Accuracy checks

A latitude can never be 120°. A longitude can. A coordinate written 43° N, 88° W belongs in the Northern and Western Hemispheres. If you swap the numbers or directions, you may point to a completely different place—or an impossible latitude.

Worked example: Point A is 20° S, 60° E. It is south of the Equator and east of the Prime Meridian, so it lies in the Southern and Eastern Hemispheres. The first number is latitude because it uses N/S; the second is longitude because it uses E/W.

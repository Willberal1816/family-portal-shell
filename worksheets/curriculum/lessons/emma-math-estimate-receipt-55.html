Estimate First, Then Check a Receipt

Emma | Math | Tuesday, November 24, 2026 | 50 minutes

What you are learning: Estimate a total, calculate the exact receipt and verify change.

Learn First

An estimate is a prediction, not the actual price. Round each item price to the nearest dollar and keep the estimate separate from the exact calculation. Multiply each price by quantity before summing.

An order-wide fee is counted once. Check the exact total by adding change to the amount charged and comparing your exact sum to the estimate.

Two worked examples

Three items at $2.40 each cost $7.20. Rounding each to $2 predicts about $6, which is not the bill.
Two notebooks at $3.50 and a folder at $2.25 cost $9.25; one $0.75 fee gives $10. Pay $15 and get $5 change; $10+$5=$15.

Source and materials

CLASS SHOP: notebook $3.50; folder $2.25; pencil $1.40; ruler $2.60; markers $4.80; one order fee $0.75; cash $25; no tax.

Directions: Use the teaching and examples, then answer the six questions below. All six questions also appear on the matching four-page printable PDF. Show work, scan the pages when requested and Finish Class in the School Portal.

All six questions

Question 1. For 2 notebooks, 3 pencils and 1 ruler, make a table of quantities and exact item subtotals.
Question 2. Round each item price to estimate the whole order, then compute the exact total with one fee.
Question 3. A classmate charges the $0.75 fee for each separate item. Explain and correct the mistake.
Question 4. Compare order A (2 folders and markers) with B (3 rulers), including one fee.
Question 5. Design an order with three item types under $25 including the fee. Show estimate and exact sum.
Question 6. Write a full receipt with cash and change. Explain two different checks.

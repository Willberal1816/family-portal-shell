Garden Survey: Say Exactly Who Responded

Natalie · Language Arts · Wednesday, November 11, 2026 · 55 minutes

What you are learning: Build accurate written claims that name the surveyed group and do not pretend a small sample represents everyone.

Learn First

  1. A survey tells us about the PEOPLE WHO ANSWERED, not automatically everyone in a town or school.
  2. Look for a sample boundary: who, where and when. A conclusion must fit that group and source.
  3. Use the pattern: 'Of the 16 visitors who answered at the east gate on Saturday, 10 preferred a garden tour.'
  4. A percentage or fraction may be useful, but first identify the denominator: here 16 respondents, not all visitors.
  5. A short summary should give the question, the group, the main result and one reasonable limit.

See the complete models

Model 1: 'Everyone wants a garden tour' is not supported. The safe claim is '10 of the 16 Saturday east-gate respondents preferred a garden tour.'
Model 2: 'Most people prefer the tour' is still too broad. 'Most of the 16 respondents (10) preferred the tour' gives the precise group and count.
A mistake to avoid: Never replace a surveyed group with 'all families,' 'the town,' or 'visitors everywhere' when those populations were not measured.

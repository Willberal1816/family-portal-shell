Geography: Date Line Direction Checks

Natalie · Geography · Saturday November 21, 2026 · 50 minutes

What you are learning: Apply east/west International Date Line calendar rules and explain real-travel limits.

Learn First

Westward crossing generally adds one calendar day; eastward subtracts one. Flight duration/time zones are separate.

Two worked models

Model 1: Simplified example: cross WEST on Monday at 10 a.m.; date becomes Tuesday for the date-rule exercise.
Model 2: Cross EAST on Tuesday at 10 a.m.; date becomes Monday in the exercise. An exact arrival clock time is unknown.
Check for this mistake: Do not infer real arrival time from the date-line rule alone.

Source and facts

  • SIMPLIFIED card A: Monday March 2, cross west. Card B: Thursday April 9, cross east.
  • Card C: Sunday June 14, cross west. Card D: Saturday August 1, cross east.
  • Ignore flight time ONLY for the date-rule practice. Actual itineraries need time zones and travel times.

Read the models, then complete your six questions using your source. Write only what the source supports.

All six independent-work questions

These exact questions are visible on screen and appear in the matching four-page PDF. Write on paper, scan all pages and finish this class in the School Portal.

Question 1. Apply westward rule to card A. State date/day and why exact arrival time cannot be known.

Question 2. Apply eastward rule to card B. State date/day and distinguish calendar date from clock time.

Question 3. Make a table of direction, starting date and changed date for C and D.

Question 4. Correct 'East always adds a day; west subtracts,' using two source examples.

Question 5. Start Wednesday September 16. Give simplified new date for a west crossing and for an east crossing, showing the rule.

Question 6. Write six travel-advice sentences with the general rule, both directions and three missing facts for an exact itinerary.

Compare Two Texts

Natalie · Language Arts · Guided Teaching
What you are learning: Compare how two authors treat the same topic by looking at purpose, evidence, emphasis, and interpretation.

Same topic does not mean same text

Two authors can write about the same event and still choose different facts, organize them differently, or reach different conclusions. That difference does not automatically mean one author is dishonest.

  • Purpose: What is each author trying to explain, argue, or show?
  • Evidence: Which facts, examples, or quotations does each author use?
  • Emphasis: What does each author spend the most time on?
  • Interpretation: What conclusion does each author draw from the evidence?
Example: Two articles describe the same storm. One focuses on emergency response; the other focuses on damage to wildlife habitat. They can both be accurate while emphasizing different consequences.

Compare with evidence

Do not write only “Text A is different from Text B.” Name the exact difference and support it with a detail from each text.

Strong comparison: Text A presents the policy as a safety measure and cites accident data, while Text B focuses on cost and quotes local business owners.

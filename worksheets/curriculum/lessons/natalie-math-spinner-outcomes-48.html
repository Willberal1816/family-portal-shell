Fair Chances: Count Every Possible Outcome

Natalie · Math · Monday, November 16, 2026 · 50 minutes

What you are learning

Use equally likely outcomes, tables and two-step trials to calculate and justify probabilities.

Learn First

  1. A probability compares favorable outcomes to every equally likely possible outcome. A probability of 0 means impossible and 1 means certain.
  2. A spinner with four equal sections labeled A, A, B and C has four equally likely sections, not three. P(A) = 24 = 12.
  3. The spinner labels are NOT necessarily equally likely: letter A appears twice; B and C each appear once.
  4. A two-step experiment must list ALL ordered pairs. If you spin twice with four sections, there are 4 x 4 = 16 equally likely section-pair results.
  5. Use a small outcome table or tree to count events such as 'A then B'. There are two ways for an A-section first and one B-section second, so 2 out of 16 ordered section-pairs meet that event.
  6. An independent second spin does not remember the first. The section counts stay four each time; do not remove a spinner section after it has been selected.
  7. Probabilities describe the model, not a promise that every set of four spins produces exactly two A results. Experimental results can vary.

Two worked models

Model 1: Model A: Equal spinner sectors A1, A2, B, C. P(A) is 2 favorable sectors divided by 4 total sectors, or 12; P(C) = 14.
Model 2: Model B: For two spins, four choices for spin 1 and four for spin 2 make 16 ordered pairs. The event 'first spin C' contains 4 of the 16 pairs, so probability is 14.
A mistake to avoid: Do not count three distinct LETTERS as three equally likely outcomes. Count four equal SECTORS. For two spins, order matters.

Time-Zone Boundary Audit

Goal: calculate longitude differences accurately, explain why eastern places are usually later, and distinguish a solar estimate from official time.

Learn first

  1. Same letter: subtract longitudes. 75°E − 30°E = 45°.
  2. Different letters: add magnitudes. 90°W to 30°E = 120°.
  3. Divide by 15 because Earth turns about 15° each hour. 120 ÷ 15 = 8 hours.
  4. Moving east makes local solar time later; moving west makes it earlier.

Worked model

From 45°W to 15°E: 45 + 15 = 60°. 60 ÷ 15 = 4 hours. The eastern location is about four hours later.

Official zones are choices

The 15° rule is an estimate. Countries bend time-zone boundaries for cities, borders, trade, and government. The International Date Line near 180° changes the calendar date when crossed.

Before independent work

Say aloud: “I subtract same-letter longitudes, add different-letter longitudes, divide by 15, and state east-later or west-earlier.”

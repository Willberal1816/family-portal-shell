Kitchen Safety: Trace the Risk and Fix It

Natalie · Health · Friday, October 30, 2026 · 40 minutes

Today's goal: Trace contamination risks, distinguish clean/separate/chill measures, and plan an age-appropriate snack with specific controls.

Watch the actual demonstration

Exact instructional source: CDC: Always wash your hands before, during, and after preparing food. If playback is blocked, open the original video link and follow the steps shown below. Additional official written safety/recipe reference.

Prepare / parent check: Hand soap, running water, towel, two printable pages, pencil. No food needed for Health.

Learn First — follow in order

  1. Food safety has four key actions: clean hands and surfaces, separate raw foods from foods ready to eat, cook potentially risky foods to safe temperatures, and chill foods that need refrigeration. Our no-heat snack does not involve cooking raw food.
  2. Contamination risk can pass from an unwashed hand or utensil to a ready-to-eat food even when the food looks normal. A clean-looking surface is not a substitute for washing properly.
  3. CDC says wash hands with soap and running water for at least 20 seconds before, during, and after making food. FoodSafety.gov advises refrigeration of perishables promptly; check labels and cold-storage directions.
  4. When building a snack, identify each risk, a measurable safety control, who checks it and when. Use only ready-to-eat items; an adult checks allergies and cold-storage conditions.

Two examples of the finished work and skills

Example A: Someone cracks a raw egg, touches the sink, then handles washed berries. Risk: contamination. Control: separate raw egg area and wash hands and surfaces before fruit preparation.
Example B: Yogurt is left out while a long project happens. Better: store it in the refrigerator until needed, make the portion immediately, and return leftovers promptly.

Read the safety case

Case study for your plan: The group has a sealed chilled container of pasteurized yogurt, a package of ready-to-eat berries, granola, clean spoons and cups, and a sink.

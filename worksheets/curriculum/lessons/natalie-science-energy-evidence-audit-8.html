Energy Evidence Audit: Measure, Convert, Explain

Natalie · Science HOLD replacement · 45 minutes · Learn First
What you are learning: repair four skills from the ramp investigation: name the changed variable and controls, average mixed feet/inches correctly, explain where motion energy goes, and design a fair follow-up that changes a genuinely different variable.

1. Variables and controls

Changed variable

The one factor deliberately changed. In the original test it was release height.

Controls

Factors kept the same so the comparison is fair: same ball, ramp, surface, no-push release, and measuring method.

Worked model: “I changed ramp height. I kept the ball, ramp surface, release method, and floor the same. I measured travel distance.”

2. Average mixed measurements

Convert every measurement to inches first. Add. Divide by the number of trials. Convert back.

Worked model: 5 ft 9 in = 69 in; 5 ft 2 in = 62 in; 4 ft 0 in = 48 in. Average = (69 + 62 + 48) ÷ 3 = 179 ÷ 3 = 59⅔ in = 4 ft 11⅔ in.

3. Energy does not disappear

When the ball stops, its kinetic energy has spread into the surroundings—mostly thermal energy from friction and deformation, plus a little sound. “Into the table” is incomplete because it does not name the energy forms or transfer.

4. Exact evidence and a new variable

Compare averages, not vague ranges: “The high release averaged 59⅔ in; the low release averaged 41 in, so the high release traveled 18⅔ in farther.”

Fair new-variable example: Keep release height fixed. Change only the surface (smooth cardboard versus towel). Keep the same ball, ramp, no-push release, trials, and measuring method.
Ready check: Can you name one changed variable, at least three controls, show an inches-first average, name thermal energy and sound, cite exact averages, and change a different variable?

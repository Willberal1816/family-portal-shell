Packing Teams: Equal Groups and Leftovers

Emma | Math | Friday, November 27, 2026 | 50 minutes

What you are learning: Use multiplication and division to organize equal groups and explain a remainder.

Learn First

An equal-group problem gives the same number to each group. The total is the number of groups times the number in each. A division question can ask how many groups you can fill, or how many items each group gets. Label the numbers before calculating.

A remainder is what is left after making as many complete groups as possible. The answer must make sense: groups times items per group plus leftovers equals the starting total. If a remainder is as large as a whole group, you can make another group. A remainder does not mean you can ignore items.

For an exact money check, multiply cents carefully, keep two decimal places, and count up from the total to the money paid. The short reminder connects to the receipt skill; the rest of the lesson is about equal sharing.

Two worked examples

Example 1: 26 cards, 6 per packet. Four full packets use 4 x 6 = 24 cards, with 2 left. Check: 24 + 2 = 26. To package every card, five packets are needed, but the last is not full.
Example 2: 23 markers shared among 5 tables. Each table gets 4; 3 remain. Check: 5 x 4 + 3 = 23. A money check: 3 stickers at $0.45 cost $1.35. With $2.00, change is $0.65; $1.35 + $0.65 = $2.00.

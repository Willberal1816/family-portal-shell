Write a Clear Summary

Natalie · Language Arts · Learn First
What you are learning: Write an objective summary that captures a text’s main idea and essential supporting points without copying, opinion, or minor-detail overload.

Learn the idea

A summary is a compressed version of a text. It preserves the central idea and most important support while leaving out examples, repetition, and details that are interesting but unnecessary to understand the whole.

Begin by identifying the main idea or central claim. Then group supporting details into larger ideas. If several sentences describe different kinds of storm damage, one summary sentence may combine them.

Use your own wording. Paraphrasing shows understanding instead of copying phrases. Keep the summary objective: unless evaluation is requested, do not add “I think,” praise, criticism, or new information.

Length depends on the source, but someone who did not read the original should still understand the essential point and sequence. If your version includes nearly every detail, you have retold rather than summarized.

Words you will use

  • summary: a shorter account of essential ideas
  • objective: focused on the source rather than personal opinion
  • main idea: the central point
  • supporting detail: information developing the main idea
  • paraphrase: accurately restating in new wording

Worked examples

Combine: several examples of volunteers repairing trails → “Volunteers repaired storm-damaged trails throughout the park.”
Too detailed: every volunteer name, tool, and hour when the main idea is community repair.
Too vague: “Some stuff happened after a storm.” A summary still needs the central idea and key actions.

More worked examples

Teaching example: Should a summary include your opinion?
Answer / reasoning: Not unless evaluation is requested.
Teaching example: What can you do with several details supporting one larger point?
Answer / reasoning: Group them into a broader accurate sentence.
Teaching example: Summary vs retelling?
Answer / reasoning: A summary selects essentials; a retelling often includes many details.

Before the worksheet

Strong work shows: central idea first, essential details grouped logically, accurate paraphrase, no unsupported additions, and a much shorter final version.

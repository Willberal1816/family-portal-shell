From Field Notes to Accurate Sentences

Emma | Language Arts | Tuesday, November 24, 2026 | 45 minutes

What you are learning: Write facts from notes without inventing feelings or motives.

Learn First

Field notes capture what a person saw, counted, timed or heard. A factual report names the event and retains the exact quantities and times shown in the notes.

An inference about someone feeling annoyed is not directly observable. Before writing a public sentence, check every number, pronoun and time against the record.

Two worked examples

Notes 'Tuesday 10am; 8 birds; oak': 'Eight birds were in the oak at 10 a.m. Tuesday.'
Notes 'visitors leave after rain starts' do not prove visitors hated rain; just state the action.

Source and materials

ORIGINAL PARK NOTES: Tue 9:00, twelve visitors at garden. 9:15 sprinkler begins. 9:20 five visitors move to a covered bench. 9:30 seven visitors at garden. No interviews.

Directions: Use the teaching and examples, then answer the six questions below. All six questions also appear on the matching four-page printable PDF. Show work, scan the pages when requested and Finish Class in the School Portal.

All six questions

Question 1. Write two full factual sentences with times and counts.
Question 2. Identify a supported claim and an unsupported claim about motives.
Question 3. Rewrite 'Everybody ran because they hated the sprinkler' as a neutral report.
Question 4. Link the four times in a chronological paragraph.
Question 5. Write a two-sentence public caption including one accurate number.
Question 6. List three facts you checked and one question only an interview could answer.

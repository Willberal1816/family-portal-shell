Pond Life: Follow the Arrows of Energy

Emma · Science · Tuesday, November 17 · 50 minutes

What you are learning: Read a food-chain diagram, distinguish a producer and consumers, and state what the data cannot prove.

Learn First — lesson and method

  1. Most food chains begin with sunlight. Plants and algae use sunlight to make the food molecules their bodies need; they are PRODUCERS.
  2. A consumer gets energy by eating other living things. A snail eating algae is a primary consumer; a fish eating the snail is another consumer.
  3. An arrow points from a food SOURCE to the organism that receives the energy. In 'algae -> snail,' the snail eats algae, NOT the other way around.
  4. Our practice chain is Sun -> algae -> snail -> fish -> heron. The sunlight is an energy input, not a living organism.
  5. If there are fewer snails, the fish MAY have less of one food source, but the chain does not prove exact changes in fish numbers.
  6. A diagram can show a possible feeding relationship without showing exact populations, dates or real measured behavior.
  7. Name exactly which organism eats another when interpreting each arrow; do not turn a possible change into a guaranteed cause.

Two complete teaching models

Model 1: Worked model A: In algae -> snail -> fish, the snail is the eater of algae, and the fish eats the snail. Energy transfers in the arrow direction.
Model 2: Worked model B: If the algae were reduced, snails might have less food, but the diagram cannot establish that ALL snails die or how quickly any change occurs.
Mistake to avoid: An arrow means 'is eaten by' (energy to eater), not 'eats'. A food chain alone is not a population survey.

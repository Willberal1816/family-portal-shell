Two Sources, One Careful Report

Emma · Language Arts · Friday, September 25, 2026 · Learn First

What you are learning: copy exact dates and details, explain each source's purpose, and combine them without adding invented facts. Read these worked examples before opening the separate Independent Work page. There is no written work to complete on this teaching page.

Know what each source can show

A repair log is written to record dates and work. A visitor note can show which entrance people used. Neither automatically proves what every visitor experienced.

Model with a different setting

Source A: “On April 3 a crew replaced two broken lights; the hall reopened April 4.” Source B: “During closure, visitors entered through the side door.” A precise report says: “Two hall lights were replaced April 3 and the hall reopened April 4 (A). During the closure, visitors used the side door (B).” A limitation: neither source counts every visitor.

Choose stronger evidence for the question

For repair date use A because it lists the actual work date. For visitor route use B because that source reports access during the closure. Do not swap the jobs of sources merely because one sounds more detailed.

Write four or five sentences

Include a careful opening claim, two accurate source details, explain the roles, and one real limit. “Visitors liked the change” would not be supported unless a source actually says so. Check every date and word against the source.

Before independent work

Read the museum records on your worksheet; include the required parts and the true limitation.

Ramp Test: What Must Stay the Same?

Emma · Science · Thursday, November 12, 2026 · 50 minutes

What you are learning: Tell the changed variable from the measured outcome and name fair-test controls before using a trial table.

Learn First

  1. A fair test changes ONE thing on purpose and keeps important other conditions the same.
  2. In this example the changed variable is ramp height: 10 cm or 20 cm. The result measured is the toy car's travel distance in CENTIMETERS.
  3. The same car, starting mark, floor surface and release method are controls. No one pushes the car down the ramp.
  4. The listed distances are imaginary practice data; you are reading and analyzing a table, not reporting a real experiment.
  5. A conclusion states what these examples show, in cm, without predicting all possible ramps.

Two worked examples

Worked example A: Low ramp trial distances 42, 44, 43 cm. Mean = (42 + 44 + 43) divided by 3 = 43 cm.
Worked example B: High ramp goes 55, 57, 56 cm. Mean = 56 cm. The sample high ramp averaged 13 cm farther in this ONE setup.
A mistake to avoid: Do not say the results are in feet. Do not invent a trial that is not in the table or claim a push was part of the method.

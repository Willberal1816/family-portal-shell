Two Markets: Compare Complete Receipts

Natalie · Math · Thursday, November 12, 2026 · 50 minutes

What you are learning: Use an item discount and then sales tax to compare two final receipts without skipping a step.

Learn First

  1. A percent discount applies to the marked price. To calculate a 20% discount, multiply the original amount by 0.20, then subtract the discount.
  2. A subtotal is the sum AFTER the item discounts and BEFORE tax. Record this number because tax uses it.
  3. In this invented example both shops charge 5% tax on the discounted subtotal. The tax is the subtotal times 0.05.
  4. Add the tax to the subtotal and compare FINAL receipts, not just the sale labels.
  5. Show a clear mini-receipt with a line for original, savings, subtotal, tax, and final cost.

Two worked examples

Worked model A: A $40 jacket is 20% off: $8 off, so sale price $32. Add a $10 scarf to get $42 subtotal. Tax at 5% is $2.10; final cost is $44.10.
Worked model B: A $30 sweater with 10% off costs $27. Add a $12 hat for a $39 subtotal; 5% tax is $1.95. Final cost = $40.95. This basket is $3.15 less than model A.
A mistake to avoid: Tax is not calculated on the old pre-discount price under the store's stated rules. Do the discount FIRST.

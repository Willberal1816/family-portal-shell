Date Line Flip Cards: West Adds, East Subtracts

Natalie · Geography · Saturday, November 14, 2026 · 50 minutes

What you are learning

Correctly apply International Date Line direction to a simplified before-and-after date card, including a month boundary, without adding unsupported flight-time assumptions.

Learn First — Teaching

  1. The International Date Line is a mostly Pacific Ocean boundary where the CALENDAR date changes when crossing. Its actual path BENDS around some places.
  2. A WESTBOUND crossing moves the calendar FORWARD one date in this simplified no-elapsed-time model. Think: WEST = ADD one calendar day.
  3. An EASTBOUND crossing moves the calendar BACK one date in the same model. Think: EAST = SUBTRACT one calendar day.
  4. Walk through the arrow BEFORE writing the answer: W arrow -> +1 day; E arrow -> -1 day. Check the weekday and the numerical date.
  5. Model: Monday, November 2 WESTBOUND becomes Tuesday, November 3. Model: Thursday, November 5 EASTBOUND becomes Wednesday, November 4.
  6. At a MONTH boundary, advance or rewind into the correct month; for example November 30 westbound becomes December 1.
  7. Real travel can also consume hours and cross civil time zones. This lesson models one instant crossing ONLY; do not make claims about specific airport arrival times.

Two fully worked models

Model 1

Concrete flip-card model A: Front: Mon Nov 2 / arrow WEST. Back: Tue Nov 3. Explain: add one calendar day because the crossing is westward.

Model 2

Concrete flip-card model B: Front: Thu Nov 5 / arrow EAST. Back: Wed Nov 4. Explain: subtract one calendar day because the crossing is eastward.

Important mistake to avoid:

The common reversal is saying westbound goes BACK one day. Check the arrow card FIRST: west adds a day, east subtracts. Do not assume a direct airplane trip takes zero hours in real life.

Unit Rates & Proportions

Natalie · Math · Learn First
What you are learning: Use unit rates, equivalent ratios, tables, and proportional reasoning to compare situations and solve real problems.

Learn the idea

A rate compares quantities with different units, such as 180 miles in 3 hours or $7.50 for 5 pounds. A unit rate tells the amount for exactly one unit. Dividing 180 miles by 3 hours gives 60 miles per hour.

Proportional situations keep the same multiplicative relationship. If 2 notebooks cost $5, then 4 cost $10 because both quantities doubled. A table of equivalent ratios makes the pattern visible.

Unit rates make comparisons fair. A larger package is not automatically a better deal; compare dollars per ounce or ounces per dollar. Use the same orientation for every option.

A proportion says two ratios are equal. Before solving, ask whether the situation is actually proportional. A taxi fare with a fixed starting fee plus a per-mile charge is not simple proportionality because zero miles does not cost zero dollars.

Words you will use

  • rate: comparison of quantities with different units
  • unit rate: a rate per exactly 1 unit
  • ratio: comparison by division
  • proportional: having a constant multiplicative relationship
  • constant of proportionality: the fixed unit rate connecting proportional quantities

Worked examples

Example 1: 12 oz for $3 → $0.25 per ounce.
Example 2: 150 miles in 2.5 hours → 60 mph.
Example 3: y=4x gives pairs (1,4),(2,8),(5,20); y/x stays 4.

More worked examples

Teaching example: A 6-pack costs $4.50. What operation finds dollars per item?
Answer / reasoning: 4.50 ÷ 6.
Teaching example: 3 cups flour for 2 batches: cups per batch?
Answer / reasoning: 1.5 cups per batch.
Teaching example: How can a ratio table be tested for proportionality?
Answer / reasoning: Check whether the unit rate stays constant.

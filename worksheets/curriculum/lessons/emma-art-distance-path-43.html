Art Studio: A Path That Looks Far Away

Emma · Art · Tuesday, November 10, 2026 · 45 minutes

What you will learn: Make an original path drawing with a horizon, one vanishing point, foreground details and accurate converging edges.

Learn First

  1. Perspective makes a flat page look like it has distance. Things farther away usually look smaller.
  2. Draw a HORIZON LINE across the page at eye level. Place one VANISHING POINT — a dot — on that line.
  3. Use a ruler or carefully guided straightedge to connect the two near edges of your path to the single dot. Their edges CONVERGE as they go away.
  4. Draw at least two near objects and two far objects (trees, posts, signs). The far ones should be visibly smaller. Let the view remain creative; you can pick a park trail, a road or a bridge.
  5. Finish the full drawing first. Then mark the vanishing point and horizon, and describe what makes the drawing feel deep. No color or purchased tools are required.

Two fully explained models

Worked drawing model: two wide path edges at the bottom of the page meet at one point on the horizon. A large post near the viewer and a small post near the dot show distance.
Incorrect example: if path edges stay parallel all the way to the horizon, the path looks flat. Repair by directing the edges to the SAME dot.
Check this mistake: The vanishing point is where parallel edges appear to meet in a picture, not where the artist literally measures the end of the path.

Do the actual lesson

Make your own finished artwork, then explain three design choices on the second sheet. Print TWO work pages, use both pages, and scan both Tracking numbers. If a direction is confusing, ask for help before guessing.

Time plan: 10 minutes teaching and demonstration + 30 minutes original drawing + 5 minutes review/scan. Materials: pencil, plain paper or printed sheet, ruler/straightedge, eraser; optional colored pencils.. No purchases necessary.

Art record and self-check

  1. Explain where you placed the vanishing point and how the most important lines converge toward it.
  2. List at least three specific depth cues you drew and explain one correction you made as you worked.
  3. Compare ONE part of your drawing to the demonstration model. Name something original about yours.

Before scanning: Show your original drawing to a parent and submit both pages. The drawing, not the reflection questions, is the main work.

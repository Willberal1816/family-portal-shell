Wetland Survey: Name the Respondents, Bound the Claim

Natalie · Language Arts · Monday, October 26, 2026

What you are learning: Report who answered a survey, describe the dates accurately, and avoid inventing causal explanations.

Learn First

A survey result describes only the people who actually answered unless the evidence supports a larger group. '15 of 24 respondents chose the shuttle' is supported; 'most town residents chose the shuttle' is not, because the survey never counted all residents.

Chronology means the order in time. If the October 12 survey happened before the October 19 survey, state that correctly and mention both dates. A change between two surveys does not prove why it happened; other factors may differ.

Strong factual writing gives a precise number, a named group, a dated source, and a carefully limited conclusion. Replace absolute words such as 'everyone' and 'caused' unless the source actually supports them.

Fully worked example or model

If 14 of 20 visitors who answered a Thursday survey chose the bus, write: 'On Thursday, 14 of 20 surveyed visitors chose the bus.' Do not claim that all visitors made that choice.

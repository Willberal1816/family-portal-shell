Unit Rates — Build, Scale & Test

What you are learning: Find a per-1 rate, write a proportional equation, scale a quantity, and test whether a situation really is proportional.

50-minute plan
  1. Concept model — 10 minutes
  2. Worked examples — 15 minutes
  3. Guided practice with an adult — 10 minutes
  4. Independent transfer page — 15 minutes

1. Name the two quantities

A unit rate tells how much of one quantity there is for exactly 1 of the other quantity. Always include the unit words.

Worked example: production rate

156 parts are made in 12 minutes.

156 ÷ 12 = 13

The unit rate is 13 parts per minute. Check: 13 × 12 = 156.

2. Build the proportional equation

If every value of y is the same constant multiple of x, the relationship is proportional and can be written y = kx. The number k is the unit rate.

Worked example: table to equation
x258
y82032

Compute y ÷ x in every column: 8 ÷ 2 = 4, 20 ÷ 5 = 4, and 32 ÷ 8 = 4. The constant ratio is 4, so y = 4x.

3. Scale by a factor

To resize a recipe or design, multiply each original amount by the same scale factor.

Worked example: recipe scaling

A recipe uses 3.5 cups for 8 servings. For 14 servings, the scale factor is 148 = 1.75.

3.5 × 1.75 = 6.125

You need 6.125 cups. Check reasonableness: 14 servings is less than twice 8, so the answer should be less than 7 cups.

4. Test the origin

A proportional relationship has no starting fee and passes through (0, 0). A fixed starting amount makes it nonproportional.

Worked example: starting fee

A club charges $18 to join plus $7 for each visit. The equation is y = 7x + 18.

At 0 visits, the cost is $18, so the graph begins at (0, 18), not (0, 0). It is not proportional.

5. Equal additions are not enough

Counterexample

The pairs (1, 3), (2, 4), and (3, 5) increase by 1 each time, but the ratios y ÷ x are 3, 2, and about 1.67. The ratio is not constant, so the relationship is not proportional.

Guided practice

  1. Say the requested “per 1” unit before computing: 224 pages in 14 minutes.
  2. For pairs (2, 12), (5, 30), and (7, 42), find the constant ratio and write the equation.
  3. Explain why y = 5x + 12 is not proportional.
  4. Scale 6 cups for 8 people to 18 people using a scale factor.
Before independent work: You should be able to say: “I divide to get the per-1 rate, use it as k in y = kx, and test whether the relationship starts at zero.”

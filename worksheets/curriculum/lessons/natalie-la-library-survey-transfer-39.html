Library Survey Transfer: Respondents, Population, Cause

Natalie · Language Arts · Wednesday, October 28, 2026 · 55 minutes

What you are learning: Use fresh survey evidence to distinguish respondents from a larger population and revise a causal claim without inventing a reason.

Learn First

A survey directly describes only the people who answered. A broader population claim requires a representative sample and evidence that supports that population.

Preference counts do not explain why people chose an option unless the survey asked and recorded reasons.

Fully worked model

If 21 of 35 Tuesday library users choose longer hours, write: “Among the 35 Tuesday library users surveyed, 21 chose longer hours.” Do not claim all residents agree or that work schedules caused the choice.

Source and materials

On Wednesday evening, librarians surveyed 50 people who entered the North Branch. Thirty chose more weekend hours, 12 chose more study tables, and 8 chose more computers. The survey did not ask why. It did not contact residents who did not enter the branch that evening.

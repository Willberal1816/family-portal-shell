Museum Exhibit: Audit Three Claims

Natalie · Language Arts · Saturday, November 14, 2026 · 55 minutes

What you are learning

Write a precise source-based review that separates an organizer's statement, a visitor count and an individual opinion.

Learn First — Teaching

  1. First classify each source by what it can document: a curator label describes an exhibit, a ticket log counts recorded visits, and a comment card reports one person's words.
  2. A claim must stay inside the source's population. A log of tickets sold is not a complete census of everyone who walked nearby.
  3. A dated label can document an announced opening; it does not prove the exhibit was open on every later date.
  4. A single visitor quotation may show a reaction but not what every visitor thought.
  5. To handle two sources together, write one fact from each, then identify a limit neither source resolves.
  6. A counterclaim is an alternative interpretation that fits the same data. It is useful only when you make clear it is possible rather than proven.
  7. Your final paragraph should use evidence labels A, B and C, correct two separate overclaims and avoid the words 'everyone' and 'caused' unless actually supported.

Two fully worked models

Model 1

Worked model: Source A says the exhibit was planned to open November 20. Safe: 'The museum announced a November 20 opening.' That does not certify it opened on time.

Model 2

Worked model: Source B lists 46 tickets on Saturday. Source C says one visitor enjoyed the posters. These support exactly a count of logged tickets and one person's opinion, not universal approval.

Important mistake to avoid:

Do not turn a single comment into a vote or confuse 'ticket sold' with 'every visitor attended and approved.'

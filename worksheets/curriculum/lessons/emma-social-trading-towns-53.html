Why Two Towns Choose to Trade

Emma | Social Studies | Monday, November 23, 2026 | 50 minutes

Your goal: Explain specialization, exchange, benefits and limits of transport evidence.

Learn First

Specialization means focusing on a good you can make or grow. Trade lets two communities exchange their extra goods for things they need. Compare known transport time, but do not invent prices.

Worked models

Pineport has extra wool; Orchard Hill has extra apples. Exchanging extras can benefit both.
Wagon road takes 3 hours and riverboat takes 5 hours: road is 2 hours faster, but prices and safety are unknown.

Check your work: Read the source labels; check calculations, evidence, or drawing guides before turning in.

Source and materials

FICTIONAL TRADING RECORD
Pineport: extra wool cloth; needs apples.
Orchard Hill: extra apples; needs wool cloth.
Wagon road: 3 hours. River route: 5 hours.
Quantities, prices, weather and safety were not recorded.

All six independent-work questions

Question 1. Draw two labeled towns with arrows showing which good travels in each direction.
Question 2. Explain in four sentences why the towns could benefit from specialization and exchange.
Question 3. Create a travel-time comparison table and calculate the difference, with units and a check.
Question 4. A trader says the road is always safer and cheaper. Give two missing facts needed to test the claim.
Question 5. Draft a trade agreement naming both goods and two details each town should confirm before agreeing.
Question 6. Invent a third town and a different product. Clearly label your example fictional and explain benefits, costs and one uncertainty.

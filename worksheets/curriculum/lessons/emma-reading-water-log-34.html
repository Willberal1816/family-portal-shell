Today's story

The Garden Watering Record

On Wednesday morning, Maya and her grandfather opened the neighborhood garden before the volunteers arrived. The garden had three raised beds, each with a wooden sign showing which plants grew there. Maya checked the watering schedule clipped beside the gate.

Overnight wind had blown the schedule into a puddle. The writing for Thursday and Friday was smeared, so the volunteers would not know which beds still needed water. Maya suggested guessing, but her grandfather said that could leave some plants too dry and others overwatered.

They opened a small waterproof notebook kept in the tool shed. Each page recorded the date, the bed number, and whether the bed had been watered. The latest entry showed that Beds 1 and 3 had been watered on Tuesday, but Bed 2 had not. First, Maya checked the notebook against the bed labels. Next, her grandfather copied the entries onto a fresh schedule. Then Maya slipped the schedule into a clear plastic sleeve and clipped it back beside the gate.

When the volunteers arrived, they read the new schedule and watered Bed 2. The other two beds were left alone until their next scheduled watering. Maya put the notebook back in the shed so the next volunteer could check the records instead of guessing.

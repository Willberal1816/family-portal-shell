Independent Reading: What Dialogue Reveals

Emma · Reading · Saturday, November 14, 2026 · 45 minutes

What you are learning

Read your actual book for 25 minutes, then distinguish spoken words from a supported idea about a character.

Read your OWN book for 25 actual minutes first, then do the work.

Learn First — Teaching

  1. Read your own current book for at least 25 ACTIVE minutes before starting the response. Record the true pages and time.
  2. Dialogue is what a character says. Look for quotation marks or spoken exchanges and record the page where they appear.
  3. A quoted line is evidence of what was said; it may not prove what the speaker secretly intended.
  4. To make a supported idea about the character, use the spoken words plus another action or description from nearby pages.
  5. If today's pages have no dialogue, choose one clearly described action and explain why you used it instead.
  6. Your answer should give the page number and exact meaning. Keep 'the character says' separate from 'I think.'
  7. Your final response should describe how the scene changes the reader's understanding, not simply copy one word.

Two fully worked models

Model 1

Invented example: On page 8 Mari says, 'I will return the bag.' Fact: Mari promised to return it. If page 10 says she carried it back, that action supports the idea she was dependable.

Model 2

Invented example: A character says 'Fine' and closes a door. It MAY suggest annoyance, but the exact feeling is an inference unless the book says so.

Important mistake to avoid:

Do not invent a quotation or cite a page outside the pages you recorded as read today.

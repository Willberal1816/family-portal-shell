Goal: use exact setting clues to explain mood and give a complete problem–action–resolution summary. Read your own book for 20–25 minutes, then read this fresh story. Plan 45 minutes total.

Learn First

When a room is quiet and empty, the story can feel tense or uncertain. When voices fill the room and a problem is solved, the mood can feel relieved. Quote two separate details to support each mood claim.

Worked example: “The chairs were empty and the clock ticked” gives two clues for an uneasy mood. A complete summary must also explain how the problem was resolved.

Today's story

The Last Label

At the community art show, Maya helped arrange paintings before the doors opened. The room was nearly silent. Only the wall clock ticked, and the empty chairs faced a row of covered displays.

As Maya checked the exhibit list, she noticed one painting had no label. The list said the painting was called Autumn Harbor and named its artist, but Maya did not want to guess where the missing card had gone. She searched the supply box and found several unused blank cards. Then she asked the organizer to confirm the title and artist before making a replacement.

With the organizer's approval, Maya wrote the information neatly and placed the card beside the painting. Visitors began arriving. Their conversation filled the room, and Maya watched a family stop to read the new label. The organizer thanked her for checking the facts rather than guessing. Maya smiled and moved to welcome the next guests.

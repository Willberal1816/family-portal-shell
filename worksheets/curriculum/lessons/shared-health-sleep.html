Sleep & Recovery

What this lesson teaches: sleep supports learning, memory, mood, growth, and physical recovery. A useful sleep routine protects enough consistent opportunity for rest. Required planning/reflection is on the printable.

Definitions

Sleep routine: a repeatable sequence that helps the body transition toward sleep.

Recovery: processes that help the brain and body restore and prepare for the next day.

Consistency: keeping sleep and wake patterns reasonably regular rather than changing them dramatically every day.

Why sleep matters

During sleep, the brain continues processes involved in memory and learning, while the body supports growth and physical recovery. Inadequate sleep can make concentration, mood, reaction time, and learning harder.

Worked routine example

Finish stimulating tasks
Prepare clothes/books
Quiet screen-free activity
Lights out at a consistent time

A routine reduces last-minute decisions and gives the body repeated cues that the day is winding down.

Realistic change

A useful improvement is specific and sustainable—for example, moving device charging outside the bedroom 30 minutes before bed and reading instead. The goal is not a perfect evening; it is protecting sleep opportunity.

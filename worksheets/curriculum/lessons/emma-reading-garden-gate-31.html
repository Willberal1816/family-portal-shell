The Locked Garden Gate

Emma · Reading · Thursday, October 15, 2026 · Learn First

Goal: continue independent reading and use exact clues to explain a character’s problem, inference, and event order. Read this teaching page before opening the separate Independent Work printable.

Worked model

When a character checks a pocket, retraces steps, and notices fresh mud by a gate, those actions support the inference that a missing key may have fallen near the gate.

Reliable method

  1. Log your own-book title, pages, and minutes.
  2. Answer in the exact numbered format.
  3. Quote or name the smallest clue that proves each answer.
  4. Put sequence words beside the events before writing the final response.

Text for today

Mina reached the garden gate just as the first sprinkler clicked on. The brass key was not in her apron pocket. She set the seed tray on a bench and walked back toward the shed, studying the damp path. Near the gate, one patch of mud held a small bright edge. Mina brushed it clean, unlocked the gate, and moved the seed tray inside before the sprinklers reached it.

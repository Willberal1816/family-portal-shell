The Bridge Letters: Compare Two Narrators

Natalie · Reading · Monday, November 9, 2026 · 45 minutes · Cycle C

Today's objective: Distinguish two viewpoints, quote the specific clue that supports an inference, and make a complete evidence-based summary.

Learn First: read each step

  1. A narrator's viewpoint includes what that person saw and what they THINK happened. Different people may see different details; neither opinion automatically becomes a fact.
  2. For a supported inference, name the direct text clue first. Write 'I infer __ because the text says __.'
  3. An outcome is the last verified event. A character's hope or fear is not a confirmed result. Separate a later event from the original problem.
  4. Refresher from the last review: a verification step must name what was checked, not simply 'they checked.'

See the two fully worked examples

Worked example 1: Tessa writes, 'The red folder was not on the bench.' Direct detail: missing folder. Inference: Tessa did not know its location. Unsupported: 'The custodian stole it.'
Worked example 2: Leo writes, 'We checked the box marked MAPS; the signed folder was inside.' The check, the exact box label and the result together explain why they could identify the folder.
Watch for this common error: Never turn 'I thought the folder was gone' into proof that someone took it. Quote the matching direct detail and verify the end result.

Read TWO fictional notes about one community exhibit:

Tessa’s note, 10:05 a.m.: 'When I opened the exhibit cabinet, the map key was not in its usual clear sleeve. I worried it had been lost. I told Leo and wrote down exactly what was missing.'
Leo’s note, 10:25 a.m.: 'I checked the binder's inventory list and found the map key was labeled "Key B." At 10:15, Tessa and I looked in the drawer marked "Backup Labels"; the envelope marked Key B was there. At 10:20, we placed the map key back in its clear sleeve. At 10:25, we checked that the key matched the map and that visitors could read both. A new group toured the exhibit after lunch.'

Neither note identifies who moved the original key or why it was not in the sleeve.

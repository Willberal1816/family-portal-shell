Independent Reading: Viewpoint and Evidence

Natalie · Reading · Monday, November 9, 2026 · 45-minute class · Cycle C

Today's learning: Read a book for 25 minutes, analyze who tells the story, support an inference with a precise passage, and distinguish a confirmed result from a character's belief.

Learn First — 5 minutes of instruction

  1. Continue a family-approved book. Read for about 25 minutes, including the next chapter if you reach a chapter ending early. Record the actual pages you covered.
  2. Notice whose perspective is given. Is this told by a character saying 'I,' or a narrator describing 'he/she/they'? Consider whether another character knows something the narrator does not.
  3. Evidence includes the exact words or actions in the book, with a page or chapter location. An inference explains what those words suggest; it must not transform a fear, opinion, or suspicion into a verified fact.
  4. After 25 minutes of reading, complete the four written tasks. If your section contains no clear final outcome, report what remains unresolved rather than inventing the ending.

Two modeled reading explanations

Model 1 — perspective: In a sample diary, Tessa writes, 'I thought the map was lost.' The text proves she BELIEVED it was lost, not that the map was gone permanently. Leo later reports finding it in a labeled drawer; his report adds information that Tessa did not have.
Model 2 — checked result: 'At 10:25 they compared the map key with the map and checked that both could be read' describes a VERIFIED action. 'They hoped the next tour would enjoy it' is only a hope. A strong response says what was confirmed and what was not.
Common mistake: A guess about a character is not a fact. Use a page detail to support every inference, and never claim an event happened if the book has not shown it.

Independent Reading — about 25 minutes

Read your own book for about 25 minutes. Read at a comfortable pace; continue into the next chapter when useful. Keep the book handy for page evidence. This reading is the main work of the class, not the short examples above.

Reading response — about 12 minutes

Print the TWO response pages. Complete the FOUR meaningful tasks using the pages you actually read. Include specific details and page locations; quality matters more than the number of answers. Then check the pages, scan both Tracking numbers, and Finish this class (about 3 minutes).

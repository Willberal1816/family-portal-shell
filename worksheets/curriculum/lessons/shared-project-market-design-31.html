Pop-Up Market Studio

Emma and Natalie · Shared Project · Wednesday, October 14, 2026 · 60 minutes

Your challenge: design a small imaginary school market with safe walking paths, build a paper booth model, calculate the money each booth could earn, and test a shopper route. This is a new project, not the unfinished Wildlife Crossing project. Each student completes her own two-page project record.

What to gather

Two sheets of scratch paper, pencils, a ruler or straight edge, and the printed two-page project record for each student. Optional colored pencils. Fold paper to make standing booth signs; no scissors, purchases, food, or Internet research required.

1. Learn to lay out a safe market

One square on the supplied market map is 1 meter by 1 meter. An 8-column by 6-row room has 48 square meters. Keep an unbroken two-square-wide walkway from Entrance to Exit. A booth is a two-square-by-two-square rectangle (4 square meters). You must fit two booths without covering the walkway, the entrances, or each other.

Worked map idea: reserve columns D and E as the two-square walkway from top to bottom. Place a 2×2 booth on the left in columns A–B, rows 2–3, and a second 2×2 booth on the right in columns G–H, rows 4–5. Walk along columns D–E to reach both booths. If a booth blocks column D, a wheelchair or two people passing may not have enough space; redraw before building.

2. Work out profit, not only sales

Use this fictional supply menu. You may choose either item for each booth; all prices are per unit.

ItemCost to make oneSale priceProfit per sale
Handmade bookmark$1.00$3.00$2.00
Seed-packet art card$1.50$4.00$2.50
Sticker sheet$0.75$2.00$1.25
Worked example: a booth sells six bookmarks. Revenue is 6 × $3.00 = $18.00. Cost is 6 × $1.00 = $6.00. Profit is $18.00 − $6.00 = $12.00. Profit is not the same as money collected.

3. Build and test a real paper prototype

  1. 12 minutes — learn and plan: study this page, choose two booth items, label the 8×6 map on the worksheet, and draw your first layout.
  2. 15 minutes — build: fold two scrap-paper booth signs so they can stand up. Write a clear name, product, and price. Position them on your paper floor map.
  3. 12 minutes — shopper test: each sibling traces a path from Entrance to both booths and Exit using a pencil; check for two-square-wide walking space. Make one intentional improvement and draw your revised map.
  4. 15 minutes — money and evidence: calculate revenue, cost, profit for the printed customer scenarios and write a claim supported by the test.
  5. 6 minutes — submit: finish both record pages, sketch or photograph your booth signs on your record, and scan your own pages.

How to work together

Discuss routes and compare prototypes, but each student records her own booth layout, calculations, test findings, and one specific improvement. You may design different markets; do not copy each other's conclusions. This project uses invented prices, so no real money changes hands.

Before you finish: both pages include the map, source price table, trial table, and all writing space. You do not need a second worksheet or an external site.

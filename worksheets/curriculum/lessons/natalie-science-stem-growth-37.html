Stem Measurements: Change, Missing Evidence, Conclusion

Natalie · Science · Monday, October 26, 2026

What you are learning: Compute an exact measured increase, separate missing readings from zero, and write a conclusion bounded by the recorded evidence.

Learn First

Scientists often compare a starting measurement with a later measurement. The change is later reading − earlier reading, with units. If a seedling is 2.1 inches tall on Day 1 and 3.1 inches on Day 5, the recorded increase is 3.1 − 2.1 = 1.0 inch.

A blank observation means not recorded, not zero. You may not invent a missing measurement or claim which plant grew fastest without enough readings. Observed height change does not, by itself, prove that sunlight, water, or one treatment caused the change.

Use accurate language: 'In this record, Plant A increased by 1.0 inch between Day 1 and Day 5.' This is stronger than 'Plants always grow one inch.'

Fully worked example or model

Model check: 0.4 inch + 0.6 inch = 1.0 inch exactly. In a measurement conclusion, include the observed amount and the interval; do not call it an estimate when the recorded numbers are exact.

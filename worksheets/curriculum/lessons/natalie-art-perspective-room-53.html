Draw a Room in One-Point Perspective

Natalie | Art | Monday, November 23, 2026 | 50 minutes

What you are learning: Draw a room using one horizon, vanishing point and correct receding guide lines.

Learn First

One-point perspective makes a flat page show depth. A single vanishing point lies on the horizon line. Lines that go away from you point toward VP; vertical edges remain vertical.

Worked models

Draw a horizontal horizon and a central dot VP; light diagonal floor guides all aim at VP.
Doorframes stay vertical. Only depth lines such as a rug's side edges travel toward VP.

Check your work: Read the source labels; check calculations, evidence, or drawing guides before turning in.

Source and materials

MATERIALS: pencil, ruler, eraser and plain paper.
GUIDE: one horizontal horizon, one vanishing point (VP).
Receding lines aim toward VP; nearby verticals stay vertical.
Finished work: room walls, floor, doorway and one depth object.

Answer all six questions using this teaching and source. Scan all four worksheet pages, then Finish Class in the School Portal. Ask for help when needed.

All six independent-work questions

Question 1. Draw and label a horizon line, VP and four faint guide lines that meet at VP.
Question 2. Draw the floor, side walls and ceiling; identify receding and vertical/horizontal edges.
Question 3. Add a rug with correct near and far edges; explain which lines go toward VP.
Question 4. Draw a bookshelf or table with convincing depth using lightly marked guides.
Question 5. Identify two perspective mistakes in a draft and redraw their corrected edges.
Question 6. Finish the room with at least three object details; write four sentences explaining horizon, VP and corrections.

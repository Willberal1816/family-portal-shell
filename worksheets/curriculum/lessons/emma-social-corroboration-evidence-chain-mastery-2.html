Check Another Source — Evidence Chain

What you are learning: Build a conclusion that says only what the evidence can prove, and choose a genuinely new source that could check the conclusion.

The evidence chain

1. Fact
What exact fact did you find?
→
2. Source
Which source gives that fact?
→
3. What it proves
What can that fact actually support?
Evidence rule: A conclusion may be smaller than your guess, but it may not be bigger than the evidence. If the sources do not prove a detail, leave that detail out or find another source.

Worked example 1 — do not overclaim

Source A: A town notice dated May 4 says the bridge closed after river water rose above the roadway.

Source B: A newspaper photo from May 4 shows water covering the bridge approach.

Safe conclusion: The bridge was closed on May 4 because high water covered the approach.

Too-strong conclusion: “The bridge stayed closed for two weeks.” Neither source gives that length of time, so the evidence cannot prove it.

Worked example 2 — a genuinely new source

Suppose Sources A and B both say a school opened in 1958. To check whether enrollment grew during the next decade, another copy of the same 1958 article is not a new kind of evidence.

A useful new source would be a 1968 school enrollment report. It could show the number of students ten years later.

Worked example 3 — chronology

Source A: A council vote happened March 3.

Source B: Construction began April 12.

Supported order: the vote happened before construction began.

Do not claim the vote caused construction unless the sources actually connect those events.

Before the worksheet

On every question: name the source → name the fact → say exactly what it proves. When asked for another source, choose something genuinely new and state what that new source could show.

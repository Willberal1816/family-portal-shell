Garden Paths: Perimeter and Area

Emma · Math · Wednesday, November 11, 2026 · 50 minutes

Today's goal: Measure the length around a garden and the space inside it. State whether an answer uses feet or square feet.

Learn First

  1. Perimeter is the distance all the way AROUND a shape. Add the side lengths. It is measured in feet (ft).
  2. Area is the space INSIDE a rectangle. Multiply length by width. It is measured in square feet (sq ft).
  3. For a garden measuring 8 ft by 5 ft, the border needs 8+5+8+5 = 26 ft of edging. The planting space is 8 x 5 = 40 sq ft.
  4. A path changes how much space is planted. Subtract the path area from the whole garden area when the question asks for planted area.
  5. You can check an answer by drawing and labeling a rectangle; do not call the perimeter square feet.

See the complete models

Model 1: A 6-ft by 4-ft garden needs 6+4+6+4 = 20 ft of border, but the inside covers 6 x 4 = 24 sq ft.
Model 2: A 10-ft by 6-ft garden has 60 sq ft total. A 2-ft by 6-ft path uses 12 sq ft, leaving 48 sq ft for plants.
A mistake to avoid: If you multiply sides to find the border, you have found area, not distance. Choose the measurement that matches the question.

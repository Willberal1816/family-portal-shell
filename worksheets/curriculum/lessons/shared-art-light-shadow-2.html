Light & Shadow: Make Forms Look 3D

Emma + Natalie · Art · Learn First
What you are learning: Use one clear light direction to place highlights, form shadows, reflected light, and cast shadows on simple forms. The goal is not darker pencil marks; it is believable light.

Start with the light source

Before shading, decide where the light comes from. Imagine an arrow traveling from the light toward the object. Surfaces facing that arrow receive more light. Surfaces turning away receive less.

Worked example: If the light comes from the upper left, the upper-left side of a sphere is brightest. The lower-right side becomes darker. The cast shadow falls mostly away from the light, toward the lower right.

Four parts to look for

  • highlight: the brightest area facing the light
  • form shadow: the darker area where the object turns away from the light
  • reflected light: a small amount of bounced light inside the shadow side; it stays darker than the highlight
  • cast shadow: the shadow the object throws onto another surface

Different forms change value differently

Sphere

Values change gradually because the surface curves in every direction.

Cube

Each flat plane can have a noticeably different value. One face may be light, one middle, one dark.

Cylinder

The curved side usually shifts gradually, while the top ellipse may receive a different amount of light.

Worked visual decisions

Sphere: Light from upper left → highlight upper left, smooth middle values, darker lower right, small reflected-light edge, cast shadow to the right.
Cube: Light from upper left/front → top plane lightest, front plane middle, side plane darkest. Keep the planes distinct instead of blending every edge.
Cylinder: Light from left → bright band on left/front, gradual turn into shadow on the right, narrow reflected light near the far edge.

Two mistakes to avoid

Outlined coloring: A heavy dark outline around every shape makes the drawing flatter. Let value changes describe the form.
Reflected light too bright: Reflected light is bounced light. It can soften the shadow edge, but it should not compete with the direct highlight.
Before independent work: Say the light direction first, then point to where the highlight, darkest form shadow, and cast shadow should go. Emma: use the staged light-direction check before shading. Natalie: pay special attention to keeping reflected light darker than the direct highlight.

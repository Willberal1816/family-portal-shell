Independent Reading: Clues and Inferences

Emma · Reading · Monday, November 9, 2026 · 45-minute class · Cycle C

Today's learning: Read your own book for 25 minutes, identify one real detail and one supported inference, then write a short account of what changed.

Learn First — 5 minutes of instruction

  1. Choose a book you are already reading or a family-approved book you want to continue. If it has short chapters, read the next chapter and continue into another until you have read for about 25 minutes.
  2. Read for about 25 minutes. Keep the book open so you can find the page containing your best evidence. You may read silently or aloud if that helps.
  3. A *direct detail* is something the author says or shows. An *inference* is an idea you can reasonably make from that detail. A good explanation includes the detail, its page, and what it suggests.
  4. After reading, use the two-page response sheet. Refer back to the book rather than guessing. Your answers describe the pages you ACTUALLY read; do not invent a conclusion if your chapter ended before the problem was solved.

Two modeled reading explanations

Model 1 — fact and inference: A sample story says, 'Mia picked up her umbrella before leaving.' The FACT is that Mia picked up an umbrella. A reasonable INFERENCE is that she expected rain; the story has not proved rain actually happened.
Model 2 — accurate summary: A story shows someone noticing a missing display label, checking a binder, replacing the sign, and confirming that a visitor found the exhibit. A supported summary names each of those events. It does not invent who lost the label.
Common mistake: A guess about a character is not a fact. Use a page detail to support every inference, and never claim an event happened if the book has not shown it.

Independent Reading — about 25 minutes

Read your own book for about 25 minutes. Read at a comfortable pace; continue into the next chapter when useful. Keep the book handy for page evidence. This reading is the main work of the class, not the short examples above.

Reading response — about 12 minutes

Print the TWO response pages. Complete the FOUR meaningful tasks using the pages you actually read. Include specific details and page locations; quality matters more than the number of answers. Then check the pages, scan both Tracking numbers, and Finish this class (about 3 minutes).

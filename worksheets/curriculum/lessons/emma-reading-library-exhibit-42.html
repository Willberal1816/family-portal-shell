The Exhibit Sign: Read, Infer, Verify

Emma · Reading · Monday, November 9, 2026 · 45 minutes · Cycle C

Today's objective: Use exact story details to distinguish a clue from an inference and summarize how a problem was solved.

Learn First: read each step

  1. A detail is something the story says directly. An inference is a reasonable idea supported by details; it is not a random guess.
  2. To explain an action, point to the evidence the character actually used. If the story never explains WHY something went wrong, do not invent a cause.
  3. A complete summary names the problem, the checked clue, what the characters did, and the verified result.

See the two fully worked examples

Worked example 1: 'Mira found a blank sign and checked the printed exhibit list.' Fact: sign blank; action: checked the list. Inference: Mira wanted to identify the display. We cannot say someone removed the sign on purpose.
Worked example 2: If a sign is replaced at 11:40 and visitors read it at 11:45, the later reading is a confirmed result. A delivery that arrived at 3 p.m. belongs to another part of the day.
Watch for this common error: A later visit or delivery does not prove who caused the original problem. Make each claim match what the story states.

From Interview Notes to a Fair Feature Story

Natalie | Language Arts | Wednesday, November 25, 2026 | 55 minutes

What you are learning: Write an attributed feature article using exact quotations, accurate paraphrases, and bounded claims.

Learn First

A direct quotation uses the speaker's exact words, quotation marks and a name. A paraphrase keeps the meaning but changes the wording; name the speaker and do not add quotation marks. A posted record is different from a speaker reporting a fact, and an observer describes what they personally recorded.

A feature article has a lead introducing the event, a middle with sourced details, and an ending that stays within the evidence. One person's experience or preference cannot stand for everyone. Check whether a number counts signups, attendees, or respondents.

Before publishing, underline direct quotations and compare them word for word with the notes. Label each report by its speaker or observer. Use “reported,” “said,” or “the posted schedule shows” precisely.

Two worked examples

Practice interview: Sam said, “The workshop began at noon.” Direct quote: Sam said, “The workshop began at noon.” Paraphrase: Sam reported that the workshop started at midday.
Practice posted register: 12 registrations; 9 check-ins. A fair sentence is “The register lists 12 registrations and nine check-ins.” It cannot prove that all 12 participated.

Source and materials

FICTIONAL COMMUNITY GARDEN NOTES
Jordan: "Eight signed up; six attended Saturday."
Ari: "We planted four raised beds."
Maya: "I want to learn how the beds will be watered."
Observer log: event lasted two hours.
No full survey, cost figures or later harvest data.

Directions: Read Learn First and both worked examples. Complete all six questions. The same questions and source appear in the four-page native printable. Scan every page, then Finish Class in the School Portal. Ask for help when needed.

All six questions

Question 1. Create a source table for Jordan, Ari, Maya and the observer. Include one exact quoted detail for each speaker (with quotation marks), the observer's recorded fact, and one limitation for each source.
Question 2. Write a two-sentence article lead with a verified detail and correct source attribution. Keep signups and actual attendees separate.
Question 3. Draft a 110-140 word feature story with a title, two exact attributed quotes, one attributed paraphrase and the observer's recorded duration. Use clear paragraphs.
Question 4. Audit the claim “Eight residents planted four beds in two hours.” Identify what the notes establish and what they do not establish. Rewrite it with accurate attribution.
Question 5. Create a three-row revision log: original phrase, source, corrected phrase. Include one exact-quote check and one signups/attendees check.
Question 6. Write your final 110-140 word revised article. End with two short sentences: one verified claim and one important unknown. Check both quotations against the notes.

Cup Cooling: Measure, Graph, Conclude

Emma · Science · Monday, October 26, 2026

What you are learning: Compare measured temperature changes in °F, graph the correct quantity, and make only conclusions the data supports.

Learn First

A measurement tells what was observed, not automatically why it happened. To find a temperature drop, subtract ending temperature from starting temperature. A cup going from 95°F to 81°F dropped 14°F because 95 − 81 = 14.

When a graph is titled Temperature drop after 30 minutes, each bar should show the DROP, not the final temperature. Give a bar the height of its number on the axis. A fair test also needs other conditions controlled before we can claim a material CAUSED the difference.

All the values below are invented practice measurements. They can support which cup cooled more in this table, but cannot prove the same result always occurs.

Fully worked example or model

Practice model: Cup D starts at 95°F and ends at 81°F. Drop = 14°F. A bar for Cup D on a drop graph must reach 14, not 81.

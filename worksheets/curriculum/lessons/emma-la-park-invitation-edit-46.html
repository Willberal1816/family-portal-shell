Park Invitation: Four Complete, Factual Sentences

Emma | Language Arts | Friday, November 13, 2026 | 55 minutes

What you are learning

Turn a short notice into FOUR separate complete sentences, with a clear subject and verb in each and no invented causes.

Learn First: follow the reasoning

  1. Start with the fact box below. An invitation must tell people WHAT is happening, WHEN it is happening, WHERE it happens and WHAT to bring.
  2. A complete statement tells WHO or WHAT performs an action. 'The volunteer club meets Saturday' includes a subject (club) and verb (meets).
  3. A fragment is a piece that cannot stand by itself as a statement. 'At the creek shelter on Saturday' needs an actor and action.
  4. An instruction such as 'Bring a notebook' can be a complete imperative sentence: the understood subject is YOU and the verb is BRING.
  5. Use one fact per sentence to prevent a long run-on. Give one sentence about the event, one about time, one about place and one about what to bring.
  6. Check the notice before writing a reason. The notice says no cause for the event, so a sentence about rain, cancellation or low attendance would be a guess.
  7. To revise, read EACH numbered sentence aloud, underline its subject once, its verb twice, and check end punctuation.

Two fully worked models

Model 1

Worked model A: Fragment 'At Riverside Park on Saturday.' Repaired: 'The cleanup team meets at Riverside Park on Saturday.' Subject = team; verb = meets.

Model 2

Worked model B: Notice says 'Bring a reusable water bottle.' Correct invitation line: 'Bring a reusable water bottle.' The understood subject is you and the verb is bring.

A mistake to avoid

Writing all required facts in one sentence does not meet an instruction to write FOUR separate sentences. Count real sentence boundaries.

Evidence Proof — Stay Inside the Sources

What you are learning

A source can prove only what its actual words, numbers, pictures, or records show. Your job is to separate proved facts from possible ideas that are not proved yet.

Use the four-step proof ladder

1. Claim: What fact are you trying to decide?

2. Point: Name the source and point to the exact detail.

3. Prove: Say only what that detail lets you conclude.

4. Unknown: Name what the source still does not tell you.

One full worked example

Source A — pool log: “3:08 p.m. — thunder heard. Pool cleared.”
Source B — weather station: “3:05–3:15 p.m. — lightning detected within 7 miles.”
Source C — visitor message: “We had to leave the water just after 3:00.”

Claim: The pool was cleared because storm danger was nearby.

Point: Source A records thunder and the pool clearing at 3:08. Source B records nearby lightning during the same time.

Prove: Together, A and B support that storm danger was present when the pool was cleared.

Unknown: These sources do not prove which staff member made the decision or the exact rule that person followed.

How to reject an unsupported claim

Suppose someone says, “A broken pump forced the pool to close.”

No source mentions a pump. That idea might be possible in another situation, but it is not supported here. A safe statement is: “The pool was cleared after thunder was heard, and nearby lightning was detected.”

How to choose a genuinely new source

If the missing question is, “Who ordered the pool to close?” Sources A–C do not answer it.

A useful new source could be the lifeguard incident report. It could identify who made the closure decision and what reason was recorded.

Naming Source A again would not be a new source.

Before you write

For every conclusion: name the source → point to the exact detail → state only what it proves → keep unknown details out. If a question asks for a new source, name a record, person, photo, object, or document that is not already in the source set, and say exactly what missing fact it could add.

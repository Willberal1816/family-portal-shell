Science: Compare Two Test Records

Emma · Science · Saturday November 21, 2026 · 50 minutes

What you are learning: Find fair-test variables, averages and evidence limits in practice data.

Learn First

Changed variable = material; measured outcome = absorbed teaspoons; mean = sum divided by trials.

Two worked models

Model 1: FICTIONAL paper towel trials 5, 6, 7 teaspoons; mean (5 + 6 + 7) / 3 = 6 teaspoons.
Model 2: FICTIONAL cotton cloth trials 8, 9, 10; mean 9 teaspoons; difference 3 teaspoons. This proves nothing about all cloths.
Check for this mistake: Practice numbers are fictional; do not describe them as real experiments.

Source and facts

  • PRACTICE data: paper towel 5, 6, 7 teaspoons; cotton cloth 8, 9, 10.
  • Both samples 10 cm by 10 cm, same cup, same water source, same 30 seconds.
  • Data were invented for calculation practice. You do not need to run a water experiment.

Read the models, then complete your six questions using your source. Write only what the source supports.

All six independent-work questions

These exact questions are visible on screen and appear in the matching four-page PDF. Write on paper, scan all pages and finish this class in the School Portal.

Question 1. Identify the changed variable, measured outcome with unit, and three controls from the facts.

Question 2. Find the mean for paper towels, showing addition, division and a multiplication check.

Question 3. Find the mean for cotton and the difference between means with full units.

Question 4. Fix the claim 'Nine teaspoons is the independent variable,' accurately identifying variable and outcome.

Question 5. Plan but DO NOT perform a future three-trial test with equal samples, timer, measurement and spill safety.

Question 6. Write six sentences using both means, difference, controls and why fictional figures cannot prove a real result.

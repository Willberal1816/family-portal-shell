Trail Elevations: Read a Height Profile

Emma · Geography · Monday, November 9, 2026 · 45 minutes · Cycle C

Today's objective: Read a real diagram of a fictional trail's elevations, compare heights and distinguish elevation from trail distance.

Learn First: read each step

  1. Elevation tells how high a place is above a reference level. On this practice chart we use feet for height.
  2. Horizontal distance along the bottom tells how far you have WALKED; vertical height is elevation. A longer walking distance is not necessarily a higher elevation.
  3. Gain means new elevation minus old elevation when the trail rises. If it falls, subtract to find how many feet it descends. Read exact data points, not a guessed slope.

See the two fully worked examples

Worked example 1: 50 feet at the trail start and 80 feet at a viewpoint: gained 80−50=30 feet of elevation. This does NOT mean walked 30 feet.
Worked example 2: 90 feet at a ridge and 60 feet at a lake: descended 90−60=30 feet. If the trail is 0.5 miles between them, that is horizontal walking distance, not 30 miles.
Watch for this common error: Do not confuse the chart's mile labels along the bottom with its feet labels up the side. A downhill section can still cover a long distance.

Build Numerical Expressions

Emma · Math · Learn First
What you are learning: Turn multi-step situations and word directions into numerical expressions, using parentheses to show which calculation must happen as one group.

Read the actions in order

A numerical expression records a calculation without needing an equals sign or variable. The challenge is deciding which quantities belong together before another operation happens.

Worked example 1: “Add 8 and 7, then multiply the result by 2.” First the sum 8 + 7 must be treated as one quantity, so write 2 × (8 + 7). The parentheses preserve the story’s order.
Worked example 2: “A box has 6 rows of 4 markers, then 5 extra markers are added.” The rows make 6 × 4, then add 5: (6 × 4) + 5.
Worked example 3: “Take 30 stickers, subtract 6 damaged stickers, then split the rest equally among 4 students.” The subtraction must happen before the division: (30 − 6) ÷ 4.

Common mistake: Writing 30 − 6 ÷ 4 for “subtract 6, then divide the remainder by 4.” Without parentheses, the expression tells a different calculation. Group the quantity that must be completed first.
Check yourself: Read your expression back in words. If the expression tells the same sequence as the situation, the structure is probably correct.

Reading: What Does This Paragraph Do?

Emma | Reading | Wednesday, November 25, 2026 | 45 minutes

What you are learning: Identify two paragraphs' functions and cite exact book pages.

Learn First

Read your OWN book independently for 25 minutes. Paragraphs can provide setting, action, dialogue or explanation. Explain what the paragraph does, not what you guess the writer intended. Recheck one quotation briefly: no repeated full-block page-order remedial lesson.

Two worked examples

A paragraph naming rooms and furniture builds setting, supported by actual room details.
A paragraph dominated by speech may develop dialogue; quote and cite the words instead of inventing feelings.

Source and materials

USE YOUR OWN CURRENT BOOK.
Read for 25 minutes; record title, author, start/end pages.
Choose two real paragraphs; cite exact pages.
Nonfiction paragraphs may explain ideas or provide evidence.

Directions: Answer all six questions using the teaching and source. The same six questions appear on the matching four-page printable PDF. Write legibly, scan every page and Finish Class in the School Portal. Ask for help if needed.

All six questions

Question 1. Record book title, author and page range; summarize today's reading in two sentences.
Question 2. Find one paragraph and page; identify its function and quote a short exact clue.
Question 3. Choose a different paragraph and page; identify its function with a precise detail.
Question 4. Build a two-row table: paragraph page, function and supporting phrase.
Question 5. Verify one quoted phrase and page against the original; explain how you checked it.
Question 6. Write a five-sentence note about both paragraphs and one unanswered question.

Reading: What Does This Paragraph Do?

Emma | Reading | Wednesday, November 25, 2026 | 45 minutes

What you are learning: Identify what two paragraphs do and support each with an exact page and short clue.

Learn First

Read your current book for 25 minutes. If you finished the last book, start your next real book. Record its title, author, first page and exact last page (not just “end”). Then select two short paragraphs from what you actually read.

A paragraph can introduce a setting, describe an action, show dialogue or explain information. Ask: What is this paragraph mainly doing? Choose one function and copy a short exact clue, with the page number. The clue must be on the page you name.

Use this four-part sentence frame: On page __, the paragraph mainly __. The words “__” support that choice because __. Do not guess why a character acted unless the book states or supports that reason.

Two worked examples

Practice passage (made-up, page 12): “The shed smelled of pine. Two dusty lanterns hung from the rafters.” Function: setting. The phrase “two dusty lanterns” describes the place, not a character's feelings.
Practice passage (made-up, page 13): “Mia lifted the latch and stepped inside.” Function: action. The phrase “lifted the latch” names an action. We cannot claim why Mia entered without another clue.

Source and materials

USE YOUR OWN CURRENT BOOK. Read 25 minutes.
Record exact title, author, first page and last page.
Choose two real paragraphs from today's pages.
Copy short clues exactly and cite their actual page numbers.

Directions: Read Learn First and both worked examples. Complete all six questions. The same questions and source appear in the four-page native printable. Scan every page, then Finish Class in the School Portal. Ask for help when needed.

All six questions

Question 1. Record your book title, author, first and exact last page read today. Write one complete sentence about the main event or idea.
Question 2. Choose one short paragraph. Name its page and function (setting, action, dialogue or explanation), and copy a short exact clue.
Question 3. Choose a different paragraph. Name its page and function, and copy a short exact clue.
Question 4. Make a two-row table: page | paragraph function | exact clue. Explain in one sentence how the two paragraphs do different jobs, or why they do the same job.
Question 5. Open the book again. Check one quotation letter for letter and its page number. Record exactly what you checked or corrected.
Question 6. Write two complete sentences explaining what the two paragraphs help you understand. Write one question the pages do not answer; do not invent an answer.

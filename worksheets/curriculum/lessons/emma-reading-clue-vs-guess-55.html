Reading: Put the Events in Order

Emma | Reading | Tuesday, November 24, 2026 | 35 minutes

What you are learning: Use sequence words to explain what happens first, next, and last without inventing a cause.

Learn First

A story has an order: something happens, then people respond, then another event follows. Sequence words like first, next, after, and finally help a reader follow the change. A sequence is not the same as a claim about why a character acted.

Read your current book for about 25 minutes. Identify actions and events that the text directly states. Record the pages you actually read. This parallel reading skill does not require deciding a character's hidden feelings or motive.

Two worked examples

Model 1: 'The gate opened; the hikers walked in; the sun set.' First the gate opened, next the hikers entered, finally it became evening.
Model 2: 'Tara took a key and closed a box.' The actions are in that order, but there is no evidence about why she closed the box. Keep guesses out of a sequence summary.

Source and materials

ORIGINAL PRACTICE PASSAGE: 'Nia set a blue notebook on the table. The clock rang. Nia put the notebook into her bag and walked to the door. A gust of wind scattered a few papers.' Also read your current book for 25 minutes.

Directions: Read your current book for about 25 minutes, then answer the six questions below. All six questions also appear on the matching four-page printable PDF. Show work, scan the pages when requested and Finish Class in the School Portal.

All six questions

Question 1. List four events from the practice passage in the order they happened.
Question 2. Write a complete sentence beginning 'First,' and a second beginning 'Next,' using facts from the passage.
Question 3. Which happened earlier: the clock ringing or Nia walking to the door? Quote supporting text.
Question 4. After 25 minutes reading your current book, record the title and actual pages read.
Question 5. Choose three events directly stated in today's book pages. List them as first, next and last with page references.
Question 6. Write a short sequence summary of today's reading, then underline an unsupported reason you deliberately left out.

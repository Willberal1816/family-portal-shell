Reading: A Clue Is Not a Guess

Emma | Reading | Tuesday, November 24, 2026 | 35 minutes

What you are learning: Use quoted text evidence to support an inference.

Learn First

An inference is a possible meaning supported by clues, not a directly stated fact. Identify the clue before explaining your idea. Another idea may also fit.

Read your current book independently for about 25 minutes. Use a genuine phrase and page number from today's pages, never a made-up quotation.

Two worked examples

'Ari checked the empty bowl twice' suggests Ari expected food, but does not prove hunger.
'Sam smiled as the door opened' might mean relief or excitement; another clue would help distinguish them.

Source and materials

ORIGINAL EXCERPT: 'Nia folded the note under the blue book. When the door creaked, she glanced toward the hall and closed the drawer quietly.' Also read your current book.

Directions: Read your current book for about 25 minutes, then answer the six questions below. All six questions also appear on the matching four-page printable PDF. Show work, scan the pages when requested and Finish Class in the School Portal.

All six questions

Question 1. Quote one action directly stated in the original excerpt.
Question 2. Give an inference about Nia and the precise supporting clue.
Question 3. Provide another explanation and what evidence would distinguish it.
Question 4. Record book title and actual pages read during 25 minutes.
Question 5. Quote a short action from today's real book pages with page number; explain one inference.
Question 6. State one direct fact and one open question that needs more evidence.

The Signal Card: Read Between the Details

Natalie · Reading · Thursday, October 22, 2026 · 45 minutes

Goal: Use two exact setting details to explain mood, infer a decision, and summarize the complete ending.

One class: Read this Learn First lesson, work through both examples, complete all six items on TWO printable pages, scan both pages, and Finish Class. Learn First and Printable are not separate assignments.

Learn First

Read your own book for 25 minutes, then read the story. Mood is the feeling created by setting details. An inference combines an exact detail with reasonable reasoning. Sequence words (first, next, finally) help show what changed. A summary must include the ending.

Two worked models

Model 1. 'The pier was empty, and the bell echoed in the fog' creates an uncertain mood because the empty space and poor visibility make the next event hard to predict.
Model 2. A supported inference: 'She checked the posted code because guessing a signal could confuse the boats.' The text must give evidence for the reason.
Check for mistakes: Use the requested details, not unrelated facts from the scene. Do not invent what a character thinks unless the text supports it.

The Signal Card

Just before sunset, Rowan arrived at the harbor museum to help prepare a small signal demonstration. Fog softened the shoreline, and the empty dock creaked whenever the wind pushed against the pilings. A bell sounded far across the water.

Inside the signal shed, Rowan found two lanterns and a faded instruction card. One lantern flashed steadily, but the other blinked in a different pattern. The museum guide had asked for the demonstration pattern used to signal 'all clear,' not a warning.

Rowan almost copied the brighter lantern. Instead, she compared the flashes with the card, which listed two short flashes for 'all clear' and three short flashes for 'caution.' She tested each lantern, identified the two-flash pattern, and labeled the correct lantern with a paper tag.

When the guide arrived, Rowan explained the difference and showed the card. Together they demonstrated the correct signal from the safe indoor exhibit window. Visitors could see how checking written instructions prevented a confusing message.

Learn the idea

A context clue is a nearby word or event that helps reveal an unfamiliar word’s meaning. Instead of only guessing, point to the wording that helps you. To explain a story, tell the problem, the character’s action, and the actual result. A prediction must stay possible, not turn into a fact that the passage never says.

Read your own book for 25 minutes, then use this new short story. Your final answer must contain exactly five complete sentences with details copied accurately.

Words that help

  • context clue: nearby text that helps a word make sense
  • evidence: the actual phrase or event that supports an answer
  • prediction: a possible future event, not something already stated as fact

Worked example 1

Example: “The pup was timid; it stayed behind the chair when strangers entered.” The phrase “stayed behind the chair” supports “timid means shy or wary.” Give both the meaning AND the clue.

Worked example 2

If a character notices a wet-floor sign and walks around, the reason is the warning and the action is walking around. Do not rewrite this as “the floor was dry” or invent that the character fell.

Use this reference while you work

Original short story: Leah set a seed tray beside the window. Two labels were smudged, so she almost placed the basil with the beans. She stopped when she noticed the basil pot had tiny oval leaves, while the bean sprouts had two broader leaves. Her brother brought the planting note, which said basil needed the smaller tray. Leah checked the note against the leaves before moving the pot. By morning the tray sat in the right place, though she still needed to watch how much water the small plants received.

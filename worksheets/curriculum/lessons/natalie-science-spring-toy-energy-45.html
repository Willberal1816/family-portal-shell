Spring Toy: Follow the Energy

Natalie · Science · Thursday, November 12, 2026 · 50 minutes

What you are learning: Describe potential-to-kinetic-to-sound-and-thermal energy transfers using a defined physical example and measurable outcome.

Learn First

  1. A compressed spring stores elastic potential energy. When released, the spring moves and makes a toy car travel: moving objects have kinetic energy.
  2. Energy changes form or moves between objects; the motion energy can become sound (a click) and thermal energy from friction.
  3. A complete path names the stored source, the movement, and at least one destination besides the main movement.
  4. To test a change, name the ONE changed variable (spring compression) and a measured outcome (distance in cm), keeping the car and surface fixed.
  5. A measured distance is evidence about movement, but it does not directly measure the amount of sound or heat. Be precise about which observation supports each claim.

Two worked examples

Model A: Press spring -> stored elastic potential energy. Release -> car rolls (kinetic energy). Wheel friction warms parts slightly (thermal) and impact makes sound.
Model B: With the SAME toy and floor, a more compressed spring produces an average 88 cm versus 62 cm in this invented example. The measured outcome is DISTANCE in cm; not 'more energy' as a unit.
A mistake to avoid: Don't stop the path at 'potential becomes kinetic'. Account for sound and thermal transfers, and do not call centimeters a unit of energy.

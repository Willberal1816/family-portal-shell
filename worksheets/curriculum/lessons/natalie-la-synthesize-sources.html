Synthesize Two Sources

Goal: Combine compatible evidence from two sources without blurring their purposes or overstating what either proves.

Learn First

A synthesis is not two separate summaries. It makes one clear claim, selects relevant evidence from both sources, explains how the evidence works together, and preserves limits.

Model: Source A, a city report, records that shade trees lowered measured surface temperatures. Source B, resident interviews, describes which shaded spaces people actually used. A synthesis can claim that trees affected both measured heat and daily use of public space. The report does not prove how every resident felt, and the interviews do not measure temperature.
Structure: Claim → evidence from A → evidence from B → connection → limitation.
Use neutral wording. Prefer project records for exact dates, measurements for quantities, and interviews for experiences.

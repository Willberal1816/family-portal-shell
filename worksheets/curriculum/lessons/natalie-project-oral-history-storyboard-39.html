Oral-History Storyboard: Source, Sequence, Verification

Natalie · Project · Wednesday, October 28, 2026 · 60 minutes

What you are learning: Plan a six-panel oral-history story that separates a person's memory from verified facts and includes a concrete verification plan.

Learn First

Oral history preserves a person's remembered experience. Attribute memory claims to the speaker; do not present every detail as independently verified fact.

A strong storyboard has a beginning, turning point, and outcome, plus source labels and a plan to check dates, places, or objects.

Fully worked model

Memory card: “Grandpa remembers the power going out during a winter storm.” Panel label: “According to Grandpa...” Verification plan: check a dated family photo or newspaper archive later. Do not invent a date.

Source and materials

Materials: six index-card-sized paper rectangles or one sheet divided into six panels, pencil, and one household object or family photo you may safely observe. No interview is required tonight. Build a plan for a future 5-minute interview with an adult family member; do not include private information.

Build a Paper Tower: Strong Enough to Stand

Emma · Project · Tuesday, November 17 · 60 minutes

What you are learning: Build a real paper tower, measure its height, test one standard load for one minute, improve a weak point, and explain a real observation.

Learn First — lesson and method

  1. The finished work is a REAL standing tower made only of paper and tape, supporting an unopened food can on a small paper platform. Look at the diagram and demonstration FIRST.
  2. The video from Science Buddies shows a paper-and-tape tower design challenge; watch the entire embedded demonstration before you build. Source: Science Buddies, Tallest Tower.
  3. Materials for BOTH girls together: twelve sheets ordinary printer paper (six each), one roll ordinary tape, ruler, scissors if needed, pencil, stopwatch/phone timer and one safe unopened 14–16-oz can from the pantry. A parent should help place the can.
  4. A tall thin tower may bend or tip. Widen the BASE and roll or fold paper into supports, then add a flat platform at the top for the can to rest on.
  5. Before building, sketch two designs in the worksheet's diagram boxes. Choose one. Use no more than six sheets for your own tower. Keep the tower free-standing on a clear table.
  6. Test the tower by carefully setting the unopened can on top for sixty seconds WITHOUT touching or holding the tower. If it falls, record that result, do not invent success.
  7. Change one specific design feature—add a triangle brace, widen the base or replace a flimsy column—then retest with the SAME can and record height and one observation. Clean up afterward.

Two complete teaching models

Model 1: Finished-model example: a wide three-legged base, rolled-paper columns and a flat paper top. The can rests on the top platform, not taped to it. This is a model, not a claim of a real completed tower.
Model 2: Engineering evidence example: 'Trial 1 collapsed after 8 seconds at 22 cm. I added a diagonal brace. Trial 2 held the can for 60 seconds at 21 cm.' These are MADE-UP numbers; use your own actual results.
Mistake to avoid: Do not fake successful testing, use a glass jar, or place a heavy can on a dangerously high structure. Have an adult supervise the load placement.

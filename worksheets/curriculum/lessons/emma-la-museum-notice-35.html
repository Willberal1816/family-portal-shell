Museum Notice: Make Each Sentence Clear

Emma · Language Arts · Thursday, October 22, 2026 · 50 minutes

Goal: Repair fragments, run-ons, and missing punctuation while preserving exact meaning.

One class: Read this Learn First lesson, work through both examples, complete all six items on TWO printable pages, scan both pages, and Finish Class. Learn First and Printable are not separate assignments.

Learn First

A complete sentence names who or what and says what happened. A fragment is missing a complete thought. A run-on joins complete sentences without proper punctuation. To fix a run-on, make two sentences or join them with a comma and a word such as and or but. Capitalize sentence beginnings and proper names.

Two worked models

Model 1. Run-on: 'The museum opened the guide was late.' Repair: 'The museum opened, but the guide was late.' Both ideas remain.
Model 2. Fragment: 'Because the hall was closed.' Repair: 'The tour moved upstairs because the hall was closed.' Now the thought is complete.
Check for mistakes: Do not change the facts when fixing grammar. A period separates complete thoughts; a comma alone does not repair a run-on.

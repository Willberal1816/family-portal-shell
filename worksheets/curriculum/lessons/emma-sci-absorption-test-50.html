A Fair Test: Which Cloth Holds More?

Emma · Science · Wednesday November 18 · 50 minutes

What you are learning: Name changed variable, measured outcome and controls, then correctly calculate trial means in familiar units.

Learn First

  1. A fair test deliberately changes ONE feature, here cloth TYPE.
  2. The measured outcome is 'water absorbed in teaspoons,' not any single reading such as 6 tsp.
  3. Both squares are 6 inches wide, use equal offered water and wait 30 seconds. Those are CONTROLS.
  4. A teaspoon (tsp) is a U.S. volume unit; all data in this worksheet are invented.
  5. Cotton mean = (6 + 8 + 7) ÷ 3 = 7 tsp. Microfiber mean = (9 + 8 + 10) ÷ 3 = 9 tsp.
  6. The fictional mean difference is 2 teaspoons. That result alone cannot prove what all actual towels would do.

Worked models

Model 1. Cotton: 6 + 8 + 7 = 21; 21 ÷ 3 = 7 tsp. 'Cloth type' is the changed variable.
Model 2. Microfiber: 9 + 8 + 10 = 27; 27 ÷ 3 = 9 tsp; means differ by 2 tsp in imaginary data.
Common mistake to avoid: A particular result (10 teaspoons) is NOT the name of the measured variable; cloth size is a control.

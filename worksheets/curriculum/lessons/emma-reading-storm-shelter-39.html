Storm Shelter Notice: Evidence, Actions, Outcome

Emma · Reading · Wednesday, October 28, 2026 · 45 minutes

What you are learning: Separate a warning from the actions it caused and identify the immediate observed outcome without adding an unsupported cause.

Learn First

A sequence summary names the problem, the evidence check, the actions in order, and the observed result.

Do not replace an observed outcome with a later event or an explanation the passage never gives.

Fully worked model

Warning at 2:10; staff checked radar at 2:12; students moved at 2:16; everyone was accounted for at 2:22. The last fact is the immediate outcome.

Source and materials

At 2:10 p.m., the office received a severe-weather alert. At 2:12, the principal checked the county radar and called teachers. At 2:16, classes moved to the interior hallway. At 2:22, attendance checks showed every student was accounted for. Rain began at 2:25. The after-school game was canceled later that afternoon.

Guided practice: immediate outcome versus later event

Model 2 (separate practice story): At 9:05 the librarian noticed a leak; at 9:08 staff moved books; at 9:15 they confirmed the shelves were dry. The museum reopened the following week. A complete evidence-only summary names the problem (leak), action (moved books), and immediate result (shelves checked dry at 9:15). Next week's reopening is not the immediate result. Model 3: “The leak was caused by a broken pipe” cannot be added unless the account says how the leak started.

Common mistake: Leaving out the evidence check, skipping a middle action, or claiming why something happened when the passage only records what happened. Underline the time beside each action, then write one sentence for problem, one for evidence, one for response, and one for the observed outcome.

Try it before printing: Retell the library example using only facts given above. Do not substitute the later reopening for the 9:15 shelf check.

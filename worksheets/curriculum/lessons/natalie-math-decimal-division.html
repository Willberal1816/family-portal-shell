Decimal Division

Natalie · Math · Learn First
What you are learning: Divide decimals using place value, equivalent quotients, and estimation instead of moving decimal points without meaning.

Learn the idea

Decimal division is ordinary division with place value attached. Dividing by 0.4 asks how many four-tenths-sized groups fit, while dividing by 4 asks how many groups of four fit. Different group sizes must produce different quotients.

To make a decimal divisor easier, multiply both dividend and divisor by the same power of ten. This creates an equivalent quotient because both quantities are scaled equally. For 7.2 ÷ 0.6, multiply both by 10: 72 ÷ 6 = 12.

If the divisor is already a whole number, divide normally and keep place value visible. For 12.6 ÷ 3, estimate 12 ÷ 3 = 4, so an exact answer a little above 4 is reasonable.

Estimate before calculating. Compatible numbers tell you the approximate size. An answer such as 0.042 or 420 should look suspicious if the estimate was about 4. In applications, label money, distance, mass, or unit rates.

Words you will use

  • dividend: quantity being divided
  • divisor: size or number of groups
  • equivalent quotient: a division expression scaled equally in dividend and divisor
  • compatible numbers: nearby numbers easy to divide mentally

Worked examples

Example 1: 8.4 ÷ 0.7 → 84 ÷ 7 = 12.
Example 2: 15.75 ÷ 3 = 5.25; estimate 15 ÷ 3 = 5.
Example 3: $18.90 shared among 6 people gives $3.15 each.

More worked examples

Teaching example: Why can 7.2 ÷ 0.6 become 72 ÷ 6?
Answer / reasoning: Both numbers were multiplied by 10, preserving the quotient.
Teaching example: Estimate 24.8 ÷ 4.9.
Answer / reasoning: About 25 ÷ 5 = 5.
Teaching example: Should 6 ÷ 0.5 be greater or less than 6?
Answer / reasoning: Greater; the exact quotient is 12.

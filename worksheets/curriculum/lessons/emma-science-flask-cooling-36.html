Learn First

To compare cooling, subtract the final temperature from the starting temperature. A smaller drop means more heat was retained during the same observation window. Compare objects fairly: same start temperature, water amount, measurement times, and thermometer. Temperature data can show which cooled more in THIS example but cannot prove a cause beyond measured conditions.

Two worked examples

Worked example 1. An insulated cup changes from 90°F to 78°F: drop = 90 − 78 = 12°F. An open cup changes from 90°F to 70°F: drop = 90 − 70 = 20°F. The insulated cup drops 8°F less in this model.
Worked example 2. If two cups start at different temperatures, comparing only their final readings is unfair. First specify what is changed (the lid or covering), what is measured (drop in °F), and what must remain constant (water amount and timing).
Common mistake to avoid: Do not say a cup gained heat when its temperature fell. A smaller temperature drop is evidence of less cooling, not proof of the exact mechanism. All numerical measurements below are supplied classroom data, not instructions to handle hot water.

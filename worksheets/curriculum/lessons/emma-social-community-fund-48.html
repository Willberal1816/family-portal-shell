Community Fund: Make a Fair, Supported Choice

Emma · Social Studies · Monday, November 16, 2026 · 50 minutes

What you are learning

Read a fictional city-budget notice, compare project costs and write a reasoned decision without inventing who voted.

Learn First

  1. A city budget is a plan for how limited money might be used. A cost list tells the OPTIONS and prices, not which choice was approved.
  2. This fictional neighborhood group has exactly $60 to spend on park supplies; no other money is shown.
  3. To decide what fits, add the exact listed costs. For example, benches $35 plus signs $15 equals $50 and leaves $10.
  4. Two proposals may each fit the budget but serve different needs. Describe the benefits you can reasonably infer from the item names, and say what people would need to check.
  5. An option over $60 cannot be purchased under the stated budget unless some item changes or more funding is documented.
  6. Do not claim 'the committee already voted' because the notice says only that a vote is planned.
  7. A careful civic recommendation gives the exact amount, what is left, one tradeoff, and one question for residents before a decision.

Two worked models

Model 1: Model A: $35 bench + $15 direction signs = $50. Budget remains $60-$50=$10. This is an affordable plan, not proof a vote already occurred.
Model 2: Model B: Bench $35 + garden plants $20 + signs $15 = $70, which is $10 over budget. You must remove or replace an item, not silently change the budget.
A mistake to avoid: A planning notice is not a vote tally. Do not confuse the $60 total budget with the price of a single item.

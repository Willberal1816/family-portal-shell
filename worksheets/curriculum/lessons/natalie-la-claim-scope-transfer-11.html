A claim is smaller than a guess

Imagine a class tested a watering plan for one week. A reliable claim is “During the measured week, the new plan used less water.” “This will save water forever” is an overclaim because only one week was measured.

A complete worked model

Source A: Monday, 18 cups used for 6 plants. Source B: Tuesday after the new plan, 12 cups used for the same 6 plants. Supported: Tuesday used 6 fewer cups than Monday for the recorded group. Reasoning: 18 − 12 = 6 using matched plant counts. Limit: two days alone cannot prove the plan caused all the difference or will work the same way in every season.

Source strength and relevance

A measurement log supports exact quantities. A participant interview can describe experience but not independently prove an exact meter reading. A poster advertising the event does not measure water use. When writing a claim, choose the source that actually records the fact.

The five-sentence response

Sentence 1: careful claim. Sentence 2: exact evidence. Sentence 3: how the evidence supports the claim. Sentence 4: a weaker source and why. Sentence 5: a real limitation (time, cause, access, or scope). Do not replace a limitation with “so far it is good.”

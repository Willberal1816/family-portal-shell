Reading: Words That Help You Picture a Place

Emma | Reading | Friday, November 27, 2026 | 45 minutes

What you are learning: Read your own book for 25 minutes and identify concrete descriptions that help you picture a scene.

Learn First

A description helps the reader picture a person, place or thing. A concrete detail tells what someone could see, hear or touch in the text. A reader may imagine extra details, but those are not automatically facts from the book.

When copying a clue, use a short exact phrase and its actual book page number. A book page number is not the worksheet page number. It is okay to say that the text does not describe something.

Read your current book for 25 minutes. Choose a short part from today’s pages that contains descriptions. If your book is nonfiction, choose a description of an object or place. Do not invent an example from your book.

Two worked examples

Model passage: "A brass bell hung beside the narrow blue door." Sight details: brass bell and blue door. A reader may picture a red wall, but the sentence does not give the wall color.
Model citation: if the sentence is on page 12, the entry is page 12 | "narrow blue door" | describes the door. A second phrase from a different page must carry that real page number.

Independent work

Question 1. Write book title, author, exact first and last page and reading minutes. Name the scene or object you chose.
Question 2. Copy one short description of a real place, person or object and give the exact book page. Tell what the words describe.
Question 3. Copy a different description from your reading, name its exact page, and tell what sense it helps you use (sight, sound or touch).
Question 4. Complete the two-row detail table using the same two phrases from Questions 2 and 3. Check one phrase against the actual book before writing it.

Question 5. Draw a small sketch of the scene or object based on the two descriptions. Label the details with their book pages. Mark any extra imagined detail as imagined or leave it out.
Question 6. Write two complete sentences about what these details help you picture, and one sentence about a detail the book does not describe.

Build a Water Filter: Observe and Improve

Emma | Project | Tuesday, November 24, 2026 | 70 minutes

What you are learning: Build, test and revise a working model filter with visible evidence.

Learn First

FINISHED BUILD: an upside-down pre-cut bottle top acts as a funnel above a collecting cup. Cloth covers the neck and filter media fill the funnel. Muddy water is poured in and collected below. This is NOT drinkable water.

An adult cuts the bottle. Build filter A, pour a measured sample, observe the result, then change one layer for filter B and retest using the same sample volume. Describe only what you see, never claim the water is safe.

Two worked examples

Cloth plus sand may trap visible specks but cannot guarantee removal of microbes.
Adding a paper-towel layer may trap more dirt and also retain more water. Record both effects.

Source and materials

NASA/JPL BUILD: one adult-pre-cut bottle, cloth or gauze, rubber band, optional sand/gravel and paper towel or coffee filter, 2 cups, spoon, muddy test water, towel. NEVER DRINK.

Directions: Use the teaching and examples, then answer the six questions below. All six questions also appear on the matching four-page printable PDF. Show work, scan the pages when requested and Finish Class in the School Portal. Adult prepares cut bottle. Never drink test water.

All six questions

Question 1. Sketch your finished filter with labeled flow direction and layers.
Question 2. List your actual materials and layer order; explain adult cutting safety.
Question 3. Predict what will happen to visible particles in half a cup of test water.
Question 4. Build and test filter A; record amount poured, time and observations.
Question 5. Change one layer for filter B with the same water amount and describe results.
Question 6. Sketch or photograph both filters and explain one improvement and why neither sample is drinkable.

Independent Reading: A Character's Words and Actions

Emma · Reading · Tuesday, November 17 · 45 minutes

What you are learning: Read for 25 active minutes, cite one action and one line of dialogue, and compare what the character says with what the character does.

Read your own real book for 25 ACTIVE minutes before starting the worksheet.

Learn First — lesson and method

  1. Read your own book for at least 25 ACTIVE minutes BEFORE you write. Record real starting/ending pages and minutes.
  2. A character's words are dialogue. Actions are the things the character actually does; they can reinforce or complicate the words.
  3. A fact is what the text says or shows; a motive is a possible reason. Unless the narrator gives the motive, treat it as an inference.
  4. Find one spoken line and one distinct action from the pages you truly read. Record both page numbers accurately.
  5. Explain whether the words and actions agree or disagree. Use two complete sentences, not just 'yes' or 'no'.
  6. If the pages contain no spoken dialogue, use a thought or stated intention and label it honestly.
  7. A strong ending names what you still cannot know without more text rather than guessing.

Two complete teaching models

Model 1: Made-up model: On page 4 Jo says 'I want to help'; on page 7 Jo carries books for a friend. The action supports the spoken intention.
Model 2: Made-up model: On page 10 Max says 'I'm fine' but walks away crying. His words and actions conflict; his exact reason remains unknown.
Mistake to avoid: Do not invent dialogue or a page number; an inference about a character's hidden feelings is not the same as a directly stated fact.

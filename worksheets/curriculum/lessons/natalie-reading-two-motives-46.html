Independent Reading: Test Two Possible Motives

Natalie | Reading | Friday, November 13, 2026 | 45 minutes

What you are learning

Read for 25 actual minutes and evaluate two possible explanations using exact evidence from the pages read today.

START WITH 25 REAL MINUTES of your own book reading, not portal time. Then fill in the actual written response sheets.

Learn First: follow the reasoning

  1. Continue your own book (Caraval if still current) and read for twenty-five ACTUAL active minutes BEFORE filling this paper.
  2. Log complete title, author and the precise FIRST and LAST page read. Evidence for this class must come from TODAY'S logged pages.
  3. A character's action can have more than one possible motive. A motive is WHY a person acts; the narrator may not reveal it.
  4. Choose a real decision in today's pages. List two different possible reasons for that decision rather than assuming only one.
  5. For each reason cite a precise word, action or line and its page. A page number outside today's logged range is not evidence from today's reading.
  6. Use careful words: 'The narrator states...' for directly given fact and 'I infer...' for a supported possibility. Keep the difference visible.
  7. Decide what additional event or statement could distinguish the two motives. You may conclude that the book doesn't provide enough evidence yet.

Two fully worked models

Model 1

Invented model A: Page 21 says Rowan hid a map. Possibility 1: he wanted to protect it; possibility 2: he was afraid someone would steal it. The hiding is a fact; both motives are possible until the book provides clues.

Model 2

Invented model B: Page 23 says Rowan whispered 'Don't tell anyone.' That clue could support a secrecy motive, but it still cannot prove the whole reason without more context.

A mistake to avoid

A page 102 citation does not support an assignment when you reported reading pages 107–121 that day. Check every page number against the range before submitting.

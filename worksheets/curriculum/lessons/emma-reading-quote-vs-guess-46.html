Independent Reading: What the Book Says, What I Think

Emma | Reading | Friday, November 13, 2026 | 45 minutes

What you are learning

Read for 25 actual minutes, correctly identify title AND author, then distinguish stated events from supported inferences.

START WITH 25 REAL MINUTES of your own book reading, not portal time. Then fill in the actual written response sheets.

Learn First: follow the reasoning

  1. FIRST read YOUR current book for twenty-five ACTUAL active minutes. Record the first and last page and only the minutes you really read.
  2. Copy the FULL title and the author's name from the book cover or title page. Do not guess who wrote it.
  3. A stated fact is an event or detail the author explicitly supplies. An inference is your best explanation based on that detail.
  4. Choose a short important passage. It is okay to copy a few words, then write the page number beside them. Make sure your handwriting is legible.
  5. A strong inference includes a fact and a reason. Example: 'The door is locked (fact, page 12), so I think the character may need help (inference).'
  6. If the pages do not tell why a character acted, write 'The book does not say why.' A guess is not automatically a fact.
  7. To finish, check that every cited page is inside the page range you recorded today. If no clear inference appears, say so and explain what you would look for next.

Two fully worked models

Model 1

Model A with an invented book: 'Tina carried a flashlight into the shed' appears on page 8. Fact = she carried a flashlight. Inference = she expected it to be dark, but the page must not be quoted as saying that.

Model 2

Model B: A child waves at someone in the hall on page 10. It may be a friend, but until the author confirms the relationship, call that a possible inference.

A mistake to avoid

Do not copy the book title and omit the author again. Do not fill in 25 minutes unless you actually read for those minutes.

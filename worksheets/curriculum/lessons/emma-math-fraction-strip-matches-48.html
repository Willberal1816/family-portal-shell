Fraction Strips: Same Whole, Different Pieces

Emma · Math · Monday, November 16, 2026 · 50 minutes

What you are learning

Build, compare, and explain halves, fourths and eighths using the SAME whole, then add compatible parts.

Learn First

  1. Every fraction names equal parts of ONE same-size whole. A numerator is the number of selected pieces; the denominator tells how many equal pieces make the whole.
  2. To compare two fractions, draw two rectangles the SAME length and split each into the number of equal pieces named by the denominator.
  3. One half covers exactly as much as two fourths and four eighths when the WHOLE is the same size: 12 = 24 = 48.
  4. Three fourths is six eighths; five eighths is smaller than six eighths, so 34 is greater than 58.
  5. To add fourths, add the selected fourths without adding the denominator: 14 + 24 = 34.
  6. To add a half and a fourth, first rewrite the half as two fourths: 12 + 14 = 24 + 14 = 34.
  7. A fraction picture is a mathematical claim: label each whole, show equal partitions, shade the numerator and explain in words what the shading proves.

Two worked models

Model 1: Model A: Two equal paper bars. Split the first into 2 equal parts and shade 1; split the second into 4 equal parts and shade 2. Both show exactly half the same bar.
Model 2: Model B: To compare 34 with 58, change 34 to 68 on the same-size strip. Six shaded eighths exceed five shaded eighths by one eighth.
A mistake to avoid: Do NOT compare drawings with different whole lengths or add the denominators. 14 + 24 is 34, not 38.

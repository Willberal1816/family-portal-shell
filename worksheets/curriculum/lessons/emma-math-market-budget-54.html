Market Day: Audit a Receipt and Prove Change

Emma | Math | Wednesday, November 25, 2026 | 50 minutes

What you are learning: Separate an estimate from an exact receipt total and prove the change with two different operations.

Learn First

A receipt has four different numbers: unit price (one item), item subtotal (quantity times price), total due (all subtotals plus one order fee), and change (cash minus total). Keep dollars and cents in columns with decimal points lined up. Estimate before calculating, but never use an estimate as the amount paid.

Use a money number line to check subtraction: count up from total due to the next whole dollar, then to the cash amount. The two jumps together equal the change. Check by adding total due + change = cash. A fee charged once is not multiplied by the item count.

Before finishing a receipt, check each multiplication, sum the exact subtotals and fee, then independently check the change. If the estimate is close but not equal to the exact total, that is normal. Write both with labels.

Two worked examples

Practice shop: 2 cards at $1.35 cost $2.70. 1 envelope at $0.80 costs $0.80. Add one $0.50 fee: $2.70 + $0.80 + $0.50 = $4.00. An estimate using rounded prices is about $4; it is a check, not a replacement for the exact calculation.
Practice ticket: total due $7.65 and cash $10.00. Count up $0.35 to $8.00, then $2.00 to $10.00. Change = $2.35. Reverse check: $7.65 + $2.35 = $10.00.

Source and materials

FICTIONAL MARKET STALL
Bookmark $1.45; postcard $2.30; magnet $3.65; sticker sheet $0.85.
One packing fee of $0.60 per whole order. No tax.
Cash limit $20.00 for each order.

Directions: Read Learn First and both worked examples. Complete all six questions. The same questions and source appear in the four-page native printable. Scan every page, then Finish Class in the School Portal. Ask for help when needed.

All six questions

Question 1. Audit this unfinished receipt: 3 bookmarks, 2 postcards and 1 magnet. Write each quantity, unit price and exact item subtotal; add the packing fee and find the exact total due.
Question 2. A helper rounds the item subtotals and calls the resulting estimate the exact amount. Write a reasonable estimate for Question 1, label it, and explain why the exact receipt must use cents.
Question 3. For the Question 1 receipt, the shopper pays $20.00. Draw a money number line or list two count-up jumps from the exact total to $20.00. State the change and prove it with addition.
Question 4. A different receipt lists 2 magnets and 4 sticker sheets, plus a packing fee of $3.60. Identify the fee mistake, correct the total and explain the exact difference.
Question 5. Create two different three-item-type orders under $20.00, each with at least one magnet. Make a short comparison table of exact totals including the fee and select the one that leaves more cash.
Question 6. Make a complete receipt for 2 postcards, 1 magnet and 3 sticker sheets paid with $15.00. Include the fee, exact total, cash, change, and two different checks: recompute the total and add change back to the total.

Market Day: Find Unit Costs and Change

Emma | Math | Wednesday, November 25, 2026 | 50 minutes

What you are learning: Calculate item totals and exact change from a limited budget.

Learn First

A unit price is the cost for ONE item. Multiply the quantity by unit price to get each item subtotal, then add subtotals. A whole-order fee is charged just ONCE. Check any change by adding it to the total cost.

Two worked examples

3 notebooks at $2 each cost $6; 2 pencils at $1 cost $2; item total is $8.
One $1 whole-order fee makes $9; from $15, change is $6. Check $9 + $6 = $15.

Source and materials

FICTIONAL MARKET LIST
Pencil $1; notebook $2; folder $3; eraser $2.
ONE whole-order packing fee of $2.
Budget $24; no tax or discounts.
Count only purchased items.

Directions: Answer all six questions using the teaching and source. The same six questions appear on the matching four-page printable PDF. Write legibly, scan every page and Finish Class in the School Portal. Ask for help if needed.

All six questions

Question 1. Make a table with quantity, unit price and subtotal for 3 notebooks, 2 pencils and 1 folder.
Question 2. Include the $2 once-only fee. Calculate money left from $24 and reverse-check the subtraction.
Question 3. A helper charges the fee for EACH of six items. Explain the error and calculate the overcharge.
Question 4. Compare Order A (2 folders, 3 erasers) and B (4 notebooks, 1 pencil), including the fee. Which is cheaper?
Question 5. Design your own order with three item types for at most $24 including the fee. Show equations.
Question 6. Create a detailed receipt for your order and explain one trade-off and two math checks.

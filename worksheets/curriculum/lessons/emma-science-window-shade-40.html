Window-Shade Test: Compare the Evidence

Emma · Science · Thursday, October 29, 2026 · 40 minutes

What you will be able to do: Compare starting and ending temperatures, distinguish measured change from proof of cause, and plan a fair repeated test.

Learn First — follow these three steps

  1. A measured change is the final value minus the starting value. If 27°C becomes 23°C, the temperature decreased by 4°C.
  2. A fair comparison holds conditions constant except for the variable being tested. If two containers are in different rooms, room conditions could be a second cause.
  3. One measurement under each condition supports only what happened during those observations. Repeating the trial makes the comparison stronger, but still requires precise wording.

See the exact worked examples

Worked example A: Covered box starts 30°C and ends 24°C: decrease 6°C. Uncovered box starts 30°C and ends 27°C: decrease 3°C. Their observed decreases differ by 3°C.
Worked example B: Good conclusion: 'In these two observations, the covered box decreased 3°C more than the uncovered box.' Unsupported: 'Shades always reduce temperature everywhere.'
Check this common mistake: Common mistake: saying 'a 6°C temperature' rather than 'a 6°C decrease.' Always name the measurement and the direction.

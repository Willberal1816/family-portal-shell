Reading a Nutrition Label

What you are learning: read serving size first, then use the label values that answer the specific comparison question. Required label reading and calculations are on the printable.

Learn the idea

A nutrition label gives numbers for one serving. Start with the serving size before reading the other numbers, because every listed amount is tied to that serving. Then use the number that actually answers the question you are trying to solve.

Words you will use

Serving size: the amount used as the basis for the numbers on the label.

Servings per container: how many labeled servings are in the package.

% Daily Value: a reference percentage that can help show whether one serving is relatively low or high in a listed nutrient; it is not a personalized prescription.

Sample label for teaching
Serving size1 cup
Servings per container2
Calories180
Fiber5 g
Protein7 g
Sodium240 mg

Worked example — whole package

If the package contains 2 servings and the entire package is eaten, the listed per-serving amounts are doubled. In the sample, 180 calories per serving becomes 360 calories for the whole package, and 5 g fiber becomes 10 g.

Worked comparison

If the question is “Which snack has more fiber per serving?”, first make sure the serving sizes are understood, then compare the fiber grams. If the question is about sodium, compare sodium instead. The relevant number depends on the question.

What a label cannot decide alone

A label can report measured nutrients and serving information. It cannot by itself decide whether a food fits someone’s entire day, preferences, allergies, medical needs, budget, or overall meal pattern.

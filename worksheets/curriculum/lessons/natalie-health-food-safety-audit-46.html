Food-Safety Detective: Find the Hidden Risks

Natalie | Health | Friday, November 13, 2026 | 50 minutes

What you are learning

Audit a fictional food station, separate clean and raw-item zones, and justify every corrective step with evidence.

Learn First: follow the reasoning

  1. Today's task is a PAPER safety audit plus a real handwashing demonstration, NOT a raw-meat cooking experiment.
  2. CDC's four home food-safety principles are clean, separate, cook and chill. This class emphasizes CLEAN and SEPARATE because you will not cook anything.
  3. Clean: wash hands with soap under running water, scrubbing all surfaces at least 20 seconds, rinse and dry using a clean towel.
  4. Separate: items that touch raw animal foods should not touch ready-to-eat food or clean utensils unless appropriate washing has occurred. Today's examples are fictional and you will handle NO raw meat.
  5. Evidence matters: 'That board was used for raw chicken' is a concrete risk; 'it is probably safe' is a guess unless the cleaning steps are known.
  6. Correct an unsafe station in order: stop task, move contaminated items apart, clean/wash appropriately with adult supervision, wash hands when necessary, place clean tools in a clean zone.
  7. A useful safety audit names the exact hazard, contact pathway, practical correction, and the evidence you would check before saying the station is safe.

Two fully worked models

Model 1

Worked example A: A fictional red board held raw chicken and then sliced fruit without washing. Contact from the raw-food board to the ready-to-eat fruit is the risk. Use a separate clean board and wash tools correctly.

Model 2

Worked example B: Someone washed 20 seconds then handled a dirty trash-bin lid and continued plating. The second contact re-contaminated their hands; wash again before touching clean tools.

A mistake to avoid

Do not claim you personally tested an unsafe raw-food procedure. Paper scenarios are hypothetical; your only physical activity is a safe real handwash.

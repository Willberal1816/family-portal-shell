Study Station: Observe, Adjust, Recheck

Emma | Health | Friday, November 27, 2026 | 50 minutes

What you are learning: Separate observations from changes you need; schedule a 45-minute break and compare evidence fairly.

Learn First

An observation is something you can see or record, such as a lamp glare or the time. A need is a proposed change. A fair recheck repeats the same measure after one change, in comparable conditions. A comfort rating is a personal report, not a diagnosis.

A break plan has a start, duration and end. Add 45 minutes to a work start by counting to the next hour and adding the remaining minutes. Use a short comfortable movement or rest break; tell an adult if anything hurts.

Worked examples

At 10:20 start work; 40 minutes reach 11:00, 5 more reach 11:05. Break begins 11:05. A 5-minute break ends 11:10.
Case: glare on page, comfort 2 of 5. Change only lamp direction, keep task and rating scale the same. New rating 4 of 5. Supported: this person reported improved comfort in that trial, not that all people will improve.

All six questions

Question 1. Write three actual observations from Before. Write a separate possible improvement for each without calling it an observation.
Question 2. Find the first break time after 45 minutes starting 10:35. Show the time steps and a five-minute break end.
Question 3. Explain why the trial changes only the lamp. Name two things kept the same and the measure compared.
Question 4. Compare comfort scores and explain what can and cannot be concluded from this single trial.
Question 5. With adult permission, observe your study area without changing furniture or electrical equipment yourself. Record two real observations, one safe proposed change and how you would recheck it.
Question 6. Make a plan for a different work start time of 1:25. Show the 45-minute break time, a safe break idea, and a cautious conclusion you could make only after measuring.

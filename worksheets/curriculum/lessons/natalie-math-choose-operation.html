Choose the Operation

Natalie · Math · Learn First
What you are learning: Choose operations in rational-number problems by understanding relationships and units instead of relying on keyword tricks.

Learn the idea

Word problems are not vocabulary quizzes. Words such as “each,” “left,” or “more” can occur with different operations. Start by identifying quantities, units, and the relationship the problem describes.

Addition and subtraction combine or compare quantities with the same kind of unit. Multiplication often scales a quantity or creates a total from equal groups. Division can find group size, number of groups, or a unit rate.

Fractions and decimals make answer-size predictions useful. Multiplying by a positive number less than 1 usually shrinks a quantity; dividing by one usually produces a larger quotient. Estimation can expose a wrong operation before arithmetic is finished.

Multi-step problems need a plan. Write what each intermediate result means. If 18.6 ÷ 3 = 6.2, label it “6.2 miles per section” or the context’s actual unit. Units are clues to the next step.

Words you will use

  • operation: a mathematical action
  • unit rate: a rate for exactly one unit
  • scale: multiply a quantity by a factor
  • intermediate result: a meaningful result used in a later step

Worked examples

Example 1: 34 cup per batch × 2 12 batches uses multiplication to scale.
Example 2: 5 14 miles split into 34-mile sections uses division to find number of groups.
Example 3: $24.60 for 6 identical items uses division to find dollars per item.

More worked examples

Teaching example: 0.6 liter per pitcher for 4 pitchers: which operation?
Answer / reasoning: Multiply.
Teaching example: 4 12 yards cut into 34-yard pieces: which operation?
Answer / reasoning: Divide.
Teaching example: Why is “more” not always an addition clue?
Answer / reasoning: The relationship and units matter more than one word.

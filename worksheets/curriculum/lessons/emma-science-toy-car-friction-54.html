Science: A Fair Friction Comparison

Emma | Science | Wednesday, November 25, 2026 | 50 minutes

What you are learning: Use recorded trial distances, fair-test controls and evidence limits.

Learn First

Friction opposes motion between contacting surfaces. A fair comparison changes ONE condition (the surface) while keeping car, ramp, starting line and release method fixed. Calculate mean by adding trial distances and dividing by trial count. No real experiment is required.

Two worked examples

Cardboard trials 28, 30 and 32 inches have mean 30 inches.
Carpet trials 16, 18 and 20 inches have mean 18 inches, so their mean differs by 12 inches.

Source and materials

FICTIONAL TOY-CAR TEST; DISTANCES IN INCHES
Same car, same ramp and release position.
Cardboard: 28, 30, 32 inches.
Low-pile carpet: 16, 18, 20 inches.
Three trials per surface; no speed measurements.

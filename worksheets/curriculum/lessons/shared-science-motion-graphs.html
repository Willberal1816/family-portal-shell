Reading Motion Graphs

What you are learning: read a distance-versus-time graph by connecting the shape of the line to how motion changes. The printable contains all required graphing and written work.

Learn the idea

A distance-versus-time graph tells a story of motion. Read it from left to right as time passes. Then watch what happens to distance: a rising line means the object is getting farther from the start, a flat line means the distance is not changing, and a steeper rise means more distance is covered in the same amount of time.

Words you will use

Horizontal axis (x-axis): usually shows time in a motion graph.

Vertical axis (y-axis): usually shows distance from the starting point.

Steeper line: distance changes more during the same amount of time, so average speed is greater.

Flat line: distance from the start is not changing during that time interval.

Worked graph

The first rising segment shows the object moving away from the start. The flat middle segment shows a stop because time passes while distance stays the same. The final rising segment shows movement again. Because that last segment rises more gradually than the first, it represents slower motion.

Axes and units

A graph is incomplete without labels and units. “Time (minutes)” and “Distance (meters)” tell the reader exactly what the numbers mean. A line without labeled axes cannot be interpreted reliably.

Graph versus map

A map tells where places or positions are located. A distance-time graph tells how distance changes over time. The same trip can be represented by both, but they answer different questions.

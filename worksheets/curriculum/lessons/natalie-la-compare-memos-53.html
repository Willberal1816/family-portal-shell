Compare Two Proposals Without Overclaiming

Natalie | Language Arts | Monday, November 23, 2026 | 55 minutes

Your goal: Compare observations, proposed actions and missing evidence fairly.

Learn First

A memo may report an observation or propose a change. Attribute claims precisely: 'Memo A recommends' and 'Memo B observes.' A suggestion is not evidence that a plan works.

Worked models

Memo A recommends opening early: it does not prove early opening would reduce the crowd.
Both memos mention a busy library after lunch. Neither provides actual counts or trial results.

Check your work: Read the source labels; check calculations, evidence, or drawing guides before turning in.

Source and materials

FICTIONAL MEMO A (student council): The library is crowded
just after lunch. Open 15 minutes early and ask for feedback.
FICTIONAL MEMO B (staff): Visits bunch near the door.
Try a two-week entry schedule and record actual counts.
No visitor counts, staffing costs or trial results provided.

Answer all six questions using this teaching and source. Scan all four worksheet pages, then Finish Class in the School Portal. Ask for help when needed.

All six independent-work questions

Question 1. Chart each memo's observation, proposal and follow-up evidence request.
Question 2. Explain one agreement and one difference between the proposals in three attributed sentences.
Question 3. Classify: 'the door is busy'; 'open early'; 'the change saves money' as observation, proposal or unsupported.
Question 4. Write a neutral 6-8 sentence comparison paragraph using at least three attribution phrases.
Question 5. Identify three facts or measurements needed before choosing a proposal, explaining why each matters.
Question 6. Write a fair two-week trial recommendation with evidence to collect, a success test and one known uncertainty.

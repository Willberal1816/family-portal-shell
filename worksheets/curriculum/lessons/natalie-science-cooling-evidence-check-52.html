Science: Cooling Data With Fair Controls

Natalie · Science · Saturday November 21, 2026 · 50 minutes

What you are learning: Compute temperature drops and compare controlled practice results.

Learn First

Temperature drop in degrees Fahrenheit = start minus end. Equal comparison time matters.

Two worked models

Model 1: FICTIONAL Cup A: (72 - 66), (74 - 67), (70 - 65) = drops 6, 7, 5; mean 6 degrees F.
Model 2: FICTIONAL Cup B drops 4, 4, 4; mean 4 degrees F. Difference 2 degrees F, not measured energy flow.
Check for this mistake: Never equate a Fahrenheit reading with a direct measure of heat energy.

Source and facts

  • PRACTICE 10-minute Cup A start/end F: (72,66), (74,67), (70,65).
  • Cup B: (72,68), (74,70), (70,66). Cup B wrapped.
  • Same water volume, cup size, room and wait time. No real hot-water test required.

Read the models, then complete your six questions using your source. Write only what the source supports.

All six independent-work questions

These exact questions are visible on screen and appear in the matching four-page PDF. Write on paper, scan all pages and finish this class in the School Portal.

Question 1. Compute all three Cup A drops with subtraction and degrees F.

Question 2. Compute Cup B drops and organize all six drops in a two-column trial table.

Question 3. Calculate both mean drops and compare with a labeled difference.

Question 4. Name changed variable, measured outcome and four controlled conditions; explain why wait time must match.

Question 5. Fix 'Cup B lost two fewer degrees of heat energy' by stating what the thermometer actually measured.

Question 6. Write a six-sentence memo naming fictional status, both means, difference, possible cause, confounder and limit.

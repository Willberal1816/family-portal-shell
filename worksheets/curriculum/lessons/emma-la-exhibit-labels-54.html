Museum Exhibit: Write Only What the Record Shows

Emma | Language Arts | Wednesday, November 25, 2026 | 55 minutes

What you are learning: Write complete, factual museum-label sentences from source notes.

Learn First

An exhibit label names the object and describes only what a record supports. Each complete sentence needs a subject and a verb. Make an editing audit: compare dates, counts and descriptions against the source. Mark unsupplied facts unknown.

Two worked examples

Fragment: 'Two metal-rimmed wheels.' Complete: 'The record lists two metal-rimmed wheels.'
Unsupported: 'The cart was the fastest.' Supported: 'The record does not give the cart's speed.'

Source and materials

FICTIONAL HARBOR MUSEUM INVENTORY
Recorded April 12, unspecified year.
Handcart: 1 wood frame; 2 metal-rimmed wheels; 1 handle.
Donated by a local workshop; a sketch is displayed.
Builder, use, year made, speed and price are NOT recorded.

Directions: Answer all six questions using the teaching and source. The same six questions appear on the matching four-page printable PDF. Write legibly, scan every page and Finish Class in the School Portal. Ask for help if needed.

All six questions

Question 1. Write five complete facts from the inventory; underline the subject and circle the verb in two.
Question 2. Repair these fragments: 'One wooden frame.' and 'A sketch next to the cart.'
Question 3. Label claims as supported, contradicted or unknown: two wheels, made in 1890, four wheels, one handle, fastest cart, metal rims.
Question 4. Draft a 70-90 word exhibit label with title and four or more verified details.
Question 5. Create a three-row revision audit listing your statement, source evidence and any correction.
Question 6. Write the final label and a two-sentence curator note naming two important unknowns.

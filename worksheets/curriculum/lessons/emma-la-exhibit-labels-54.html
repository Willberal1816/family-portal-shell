Museum Exhibit: What Can the Record Prove?

Emma | Language Arts | Wednesday, November 25, 2026 | 55 minutes

What you are learning: Write complete source-backed sentences and distinguish recorded facts, stated reasons and unknown motives.

Learn First

Read the source as a detective. First mark a recorded fact (what was counted, seen or written). Next ask whether a reason is actually stated. Words like because, so and wanted need evidence. Two events happening in order do not prove why the later event happened.

A complete sentence has a subject (who or what) and a verb (what it did or is). Add a time or count only when the record gives it. A note such as “three lamps” is not yet a sentence. Write “The inventory lists three lamps.”

Use three columns: claim | exact record | verdict. A claim can be supported, contradicted, or unknown. If the record does not give the reason, write “The record does not explain why.” A direct quote can support a stated reason if the speaker is named.

Two worked examples

Practice record: “10:00, two guests enter. 10:10, one guest leaves.” Fact sentence: “At 10:10, one guest left.” Unknown: “The guest left because the room was cold.” The record gives no reason.
Practice donation note: “Lee said, 'I donated the clock so children could see its gears.'” Supported stated reason: “Lee said the clock was donated so children could see its gears.” Do not claim that Lee built the clock unless another record proves it.

Source and materials

FICTIONAL HARBOR MUSEUM RECORD
April 12: inventory lists 1 wooden handcart frame, 2 metal-rimmed wheels, 1 handle.
10:00: cart placed near entrance. 10:20: cart moved to an exhibit case.
Workshop donor: "We donated it so visitors could see the wheel design."
Builder, manufacture year, price and reason for the 10:20 move are not recorded.

Directions: Read Learn First and both worked examples. Complete all six questions. The same questions and source appear in the four-page native printable. Scan every page, then Finish Class in the School Portal. Ask for help when needed.

All six questions

Question 1. Turn these notes into three complete factual sentences: one wooden frame; two metal-rimmed wheels; one handle. Underline a subject and circle a verb in each sentence.
Question 2. Make a three-row claim/evidence/verdict table: the cart has two wheels; the cart cost $50; the cart was moved because it blocked the entrance. Write supported, contradicted or unknown and the precise evidence or missing fact.
Question 3. A draft says: "At 10:20 the cart was moved because the staff disliked it." Rewrite as two complete sentences, one naming the recorded event and one naming what is unknown.
Question 4. Write one complete sentence giving the donor's stated reason, with attribution. Write a second sentence explaining why that does not tell us who built the cart.
Question 5. Draft a museum label of four or five complete factual sentences using at least four verified details, one exact time and the donor's stated reason. Use no invented cause.
Question 6. Audit your label using three entries: sentence, exact source evidence and correction if needed. Write the corrected final label and one sentence naming a question the record cannot answer.

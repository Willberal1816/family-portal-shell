Build a Strong Paragraph

Natalie · Language Arts · Learn First
What you are learning: Build a focused paragraph with a clear point, specific evidence or details, explanation, logical order, and a satisfying ending.

Learn the idea

A strong paragraph is more than several sentences about the same broad topic. It has a controlling idea: one focused point the paragraph develops. Every sentence should help the reader understand that point.

Specific evidence or details make the paragraph believable and useful. If the point is “The storm changed the town quickly,” details such as flooded streets, a closed bridge, or a power outage do more work than vague wording.

After a detail, explain why it matters. Evidence answers “What happened?” Explanation answers “So what does that show?” Writers often skip this step, leaving the reader to make the connection alone.

Order matters. Arrange ideas so the reader can follow them—by cause and effect, time, importance, or another logical pattern. Transitions should clarify the relationship rather than decorate the paragraph.

Words you will use

  • controlling idea: the focused point the paragraph develops
  • evidence: specific information supporting a point
  • explanation: reasoning showing why evidence matters
  • transition: a word or phrase connecting ideas

Worked examples

Weak: “The hike was difficult. It was hard. We were tired.”
Stronger: “The hike became difficult after the trail turned uphill. Loose rocks slowed each step, and we stopped twice to catch our breath. Those details show that the steep, rocky section—not simply the distance—made the hike challenging.”
Useful structure: point → detail → explanation → another detail → explanation → closing sentence.

Guided checks

Which is stronger evidence: “the room was messy” or “three stacks of books blocked the doorway”?
The second is concrete and observable.
What should usually follow evidence?
Explanation of what the evidence shows.
What should happen to an interesting sentence that does not support the controlling idea?
Remove, move, or revise it so the paragraph stays focused.

Before you practice

Strong work shows: one focused idea, precise details, explanation after evidence, purposeful order, and revision for clarity—not mere length.

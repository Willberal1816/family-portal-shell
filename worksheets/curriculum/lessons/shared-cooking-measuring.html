Make Pancakes from Scratch

Emma + Natalie · Cooking / Life Skills · Learn First
What you are learning: Read and follow a real recipe, measure accurately, mix batter correctly, use heat safely, recognize doneness, work as a kitchen team, and clean the workspace when the food is finished.

Today you are actually cooking

Measuring is one cooking skill, not the whole cooking class. Today you will use measuring cups and spoons while making a batch of pancakes from scratch. Read the entire recipe before starting so you know which tools, ingredients, and steps are coming next.

Dry and liquid ingredients are measured differently. Spoon flour into a dry measuring cup and level it rather than packing it down. Read milk in a liquid measuring cup on a flat surface at eye level. Measuring spoons are used for the smaller amounts of sugar, baking powder, salt, and oil or melted butter.

Mixing also changes the finished food. Whisk the dry ingredients together first so the baking powder and salt are spread evenly. Mix the wet ingredients separately, then combine wet and dry. Stir only until the dry flour disappears. A few small lumps are fine; beating pancake batter for too long can make the pancakes tougher.

The skillet or griddle is the hot part of this lesson. A responsible adult should be nearby for the heating and cooking portion. Keep handles turned away from the edge, use a spatula instead of fingers near the hot surface, and never taste raw batter because it contains uncooked egg and flour.

Watch the pancake rather than only watching a clock. When bubbles appear on the surface and the edges begin to look set, slide the spatula underneath and flip once. The second side usually cooks faster. A finished pancake should be cooked through, lightly browned, and not wet in the center.

Recipe — about 8 medium pancakes

  • 1 cup all-purpose flour
  • 2 tablespoons sugar
  • 2 teaspoons baking powder
  • 1/4 teaspoon salt
  • 1 cup milk
  • 1 large egg
  • 2 tablespoons neutral oil or melted butter, plus a small amount for the pan if needed
  • Optional after cooking: fruit, syrup, cinnamon, or another family topping

Equipment

Mixing bowl, whisk or fork, dry measuring cup, liquid measuring cup, measuring spoons, skillet or griddle, spatula, plate, and a towel or cloth for cleanup.

Cooking steps

  1. Wash hands, tie back long hair, clear the counter, and gather everything before turning on heat.
  2. Whisk flour, sugar, baking powder, and salt in the bowl.
  3. In the liquid measuring cup or a second bowl, combine milk, egg, and oil or melted butter.
  4. Pour the wet mixture into the dry mixture. Stir just until the flour disappears; do not try to make the batter perfectly smooth.
  5. With an adult nearby, heat a lightly greased skillet or griddle over medium to medium-low heat.
  6. Pour roughly 1/4 cup batter for each pancake. Cook until bubbles form and the edges begin to set, usually about 2–3 minutes.
  7. Flip once and cook the second side until golden and cooked through, usually another 1–2 minutes.
  8. Each student should measure ingredients and cook at least two pancakes rather than one person doing all the work.
  9. Turn off the heat, serve the pancakes, and evaluate whether they are browned, fluffy, and fully cooked.
  10. Wash dishes, wipe the cooking surface after it is safe to touch, and leave the kitchen ready for the next person.

Guided checks

Why do you level flour instead of packing it into the cup?
Packing can add too much flour and make the batter thicker and the pancakes heavier.
What tells you a pancake may be ready to flip?
Bubbles appear on top and the edges begin to look set.
Why should you stop mixing once the flour disappears?
Overmixing can make pancakes tougher; small lumps are okay.
What should happen before anyone walks away from the cooking area?
Turn off the heat and make sure the hot pan is in a safe position.

50-minute plan

About 5 minutes: read/setup · 10 minutes: measure/mix · 20 minutes: cook · 5 minutes: taste/evaluate · 10 minutes: wash and reset the kitchen. The goal is competent real cooking, not filling time.

Culinary Foundations 1: Kitchen Setup & Pancakes

Emma + Natalie · Culinary Foundations · Lab 1
Starting from zero: Today is your first culinary lab. You are learning how a cook gets ready before food touches the pan: sanitation, recipe reading, mise en place, choosing measuring tools, safe heat habits, teamwork, cooking, tasting, and complete cleanup. You will use those foundations while making pancakes from scratch.

1. Set up like a cook

Sanitation first: wash hands with soap, secure long hair and loose sleeves, clear the work surface, and use clean tools. If you touch something dirty, raw egg, your face, hair, a phone, or the trash, wash your hands again before returning to food.

Read before doing: a recipe is a plan. Read the ingredient list, equipment, and every step before starting. Notice which steps use heat and which ingredients must be measured before the pan is hot.

Mise en place means “everything in its place.” Gather ingredients and tools, then arrange them so you are not searching for a measuring spoon while something is burning.

2. Learn the measuring tools

Use dry measuring cups for flour and other scoopable ingredients. Spoon flour into the cup and level the top; do not pack it. Use a liquid measuring cup for milk and read it on a flat surface at eye level. Use measuring spoons for smaller amounts such as baking powder, salt, sugar, and oil.

Kitchen math: 3 teaspoons = 1 tablespoon. 4 tablespoons = 1/4 cup. 8 tablespoons = 1/2 cup. These relationships will matter throughout the course when you scale recipes.

3. Understand mixing

Whisk the dry ingredients together first so baking powder and salt spread evenly. Mix the wet ingredients separately, then combine wet and dry. Stir only until the dry flour disappears. Pancake batter can have small lumps; overmixing can make the pancakes tougher.

4. Learn basic heat safety

A responsible adult should be nearby while the skillet or griddle is hot. Keep the handle turned away from the edge. Use a spatula instead of hands near the cooking surface. Never leave active heat unattended. Do not taste raw batter because raw flour and egg are not ready-to-eat foods.

Heat is a control, not simply “on” or “off.” Medium or medium-low heat gives the inside time to cook before the outside burns. If pancakes brown too quickly, lower the heat; if they stay pale and take too long, raise it slightly.

Recipe — about 8 medium pancakes

  • 1 cup all-purpose flour
  • 2 tablespoons sugar
  • 2 teaspoons baking powder
  • 1/4 teaspoon salt
  • 1 cup milk
  • 1 large egg
  • 2 tablespoons neutral oil or melted butter, plus a little for the pan if needed
  • Optional after cooking: fruit, syrup, cinnamon, or another family topping

Equipment

Mixing bowl, whisk or fork, dry measuring cup, liquid measuring cup, measuring spoons, skillet or griddle, spatula, plate, and towel or cloth.

Cooking procedure

  1. Wash hands, secure hair/clothes, clear the counter, and read the full recipe.
  2. Gather every ingredient and tool before turning on heat.
  3. Measure flour, sugar, baking powder, and salt. Whisk the dry ingredients together.
  4. Measure milk, egg, and oil or melted butter. Mix the wet ingredients.
  5. Combine wet and dry just until the flour disappears.
  6. With an adult nearby, heat a lightly greased skillet or griddle over medium to medium-low heat.
  7. Pour about 1/4 cup batter per pancake. Watch for surface bubbles and edges beginning to set.
  8. Flip once. Cook the second side until golden and the center is no longer wet.
  9. Each student measures ingredients and cooks at least two pancakes. Switch jobs so both practice the same skills.
  10. Turn off heat, taste and evaluate the pancakes, then wash dishes, put ingredients away, and reset the kitchen.

More worked examples

Teaching example: What does mise en place mean for this recipe?
Answer / reasoning: Read first, gather ingredients and tools, measure what you need, and organize the workspace before cooking begins.
Teaching example: Why level flour instead of packing it?
Answer / reasoning: Packing adds extra flour and can make the batter too thick and the pancakes heavy.
Teaching example: What tells you a pancake may be ready to flip?
Answer / reasoning: Bubbles appear on top and the edges begin to look set.
Teaching example: What should happen before anyone leaves the cooking area?
Answer / reasoning: Turn off the heat, place the hot pan safely, and make sure the cooking area is secure.

50-minute lab plan

8 minutes: sanitation, recipe reading, mise en place and tool orientation · 10 minutes: measuring/mixing · 18 minutes: cooking and heat-control practice · 4 minutes: taste/evaluate · 10 minutes: wash, put away and reset.

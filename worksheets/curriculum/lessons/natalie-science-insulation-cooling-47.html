Insulation Trial: Trace the Heat

Natalie · Science · Saturday, November 14, 2026 · 50 minutes

What you are learning

Compare controlled temperature-change data and describe energy transfer without pretending a temperature reading is a measurement of energy itself.

Learn First — Teaching

  1. Thermal energy tends to flow from a warmer object toward cooler surroundings. Insulation can slow this transfer, but a thermometer records TEMPERATURE, not a direct total-energy measurement.
  2. A fair comparison starts both cups at the same temperature, uses the same volume, room, thermometer method and 15-minute wait, and changes only the cup covering.
  3. The invented data begin at 40 degrees Celsius for both cups. A trial's temperature DROP is 40 minus its ending temperature, in degrees Celsius.
  4. A is insulated: end readings 33, 32, 34 degrees Celsius; drops are 7, 8, 6, with mean drop 7 degrees.
  5. B is unwrapped: readings 30, 29, 31; drops are 10, 11, 9, with mean drop 10 degrees.
  6. The unwrapped cup's mean temperature DROP was 3 degrees Celsius larger in these imaginary trials. State only what the given data show.
  7. Real experiments would require care with hot liquids and adult oversight; this is paper-only fictional data. The results cannot prove every cup in the world cools this way.

Two fully worked models

Model 1

Worked model: Insulated cup A, trial 1 starts 40 and ends 33 degrees; change = 40 - 33 = 7 degrees of cooling. The result is a temperature CHANGE, not 'seven units of heat.'

Model 2

Worked model: Three A drops are 7, 8 and 6; mean = (7 + 8 + 6) divided by 3 = 7 degrees. B's mean drop 10, so the observed difference is 3 degrees.

Important mistake to avoid:

Do not compare final temperatures without naming the same starting temperature and wait time. Do not say the thermometer directly measured thermal energy.

Shading: Five Steps From Light to Dark

Emma | Art | Monday, November 23, 2026 | 50 minutes

Your goal: Use five values and one light source to draw a solid-looking object.

Learn First

Value means lightness or darkness. Make five tones from white paper to the darkest pencil mark. To shade a sphere, keep the side facing light bright and shade the far side darker.

Worked models

A value scale goes evenly from 1 = white to 5 = dark; tone 3 is not as dark as tone 5.
When light comes from upper left, shade the sphere's lower-right side more and cast a shadow to the right.

Check your work: Read the source labels; check calculations, evidence, or drawing guides before turning in.

Source and materials

MATERIALS: pencil, eraser, plain paper; colors optional.
VALUE SCALE: WHITE | LIGHT | MID | DARK | DEEPEST
Sphere: highlight, midtone, darkest core and cast shadow.
No glue, cutting, heat or purchased supplies required.

Answer all six questions using this teaching and source. Scan all four worksheet pages, then Finish Class in the School Portal. Ask for help when needed.

All six independent-work questions

Question 1. Draw five equal boxes and fill them from white to darkest pencil shade; label 1 through 5.
Question 2. Draw a large sphere lit from upper left. Label highlight, midtone, core shadow and cast shadow.
Question 3. Draw a second sphere lit from upper right, then explain how its shading differs.
Question 4. Compare the two studies, mark one transition that needs improvement and carefully correct it.
Question 5. Plan a still life using a cube and sphere with one consistent light source and at least three values.
Question 6. Finish the still life. Write four sentences about the light direction, brightest area, darkest shadow and one improved technique.

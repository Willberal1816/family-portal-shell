Creek Notice: Facts and Complete Sentences

Emma · Language Arts · Wednesday, November 11, 2026 · 55 minutes

What you are learning: Write a short factual report using only a given notice, with exact numbers and complete sentences.

Learn First

  1. A FACT from a notice can be checked against its words or numbers. A guess may sound reasonable but is not supported.
  2. A complete sentence needs a subject and a predicate. 'At the creek before noon' is a fragment because it does not say what happened.
  3. Use a specific noun and verb: 'Volunteers counted 14 birds on Wednesday' is clearer than 'They did some things.'
  4. To report a change, copy the right count and date before forming a claim. Do not add a cause unless the source gives one.
  5. A clear report can have an opening, two supported details and a closing that does not overstate the evidence.

See the complete models

Model 1: Notice says 14 birds were counted Wednesday. Weak: 'A lot of birds.' Better: 'Volunteers recorded 14 birds on Wednesday.' This keeps the exact evidence.
Model 2: Fragment 'Near the creek bridge.' Repair to 'The group met near the creek bridge.' The revised sentence names who did what.
A mistake to avoid: The notice does not prove why the bird count changed; a sensible guess is still not a printed fact.

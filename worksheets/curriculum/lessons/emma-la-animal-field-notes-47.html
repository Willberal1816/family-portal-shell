Animal Field Notes: Build Complete Facts

Emma · Language Arts · Saturday, November 14, 2026 · 55 minutes

What you are learning

Turn a field-table into factual sentences with a named subject, an action verb, and no invented explanations.

Learn First — Teaching

  1. A field note records what the observer actually saw or read. This practice table is FICTIONAL; use only the listed details.
  2. A complete factual sentence needs a subject (who or what) and a verb (what the subject does or is). A place phrase by itself is a fragment.
  3. Start by selecting one table row. Say the animal's name as the subject; attach an accurate verb and detail from that SAME row.
  4. An exact number is a fact; an explanation that something happened 'because' of weather needs evidence the table actually provides.
  5. An observer cannot use one day of notes to prove an animal always acts the same way. Name the date when reporting the behavior.
  6. Make each requested sentence distinct. When directions say FOUR sentences, write four sentences and number them before revising.
  7. Check every sentence by underlining its subject and circling its main verb. Then compare the facts with the table.

Two fully worked models

Model 1

Worked model: Fragment 'At the pond after noon.' Repair: 'The heron stood at the pond after noon.' Subject = 'heron'; verb = 'stood.'

Model 2

Worked model: The table says three frogs were near the reeds on Saturday. Safe: 'Three frogs were near the reeds on Saturday.' Unsafe: 'The frogs were hiding from a storm,' because no storm is recorded.

Important mistake to avoid:

A long phrase with a date is still not a complete sentence unless it has a subject and verb. Do not make up causes or quantities.

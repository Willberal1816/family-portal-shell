Learn First

A survey supports a claim about its actual respondents, not everyone who was invited. Use both numerator and denominator when writing a count-based claim. A record is evidence of what it documents; a promotional statement is an attempt to persuade, not independent proof. Strong sentences stay within the evidence.

Two worked examples

Worked example 1. If 15 of 24 respondents want afternoon hours, write exactly: 'Fifteen of the 24 respondents supported afternoon hours.' Do NOT write 'Fifteen people in town supported it' as the population isn't established.
Worked example 2. If a volunteer log says a door was repaired on October 20, you can say the log recorded a door repair on that date, but it cannot prove the repair caused attendance to increase.
Common mistake to avoid: Avoid 'most residents', 'everyone', 'definitely caused' without appropriate evidence. Always name the survey's responder group and do not turn unanswered invitations into votes.

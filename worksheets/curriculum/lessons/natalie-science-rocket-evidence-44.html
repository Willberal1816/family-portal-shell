Flight Evidence: Averages and Fair Claims

Natalie · Science · Wednesday, November 11, 2026 · 50 minutes

What you are learning: Compare repeated test distances, calculate a mean and range, and explain limits of a small experiment.

Learn First

  1. A fair comparison holds important conditions constant and deliberately changes only the design feature under study.
  2. The mean of three measurements is their sum divided by three. The range is largest minus smallest; the two statistics show different information.
  3. Measurements must include units, here feet. A higher mean does not prove the same result under every condition.
  4. Observations from three trials can support a narrow claim about this test, not a claim about all possible rockets.
  5. A controlled next test should name the variable to change, the measurement, and at least two conditions to keep fixed.

See the complete models

Model 1: Model X distances are 7, 9, 8 ft. Mean = (7+9+8)/3 = 8 ft; range = 9-7 = 2 ft.
Model 2: Model Y distances are 8, 11, 8 ft. Mean = 27 ÷ 3 = 9 ft; range = 11-8 = 3 ft. Y averages 1 ft farther, but its results vary more.
A mistake to avoid: Never conclude a difference is certain everywhere from only three trials; weather, launching force and measurement could change results.

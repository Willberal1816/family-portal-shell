Independent Reading: What a Narrator Knows

Natalie · Reading · Tuesday, November 10, 2026 · 45 minutes

What you will learn: Continue your own book for 25 minutes and distinguish one character's belief from a verified event.
READ FOR 25 MINUTES BEFORE completing the response. Your book is the main source. You do not need to stop after a short passage. This is real reading time, not worksheet time.

Learn First

  1. Continue your current novel from the previous stopping point. Read for 25 active minutes; write down the actual chapter and page range.
  2. Narrator perspective changes which facts are shown. Third-person narration can follow a character's thoughts without making every thought true.
  3. When you describe an inference, use a specific text detail and its correct page location. State one plausible alternative if the passage leaves it open.
  4. Finish with a concise outcome check: what is verified, and what is still unresolved? Do not force an ending.

Two fully explained models

Model: A narrator says 'Ava was sure the door was locked.' This verifies Ava's BELIEF, not necessarily whether the door was locked; the text needs further evidence.
Model: 'Later, Ava tested the handle and it would not move.' Now there is a new observed result. A careful response distinguishes her earlier belief from the later test.
Check this mistake: The narrator may describe fear, memory or suspicion. Keep those distinct from actions and outcomes that the book confirms.

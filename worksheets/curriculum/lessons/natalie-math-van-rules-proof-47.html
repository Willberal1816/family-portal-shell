Library Vans: Test Two Cost Rules

Natalie · Math · Saturday, November 14, 2026 · 50 minutes

What you are learning

Compare fixed starting charges and per-mile costs with complete substitutions, an exact tie, and a counterexample.

Learn First — Teaching

  1. A fixed charge is paid even before travel begins. A per-mile rate repeats with every mile. A rule must keep these amounts separate.
  2. Plan Pine charges $8 to start and $5 per mile. Its total at m miles is 8 + 5 times m. Plan Cedar charges $20 to start and $3 per mile.
  3. To compare correctly, use the SAME number of miles for both plans and show both substitutions in full.
  4. A TIE occurs at an input for which both plans give the same total. One equal example shows a tie only at that distance; it does not mean the rules are identical.
  5. For Plan Pine at 6 miles, 8 + 5 times 6 = 38 dollars. Cedar at 6 miles: 20 + 3 times 6 = 38 dollars. They tie there.
  6. A counterexample is one valid input that disproves a universal claim such as 'Pine is always less expensive.' At 8 miles, Pine is 48 dollars and Cedar is 44 dollars.
  7. Do not assume one trip price proves a rate. Describe separately what the start fee and rate do to the cost.

Two fully worked models

Model 1

Worked example: At 2 miles Pine costs $8 + $5 x 2 = $18. Cedar costs $20 + $3 x 2 = $26. At TWO miles, Pine is $8 cheaper.

Model 2

Proof model: The plans tie at SIX miles because Pine and Cedar both equal $38 when m = 6. At EIGHT miles Cedar is cheaper. The correct claim is conditional, not universal.

Important mistake to avoid:

Showing just a total is not a proof of a rule comparison. Show each fixed fee, each rate times the SAME mileage, then the completed cost.

Science: One Change, Four Things Stay the Same

Emma · Thursday, October 15, 2026 · 50 minutes · Review and practice, not a new science topic

What you will learn

Yesterday you correctly calculated three averages and explained how energy changes form. Today you will practice the two things that need more work: designing a fair test and writing a conclusion without making up measurements.

Your finished work: a simple two-page science plan. You do not need to build a ramp or buy supplies. This is a paper design, not a real experiment.

1. Look at the model

Changed variable: the landing surface. Measured outcome: distance rolled in inches (in). Keep the ball, ramp, slope, release height, and measuring tool the same. The starting line is the end of the ramp.

2. A complete worked example

Question: Does a marble travel farther on cardboard or felt?

Change one thing: cardboard versus felt. Measure: distance in inches, starting at the end of the ramp.

Keep the same: marble, ramp, ramp height, release line, and ruler. Safety: keep the marble away from mouths and clear the floor so no one slips.

Do three trials for each surface. Because we are only planning a test, leave the six trial-result boxes EMPTY. You cannot know the results before actually testing.

Correct future conclusion sentence: “After testing, the average on ______ was ______ in and the average on ______ was ______ in. The difference was ______ in, so ______ rolled farther in this test.”

3. Now plan a DIFFERENT test

On the printable, compare construction paper with an old towel. Write the one changed variable, the measured outcome and its unit, four things kept the same, the exact measuring start line, and one safety step.
Draw a simple ramp and label the release line, ramp end, and distance measured. You can copy the kind of diagram above, but use your two new surfaces.
Set up a blank table for three trials on each surface. Do not put invented numbers in it. Instead write one complete conclusion frame with blanks for averages and difference. You will fill those blanks only after a real experiment.
Remember: Feet and inches are valid units. In this lesson, use inches (in) consistently; do not mix feet and inches without converting. Most importantly, leave all six measurements blank until a real test is performed. That is good scientific honesty.

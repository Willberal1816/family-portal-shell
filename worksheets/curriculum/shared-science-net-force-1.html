What Changes Motion?

Emma + Natalie · Science
Directions: Draw force arrows for at least four problems. Include magnitude and direction in nonzero answers. Predict means state the first change you expect in motion. Explain means give the answer and one sentence using net force, velocity, or inertia as the reason.

Combine forces

1. 5 N right + 7 N right
2. 9 N left and 4 N right
3. 12 N forward and 12 N backward
4. 15 N east and 6 N west

Predict motion

5. A cart at rest gets 20 N right and 8 N left. Write the net force with magnitude and direction. Then write one sentence predicting the cart’s first motion change.
6. A puck is sliding right when the net force becomes 3 N left. Write one sentence describing what can happen to its rightward velocity before it ever reverses direction.
7. A bicycle is moving steadily when the forces become balanced. Write Yes or No: does it instantly stop? Then write one sentence using inertia/net force to explain.

Draw and explain

8. Box: 10 N right, 4 N left, 2 N left. Draw arrows and find net force.
9. Invent two force combinations both producing 5 N right.
10. A student writes 8 N right + 3 N left = 11 N right. Write the correct net force with direction, then write one sentence naming the student’s error.

Apply

11. Emma: Give (1) one balanced-force playground/household example and one observable motion clue, and (2) one unbalanced-force example and one observable motion-change clue.
12. Natalie: An object moves west while net force points east. Give two possible velocity changes over time and explain why force direction is not always motion direction.
13. Connect net force to Newton’s First Law/inertia in one sentence.

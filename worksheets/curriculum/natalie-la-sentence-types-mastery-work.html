Kinds of Sentences — Independent Work

Complete all parts independently after Learn First. Mark whole clauses before choosing a sentence type.

Reference: S = one independent clause. CD = two independent clauses joined correctly. CX = an independent clause plus at least one dependent clause. A clause includes a subject and a verb.

Identify the structure

1. For each sentence, write S, CD, or CX. Then underline every full clause directly in the printed sentence.
SentenceType
My sister and I packed the cooler.
The clouds grew dark, so we hurried to the car.
Although the trail was muddy, we kept walking.
The dog barked and scratched at the door.
When the timer rang, the cookies were ready.
I finished the puzzle, but my brother kept working.
2. Explain in 1–2 sentences why My mom cooked dinner and washed the pans. is not compound even though it has two verbs.

Repair clause boundaries

3. Repair The rain stopped we went outside. in two different ways: once with a period and once with comma + a joining word.
4. Add the needed punctuation: Because I finished early I read my book. Then label the sentence S, CD, or CX.
5. Repair the comma splice: I was tired, I finished the chapter. Write one correct version and name the rule you used.

Build the types

6. Combine The rain stopped. We went for a walk. into one correct compound sentence using so.
7. Combine I finished my homework. I watched a movie. into one correct complex sentence using after.
8. Write one original simple sentence with a compound subject or compound verb. Underline the one independent clause.
9. Write one original compound sentence and one original complex sentence about the same topic. Underline the independent clause(s) and circle the word that joins or begins the dependent clause.
10. Transfer check.

Read: Before the game began, we found our seats, and my cousin bought popcorn.

Mark the clauses. Then explain whether the sentence contains more than one independent clause and at least one dependent clause. Do not rely only on the joining words.

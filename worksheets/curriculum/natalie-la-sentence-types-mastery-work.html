Kinds of Sentences — Independent Work

Complete this on your own after today’s teaching and guided examples. Mark whole clauses, not just one word.

S — simple: one independent clause. It can still have more than one subject or verb.
CD — compound: two independent clauses joined correctly, often with comma + FANBOYS.
CX — complex: one independent clause plus one dependent clause. Common dependent-clause starters include because, when, after, although, while, since, and if.
1. Write S, CD, or CX: My sister and I packed the cooler.
2. Write S, CD, or CX: The clouds grew dark, so we hurried to the car.
3. Write S, CD, or CX: Although the trail was muddy, we kept walking.
4. Underline both full independent clauses directly in this printed sentence and circle the joining word: The power went out, but we found flashlights.
Optional rewrite if you need more room:
5. Make one compound sentence using so. Include the comma where it belongs. Start with: The rain stopped. We went for a walk.
6. Make one complex sentence using after. Start with: I finished my homework. I watched a movie.

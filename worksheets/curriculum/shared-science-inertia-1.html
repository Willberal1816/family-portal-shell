Newton's First Law & Inertia

Shared Science
After the Newton's First Law video: use the examples below to connect the rule to real motion. Inertia means an object tends to keep doing what it is already doing—staying still or moving the same way—unless an unbalanced force changes it. When a question says explain, answer the question and give one reason using force, motion, or inertia.

Part A — Check the main idea

1. A book is resting on a table. Gravity pulls down and the table pushes up with equal force. Is the book's motion changing? Explain.
2. A hockey puck moves at steady speed in a straight line on nearly frictionless ice. Does it need a forward unbalanced force just to keep moving? Explain using Newton's First Law.
3. A soccer ball is sitting still. What must happen for its motion to change?
4. In one sentence, explain inertia in everyday words.

Part B — Small hands-on test

You need: a cup, an index card or stiff piece of paper, and a coin. Put the card across the top of the cup and the coin on the card. Before touching it, predict: What will the coin do if you flick the card sideways quickly?
5. Write your prediction and why.
6. Try it. Describe what happened. If you do not have the materials, explain what you expect from Newton's First Law instead.
7. In 1–2 sentences, say what the coin was already doing before the card moved and explain how inertia accounts for what happened next.

Part C — Balanced or unbalanced?

8. ← 5 N   [box]   5 N →   Are the horizontal forces balanced? What happens to the box's motion?
9. ← 3 N   [box]   8 N →   Are the forces balanced? Which direction is the net force?
10. A bicycle is moving forward but slowing down. What does that tell you about the net force compared with the bicycle's direction of motion?

Part D — Explain real situations

11. A car stops suddenly and a loose backpack slides forward. In 1–2 sentences, explain what motion the backpack was already making and why it keeps moving forward when the car stops.
12. Why does a seat belt matter when a car stops quickly? Write 1–2 sentences that name inertia and explain how the belt changes the passenger's motion.
13. A rolling ball eventually stops on carpet. Does this disprove Newton's First Law? Name the force that changes the motion.
14. Correct this statement: “Anything that is moving must have a force pushing it forward.”

Part E — Apply the idea

15. Give two everyday examples: (1) one object that stays still because forces are balanced and (2) one object whose motion changes because forces are unbalanced. For each example, name the important forces and write one sentence explaining the motion.

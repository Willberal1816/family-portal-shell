Remember: steady velocity can occur with zero net force. A nonzero net force changes velocity—speed, direction, or both.
1. The puck is already sliding right at a steady speed on nearly frictionless ice. Circle YES / NO: does it need a forward net force to keep moving? Explain using velocity and net force.
2. A cart travels east at a constant 2 m/s in a straight line. State the acceleration and the net force, then explain how the motion evidence supports both answers.
3. The moving box has equal 6 N horizontal forces. Find the horizontal net force and describe what those forces alone would do to the box’s horizontal velocity.
4. A ball rolls right across carpet and slows. Name the force responsible for the change, state its direction, and explain why the ball’s motion is evidence that the horizontal net force is not zero.
5. The car and backpack are moving right. The car stops suddenly. Draw the backpack’s initial motion tendency and explain the event using inertia. Then name the force that eventually changes the backpack’s motion if it hits the seat/dashboard.

7. Mistake analysis. A student says, “If an object is moving east, the net force must point east.” Explain why motion direction and net-force direction are not the same thing. Give one counterexample.
8. Transfer. A spacecraft is already moving through a region where external forces are extremely small. Its engines turn off. Predict the motion over the next several minutes and explain how Newton’s First Law supports the prediction.

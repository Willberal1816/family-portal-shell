Fraction Division — Groups in Real Problems

For every story problem: 1) label the total amount, 2) label one group’s size, 3) say what the quotient counts, 4) calculate, and 5) write the answer with a unit.
1. Trail mix bags. You have 4 12 cups of trail mix. Each bag holds 34 cup. How many full bags can you fill? Show the division and check by multiplying back.
2. Ribbon pieces. A ribbon is 6 23 feet long. Each piece is 56 foot. Find how many pieces fit and explain in one complete sentence what your quotient means.
3. Predict before solving. Without calculating first, circle whether 3 ÷ 25 should be greater than 3 or less than 3. Then solve and write one sentence explaining why your prediction makes sense.

4. Recipe batches. You have 2 14 cups of oats. One batch uses 35 cup. Solve. Then state both: (a) how many full batches can be made and (b) what the fractional part of the quotient means.
5. Error check. A student writes 5 ÷ 12 = 5 × 12 = 2 12. Correct the work. In 2–3 sentences, explain the mistake and give a multiplication check.
6. Match the equation to the story. Which equation fits this situation: “A 3 34-yard roll is cut into pieces that are 38 yard each”? Write the correct division equation, solve it, and label what the quotient counts.

7. Compare two group sizes. You have 4 liters of juice. Cup A holds 12 liter. Cup B holds 23 liter. Find how many of each cup size can be filled. Then write 2 sentences explaining why the smaller cup produces the larger quotient.
8. Decide whether the answer is reasonable. A student says 2 12 ÷ 14 = 6. Without redoing the student’s exact steps, use meaning and a multiplication check to decide whether 6 is reasonable. Show your check and write one sentence with your conclusion.
9. Create and solve. Write your own realistic “how many groups fit?” problem using one mixed-number total and one fractional group size. Then solve it, label what the quotient counts, and verify by multiplying back.

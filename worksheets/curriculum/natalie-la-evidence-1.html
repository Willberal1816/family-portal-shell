Use a Clue from the Text

Natalie · Language Arts
Directions: Separate what the text directly states from what you infer. Evidence is an exact word, action, or detail from the text. Explain the connection means state the idea, name the evidence, and write one sentence saying how that evidence supports the idea.

Evidence or inference

1. “Maya placed the untouched sandwich back in the bag and pushed the bag away.” Write one direct observation and one reasonable inference.
2. “The porch light was on at noon, newspapers stacked by the door, and no one answered.” Write two possible inferences. Then choose the better-supported one and write one sentence naming the clue that makes it stronger.

Claim + evidence + reasoning

3. “Eli reread the directions, erased the first line, and began again more slowly.” Support the claim that Eli cares about accuracy: name one exact action from the sentence, then write one sentence explaining how that action supports the claim.
4. “Nora laughed with the group, but after everyone left she returned to the table and smoothed the torn photograph.” State the feeling you infer. Then name two details from the sentence that support that feeling.
5. Explain why “The character is nervous because she is nervous” is circular and rewrite with observable evidence.

Weigh evidence

6. Claim: “The town expected flooding.” Rank these clues from strongest to weakest support: sandbags outside stores; a child wore red boots; weather office issued a river warning. Then write one sentence explaining why your #1 clue is strongest.
7. Why can a relevant clue still fail to prove a claim by itself?

Apply

8. Science fair passage: Jalen straightens labels, takes a slow breath, explains before being asked, then writes a note afterward. Infer before/after feelings with two details.
9. Write 6–8 sentences supporting “Jalen is prepared even though he may be nervous,” including evidence, reasoning, and one uncertainty.
10. From your current book, state one inference, give two clues, and explain each.

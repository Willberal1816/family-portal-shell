Claim, Evidence & Reasoning

Natalie · Language Arts · Independent Work
Directions: Separate what an author claims from the evidence used to support it. Strong evidence must actually connect to the claim; reasoning explains that connection.
Passage: Our town should add more shade trees near playgrounds. On hot sunny days, playground equipment can become uncomfortable to touch, and shaded areas give families a cooler place to rest. A city report from a nearby community found that shaded play areas were used for longer periods during summer afternoons. Planting trees also takes planning because roots, water, and maintenance matter.
1. State the author’s main claim in your own words.
2. Identify two pieces of evidence from the passage. For each one, explain how it supports the claim.
3. Which sentence is a limitation or complication rather than supporting evidence? Explain why recognizing it makes the argument more accurate, not weaker.
4. A writer adds: “Trees are beautiful.” Is that strong evidence for this particular claim? Explain what makes evidence relevant.
5. Write one additional piece of evidence that would strengthen the argument. Name what kind of source could provide it.
6. Write a short claim about a school or community improvement. Add one relevant piece of evidence and two sentences of reasoning that connect the evidence to the claim.

Edit Your Sentences

Natalie · Language Arts
Directions: Name the issue when asked and write a complete corrected version. When a question says explain, write one sentence naming the effect of your editing choice on meaning, pause, or emphasis.

Boundaries

1. Identify and repair: “The experiment worked, the measurements were inconsistent.”
2. Repair: “Because the second source was written twenty years later.”
3. Give two correct punctuations for “The first plan was cheaper it required a longer drive.” Then write one sentence comparing how the pause or emphasis changes between the two punctuation choices.

Concision and precision

4. Revise: “In my personal opinion, I think the ending was unexpected and surprising.”
5. Revise: “The reason the bridge failed was because the center part in the middle bent downward.”
6. Replace vague wording: “The source says some things that show the event was bad.”

Parallel structure

7. Revise: “The committee needed to gather evidence, interviewing witnesses, and a written report.”
8. Complete a parallel list: “A strong lab report should describe the question, ___, and ___.”

Passage edit

9. Edit: “The witness remembered the storm clearly, she wrote her account many years later. Because memory can change. The record is still useful due to the fact that it gives details, but we should also compare it with other sources too.”
10. Write 7–9 sentences about a decision/problem. Mark one boundary edit, concise rewrite, precise-word revision, and parallel-structure check.
11. Copy one sentence from your work where more than one punctuation choice could be correct. Name the punctuation you chose, then write one sentence explaining why it fits the meaning you wanted.

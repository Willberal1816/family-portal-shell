Check Another Source — Practice

Shared Social Studies
History File: The Riverbend Flood
These are fictional practice sources. Corroboration means checking two or more independent sources to see where their evidence agrees, differs, or adds detail. When a question says explain, state your answer and point to the specific source evidence that supports it.

Source Set

Source A — Letter from a shop owner, April 14:
“Just after nine, people began moving boxes from the shops closest to the river. The water had already crossed the footpath, but Main Street was still open when I left.”
Source B — Town meeting record, April 14:
“At 9:40 a.m., the road supervisor ordered the east end of Main Street closed because water had reached the roadway near the bridge.”
Source C — Newspaper published April 15:
“Yesterday's flood forced Main Street to close during the morning. Several merchants moved goods to higher shelves, and the bridge area remained blocked until late afternoon.”
Source D — Photograph caption in a family album:
“Main Street bridge during the April flood, sometime before lunch.” The photo shows water over part of the road and people standing behind a wooden barrier.

Part A — Compare the evidence

1. List three facts that are supported by more than one source. Name the sources that support each fact.
2. What does Source B tell us that Source A does not?
3. What does Source A tell us that the official town record does not?
4. Choose one: the sources mostly contradict each other, or they mostly describe different parts/times of the event. Then give two specific source details that support your choice.

Part B — Choose evidence for a question

5. If you wanted to know the official time Main Street was closed, which source would you rely on most? Why?
6. If you wanted to know what local shop owners were doing before the closure, which source is most useful? Why?
7. Which source gives visual evidence? What can it confirm, and what can it not tell you by itself?

Part C — Make a careful conclusion

8. Write a 4–6 sentence account of what most likely happened that morning. Use evidence from at least three sources and avoid claiming more than the sources show.
9. Name one important question that these four sources still do not answer. What new independent source would you look for?
10. In 2–3 sentences, define corroboration in your own words. Then give one example of two sources in this set that support the same fact.

Challenge

11. Imagine a fifth source says, “Main Street was closed before 8:00 a.m.” Name two next steps you would take to check the new claim before accepting or rejecting it.

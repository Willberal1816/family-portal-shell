Classroom simplified longitude strip (not a political-zone map):

West90°W60°W30°W0°30°E60°E90°EEast

Move EAST → later local solar time. Move WEST → earlier. Difference in degrees ÷15 ≈ hours. Across 0°, ADD both magnitudes. Across the International Date Line: west adds a date, east subtracts a date (clock offsets also matter).

1. From 75°W to 30°E, show the crossing-zero longitude calculation, hours and east/west direction.
2. From 45°E to 90°W, show the crossing-zero calculation and whether local solar time is earlier or later.
3. At 60°W it is 7 AM. Estimate solar time at 30°E; show 90÷15 and the whole clock arithmetic.
4. A learner calculates 90°W to 30°E as (90−30)÷15. Diagnose the mistake and show the correct result.

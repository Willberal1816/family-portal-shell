Find Clues in the Story — Practice

Emma · Language Arts
For every answer: first decide what the clues show, then point to the exact words that support your idea.

Part A — Read closely

Passage 1: Priya set her alarm thirty minutes early. Before bed, she placed her new hiking shoes beside the front door and checked the weather forecast twice. In the morning, she was dressed before anyone else came downstairs.
1. What can you figure out about how Priya feels about the hike?
2. Copy or name two exact clues that support your answer.
Passage 2: Marcus carried the poster board into class with both hands. When one corner bent against the doorway, he stopped, smoothed it carefully, and checked the tape on every picture before putting it on the table.
3. What can you figure out about the poster or Marcus's feelings about it?
4. Name the strongest clue. Then write one sentence explaining why it supports your idea better than the other clues.
Passage 3: The sidewalk shone under the streetlights. Water ran along the curb, and three umbrellas leaned open on the porch to dry. Tessa stepped outside in boots and avoided the puddles.
5. What probably happened recently?
6. List three details that support your answer.

Part B — Choose the stronger idea

Text: “Eli read the same message three times, smiled, and immediately called his grandmother.”
7. Choose A or B. Then copy or describe two details from the sentence that support your choice.
8. Write one different idea that could be true but is not supported well enough. Then write one sentence naming what evidence is missing, so the idea stays a guess.

Part C — Show instead of tell

9. Choose one feeling: excited, worried, annoyed, proud, embarrassed, or impatient. Write a 5–7 sentence scene that shows the feeling without naming it. Use actions, dialogue, or details as clues.
10. Underline two clues in your scene. Then write what a reader should be able to figure out from them.
11. Reread your scene. Add or revise one detail that would make your intended idea clearer without directly stating the feeling.

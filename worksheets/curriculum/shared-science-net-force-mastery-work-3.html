4. Three-force cart. Two people push a cart right with 5 N and 4 N. Friction pushes left with 6 N. Draw all three force arrows. Combine forces pointing the same way first, then calculate the horizontal net force and predict the velocity change.
5. Balanced while moving. A puck slides east at a steady velocity while forces of 2 N east and 2 N west act on it. Write 3 sentences: (1) the net force, (2) whether the forces are balanced, and (3) why the puck can still be moving.
6. Catch the misconception. A student writes, “If an object is moving right, the net force must point right.” Give one situation that proves this statement can be false. Include the object’s starting motion, the force direction, and what happens to its velocity.

7. Vertical forces. A hanging sign has 18 N of gravity downward and 18 N of support force upward. Calculate the vertical net force. Then write 2 sentences explaining what balanced vertical forces mean for its vertical velocity.
8. Turning counts. A toy car moves north at constant speed, then a sideways force makes it curve east while its speed stays about the same. Did its velocity change? Was the net force zero or nonzero while it turned? Explain in 3 complete sentences.
9. Design a force case. Create your own force diagram with at least three horizontal forces. Make the net force nonzero. Label every force, calculate the net force and direction, and write 2 sentences explaining how an object’s velocity would change.
10. Final rule. Complete both statements in your own words: Balanced forces tell me… and Unbalanced forces tell me… Your answer must mention net force and velocity change.

Coordinate Clues — Read, Plot & Explain

Use x first, then y. For every plotted point, say the horizontal move, the vertical move, and the quadrant or axis as requested.
1. Read the signs. For each point, write its horizontal move, vertical move, and quadrant or axis: A(−4,6), B(5,−3), C(−7,−2), D(0,4), E(8,0).
2. Plot and label. Plot P(−6,4), Q(3,7), R(5,−5), and S(−3,−6).
3. Check your plot. From your graph in #2, list the two points below the x-axis and write one sentence explaining the coordinate clue that proves they are below it.

4. Catch the swap. A point should be (−2,5), but someone plotted (5,−2). Plot both points, label the intended point I and the mistaken point M, then write 2 sentences explaining what was swapped and how the quadrants prove it.
5. Axis reasoning. Write one ordered pair on the x-axis with a negative x-coordinate and one ordered pair on the y-axis with a positive y-coordinate. Then write one sentence explaining why neither point is inside a quadrant.
6. From moves to a point. Start at (0,0). Move left 7, up 2, right 4, then down 6. Show the route with arrows on the plane. Write the ordered pair after each move and circle the final pair.

7. Mystery point. A point is in Quadrant III, its x-coordinate has an absolute value of 4, and its y-coordinate is 3 units farther from 0 than its x-coordinate. Find the point, plot it, and write 2 sentences showing how every clue fits.
8. Rectangle transfer. Plot A(−5,4), B(3,4), C(3,−2), and D(−5,−2). Connect them in order. Write the horizontal and vertical side lengths and explain in 2 sentences how matching x- or y-coordinates helped you find them.
9. Create and prove. Create four different points: one in each quadrant. Plot and label them. Then write each ordered pair and a sign-pattern check showing why each point belongs in its quadrant.

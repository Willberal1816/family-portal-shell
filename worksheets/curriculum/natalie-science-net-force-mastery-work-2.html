Tracking #

Why Things Keep Moving — Net Force Mastery

For each situation, state: (1) whether velocity changes, (2) whether net force is zero or not zero, and (3) the force(s) causing or balancing the motion.

1. A puck glides east at a steady 3 m/s on nearly frictionless ice.
2. A book rests on a table. Name the two main vertical forces and explain why it stays still.
3. A bicycle moving forward slows when the brakes are squeezed.
4. Two students push a cart in opposite directions with equal 30 N forces. The cart is already moving steadily right.
5. A car turns left while keeping the same speed. Did its velocity change? What does that tell you about net force?
6. A passenger moves forward when a car stops suddenly. Explain what keeps the passenger moving and what force changes that motion.
7. Correct this statement: “An object needs a forward net force to keep moving at constant speed in a straight line.”

2. Tug rope. Team A pulls left with 6 N and 8 N. Team B pulls right with 11 N. Combine each side, find net force, and write 2 sentences explaining which way the rope accelerates and why.
3. Moving puck. A puck is already moving right. Forces are 3 N right and 9 N left. Find net force and write 2 sentences explaining how its rightward motion changes.

4. Three-force sled. Forces are 2 N left, 5 N left, and 9 N right. Make a direction ledger, find net force, label balanced/unbalanced, and predict the velocity change.
5. Balanced while moving. A skateboard rolls west at steady velocity. Forces are 10 N west and 10 N east. Write 3 sentences: net force, whether forces are balanced, and why the board can still be moving.
6. Do not count arrows. A cart has two forces right: 3 N and 3 N. One force points left: 10 N. A student says right wins because there are two right arrows. Correct the student with the calculation and a 2-sentence explanation.

7. Vertical ledger. A hanging decoration has 24 N downward from gravity and two upward support forces of 14 N and 10 N. Calculate the vertical net force and explain what happens to vertical velocity.
8. Mixed direction challenge. A toy car is moving left. Horizontal forces are 12 N right, 4 N left, and 3 N left. Find net force. Then explain in 3 sentences what happens to the car’s leftward velocity before its motion could eventually reverse.
9. Build your own ledger. Create a situation with at least three forces where the object has a 4 N net force left. Draw or list the forces, show your totals, and explain the velocity change in 2 sentences.
10. Final transfer. In your own words, explain why these are two different questions: “Which way is the object moving?” and “Which way is the net force?” Use one example in 3–4 sentences.

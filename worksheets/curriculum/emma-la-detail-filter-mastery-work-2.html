Strong Details — Aim Every Detail at the Main Idea

For each detail ask: Does it prove the main idea? Is it specific enough to picture or understand?
1. Detail filter. Main idea: The hike was exhausting. For each detail, write KEEP or REPLACE and give a short reason: (a) “My backpack was red.” (b) “I stopped twice on the last hill because my legs were shaking.” (c) “We climbed for three hours before reaching the overlook.” (d) “My shoes had white laces.”
2. Make it specific. Rewrite each weak detail so it gives a concrete picture: (a) “The dog was loud.” (b) “The storm was bad.” (c) “The room was messy.”
3. Precise verb. Replace the weak verb in each sentence with a more exact verb, then explain what your new verb shows: “The puppy went across the kitchen.” “The cyclist went down the hill.”

4. Keep every detail on target. Main idea: The kitchen was busy before dinner. Choose the three details that best support the main idea, then explain why each one belongs: A. Dad stirred sauce while checking the oven timer. B. The window curtains were blue. C. Mom chopped vegetables while the girls set plates and cups. D. The dog slept near the couch. E. Two pans sizzled while someone rinsed dishes at the sink.
5. Replace the off-target detail. Main idea: The soccer practice was challenging. Details: “We ran five laps before drills. I had to retry the passing pattern three times. My water bottle had a sticker on it.” Replace only the third detail with a new specific detail that proves the main idea. Then explain how it connects.
6. Show the feeling. Write 2–3 sentences that show a character is excited about a surprise trip without using the words excited, happy, thrilled, or eager. Use actions, dialogue, or body language.

7. Error analysis. Main idea: Our science experiment was difficult. Paragraph: “We measured the water three times because the first two amounts did not match. The thermometer kept slipping out of place. My notebook has a green cover. We had to restart when the timer was set wrong.” Identify the detail that misses the target, explain why, and replace it with a better detail.
8. Build a detail ladder. Main idea: The park was crowded on Saturday. Write three different specific supporting details. Beside each, finish the sentence: “This proves the main idea because…”
9. Fresh paragraph — independent mastery. Choose ONE main idea: (A) Getting ready for the family trip was hectic. OR (B) Our project took more work than I expected. Write a 7–9 sentence paragraph. Begin with a clear main-idea sentence. Include at least five specific, relevant supporting details. Underline the main idea and put a ✓ beside each detail only after checking that it proves the main idea. End with a useful closing sentence.
10. Self-check. Name one detail from your paragraph that is especially strong and explain why it is both specific and relevant. Then name one detail you considered but rejected because it did not support the main idea.

Write a Clear Summary

Natalie · Language Arts
Directions: Read the passage twice. First identify the central idea; then select only details needed to understand it. When asked to explain or defend, state your choice and give one specific reason tied to the central idea.

Read first

1. River Garden Project: For years, an empty lot beside the Riverbend library collected weeds and trash. Last spring, library staff invited neighbors to suggest new uses. Most proposals focused on a community garden. Volunteers cleared debris, tested soil, and built raised beds after learning the original ground contained poor-quality fill. A hardware store donated lumber while families brought seeds and tools. By midsummer, the beds held vegetables, herbs, and native flowers. The garden became a teaching space for lessons on insects, plant growth, and cooking. Organizers say it still needs regular watering and maintenance, and they plan two more beds next year.

Select

2. State the central idea in one sentence.
3. List four details important enough to consider for a summary.
4. Identify two details that can be omitted without losing the main idea. For each detail, write one short reason it can be left out.
5. Choose essential or optional for the reason raised beds were used. Then write one sentence explaining how your choice affects understanding of the central idea.

Paraphrase

6. Paraphrase the sentence about donated lumber and family supplies.
7. Combine the crop list into a broader accurate phrase.

Draft and evaluate

8. Write a 4–5 sentence objective summary including original problem, response, result, and ongoing/future work.
9. Revise any unnecessary copied wording into your own words.
10. Explain why “I think this was an amazing project because gardens are fun” is not an objective opening; revise it.
11. Write a 3–4 sentence summary of a chapter, article, or lesson from this week.
12. Name one strategy you used to decide what to leave out.

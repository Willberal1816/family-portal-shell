Specific Summary + Evidence

Natalie · Language Arts

Mini Lesson

A useful summary names the most important event or idea. Avoid vague statements such as “not much happened”; identify the concrete change, decision, discovery, or conflict.

Practice

1. Write a two-sentence summary of something you read recently. Name one specific event or change.
2. Add one detail from the text that proves the event mattered.
3. Rewrite this vague sentence to be specific: A lot of things happened at the meeting.
4. What is one detail that belongs in a summary and one detail that could be left out?
5. Explain how a summary differs from a prediction.

Add Strong Details

Emma · Language Arts
Directions: Revise for specificity. Do not make sentences longer just to make them longer. When a question says explain, choose your answer and write one sentence giving a specific reason.

Make it specific

1. Rewrite “The cat did something funny” with one clear action.
2. Rewrite “We had bad weather” with at least two observable details.
3. Replace the weak verb in “The horse went across the field” and explain why your verb fits.

Pick useful details

4. Main idea: “The library is a good place to concentrate.” Write three supporting details.
5. Choose the detail that does not support a paragraph about a difficult bike ride: steep hill, loose chain, purple helmet, or strong wind. Then write one sentence explaining why it does not support the main idea.
6. Main idea: “The puppy was nervous at the vet.” Show nervousness with two actions without using “nervous.”

Expand and explain

7. Turn “The cake failed” into 3–4 strong sentences using observable details.
8. Explain why this matters: “The map showed the trail ending at the river, but the bridge symbol was missing.”

Write and revise

9. Write a 7–9 sentence paragraph about a place you know. Give one clear main idea and at least four supporting details.
10. Underline two specific nouns/verbs, circle one improved sentence, and replace one vague word.
11. Describe an ordinary object in three precise sentences without naming it.

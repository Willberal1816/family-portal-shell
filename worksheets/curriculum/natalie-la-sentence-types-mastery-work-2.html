Clause Blueprint — Independent Work

Complete all parts independently after Learn First. For every classification task, mark the whole clauses first, label each IC or DC, and only then name the sentence type.

Blueprints: Simple = IC. Compound = IC + IC. Complex = IC + DC or DC + IC. A clause contains a subject and a verb. A compound predicate can still be one IC.

Build the blueprint first

1.

For each sentence, bracket every whole clause in the printed sentence, write IC or DC above each bracket, then classify it as simple, compound, or complex.

Repair punctuation from the blueprint

4. Run-on repair.

The oven beeped we checked the bread.

First write the blueprint. Then repair the sentence in two different correct ways: one using a period, and one using comma + a coordinating conjunction.

5. Opening dependent clause.

Although the water was cold we kept walking along the shore.

Bracket and label the clauses, add the needed punctuation, and name the sentence type.

6. Comma splice.

I finished the drawing, I still needed to label it.

Explain why the comma alone is not enough. Repair the sentence using a semicolon or comma + coordinating conjunction, then give its blueprint.

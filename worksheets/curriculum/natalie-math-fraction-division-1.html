Fraction Division 1

Natalie · Math
After the teaching video: work slowly enough to explain why fraction division works. Explain means give the answer and one math reason. A reasonableness check means compare the size of your quotient with the numbers in the problem and say why that size makes sense. Fewer strong problems are better than a page of repeated drill.

Part A — What does the division mean?

1. Without using the reciprocal rule first: How many 1/4-cup scoops fit into 3/4 cup? Draw or explain the groups, then write 3/4 ÷ 1/4.
2. A 2-foot ribbon is cut into pieces that are each 2/3 foot long. How many pieces can be made? Write the quotient, then write one sentence saying what that number counts in the ribbon situation.

Part B — Guided procedure

Example: 5/6 ÷ 2/3.
1) Keep 5/6. 2) Change division to multiplication. 3) Flip only the divisor: 2/3 becomes 3/2. 4) Multiply: 5/6 × 3/2 = 15/12. 5) Simplify: 5/4 = 1 1/4.
Reasonableness: Dividing by 2/3 asks how many groups smaller than 1 fit into 5/6, so an answer a little bigger than 1 makes sense.
3. Work 3/5 ÷ 3/4 using all five steps above. Before moving on, check your answer: Is it reasonable for the quotient to be less than 1, equal to 1, or greater than 1? Write one sentence explaining why the size of your answer fits the division problem.
4. Work 1 1/2 ÷ 3/4. Show the mixed-number conversion before using the reciprocal.

Part C — Independent practice

5. 7/8 ÷ 1/2
6. 4/5 ÷ 2/3
7. 3/4 ÷ 5/8
8. 2 1/4 ÷ 3/5
9. 1 2/3 ÷ 5/6
10. 3 1/2 ÷ 1 1/4

Part D — Use it in a real situation

11. A soup recipe uses 3/4 cup of broth per batch. You have 4 1/2 cups. How many full batches can you make? Show your setup and explain the answer in words.
12. A 5 1/4-mile trail is divided into sections that are each 7/8 mile. How many sections is that? If the result is not a whole number, explain what the remainder means.
13. You have 2 2/3 cups of flour and use 1/3 cup for each small loaf. Before calculating, estimate whether the answer should be less than 3, between 3 and 6, or more than 6. Then calculate.

Part E — Find the mistake

14. A student writes: 2/3 ÷ 4/5 = 2/3 × 4/5 = 8/15. Identify the mistake, correct the work, and explain which fraction gets flipped.
15. Write your own fraction-division word problem whose answer is greater than 2. Solve it to prove your problem works.

Evidence Proof — Stay Inside the Sources

Fresh case: Town Trail Closure. Read Sources A–E. Every question tells you exactly what to include. When you use evidence, write the source letter.
Source A — parks notice: “North Loop Trail closed at 9:20 a.m. after staff found water covering the low bridge.”
Source B — rain gauge: “6:00–9:00 a.m.: 1.8 inches of rain recorded at Pine Park.”
Source C — ranger log: “9:12 a.m. — water over bridge boards; orange closure barrier placed at 9:19.”
Source D — visitor photo caption: “Photo taken 9:15 a.m.; water visible across the wooden bridge.”
Source E — social post: “The bridge must have broken overnight.”
1. Find a fact two sources prove. Write one complete sentence stating a fact supported by both A and C. Name both source letters in the sentence.
2. Point to exact proof. Write two bullet points: one exact detail from C and one exact detail from D that support the same conclusion.
3. Decide what is not proved. Is “the bridge broke overnight” proved by Sources A–D? Write YES or NO, name at least two sources that matter, and give 2 sentences explaining your decision.

4. Build the proof ladder. For the claim “The trail was closed because water covered the low bridge,” write four labeled lines: Claim, Point (name the source and exact detail), Prove (what can safely be concluded), and Unknown (one detail the sources do not tell us).
5. Use timing carefully. Using B and C, write 2–3 sentences saying what happened before the barrier was placed. Do not claim that the rain definitely caused the water unless the sources prove that cause.
6. Choose a genuinely new source. The town wants to know whether the bridge itself was damaged. Name one new source not listed as A–E and write one sentence stating the exact information it could add.

7. Rewrite an unsupported claim. Someone writes, “Heavy rain broke the bridge, so staff closed the trail.” Write 2 sentences: first identify the unsupported part; then rewrite the conclusion so every detail is supported by the sources.
8. Write a source-based conclusion. Write 3–4 sentences explaining why the trail was closed. Use at least three named sources. Include one sentence stating something that remains unknown.
9. Transfer the rule. A museum sign says an exhibit closed at 2:00. A maintenance log says a water leak was found at 1:52. A visitor says the room was empty by 2:05. Write 3 sentences: (1) one fact the evidence supports, (2) one cause/detail it does not prove, and (3) one genuinely new source that could answer the missing question.

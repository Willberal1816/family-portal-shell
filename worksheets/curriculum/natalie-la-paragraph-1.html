Build a Strong Paragraph

Natalie · Language Arts
Directions: Answer in complete sentences. Use precise details and explain why they support your point.

Find the focus

1. Write a broad topic sentence about school mornings, then revise it into a focused controlling idea. Explain why the second is stronger.
2. Controlling idea: “The new library layout makes books easier to find.” Which fits better: labeled shelves or blue carpet? Explain.

Strengthen weak writing

3. Revise “The game was exciting. A lot happened. Everyone was happy.” with two concrete details.
4. Add explanation after: “By noon, the creek had risen over the walking path.” The point is that the storm changed routines.
5. Replace vague wording in “The scientist did stuff carefully and got a good result.”

Organize

6. Order and explain: bridge closed; heavy rain fell overnight; buses took a longer route; river rose above the road.
7. Write two cause-and-effect transitions without using “because.”

Build and revise

8. Write an 8–10 sentence paragraph answering: What makes a place comfortable to read or work? Use at least three concrete details and explanation.
9. Underline your controlling idea, box two pieces of evidence, star one explanation, and revise one vague/repetitive/out-of-order sentence.
10. Write 5–7 sentences supporting “Working with a partner can improve a project,” while naming one situation when working alone may be better.

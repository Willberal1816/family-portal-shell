Build a Strong Paragraph

Natalie · Language Arts
Directions: Answer in complete sentences. When a question says explain, state your answer and add at least one specific reason or detail that shows why it works.

Find the focus

1. Write (1) one broad topic sentence about school mornings and (2) a revised sentence with one clear controlling idea. Then write one sentence naming what makes the revised version more focused.
2. Choose labeled shelves or blue carpet. Then write one sentence explaining how your choice supports the idea that books are easier to find.

Strengthen weak writing

3. Revise “The game was exciting. A lot happened. Everyone was happy.” with two concrete details.
4. After “By noon, the creek had risen over the walking path,” add one sentence naming a specific way that blocked path would change what people could do.
5. Replace vague wording in “The scientist did stuff carefully and got a good result.”

Organize

6. Put these four events in cause-and-effect order: heavy rain fell overnight; river rose above the road; bridge closed; buses took a longer route. Then write one sentence explaining how one event caused the next.
7. Write two cause-and-effect transitions without using “because.”

Build and revise

8. Write an 8–10 sentence paragraph answering: What makes a place comfortable to read or work? Use at least three concrete details and explanation.
9. Underline your controlling idea, box two pieces of evidence, star one explanation, and revise one vague/repetitive/out-of-order sentence.
10. Write 5–7 sentences supporting “Working with a partner can improve a project,” while naming one situation when working alone may be better.

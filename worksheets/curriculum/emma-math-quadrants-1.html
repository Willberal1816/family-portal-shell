Coordinate Plane: Four Quadrants

Emma · Math
After the teaching video: today you will use all four quadrants, not just name them. Keep the coordinate order straight: (x, y) = horizontal first, vertical second.

Part A — Build the sign map

1. Complete this from memory: Quadrant I = ( __ , __ ), Quadrant II = ( __ , __ ), Quadrant III = ( __ , __ ), Quadrant IV = ( __ , __ ).
2. Explain in your own words what a negative x-coordinate tells you to do and what a negative y-coordinate tells you to do.
Worked example: For (-4, 3), move 4 left because x is negative, then 3 up because y is positive. That point is in Quadrant II.

Part B — Plot accurately

Plot and label A=(-4,3), B=(2,5), C=(-3,-4), D=(5,-2), E=(0,4), F=(-5,0), G=(1,-3), H=(-2,1).

3. Which plotted points are on an axis instead of inside a quadrant? Explain why.
4. Which two points are in Quadrant II? Which are in Quadrant IV?

Part C — Read coordinates and reason

5. A point is 3 units left of the y-axis and 6 units above the x-axis. Write its coordinate and quadrant.
6. A point has x=4 and y=-7. Describe how to move from the origin to find it.
7. Point P=(-2,5). What would happen if someone accidentally plotted (5,-2) instead? Are they the same point? Explain the change in quadrant.
8. Give one coordinate in each quadrant whose x- and y-values have different absolute values.

Part D — Coordinate route

9. Start at (-4,-2). Move 6 units right, 5 units up, 3 units left, then 7 units down. Write the coordinate after each move and the final quadrant.
10. Start at (3,4). Create a four-move route that ends in Quadrant III. Write each move and every coordinate along the way.

Part E — Mystery points

11. My x-coordinate is negative. My y-coordinate is positive. I am 5 units from the y-axis and 2 units from the x-axis. What am I?
12. Write your own mystery-point clue for a point in Quadrant IV. Make it specific enough that someone can find exactly one point.
